# Runway rules

Applies on top of `CLAUDE.md` and `docs/conventions/` to everything under `src/apps/runway/`. This file is the one copy of these rules.

- **Built for its people.** `PRODUCT.md` "Who It Is For" names them: the owner who tracks (several cards paid in full, pay into Capital One A, cards and bills pulled from a lean Capital One B, goals in one Wealthfront cash account, moving to Link), his brother who plans paycheck to paycheck (Plan only, never logs a purchase) and, later, someone paying off card debt. Every change names which person it serves and is walked as that person, and a change for one never breaks another's journey. For the owner it must never need a transfer between accounts to keep the budget true, and a card charge, a card payment or a purchase must stay quick to add by hand (linking is wanted now, but a card that cannot be linked is still added by hand); a move between their own accounts is never spending or income.
- **A card's due warning sticks, and histories keep a running total (owner, card-first round 3).** A card with money waiting warns 7 days before its due day and stays warned past it until a full or minimum payment, or until the next due day is within 7 days (then "Not paid by Oct 14th" is the subtext); a part payment leaves it up. When the bank's minimum is known, any payment of at least it counts as the minimum, and that minimum is read only for the due date its statement names (`getMinimumDue(statement, forDueOn)`), so last month's never prints against the next due day. A transaction-like history (a card's, any later account log) shows the running balance after each row, worked back from today's figure so it stays right past the listed window. Paying a card never cascades into the plan: what a card owes is already set aside. A change-log entry carries `effectiveFrom` only when it changes what the plan covers from a day; the log reads any non-null value as "Covers income days from…", so a card or purchase entry writes null and says its day in the sentence. **Paid by is never assumed (owner, cards round 4):** a saved reference to another record (a card, an account) that is archived or no longer qualifies starts the form unanswered, never shown as if still chosen, and every optional picker carries its own none pill ("Not chosen yet", "Cash or debit"); a bill asks how it is paid once ("What is it charged to?", its credit cards at Track and, with two or more pull accounts, those accounts, as one pill row), never a card question and an account question side by side (owner, accounts review); a new purchase's card is unanswered until picked, or suggested from the card used most on that envelope's last purchases (`getUsualCardId`) with a thin "What you usually use for Groceries" line that stops after three kept suggestions on this device; what it adds to the card is its own info box under the envelope notice, written as what changes ("Adds $40 to what you owe on Venture X").
- **Tone: plain and matter-of-fact, an exception to "Playful by default".** Runway speaks like a calm advisor: it reports what happened and what it affects ("Dining is $18 over. It would take $18 from Everyday spending.") and stays considerate, never curt, cold or blaming. No scoring, streaks, scolding or cheerleading, and no cute emoji: an emoji appears where the person chose one for an envelope, bill or goal, on a quick pick that sets one (a suggested bill or envelope), and as the fixed mark of a Plan view (💵 Income, 🧾 Bills, ✉️ Envelopes, 🎯 Goals, kept at every width); never in copy, toasts or warnings. A purpose's emoji is one the person chose (or accepted as a suggestion) and cascades to its bills, envelopes and goals unless one has its own. Empty states use a lucide icon in `EmptyCard`, not an emoji.
- **Don't make me think (or read) too much.** Runway handles more money detail than its screens should show at once, so every screen is judged by how little a person has to read and decide before they can act (TurboTax is the model: one question at a time in large type, a running summary, the figure that matters always the biggest thing). Seven rules follow from it, and a screen that breaks one is a finding:
  - **The amount is the hero.** Any money entry (a purchase, a bill, an envelope, a goal, income, Confirm money arrived, a change of amount) opens on a large centred figure (`@/components/AmountHero`, about 48px, tabular, focused on open with a mouse only; on a phone the person's tap raises the keyboard), never an amount box the size of the other fields. A prefilled amount the person usually just accepts (Confirm money arrived) stays unfocused, so the keyboard never hides the button they came to press. The figure reads grouped ("2,140.00") whenever it is not being typed in, and tabbing in and typing replaces it. The ways to change what it is ("It varies", which splits it into two halves "$100 to $140"; "This is a refund"; "A % of pay"; "Whatever is left") are `RevealChip`s under the figure, at most two visible and the rest behind one "More ways" chip, never a pill row above the amount.
  - **One question at a time; answered ones fold.** A create form is a `@/components/QuestionFlow`: the next question in `text-2xl` with only its control, every answered question above it as a one-line summary row that reopens it, and nothing below it. Once every answer has been seen, and on every edit, the form is only summary rows and a row opens its question in its own sheet (a drawer on a phone, a popover or modal from `sm`, with Done), never in place between the other rows. Next is the pinned footer's primary while a question is open and becomes Add only at the review; a Next that is off says why in the footer note beside it. A default that is nearly always right (priority Should, today, every income day, from now on) is pre-answered and shown folded, never asked, but a fact only the person knows (a bill's due date) is never preselected. Escape, back, Cancel and the close button ask Keep editing / Discard once the person has typed an answer. A question whose answer is one tap (an envelope pill, a repeat once its date is in) moves on by itself, so the most frequent entry never gains a Next, and when that answer changes what happens to the money its summary row says so, since the note under the question is passed over ("Yearly, saved up each pay"); a field a chip reveals takes focus. The last step is the folded rows and one primary. Check the primary at 844×390 and 320×568 on the longest flow's last question.
  - **A row is a glance: name, one line, the figure.** Every list row (income, bills, envelopes, goals, a draft's lines, purchases, income days) is the item badge, the name, at most one muted line with the single most useful fact ("Due Nov 1st", "$350 of $600", "Every income day"), the amount on the right and a chevron. No chips, no priority pill, no second detail line. A state that takes the line (an account "· $542.00 short") is added after what tells the row apart (its bank and last 4 digits), never in its place (accounts round 3). The one line sits directly under the name, never pushed down by a figure that has a word under it ("$350" over "of $600" sits beside the name and its line, `ItemRow`; owner, priority extras), and is never cut: when the figure is one line it runs under the figure, it clamps at two lines, three below 360px for a stacked row (never `truncate`) and is written short ("Due Nov 1st", short month names), measured at 320px and at 360px (the first width the two-line clamp applies) with the longest real fact; the figure itself never wraps inside a number (`whitespace-nowrap`, the text column shrinks instead), and only a worded figure ("$720 a year") wraps, between words. The row opens the item's sheet (`ItemSheet`): its head is the figure and one fact line (a change ahead is its own row, notes behind a `HelpTip`), its first action is one honest "Change" door (subtitle naming what is inside: "Amount, name, due date and more") that opens on the most frequent question, the amount, with the item's other useful fields folded under it as the edit view's own summary rows and the rest behind "More details"; only a first date for an undated bill keeps "Set the due date" (owner: "Change amount" hid that a name could be changed). An action row has a subtitle only when it says a consequence or what a door holds, and it does not scroll at 390×844. Moving an action off a row counts its taps before and after for each moment that uses it, and the moment that needs it most keeps it at one tap: ticking a bill on the payday checklist records it paid, with an Undo. A tick that writes shows at once and comes back with a toast that says why when the write fails (marking paid needs a connection); a quick Mark paid that skips the "comes out of Free to Spend" confirm says in its toast what it took from Free to Spend, and its Undo says so when it can't run. A bulk tick ("Mark all paid") writes in transactions that re-read each record, reports the real count, has one Undo that reverses only what it wrote, and never un-does a record made elsewhere: once all is done it is a state, not a control. An Undo of a move between holders (what an account holds) also re-reads every other holder and refuses, naming it, when one has since taken an item it would give back, so one item never ends up in two places.
  - **A shortfall is "$X short" everywhere** (a row, a sheet, a footer note, Home, Days), and a confirmed record says it in the past ("Was $X short" under "Got less than planned"), and "held" names only money that is actually held.
  - **Add actions float.** Each tab's add action ("Add a purchase", "Add expected money", the Plan view's add) is one floating pill at the bottom right (`FloatingAction`: an icon and a label, never a "+", above the tab bar, hidden while a sheet is open), never a header button beside other controls; it may sit over a row mid-list, but every list's last row and chevron scroll clear of it, and it is never hidden to avoid a row (owner, round 4; round 5 dropped hiding it at rest, which made a long page's add action vanish). This replaces the repo's "add action in the header" for Runway.
  - **A correction has a window.** "Change what you started with" is offered only until the first income day after the start is confirmed and no later than 14 days after it; later a balance is fixed by correcting a day. A figure built from parts (Free to Spend) shows its worked sum, one operand per row.
  - **Spend's filters live in a Filter drawer on a phone**, behind one icon beside the search: Kind (Purchases, Money in, Not counted; Money in is every deposit and refund, own-account moves left out, so money that raised Free to Spend is always findable, and Home's Free to Spend breakdown opens it from "Money in this month"), Card or account (several at once, shown from two), Amount (a two-handle `RangeSlider`, hidden while amounts are hidden or made up, since its bounds would put real cents in the URL) and Envelope, each kept in the URL with a chip per filter that is on; a merchant is found with the search, never a filter. Each row names the card or account after its day without a number (`getPaidWithName`, `cleanBankName`), and what is left out (no lines) shows only under Not counted, so it is never on no screen. A card payment or a move between the person's own accounts that the bank names as one (`isBankMove`, its category) is not spending even before it is paired: it waits under Account moves' "Waiting to pair", where its sheet offers "This is spending" (back to Purchases) or says the other side is an account Runway doesn't see. It is judged only by the bank's category, so a send to a person the bank files as an account transfer waits there too, one tap from Purchases; once the person has answered (unpaired it, undone a choice, `assignedBy: 'person'`) it goes to Needs a home instead. While it waits it still counts against Free to Spend through its free line. Names are cleaned only of a masked number (a lone mask letter must touch its digits, so "Venture X 2024" stays): when a link brings them in and on read for display, never on the read an update reseals, so a stored name is never rewritten behind the person's back.
  - **Every Runway list searches from 5 rows** (`RUNWAY_SEARCH_THRESHOLD`, the same for envelopes, bills, goals, purchases, a draft's lines and Starting out's rows), lower than the repo's 8, because people look a bill or goal up by name (owner, round 4).
  - **A count names the first thing it counts.** A row that replaces sentences with a count says the soonest one in the plan's words and counts the rest ("The plan leaves Rent $30 short on Nov 1st · 4 more") from `sm` up; on a phone it is the count alone on one line, in the list's unit, even for one ("1 thing to fix"), and the single-problem sentence shows only from `sm` up; a note that needs no action is not counted. Problems on the same day are one row for that day ("Nov 2nd" over "Groceries $150, Phone and internet $23.75 and Rent $30 short", three names then "and N more"), and the summary counts in the list's own unit, so its count and first item match the rows it opens.
  - **Read nothing you didn't ask for.** A caveat, an explanation or how a figure was worked out sits behind an info icon (`HelpTip`) beside the figure it qualifies, or in the item's sheet; a field's hint is one short line or none, and no sheet body holds a paragraph over two lines.
  - **The biggest number is the one you came for:** Free to Spend on Home (or All money, the card's one switch, kept on this device; a finger on its chart reads that day into the same figure, and lifting it brings today's back), the pay on an income day, saved-of-target on a goal, the amount on a form. Everything else is 14 to 16px.
  - **Budgets, measured at 390×844:** a tab front's first list row starts in the top 40% of the screen with at most 25 words above it; a row is at most two lines of text (three when its name wraps); a form shows one open question and at most four controls besides its footer on open. Print the counts in the PR.
  - **A calm view: nothing competes with the one job (owner, round 4).** Like TurboTax, a person should never feel there is a lot going on. Every view (a tab front, a sheet, a swapped-in view, an editor) has one job named in its title, one emphasized surface, one filled primary and nothing else that asks for attention: what is not needed for that job right now is behind a door, an info icon or the ⋯ More view, never drawn "in case". On a phone, a sheet or view volunteers at most two things beside its subject (a facts line, one warning), draws no repeated label, decorative icon or caveat that is not a decision, and no second control near the primary that looks like it. Before a view is done, list everything it draws and class it subject / decision / status / reference / noise; noise is removed and a reference that isn't why the person opened the view goes behind a door. A view the owner calls busy is a finding of this rule, not a matter of taste. Information a sheet adds beside its subject is a chip that opens its sentence (`HelpTip` `badgeLabel`: a goal's "Interest ~$2.11 · Nov 30th"), never the sentence at rest (owner, goal sheet), and a notice on a bill's sheet uses quiet actions (a `secondary` or text button, never two filled ones), and the sheet shows one notice at a time (Paid from, else Charged to, else the amount); a note that would only say nothing differs ("matches the plan") is not drawn, and a chip that exists only to compare figures is not drawn at all while amounts are hidden (its presence would say they differ; a chip with no figure says nothing); a figure that compares the bank with a typed balance is drawn only when the bank's reading is not older than the typed one (review of goals at the bank).
- **A row leads with what it comes to a month wherever a monthly total sits beside it (owner, yearly bills).** A yearly, every-few-months, weekly or fortnightly bill reads "$12.50 a month" in the bold figure and names its real payment first in the one line ("$150 yearly · due Mar 1st, 2027"), converted by `toMonthlyRange` with the same factors the group total uses, so a live row never out-sizes the total it is part of (a row not yet begun still shows its month though the total counts it as 0) (`describeBillMonthly`, `joinPaymentAndState`). An envelope with an exact amount and a regular beat does the same through `describeEnvelopeMonthly`, a calendar period by its factor and an income-day one by the income days a usual month holds for its pay (`useIncomeDaysPerMonth`, the count the group total uses): "$216.67 a month" over "$100 every income day" (owner, income-day envelopes); one with chosen days, no regular pay or no fixed amount keeps its each-time figure. In a list beside a monthly total a bill paid once reads "once" under its figure; a bill with no fixed beat or a monthly beat keeps its payment. A range figure breaks at "to", never inside a number. A list with no monthly total beside it, or that mixes bills, envelopes and goals (Priorities: `BillRow` and `EnvelopeTile` both take `figureKind='payment'`), and a row on the day the bill is due (the Days calendar, a to-pay card, a checklist) is the payment (`BillRow` `figureKind='payment'`); a bill's own sheet says "Next payment". The converted figure is a long-run share, rounded per row, so rows can differ from their total by a cent or two. A new list beside a monthly figure uses the helpers and never writes its own division.
- **A call to action is a verb and a noun, with no "+".** Runway's buttons, header actions and reveal chips read like a bank's or TurboTax's ("Add a bill", "Add expected money", "Add a merchant"), never "+ Expected" or "+ Add": the words carry the action, so the glyph adds nothing. A screen's one add action names what it makes.
- **A warning looks like one.** Anything that says the plan falls short (the problems row on Home and Days, a short line, a day that leaves a bill short) uses the warning tokens (`warning` tint, a warning-coloured icon chip, readable text in light and dark), never the app's blue, which reads as information. One warning surface per screen; the rows it opens keep the colour on their icon only. Home also asks for one thing at a time: the quiet slot under Free to Spend (the Priorities nudge, else the Track invite) draws nothing while the warning row or "To pay now" shows, or while the lookahead loads; a nudge's dismiss is worded ("Don't ask again"), never a bare X (owner, #608 follow-up).
- **Ask, don't act (owner, round 7).** Runway never moves, releases, covers or cuts money on the person's behalf. When an act leaves money that needs a home or a gap that needs filling (archiving or ending an item that holds money, money that arrives short, a correction that changes a day), the screen asks with the choices side by side, nothing preselected, each saying where the money goes, and writes only what the person picked, with an Undo. A default that saves a tap is still a question the person answers. The one exception the owner chose (Issue 60): a bank charge that is clearly a purchase typed by hand (one candidate, same amount) is merged on its own, only when the charge is recent (14 days), so a first link never merges weeks at once, with an Undo in the toast; **a third exception (owner, remembered moves): a move on a route the person paired by hand before (money out of this account into that account or card), or a deposit from a payer they linked to a pay line by hand before, is paired or linked on its own with an Undo, and only the tie or the amount that the memory settles changes: the amount, the 3-day window and the other rules never widen, the automatic pass never writes a memory, and an account's or pay's sheet lists what is remembered with a way to forget it (`MemoriesSheet`); and the card tie-break: a card payment both sides of which the bank flags (`LOAN_PAYMENTS_CREDIT_CARD_PAYMENT`) and each has exactly one such card candidate is paired on its own with an Undo, after routes and before plain uniqueness, so a same-amount deposit elsewhere no longer blocks it**; **a fourth exception (owner, bills cover themselves): a dated bill due within 30 days or before the next pay, whichever is further, that its pays won't fill in time is covered from Free to Spend without asking**, in the plan's order (Must, Should, Want, then rank) and the earlier payment first, worked out live from the bills, pays and money and never written (`computeBillCover`, `selectBillCover`); the cover is counted in "Held for bills" and nowhere else, so the screens read one figure (`billCoverSelectors.selectBalances`; only selectors that feed bill payments read `selectLedgerOnlyBalances`), it is explained where the person meets it (the Held for bills breakdown and the bill's "Already put aside" row), a Mark paid it already covers asks nothing, and only what Free to Spend can't cover is a Thing to fix; anything less clear asks (a placed history purchase that clearly matches one typed is merged at any age, the second exception below), and an offer that changes a figure says so on the row that saves it ("The bank's is $2.15 more"); a one-tap write that changes a bill's amount says the difference on the control and, when bills cover themselves would take the extra, says Free to Spend covers it ("Free to Spend covers the extra $3.00 for Nov 20th"), and one that would create a Thing to fix opens the bill's Change flow with the amount filled in instead of writing, so its effects and warning show (`BillAmountNotice`). An Undo that moves a record's photos or other child docs re-reads them in its transaction and moves only what still exists.
- **A switch that acts for the person acts only from the day it was turned on, and only where they asked (owner, bills that pay themselves).** Autopay ("It's on autopay", data `paysItself`) is the one exception to Ask, don't act: the person turned it on, so Runway marks the bill's payments paid on their due day at Track; at Link it waits for every charge, card or bank, since the charge already counts against its card or account and a mark beside it would count twice. It never back-pays a payment due before the switch's newest run began (`getPaysItselfFrom`), a mark the person took back is theirs from then on, a reversal the app writes for its own match (Undo, unmatch) is not the person taking a payment back, and every take-back of a mark that settled a charge unmatches the charge with it, and what it could not settle is a quiet task ("Enter what it charged", "Which charge was this?"), never an overdue warning; at Link one unmatched past 45 days goes back to the to-pay list. A switch shown inside a pick-one step turns off that step's auto-advance, so the step gets Next. A new sealed field (`billRef` on a purchase) is copied by every action that rebuilds the payload; grep each `sealJson` call for the payload type before shipping one.
- **A managed item is changed only where its source lives (owner, card annual fees).** A card's annual fee is a bill in the fixed "Card annual fees" group (`CARD_FEE_GROUP_ID`, `cardFeeOf`): its name, amount, date, group and card change only on the card, in the card's own transaction (`getFeePlan`, `applyFeePlan`), and Bills offers only what is the person's to set (the priority, Comes out of, Already put aside, History) plus a door to the card. The group takes no name, emoji, archive or new members, and is left out of every list of groups a name is matched against (the pickers and Starting out's commit); a screen that lists items to remove or start over (Starting out) leaves managed items out of its rows but still counts their ranks and names, and a card edit re-reads the card as the form first saw it, whatever the fee step, since it rewrites the whole sealed card. A future managed item follows the same shape: fixed id, a marker field, one write path from its source, and every picker and editor checked for the lock.
- **A link's history is never counted, and Needs a home never has to be cleared (owner, link round).** Linking never moves Free to Spend through its history: what a link brings in dated before the day it was made is history (`selectIsLinkHistory`), turned to no lines by `useLinkHistory` with one info toast and never read by a pass or an offer (Needs a home lists it only until it is turned, so a charge that still counts is never on no screen; the one-time cleanup of plans already linked only turns what a rule placed, and is remembered as done only once it read loaded inputs and turned everything; one placed by hand is never removed: it is matched to the purchase typed for it, a clear one merged on its own at any age with an Undo (the owner's choice), an unclear one asked under Needs a home's "Might be in twice"); this is the second exception to "Ask, don't act", since the figure the person had already covered it. A waiting charge already counts against Free to Spend, so Needs a home is an optional place to sort, never a demand: its copy never says a purchase must be placed, and every charge offers "Keep in Free to Spend" (no envelope, off the list, with an Undo). **A bank refund never offers to go back to Free to Spend** (owner, bank refund): it already counts there, so its form's Returns to lists envelopes and goals with nothing preselected, and staying in Free to Spend is the sheet's "Leave it in Free to Spend" (the opener passes `isCountedAlready`, since a lost pair or deposit waits without `needsHome`). **Nothing offers "Leave it out of spending" at Link** (owner, money-in round): Free to Spend follows the bank balance there, so a leave-out could never be honoured; the same write is worded for what it means ("Not a pay", "To an account Runway doesn't see"). **Pending money in is "On its way", never counted and never asked** (owner, pending money in): the bank's balance leaves it out, so Spend's Money in lists it under an "On its way" group and Home's Free to Spend breakdown adds an "On its way" row, none of it in "Came in", "Money in this month" or the daily push; a posted money in that a pending money out of the same amount may be the other side of (`findPendingMoveHolds`) waits with it ("Waiting for Checking to clear"), and its sheet offers no pay or leave-out choice, since Plaid gives a posted transaction a new id when a pending one posts. A pending purchase never pairs, not even by hand. Money in waits only when an answer changes something (a card refund, a pay day close by, a likely move whose other side is not already waiting under Account moves, `selectMoneyInAsks`); any other deposit is simply counted, and its sheet says it is already in Free to Spend with "This was a pay" (a one-time pay on its date), and "Which pay is this?" shows only when a pay day is close. The "I'm not sorting yet" switch quiets charges only (money in and moves between accounts still ask), from the moment it is turned on, and turning it off brings back nothing earlier; the daily push counts what the screen lists, so any rule the screen uses to keep a quiet charge (a possible move) is mirrored in the push's count, or named in its doc as skipped. Walk any flow that brings data in (a link, an import, a level change) with Free to Spend read before and after.
- **A row or notice that says "your rule" names the rule's sentence** ("Your rule, Safeway goes to Groceries, waits until you answer the offer above."; a row reads "Your rule" over its sentence), and a rule held for a purchase is the rule that is named, not an offer to make a second one.
- **Speak in the person's nouns, never the data model's.** Copy never says "line", "lines" or "draft": an income day gives each item an amount ("Groceries gets $120 this time", "Change what each item gets", "Why it changed"). Code and data names stay (owner, round 7).
- **Money that arrives different from the plan is a journey, never a dead end (owner, round 7).** Less than planned is a warning `InlineNotice` under the amount. The sheet then says what Free to Spend is and, when it covers the gap, offers to cover it as one tap with an Undo; nothing is ever covered or cut on its own. Whatever Free to Spend does not cover, the sheet proposes what gets less, by priority (Wants, then Shoulds, never a Must or a bill due before the next income day while anything else remains), accepted as a whole or changed one by one. A disabled Confirm says why in the pinned footer with a warning icon, as a door to the fix. More than planned is good news: a success notice, matter-of-fact and warm, as an advisor would put it.
- **Amounts are private until the person shows them.** Every money figure and every line that gives one away ("$30 short", a goal's saved amount) is masked by default on every surface (`@/components/SensitiveValue`), forms and Starting out included, except the field being typed in (owner, round 7). Each tab's header has one eye button on its title line, and every sheet, screen or subview that draws a masked figure carries the same one (`ValuesEyeButton`) on its title line, so the person can show amounts from where they are. The choice is kept on this device only, like a bank app, and hides itself again when the tab has been in the background for five minutes. **Hidden means nothing about the amount shows:** a hidden figure is one fixed mask (`SensitiveValue`: "••••" in the muted colour), never a blur, which keeps the figure's width, minus sign and red; nothing around it may say what it hides either (no lock or open state that compares two amounts, so the held view's account choice reads "Show amounts to see what is free in it" while hidden; no "over"/"left" word, no "refund to", no red or step-down size keyed on its value, no bar, ring or chart drawn to its scale), and a plan-level warning gives away neither amount nor item: while hidden every warning surface (Home, Days, Things to fix, an effect preview, a footer note) becomes one neutral row, "Something needs a look · Show amounts to see it", a door that shows amounts (owner, round 7). Text goes through `MaskedText`, `useMaskedText` or `maskFigures`; a new surface that draws an amount does too, and a row that is not `isPrivate` masks its `detail` and `ariaLabel` as well as its name, since a screen reader reads the label in full (owner, round 4: a red, negative figure was obvious under the blur; round 5: one tap on a row defeated the mask). **Made-up amounts** (Settings) swap every formatted figure for a believable one to show the app off; a figure that bypasses `formatCents`/`formatCentsShort` (a hand-built `$`, a `toFixed` shown as text) would show the real amount there, and a receipt picture is a tile while it is on.
- **"See all" sits on its section's header line; "Show more" is a quiet row under the list.** A list that leads to a fuller screen puts a small "See all ›" right-aligned on its header's title line, as a bank app does; a list that unfolds in place ends in one compact muted row. Neither is larger than the rows' own detail text on a phone.
- **A bill's Mark paid is a button, not a row (owner).** On a bill's sheet it is the one filled, full-width button under the figure, with what it takes as one muted line under it ("$40 comes out of Free to Spend"), since it acts at once rather than opening anything; once paid, "Mark not paid" is a quiet row in the action list.
- **An action row is compact.** The rows of an item's sheet ("Change", "Charged to", "History") use a 16px icon, a 15px title and a 13px subtitle at every width (`ActionRow` without `isRoomy` sizes the icon itself, so callers pass a bare Lucide icon), so four rows fit above the fold at 390×844 with the figure.
- **Theme: restrained blue.** `runway.css` sets the tokens on `html.runway-theme` (cool near-white surfaces, a deep slate-blue primary, a pale blue tint for selections), switched on by `useRunwayTheme()` while the app is mounted. Components use semantic tokens (`bg-primary`, `bg-accent`), never raw palette colors. Body and row text is the normal text color; blue is for filled buttons and real links. Check a filled button, a selected pill, a badge and a highlighted card in light and dark after any token change. **Every Runway chart draws its line and fill in `--chart-line`**, a brighter blue than the primary set only for charts (owner, money chart), and a forecast is the same blue dotted after a Today mark (`isSecondaryAhead`), drawn a year ahead (the lookahead's reach), or only to the last income day when the plan's pay ends sooner (a year of bills with no pay drove the line below zero), only from what the plan knows: income days, bills due, and envelope money counted as spent the day it is given, never a growth guess. A lowest-point callout marks only a real dip (a tenth of the chart's height, not the line's last point), so it never covers the line for a few dollars. A chart on a tab front sits on the page, not in a tinted card, its figure switch is a compact one that hugs its words (`SegmentedControl isCompact`), and it starts folded to a "Show chart" row, then stays open or closed as the person leaves it, remembered on the device (owner, money chart rounds 2 and 3). While amounts are hidden no chart is drawn: a door that shows amounts stands in its place.
- **The level decides the wording.** **Plan and Track stay apart:** a Plan screen never mentions a purchase or a card (the one exception is the Track invite's sheet, whose job is to say what Track adds), and Track only adds to Plan, so the brother's Plan-only journey reads the same with Track on or off. An envelope figure reads _assigned_ at Plan, _left_ at Track and _safe to spend_ at Link, never one word for all three; _safe to spend_ is still the planned figure (Issue 61): money the accounts cannot cover reads as one "Over-assigned $X" notice in Where your cash is and changes no envelope. **At Link, Free to Spend is the cash figure (owner, balance-backed round):** the gap between the accounts and the ledger is one adjustment to the money on hand (`selectBankBacking`, `bankAdjustCents`), so every figure that reads the balances agrees with Where your cash is; it lands in Free to Spend, never in an envelope or goal, typed balances stand in for unlinked accounts, it is off while a counted account has no balance, and Home explains it once (`BankBackedRow`). **A linked account's balance is the bank's (owner, bank source of truth):** once the bank sends a figure it replaces the typed one without asking (`withBankBalances` in the slice, no write) and the account offers no way to type one (`hasBankBalance`); a typed balance stands only where the bank has said nothing. Once Free to Spend comes from the bank (`selectBankBacking().isBacked`), every way to correct a typed start goes too: Change what you started with, Check your balance and the later-fix note. A cash figure built from the accounts that count leaves out every goal and envelope an uncounted account holds, names the accounts it leaves out, says how it differs from Free to Spend, and is unknown, never short, while a counted balance is missing or unread. On Plan's Envelopes list the big figure is always the plan's month at the dial's levels, at every level, so moving the dial moves it; from Track up, the row's line leads with what is left ("$517.72 left · $200 every income day", or "$517.72 left · +$50 want not counted" while the dial leaves an extra out, so it fits three lines at 320px), so what's left is seen without opening each envelope (owner, priority dial: the figure that didn't move read as a bug). **Hidden, not disabled:** what depends on a level the person is not using (a Spend tab, Add purchase, Needs a home, Check an amount) is not on screen at all; `LevelGate` draws its children only at the level that needs them. Data for a hidden level is still listened to.
- **Over is shown, never blocked.** At Track an envelope that a purchase takes below zero reads "−$18.00 over" (`describeEnvelopeFigure`); where what's left leads a row's muted line (Plan's Envelopes list) the "$18 over" lead is in the destructive colour (`ItemRow` `detailLead`), since a muted word can't carry it, Free to Spend falls by exactly that overspend (an envelope total counts an over envelope as zero), and the form says so before saving in plain words. A purchase's "Add" is disabled only for what cannot be saved (no amount, no envelope, a day outside tracking), never for spending too much. **An explanation of money that moved names its cause first and never makes the receiving envelope the subject** ("Dining out ran out, so the rest came from…", then each source with its figure; never "Gifts · ran over from Dining out"); with amounts hidden it is treated like a warning, a neutral line ("Split across envelopes") or nothing, and a preview draws it only when the save would write lines that differ from the saved ones.
- **A range is always labelled** wherever it drives a figure ("planned at the low end, $140"), through the one `RangeNote` component, **and is never recorded as what happened:** marking a ranged bill paid (`PaidAmountSheet`, on every path: the sheet, the to-pay card, a checklist tick; Mark all skips it and says so) and confirming ranged pay ask the real amount, empty, with the range as the hint (`needsPaidAmount`).
- **Plaid is read from Plaid, and what Runway uses of it is written down (owner, fresh balances).** A decision about Plaid (a product, a limit, a cost, an institution's quirk) is checked against Plaid's own docs, read as Markdown (`https://plaid.com/docs/<path>/index.html.md`, the list at `https://plaid.com/llms.txt`), never from memory or a third-party page. The README's Plaid section lists the products Runway calls, the Trial logins used and what to request when leaving Trial; a PR that starts or stops using a Plaid product, or changes how often it calls one, updates it. A bank figure says how old it is, in one wording for accounts and cards ("Updated 4 min ago" on a sheet, "4 min ago" after the bank and last 4 on a row, a dot while under 5 minutes), is never called live, since each one is a single read, and a balance with no date from the bank is not written rather than stamped as new; an account or card that gets the five-minute check carries a "Checks every 5 min" chip on its sheet (never on a row), whose tap explains it and why others lack it; one that doesn't (a bank that shares only a few times a day, or a link whose live reads stopped after a sign-in request) carries nothing extra, never a note that it is slow or off.
- **A bank is not a sign-in (owner, card minimums round 2).** One institution can sit on several links (Plaid Items): the person linked a second card at the same bank later, or the bank keeps separate logins. Consent, reconnecting, account picking, sync and Plaid's billing are per link, never per institution, so anything shown on one card or account that acts on its link says which cards it covers ("Covers Quicksilver and Savor too") or that this card asks on its own ("This card is on its own Capital One sign-in"), and a flow that opens the bank's window for one purpose never picks accounts unless that is the purpose (agreeing to card details opens with account selection off, or the person ticks another sign-in's accounts into this one). Walk every link feature with two links at the same bank, each holding a card. **A bank feature the person turns on is offered as new, never drawn as a standing row:** a card that says what it adds, that it may not apply ("Not every card has one"), its action and "Not now", which folds it to one quiet row kept on this device; once the bank has answered, the screen shows that answer in every state (owed, paid since the statement, nothing due, not shared), never nothing.
- **Money is integer cents, dates are date-only UTC midnight.** Format money with `formatCents` and `MoneyInput`; display a date-only value with `formatDateOrdinalUTC` (an instant with `formatDateOrdinal`), write it with `fromDateInputValue`. Schedules use `getUTC*` math and "today" is `todayAsUtcDate()`. **Dates read with an ordinal and a short month ("Feb 1st", "Mar 31st", "Dec 2nd", the year added outside this one)** (owner, Horizon planning): every Runway date goes through those two shared formatters, a relative day passes `formatDateOrdinalUTC` to `formatRelativeDayUTC`, and only a calendar's day number (a grid cell, an income day's date tile) stays bare. The formatters join a date with no-break spaces, so "Mar 18th, 2028" never splits across lines in a row; measure the longest one at 320px. **A repeating bill's due day is said in its repeat's terms** (owner, due dates): a row and its sheet say "Due the 10th", "Due the last day" or "Due Fridays" (`describeDueDay`), and the full date only where it adds something: inside a week ("today", "tomorrow", "Fri"), once it has passed, and for a once, yearly or every-few-months bill; the form's folded Due row reads "The 10th of each month" (`describeDueAnswer`), its question asks "When is it next due?", and an edit opens on the next due date while keeping the saved first date as long as the schedule counted from it still lands on the date shown, so opening and saving moves nothing. Surfaces that say what is due (the Bills row and sheet, the form, Home's to-pay card) use these words; a line about one dated payment (paid from, a shortfall, a pay plan's set-aside, a move, the change history) keeps the exact date.
- **Sealed documents.** A document has a few readable fields (ids, dates, enums, flags, `createdAt`) and one `sealed` payload for every sensitive value. A readable field never holds a name, an amount, a merchant or a note. Only `security.ts` seals and opens; the data key lives in the query cache and `security.ts` closures, never in Redux or the query persister; the one copy on the device is the opt-in **Trust this browser** one (`runwayKeyStore` from the shared `createTrustedKeyStore`), sealed under a non-extractable key and removed on switch-off, plan delete and sign-out. A per-merchant preference kept on the device (a declined rule offer) stores a digest of the uid and the name, never the name (`utils/ruleDeclined.ts`); a dismissal kept on the device (a suggestion's "Not this one", a declined charged-to) is keyed by that same digest of the uid and the key, never the ids or amounts in plain text. A form's draft kept on the device is sealed too: every draft key starts with `FORM_DRAFT_PREFIX`, which `constants.ts` declares sealed, and `useRunwaySync` hands the draft store `createDraftSealer`, so the key stays in its closure and nothing is kept until it arrives. Home offers one new item's kept form back (`DraftPickUpRow`): a quiet card-coloured row under Free to Spend, never above the money, that opens the form on its own tab after the tab's history entry is written (`showAddOnTab`), so Back still asks Keep editing or Discard; its "Not now" lasts until the app reloads, and a draft for a form the screen would not show yet (a purchase or card below Track, a bill, envelope, goal or card before any income) is not offered.
- **Taking money back checks every holder it returns to, and unlinking asks about what the link added.** An Undo that gives money back to a holder (a goal that paid part of a cost) re-checks it live and refuses with a sentence naming it when it is archived or ended, so money never lands where no screen shows it; unlinking a pairing that added something (a goal's target as an event cost) asks whether to keep it, nothing preselected, unless it is already paid.
- **A link held by a derived key has a way back, and every way out of a pairing undoes all of it.** A day's key holds its date, so a record linked to a day (a deposit's `incomeDayId`) can outlive the key when the payday moves, or lose the day to a second link a transaction could not see. A selector finds such records (`selectLostDeposits`) and returns them to the list they wait in, where the person relinks or leaves them out; nothing linked is ever left on no screen. The same holds for a pair of purchases that are a move between the person's own accounts (`selectLostPairs`): a pair whose other side is gone, points elsewhere or **no longer cancels** (the bank repriced a posted side) is lost, both sides, and returns to Needs a home. A pairing's side effects (the card's `cardPayment` move, the "Move to" tick recorded as `tickedDayKey`) are undone by **every** way out, in the same transaction (`readPairExit`), not only the toast's Undo: unpairing, filing, leaving out, editing, linking a deposit to a pay, pairing again, and an action that reverses part of a pair (Take back on the card's payment) **unpairs both sides**. A send-back (`assignedBy: 'person'`) stops only the automatic pass, never the offer: Needs a home still offers the other side, and when it does, it is the only question the sheet asks. **Every return to waiting by the person (an Undo of a send, of a leave-out or of a rule's batch) writes `assignedBy: 'person'` and `ruleId: null`**, or the pass would place it again. A pass and every helper that holds or excludes for it (`getPairSideIds` for the link-history sweep) read the same memory inputs (remembered routes and payers) and wait for them to load, or the helper races the pass. A memory changes only what the automatic pass does: every offer the person picks from is worked out without memories, so a memory never hides a choice they had, and the Undo of the hand action that wrote a memory forgets it.
- **Rules are the beta tier: owner only, and history is append-only.** Everything under `apps/runway/plans/{uid}` is the owner's alone; `versions`, `changes`, `moves` and a day's records are create-only, so a write over an existing one is refused rather than overwriting (moves and records build their ids from their own fields, so a repeat fails on the id). The rules check no shape (sealed envelope, types, caps, write-once fields, a day's moves): the app's actions keep them. **The link sync's `isChanged` covers every sealed field it writes** (date, status, amount, merchant, category, lines), or a changed value is dropped. **A pass write and a hand write of the same purchase agree on one outcome and one toast:** on a conflict the hand write reads the stored purchase fresh (not the lagging store) and reports what the rule did, with its Undo. A new collection the app edits or deletes in joins the `update`/`delete` lists, and `npm run check:runway-rules` (its `COLLECTIONS` table) says what each one allows.
- **The engine is pure.** Everything under `engine/` takes values and returns values and imports nothing from React, the store or Firebase. A purchase with no lines (money in waiting in Needs a home, a purchase left out of spending) counts nowhere: not as spending, not on a card. **A reader of purchase lines groups by envelope, never by line:** one envelope can appear in two lines (a chosen B and a spill B), so a refund works out what each envelope took and returned and then apportions it to that envelope's lines, and a bank-versus-typed difference lands on the last line that is not a spill (`getRefundLines`, `repriceTyped`). The draft for an income day is computed, never stored or edited in place; the same inputs always give the same lines. Every engine change adds cases to `npm run check:runway-engine`.
- **A repeating bill says which pay carries it ("Comes out of"; data `fundedBy`).** "Spread across my pays" is the default, "The pay right before it's due" puts the whole payment on the last pay in its window, and "A pay I pick" (offered only when there is more than one pay) names a pay line, and for a monthly or twice-a-month line one day of the month ("Work pay on the 15th"). When that pay does not come before the due date or leaves the bill short, and Free to Spend does not cover it (Ask, don't act's fourth exception), the bill's sheet asks with a warning and two quiet choices ("Spread across my pays" with an Undo, "Pick another pay"); nothing is respread on its own (`BillPaidFromNotice`, `planBillPayments`). An envelope or goal can name a pay the same way ("Comes out of", data `funding.pay`, rows don't say it, the sheet's fact line does), and a pay's sheet lists everything that comes out of it, "Paid first" above the rest, as one two-way list: tying an item there writes the item's own Comes out of (owner, envelopes from a pay).
- **A paycheck's plan is steerable: change a pay's share once, see its fallout on the other pays, then be offered to keep it for every pay (owner, Comes out of).** On an income day a bill's first action is "Change what this pay puts in" (`BillShareView`), not the bill's amount: the question names the bill, the figure is this pay's share, and an effects card lists this pay, every other pay in the same payment's window ("Nov 30th pay $750 → $1,500"), "Left from this day" when it moves, and a warning when no pay would carry the rest, all from the engine (`previewBillShare`), never guessed. The share is the day's own (`DayOverrideSealed.billCents`, which becomes `fixed` for the engine, so the draft, the other pays and the lookahead all follow it) and the line stays at nothing instead of vanishing so it can be taken back ("Back to the plan's amount"). When the change is one pay doing it all, a second question asks "Keep it this way for every paycheck?" with "Yes, always from the pay on the 30th" and "Just this time" side by side and nothing preselected; Yes writes the bill's Paid from and drops the day's own share, and both end in an Undo toast; closing the question any other way counts as "Just this time". One-time pays (a freelance payment, a bonus) are never part of a rule and never a rule's pay; when the question is withheld because two or more repeating pays are left, the saved view says so in one line with a door to Paid from. A bill's sheet says which pay it comes out of ("Paid from · The pay on the 30th", one tap to that question), changing Paid from clears the bill's per-pay changes (and the form says "This replaces 1 change you made for one pay"), and a form's preview names only the problems the choice causes, never the plan's standing ones. A per-pay preview's shortfall comes from the drafted lines, never from planned shares; a pay that puts in less pushes the rest onto the next repeating pay (never a one-time pay); the keep question shows the rule's effect; and on an income day every first action on a bill line changes this pay's figure (or says why it cannot: a pay that pays the bill first, a bill that comes from another pay). A figure a person pins below what a payment needs is reported by the lookahead as a shortfall ("the plan leaves Rent $750 short on Dec 1st"), and a pay rule whose pay isn't there says the pay doesn't come before it is due. **A two-write action reports the partial result it reached, never "Nothing was changed"** (Yes writes the rule, then clears the day's own figure: if the second fails it says the rule is saved and offers the same Yes again, which skips the write already made), and its Undo re-reads the bill and the days as they are now, changes only the fields its action wrote and only while they still hold what it wrote (it refuses and says so otherwise), and words the log line by what it restores. **The "What changes" card is a diff of drafts:** each pay's line for the bill is the one the day screen draws (`computeDraftView` on the open days and with the figure layered), with the other items that move beside it (three and "and N more"), never the plan's shares; `previewBillShare` only gives the cap, the shortfall and the rule. **One helper owns a bill's per-pay figures:** every writer of a bill's Paid from (the keep question, the sheet notice, the bill form) calls `clearBillPins` in the same flow, from the day the change starts, with one history line and the cleared figures kept for its Undo (`getBillPins` is the one list a count and a clear share); a pay that pays the bill first takes no per-pay figure, so its sheet does not offer one.
- **Phone first, nothing disabled to explain.** Home, Days and Plan are the only tabs; every other section is a `Subview` or sheet reached from them. A subview title fits 200px at 320 wide at `text-2xl` ("Account moves", not "Moves between your accounts"); a door with a long name keeps it on the Spend row and uses the short one on its screen. A figure says what it counts and what it leaves out, and a gap in the plan is never shown as a gap in the person's finances.
- **Every write dated in a closed month marks its closing stale.** A closing caches what the ledger summed to at a month's end, so a write that adds, changes or removes a day (confirm, correction), a bill paid or undone, a purchase or the starting day reads the plan in the same transaction and writes `dirtyFrom` through `getLedgerMark`/`markLedgerWrite` (`store/actions/closingActions.ts`); a new action or server pass that writes one of these does the same (a server batch that can't share the plan's transaction marks before and again after it lands, and marks a changed amount or lines as well as a changed date), and a new figure the balances read from the ledger is added to `closing()` in `engine/closings.ts` and to the equivalence check in `check:runway-engine`. Spending stays apart in a closing so the level can change. While closings are rebuilt the app draws "Updating your history" and nothing else, never a figure from before the change.
- **Scale.** Lists of envelopes, bills, goals and purchases are checked at 5 and at 50. `scripts/seeds/runwayScale.ts` is the oversized fixture (`npm run seed:runway-scale`, then sign in as Morgan: five years of Track with 25,000 purchases and no closings, so the first open builds them; `RUNWAY_SCALE_CLOSINGS=1` seeds them too; eight of its bills are left unpaid for their last 40 days, so the to-pay card is crowded); extend it whenever a list is added, and judge performance on it per CLAUDE.md. A long list of rows mounts in chunks of 12 under `LazyMount`, and the lookahead, the one figure that walks a year of income days, is drawn after the screen has painted (`useDeferredLookahead`), so no figure holds up Home or Days.
- **A trimmed total never outlives the visit, and the figures beside it follow it.** A setting that makes a figure count less than everything (Plan's priority dial) is held in memory only and cleared when Runway unmounts, marks its control while it trims (a halo on the dial's knob), says what it counts in the figure's own words ("a month, Musts only"), and trims every subtotal on the same screen (group headings) too, so no two figures on one screen disagree. The dial's monthly total counts every kind of commitment the plan holds (bills, envelopes, goals and Horizon items, each Horizon cost at its own priority, `cost.priority ?? item.priority`), shows at every dial position ("Everything" at Want, with a help icon naming what it counts), and a new kind of commitment joins it, the dial's count and its dimming in the same PR. Every part is on one basis, a usual month: a dated thing (a goal, a Horizon cost) counts at the steady pace that meets its date from what is not yet held, never what a calendar window happens to ask, so one big payment entering next month never jumps the total. Money is counted once: an event's goal cost is left out of the total only while its linked goal's own pace counts it (a dated goal being saved for, not funded by whatever is left, and counted at the dial), and kept in otherwise. A row it leaves out dims its badge and figure only (and its warning line turns plain), never the whole row's text, and says "Not in this total" to a screen reader (owner, priority dial).
- **An item's priority parts are named by level wherever they appear apart (owner, priority extras).** An envelope or goal can add an extra at each level below its own (never on a bill, a share of pay or "whatever is left"); wherever its parts are drawn separately (an income day's lines, a record, Priorities) each says its level after the name ("Groceries · must", "Groceries · want"); a row for the whole item shows what its parts ask together ($200 for $150 Must +$50 Want, owner) and names an extra only when the dial leaves it out ("Every income day, +$50 want"), and its sheet spells out the split ("$150 must, +$50 want"); a sheet for an item the dial leaves out says "not in this total" and a figure line that shows an item's parts never also shows its balance; a part never moves past its neighbours' levels, and every total the dial trims counts each part at its own level. Every path that picks a new rank counts extras' ranks too (`getNextTierRank`, Starting out's `toTopRanks`), every path that changes an item's priority, amount or kind keeps its extras or says which come off before saving, a rewrite of older stretches addresses an extra by its level, never by its position, and a screen that splits an item into rows draws its balance once.
- **An envelope's priority and emoji wear one badge each.** `PriorityBadge` is a soft tint (Must a primary tint, Should the accent, Want muted), never a solid fill; `ItemBadge` is the envelope's emoji, or the first letter of its name when it has none. A row pins both to its first-line band so a long name wraps without moving them.
- **A goal is being saved for or not, one idea with one test (owner, goal fields).** "Not saving for it yet" (the "Skip for now" button under "How often does money go in?" when a goal is added, never a pill among its answers; an envelope gets the same button and reads "Nothing goes in for now") and Pause are the same state to the person: the row says "Not saving for it yet" for both, the sheet's one way back is "Start saving" (it opens How often when nothing goes in, and also ends a pause) and its one way out is "Stop saving for now", and an edit never offers the skip again unless the goal is still idle. `isSavingFor` is the one test, and no total, pace line, calendar entry or pay-first line spells it again: the monthly figure, the track line, Days and every pace figure count only goals it passes, while what is saved (the Goals total, the Plan overview's Goals tile, a group's "$X of $Y saved") counts every goal's money, since money already held is saved whether or not more goes in (owner: linked goals read "$0 of $0 saved"). An edit of a goal idle today and in every later stretch moves no money; a funded stretch ahead keeps "When it starts", and a quiet line under the total counts the rest ("2 goals you are not saving for yet"), and the goal's sheet line says it too in place of its funding. A pace line about the coming days ("not reached in the next year at this amount") is said only of a goal money goes into.
- **A field's label is a short phrase, never a full question.** A folded row, a section caption or a field heading reads like its neighbours ("When it starts", "How often", "Comes out of"); a question is asked only by the open question a `QuestionFlow` step shows (owner, goal fields). A label's choices answer it in its own words: under "When it starts" they read "From now on", "From a later date", "From this income day on", "Just this income day" and "For a while, then back", never a term the person has to decode ("For a stretch").
- **An edit form's title follows the name being typed.** The four item forms title themselves with the typed name (the saved one when the field is empty), so every question opened after a rename carries the new name.
- **Stopping an item is not erasing it.** An item's sheet (`ItemSheet`) pauses a goal, ends an item after a last day, or archives it; each shows its effect on the coming income days first and asks "Why?" once. Ending and archiving set a write-once header field and leave every version, record and confirmed day as it was. A change that lasts for a stretch is a version with `effectiveUntil`, and the draft says so on the first day the earlier amount is back ("Vacation fund returns to $300.00 this time").
- **A reference from one record to another's versions checks it is still alive.** A Horizon link (`{ changeId, itemId }`) is dead when its item was archived or ended on or before the change's day or a later change from the same day replaced it (`getLinkSpan` `isLive`, `getItemStops`), and a link to an item's end is dead once that end is cancelled; a dead one never decides a derived state (a changes-only event's Done), reads "No longer planned" where it is listed, and stays until the person unlinks it. Any new stored pointer at another item's versions gets the same check, and its PRODUCT.md says what a dead one shows. A view that ties, moves or cancels a change checks "not started" against `todayAsUtcDate()` when it writes, never only against a `today` held since it opened, and shows the person only the failure sentences the app wrote, never a raw Firestore error.
- **A form's preview shows the effect of what the person changed, never of the form's starting values.** A change form seeded from the version in force draws nothing until a field differs (`useChangeFlow` sends no change while `isUnchanged`), so opening it never previews a move nobody made.
- **A single-field edit never overwrites a later-dated version.** An edit that lasts from a day on (any change through `useChangeFlow`, a group's due date, a group move) is written once per stretch of the item's versions (`getVersionStretches`), carrying only the fields that differ (`applyFieldChanges`), so renaming Rent keeps its scheduled "From Jan 1st, 2027 · $1,250 → $1,300"; a window ("for a stretch") is clipped the same way, and an edit to the field a later change also sets says so ("This replaces your change from Jan 1st, 2027 (…)") in the review and the reason step. The effects preview is built from the same stretches the write uses. Its "With this change, …" warning names only what the change adds, matched one to one by item, kind and day (`getCreatedProblems`): a problem is old only when the same item had it on the same day or earlier with at least the same shortfall, an earlier day, an extra day or a bigger shortfall is the change's and says "$2.00 more short", and "nothing moves" is never printed beside a warning. The effect block is not drawn until there is an effect, and the review step repeats the one-line warning. Save compares what it would write with what the person reviewed and refuses, staying open, when the item changed elsewhere. A change that touches only a name, emoji, group or card, or any change to a goal idle in every stretch from today, moves nothing: no "When it starts", no effects, Save instead of Next with no reason step, and its change entry writes `effectiveFrom` null.
- **A bulk write offers one Undo, unless it confirms first.** A write that changes several items at once (a group's one due date, a change to items ticked in a Plan view) captures each item's stretches before writing and puts one Undo on its success toast that rewrites exactly those; Plan's pick-several (press and hold a row or a group heading in Bills, Envelopes or Goals) stays in the view it began in, pauses the swipe and asks "Leave selecting?" before another pill clears it, skips items a change would not touch, keeps failed ones ticked, and offers a view only the changes its own form offers, the same way the form offers them (Paid with only where the bill form asks it; Comes out of with its three cards and every real pay, leaving a one-time bill's days alone); the bar says what the selection comes to a month through the screen's own totals (`usePlanTotals`, `useGoalsMonthly` with `onlyIds`), at the dial, naming how many add nothing (`usePlanSelection`, `usePlanBulk`, `PlanBulkActions`); a partial write or partial undo names the items that did not change, and an item edited since the save is left alone and named.
