# A-List Tracker rules

Applies on top of `CLAUDE.md` and `docs/conventions/` to everything under `src/apps/a-list/`. This file is the one copy of these rules; `.github/instructions/a-list.instructions.md` only points here.

- **Theme: AMC and the theater.** Casual, warm and playful, never corporate or masculine. The palette (AMC red primary, theater-gold accent, rose-tinted neutrals) lives in `a-list.css` as tokens on `html.a-list-theme`, switched on by `useAListTheme()` while the app is mounted so portaled modals and drawers match. Change the look there, not per component, and use semantic tokens (`bg-primary`, `bg-secondary`, `bg-accent`) rather than raw colors.
- **Round everything.** Inputs are `rounded='full'` (also on `FormFactories` fields), buttons that are pills or CTAs are `rounded='full'`, cards and sheets `rounded-2xl`, badges `rounded-full!`. Options a user toggles or picks among are `Pill`s, never radio buttons or tab strips.
- **Emoji carry meaning.** Every status, priority, stat and empty state gets its own fitting emoji (`WATCH_PRIORITY_EMOJIS`, `ViewingStatusBadge`); never reuse one emoji across a list. No green check marks for "seen".
- **Badges are ours.** Use `PriorityBadge`, `ViewingStatusBadge` and `FormatBadge` from `components/shared`, not a stock `Badge` with ad-hoc text.
- **Subviews, not tall drawers.** A flow that needs search, several steps or a long settings form (adding a movie, Membership settings) is a full-page `@/components/Subview` that renders its own back control (`AddSubview`, `MembershipSettingsSubview`); see docs/conventions/design-ux.md "Subviews" for when to use one; the app's screens stay mounted underneath. Short lists and details use a `Drawer` that swaps its content in place with a "‹ Back" link (`DayDrawer`); never stack one overlay on another.
- **No "Back home" in the page.** The app shell gives Home and the other apps through the header's menu (hamburger) and, on sm+, the header's home icon. Only the app's landing page (`AppEntryFallback`) says "Back home". Inside a subview the header is hidden and Back is the way out.
- **Setup can be dismissed.** Closing the setup modal lands on `AppEntryFallback` (Enter app / Back home), like the other apps.
- **Ticket price is part of the journey, not an extra.** The add form asks "🎟️ Already bought your ticket?" with `Pill`s ("Yes, I paid" / "Not yet", defaulting to "Not yet") and reveals the ticket fields in place on yes. Never a "+ Add ticket details" link; unpaid tickets still surface under "Needs a price". Any new detail the savings math depends on follows the same shape.
- **Fee and tax are learned per theater, then confirmed.** Sales tax follows the theater and the convenience fee follows the theater and format, so the ticket form and the welcome-back question start with the member's usual values (the fee and tax inputs filled, a `SuggestionNote` saying where they came from) and the common case is one tap to save. A value stops following the suggestion the moment the member types or picks a chip (`isFeeEdited` / `isTaxEdited`); with no history the fields start as before. AMC's own per-ticket tax for a picked showing comes first and is marked `isTaxFromAmc`; a later pick with none gives the learned rate back, and a $0 tax from AMC is read as unknown.
- **Theaters are optional and quiet.** Setup's last step is skippable, the Calendar offers a one-line nudge only when none are saved, and a showing's theater is a row of `Pill`s with the favorite preselected, never a required field. A theater is normally found through AMC (location is asked for only on a tap of "Use my current location"), by zip code, by city or state (the finder confirms the place first, "Did you mean…", then looks up its theaters) or by part of the theater's name, and a bad zip code is caught before AMC is asked; one AMC doesn't list can be typed by name, and a typed theater is flagged and can be linked to its AMC theater in place (never delete and re-tag); linking says plainly that AMC's name and city replace the typed ones, and asks first.
- **Showtimes are grouped by format, premium first, and sold-out ones stay visible.** Dolby Cinema, IMAX, PRIME, RealD 3D, Laser, then Standard (`AMC_FORMAT_DISPLAY_ORDER`); within a format the open times come first, then sold-out ones as muted pills that say "Sold out". The pills are thin (`isThin`) like the page's other filters. A typed theater in a picker is nudged to link ("See showtimes by linking X to AMC"), and adding or linking a theater opens `TheaterSheetModal` over the form so the member never leaves it (an owner-approved exception to "never stack an overlay on an overlay"; the results scroll in a capped box so the modal stays under ~70% of a phone).
- **A drawer action is named by its outcome, with a one-line subtitle, and the manual path is always one tap from the AMC path.** "Buy tickets", "I already have a ticket" / "Add what you paid" / "Ticket details" and "Change day, time or theater" replace "Mark paid" and "Edit"; the Buy tickets panel ends with "Already bought it somewhere else? Add what you paid", so a typed theater or an unmatched title never dead-ends. The welcome-back question's buttons are "Ask me later" (asks again next open) and "I changed my mind" (clears the plan and stops asking).
- **Buying is a hand-off, not a checkout, and only while it can happen.** "Buy tickets" and the welcome-back question never appear for a showing that has a ticket or has started/ended, and showtimes are shown in the theater's own time zone, flagged. The app never takes payment: it shows AMC's showtimes, opens amctheatres.com in a new tab, and welcomes the member back with one question (the fee and the tax, price prefilled). That question is an overlay only because it needs an answer, and it queues behind the Seen prompt instead of stacking on it.
- **A savings baseline is matched in time.** The Standard price a premium ticket is compared with is the Standard showing nearest in time, never the day's cheapest (a matinee), and a movie is matched to AMC's listing by exact normalized title, never a prefix (sequels share their predecessor's title).
- **Time-based helpers are quiet.** Something that appears because of the clock but isn't asking a question right now (the trailers bubble around a showing) is a small nudge anchored to the nav that folds into a chip so it can be reopened, never a drawer, modal or auto-opened screen. Its copy follows the phase it is in (before the start, during the trailers, after them), never one sentence for the whole window and never a bare "Started 25m ago". Its push twin (the trailers notification) names the movie and is worded as a hedge about what is on the screen. Only a question that needs an answer (the Seen prompt) may be an overlay, and it never stacks on another.
- **Money inputs** use the shared `@/components/MoneyInput`, which settles to two decimals on blur. Date-only fields that can't be in the future get `max` set to the viewer's local today.
- **A release is a mark on its day, not a poster.** Posters in a calendar cell are only for what is planned. A watchlist movie opening that day (active, unseen, with no planned showing, today or later) is a dashed gold outline around the cell, an open slot waiting to be filled, with no count and no glyph (a cell can already hold three posters; the date chip owns top-left and a split's "+N" bottom-right; owner: the old corner dot read poorly). Releases are on by default and Membership settings' "Show release days on your calendar?" (`showReleasesOnCalendar`, null means yes) turns them off for the cells, the peek and the day drawer alike; the Watchlist's Opening filter is unaffected. The day drawer shows releases as an offer, not as plans: a tinted "🎬 Opening this day" card of small poster tiles, never the same row as a plan; on a day with plans it is folded behind one centred `RevealChip` ("Show 2 movies opening this day", owner: plans come first) that opens it in place, and with nothing planned it shows straight away with no empty line above it; a tile tap plans it for that day. Releases come only from the member's own watchlist, never TMDB's upcoming list, and never appear in a shared calendar.
- **Pointer vs touch.** Hover-only affordances (the calendar's day peek) are `max-sm:hidden` and never the only way to reach something; touch goes through the day's drawer.
- **A week is AMC's week: Friday to Thursday.** Anything the app calls a week (the "Since Friday" counter, the share quick options "This week" / "Next week") uses `WEEK_STARTS_ON` (Friday), never Sunday or Monday; where a week is a choice, say so on screen ("Weeks run Friday to Thursday, like AMC's."). The only Sunday-first thing is Dreamer UI's calendar grid, which is a layout, not a week.
- **Sharing is a frozen, allowlisted copy.** The Calendar's Share button opens the Shared calendars subview (`ShareSubview`): up to 10 links to a snapshot of this/next AMC week (Friday to Thursday), this/next month or dates tapped out on a range calendar (a year at most), each optionally locked by a random 4-character PIN the owner can switch on or off, and deleted to end access. A share lists a movie's poster, title, time, runtime, rating, format and theater and nothing else: no name, email, uid, price, fee or star rating. Add a field to `SharedViewing` only after deciding it is safe for anyone with the link to see, and say so in the PR's privacy note (docs/conventions/security-privacy.md "Security and privacy"). Visitors read through the `getCalendarShare` function, never Firestore.
- **The picker's suggestions are gated per mode and say what each row is.** "In theaters and coming soon" (TMDB's US now-playing and upcoming, the first 8 with "Show all N", watchlist films left out by key, by title and year, or by title alone when the saved film has no date) shows in watchlist adds, trailer picks and calendar plans, never when backfilling movies already seen (`showPopular={!isPast}`); a row's detail is "In theaters" or "Opens Oct 30" (`formatDateUTC`), never a year repeated down the list. Its heading carries an info icon (`HelpTip`) saying what is left out first (films already on the watchlist, seen or not); there is no per-row control. The trailer is one labelled button, "Watch trailer on YouTube" (`target='_blank'`, `rel='noopener noreferrer'`), on the details screen a pick opens, so nothing is embedded or loaded from a third party until it is tapped. The details screen's trailer link is an outlined chip with a link-out glyph (never the answer-pill look beside it) that says "Watch trailer" below `sm`; trailer picks (quick mode) have no details step and so no trailer link, on purpose. When every film in the list is already on the watchlist the heading and info icon stay, with one line saying so; a failed or empty (OMDb) fetch draws nothing. The watchlist group above it folds to 5 with "Show all N" from 9. **A suggestion is not a decision.** A film picked from that list on a watchlist add opens a decision view first (release, runtime, rating, genres, a synopsis folded to four lines, the trailer chip) and "I want to see this" is the tap that brings up "How badly do you want to see it?" and the format in place, under the same facts (Back returns to them, the primary becomes "Add"); never ask priority on a tap that only meant "tell me about this". The same facts show above a calendar plan's form for a suggested film. A suggestion with nothing to read skips the decision view. Searched films keep their form, since naming a film is intent.
- **A projection is an estimate that honours the minimum.** The trend-line icon button in the corner of the dashboard's net savings tile (its only door) opens the Projection subview (nothing saved, click to view) whose every scenario pays at least `MIN_CONTRACT_MONTHS` bills; a pace or calendar showing is valued at the member's average ticket (editable) or, for a planned showing, AMC's price plus tax and the average avoided fee, and a showing with neither counts as $0 and says so. Billing dates come from `getCycleDate`, never ad-hoc date math, and a showing's day is the viewer's local day. The live result sits above the controls that drive it (so every change is seen at 320×568), a projection never calls a future result achieved ("Would break even"), and its period labels all name the same date: the day the paid months run to (`coveredUntil`). The plan row asks one question ("How many more movies will you watch?") whose options are answers (None, Everything planned, A set pace), never internal modes or parameter variants (a week or a month is a unit switch on the pace, not a second option), with a one-line meaning of the chosen answer; the date pills say "Cancel before the bill on", the chart's guide and comparison lines look different and are named in a legend, and a tap or horizontal drag reads the day and amounts at that point. A set pace keeps the y-axis sized for the fastest pace and the month counter steps by two, so each tap moves the line, the line starts up to 120 days in the past (a "Today" divider separates what happened from what is projected) so the day the member broke even is marked on the $0 line by a small dot with an always-visible callout and arrow ("Broke even Oct 12", never a bold ring or a hover) and, for a day still ahead, said in words ("You'd break even around …"), "Start after everything planned" runs a set pace only from the last planned showing so nothing is counted twice and shows where planned stops: a hollow dot with an always-visible callout ("Planned ends Nov 15"), a shaded "Then 2 a week" section after it (dropped when too narrow to read), a comparison line that becomes "Planned only" and starts there so the gap is exactly what the pace adds, and a sentence saying that amount; the switch is on by default when something is planned, and it uses the same cancel date as "Everything planned" (the first date pill), so the two compare on one date, and, for a set pace without the planned showings, a line under the counter says what one more a week or month adds and, while the member is still short of break even, how many days sooner it breaks even. The callouts always sit above their dots (one leaning left, one right, flipping when there is no room), never below, so they stay off the axis labels, and they step aside while a point is being read.
- **Check lists at scale.** A-List needs an oversized seed fixture like Waypoint's (`scripts/seeds/waypointScale.ts`): many viewings, a long watchlist and a busy calendar. Open new lists on it at phone width with the dev build throttled, and keep per-row work cheap (see docs/conventions/state-data.md "Performance is a design check").
