# Helm rules

Applies on top of `CLAUDE.md` and `docs/conventions/` to everything under `src/apps/helm/` and `functions/src/apps/helm/`. The full design is `src/apps/helm/UX.md` (section 1 is the source of these rules), the look and example world `canvas.md`, the data and server `TECHNICAL.md`.

- **Built for one person's whole life, mostly by voice.** Helm is private to its person; nothing is shared with another member. Every change is walked as the person talking to the assistant (Nomos by default, renameable everywhere, `{Name}` in copy and the wake phrase), and is checked at a scale of a lifetime: 2,000 people, 20,000 key dates, hundreds of topics.
- **Home is the conversation; content pages end in the ask bar.** Home is a voice-first surface: the assistant's name, at most two quiet lines and the dock (Browse · Talk · Type). The dock lives on home only. Every content page (a who, place, project, task, topic, note, procedure, Today, Needs you, the lists, Coming up) ends in one full-width **ask bar** ("Ask about Grandpa", mic and keyboard), drawn once by the shell, fixed at the page's foot, part of the layout and never floating; utility screens (Settings, Connected apps, Activity, Search, forms) have none. It opens the conversation over the page with a removable "About Grandpa" chip and closes with "Back to Grandpa" at the same scroll position. A content view fills that slot by publishing what it shows with `useScreenContext` (typed to `VIEW_KINDS` content views); it never draws its own. Content pages carry Back, Home (left out when Back says Home) and ⋯; there is no tab bar and no per-view add-a-task button: a hand-made task is New task in a list's ⋯.
- **What a live read may write.** A turn that searched mail, documents or an app's live category writes only its answer and at most one fact, and the fact needs an email quote: a document quote is refused in code, since anyone can share a file to the person. Where the answer looked is shown even when nothing answered, so a failed search's Reconnect is never hidden. A site app's live category (Runway's transactions) is asked through `search_app_records`, offered only while its grant has the category on; its rows quote as `Runway · Costco` with Open in Runway, and like a document quote they never carry a fact.
- **An answer's source rows.** A row built from a stored ref whose live record is gone says it is gone ("A task that is done or removed", "Something from a connected app that is no longer there"), never the decryption-failure line; inside a sheet (Activity) a row only names its source and opens nothing, since a sheet never opens a sheet; a `Button` row in a text card overrides its padding with `px-0!` so its text starts on the card's text column.
- **A settled question comes back when its answer is undone.** Undoing an execution that answered a code-built question reopens it when what it settled returns, so a reversible answer never leaves a record disputed with nothing to settle it; a pair-wise state (two facts pointing at each other) counts only when both halves agree, and closing it as stale frees the half left behind. Every option a voice reader can hear resolves by voice or says where to finish it ("Neither" needs typed words, so it points to the card), never a failed call. A new code-built question kind or answer shape is wired on every answer path (the card's tap, the voice hook, the sheet, and `planReopen`'s kind list when its answer can be undone), draws its own option labels rather than a generic Yes/No, and its check covers the voice path; the topics question first shipped tap-only, and the grouping question first showed Yes/No and stayed closed after Undo. An Execution that Activity's Undo cannot take back (a `writeHelmTopic` batch write, undone by restoring a version in History) carries empty `effectRefs`, so Activity never offers an Undo that plans nothing; a turn's own topic change (added, revised, moved, grouped) carries a `version` or placement effect that `planUndo` restores as a new version, so its card's Undo works.
- **A reply is words first, then what it did.** The assistant's words beside its mark (its name's first letter in the title serif, in a tinted disc), then one compact card of what it did with one Undo ("Undo all of this" for more than one line). Messages sent while it is still thinking join that turn and get one reply. Every surface that shows a turn's words (home thread, Inbox, Activity) groups a lead with its joined messages the same way; only the last bubble of a group takes the tail, and the group's time shows once under it. Spoken replies only answer spoken turns. A thing a turn handled is said once: a kept photo is the summary's "Kept the photo" plus its own line under the card naming where it went ("See it on {title}", the line that opens it), never also a card row, and a new kind of outcome (a photo kept or let go) counts in the test that sends an unread turn to Needs you, so a turn that only handled that never asks "didn't turn into a task". A send guard (photos still uploading) lives in the one `canSend` the button, Enter and voice all read, never only in which button is drawn. Every effect an execution records carries the title of what it changed, for every op (made, completed, a new day), so its card line never falls back to a generic word. A person, pet, organization or place a turn made or linked is named on one quiet line of that card ("Added Jordan · Linked Miso"), never counted in its lines or its Undo's words ("Took back 2 tasks."). An import whose subject is people (from Google Contacts) counts them, since they are what it made ("Took back 3 people."). A reply normalizer's "nothing to change" test counts every field the executor acts on, links included, so an update that only links a person still reaches it.
- **Suggested looks suggested; guessed looks guessed.** A dashed verdigris edge means "suggested, not yours yet" and nothing else is dashed; verdigris means "your call" and nothing else. An inferred fact carries a hollow ring and "{Name} inferred", a said or confirmed one a filled ring. General knowledge sits below a "Not from your records" `SectionDivider` in its own card, never in the records card.
- **Fixed is solid, flexible is Helm's.** A calendar event or another app's record is a plain card with its calendar mark or `AppLogo`, read-only; a planned task has a round tick; a suggestion is the dashed card. A trip's stays, rentals, flights and drives (Waypoint's `itinerary` events) are parts of the trip, never a run's or plan's own target: fold them out wherever a trip or event is offered or read from a turn. A tick or bulk act by asking needs a done word, refuses a negated sentence, and its line names what it ticked.
- **Your words stay yours.** The assistant adds to a topic, a procedure or a record on its own only as a new version with Undo, and never removes or rewrites the person's words, or deletes a record, unless asked; `reviseTopic` and `reviseProcedure` need a verbatim quote of the request, and a command that makes a record the person didn't describe (`createProcedure`) needs a quote that asks to make one: a cue word anywhere in the turn ("my morning routine") is not asking. Every part of a topic says where it came from.
- **Ask, then file.** Knowledge without a fitting topic asks "Where should this go?" once; after that it files itself with "Undo · Change topic". A new parent topic is always proposed, never created unasked.
- **What was read shows as chips, before Send.** Under the field, one quiet pill per thing read for certain (`@` who, `#` place, `^` procedure or note, `!` urgent, a date), all one height, labels capped with an ellipsis, at most six and then "+N more" so Send stays on the first screen; a chip's ✕ reads it as text with an Undo line, and a date chip fixes its day. The same words are one chip.
- **No made-up numbers.** No percentages, progress bars, scores or health colours; a state is an evidence line, and only "overdue" takes the warning colour.
- **The conversation is one surface.** Home's header (the name, search and settings, with the next event and "N need you" under it) stays pinned while the history scrolls; earlier sessions fold behind "Earlier · N", and so does every session from before the conversation last started fresh (New conversation under the thread, or the person's midnight with Start each day fresh on); starting fresh deletes nothing and leaves Needs you as it is. Home shows "Opening your conversation" with `WorkingDots` until the first page of turns is read, never an empty page or the first-open hint. Every question but a dispute between facts (`isDismissable`) offers Dismiss beside Answer later, also once it waits in Needs you; a turn that couldn't be sorted offers Dismiss in its sheet (Retry clears its `dismissedAt`, so a new failure shows again); and Needs you has Clear all, whose confirm counts what it clears of the whole list and says what stays. The composer is one floating card (attach, @, the field, Send) above Browse · Talk · Type with a fade above it; the shorthand pills show only when the field is focused or @ is tapped, and read chips sit inside the card. Settings captions are sentence case, not eyebrows. Another app's all-day rows on Today (two or more from one app) fold into one group row that names the count and expands in place. No bottom drawer at desktop width: Browse is a modal from `sm`.
- **Look: the household's private secretary.** The default palette is Chestnut & Sand (warm paper, brown for the one filled button and Talk); the person can choose Plum & Pewter, Mist & Deep Teal (mist page, deep blue-teal) or Porcelain & Indigo in Settings > Colours, saved on the profile as `theme` and applied as `data-helm-theme` on `<html>` by `useHelmTheme` (chestnut is no attribute). The description that follows is the Mist & Deep Teal shape: white cards, deep blue-teal for the one filled button and Talk, a pale teal tint for the assistant, verdigris only for "your call", amber only for overdue. Titles in the serif at 600 (a drawer's title included), everything else the site sans, tabular figures, sentence case. Cards 14px, fields 10px, pills and ticks round, hairlines with no card shadows. Tokens live in `helm.css` on `html.helm-theme` (chestnut) and `html.helm-theme[data-helm-theme='…']` (the others) (table in `canvas.md` "Look and tokens").
- **Tone: plain, short and assured, and it reads like a person answering**, an exception to "Playful by default": past-tense reports ("Made a task and linked it to Wedding"), no cheerleading, streaks or emoji in fixed copy, and no first person ("Too much to file at once", never "I could file"). Not chatty, but never a template either: a reply says what became of what was said the way a person would ("Dropped it.", never "Got it: Picked Drop it."), one tap is echoed once, and when the assistant can't tell what was meant it asks, briefly, rather than going quiet (owner, Helm retry round: "it should be like talking to a human"). So the words beside the mark are the model's own `replyLine`, written in the tone the person chose and their learned style ("Added it for Saturday."), checked in code for first person and templates, and kept only when everything the turn asked for was done; otherwise code's exact summary stands, and the card under it is always code's. The assistant's fixed copy follows the same voice ("Nothing came through. Tap to try again.", never "I didn't catch anything"). An emoji appears only where the person chose one. Copy never guesses a pronoun, and never names the app or the default assistant literally: the person talks to their assistant by the name they chose (`check:helm-copy`).
- **The assistant's name is the person's, so it is read, never trusted.** The stored name reaches a prompt and a wake phrase only through `readAssistantName` (the same reader on the device and, vendored, on the server), and every check that keeps the assistant from being filed as a person or place drops every name it answers to that turn (the saved one, the word the person addressed it by, the default), so an old name said after a rename is never someone. Copy that says the name wakes it also says where Hey is turned on, since it is off until the person turns it on.
- **Sealed by default.** Content is sealed on the device or the server under `HELM_MASTER_KEYS`; only what a query needs stays readable, and nothing sealed is indexed anywhere, key dates included (`TECHNICAL.md` sections 2 and 3). A photo's bytes are sealed on the device before upload and opened only through `useMediaUrl` (`useSealedObjectUrl`), so Storage holds ciphertext and a photo shows as a thumbnail in lists and the full picture only in its sheet and `ImageViewer`. A photo sent in a turn is one sealed 1600 px copy plus a thumbnail, shrunk on the device whatever its size; the server reads it only for the capture that sent it (`sourceCaptureId`), the model's keep is accepted only for a task or fact that exists, and a photo it lets go is deleted 24 hours later by the sweep (`deleteAfter`), never as soon as it is read, so "Keep it" can work. The model sees only what the server re-reads under the person's own account; a screen publishes record ids, never content. Each published id keeps its place in what the person sees, through the server and the prompt, so "the second one" never slides onto the next record when one is dropped as unreadable or gone; a word that points ("this", "the fifth one") with nothing to point at files nothing and waits in Needs you, never a task titled with the sentence (`check:helm-screen`). What decides which of the person's words reach the prompt (a note's body only on a question) is a pure exported function with its own case in a `check:*` script, never an inline condition.
- **A file's words are data, and each kind of medium is decided in one place.** What a person sends (a Markdown file, a PDF's text) goes to the model inside its own block with that block's closing tag and raw HTML escaped, and nothing in it is ever obeyed; a voice note's audio never reaches the model at all. Which Storage objects a medium has comes from one helper keyed by its kind (a Markdown file has none), shared by upload, removal, Undo and the sweep, and copy that names a medium comes from one kind-to-word mapping, so a new kind is added in those two places.
- **A new assistant tool says who gets it.** Every tool in `buildTools.ts` declares `audience`: `nomos` (Helm's own assistant only), `external` (Claude, Gemini and other connected assistants only) or `both`. A tool that reads mail, documents, another app's records or anything a third party could plant stays `nomos`, with two named exceptions: `get_calendar` gives event titles and times from synced calendars, since "what's on Friday" needs them, and an outside assistant gets the mail (`search_mail`, `read_mail`), document (`search_documents`, `read_document`) and synced or app-record (`search_synced`, `search_app_records`) tools only behind its own matching permission ("Read Gmail", "Read Google Drive", "Read your apps", each off by default, stored as `readScopes` on its client document and mapped to tool names by `READ_SCOPE_TOOLS`); every outside tool and every permission's tools are listed by name in the check, so a new one is a deliberate choice; anything that files a turn reaches an outside assistant only through `tell_helm` and `ask_helm`, behind the "Add things to Helm" switch (`check:helm-mcp`). An outside turn gets no live read (mail, documents, an app's records), since its words come from another company's assistant, and its dates are read on the server in the person's time zone, never taken from the outside assistant. An outside assistant is told the connection is text only (no photos, files or other media) and to send the person to Helm itself for one, in the server instructions and `tell_helm`'s description, until a file tool exists. An outside assistant is told the assistant's current name in its server instructions and tool descriptions, so "tell {Name}" routes to Helm, and what each source the person connected is (its manifest's `about` and the `meaning` of each category switched on, built by the pure `sourcesBriefFor`), so it understands the person's words for what it can't read and sends them to Helm for it, or names the exact permission (`READ_SCOPE_LABELS`, the same words the device shows) to turn on for that assistant; the brief is built from the tools the server actually registers (`offeredHelmTools`), so it never promises a tool a grant or a closed switch left out, and a source behind a closed switch isn't described at all; while an assistant can add things, its Read Gmail and Read Google Drive lines say a message or file from someone else could try to steer what it adds; the tool names stay fixed, and renaming with assistants connected says that open chats and Claude's connector label keep the old name. A connected assistant's name is the verified product only on its exact host (Claude on claude.ai); anything else shows the name it registered, cleaned and never one that poses as a verified one.
- **The scale fixture grows with the app.** Every list Helm adds (a new collection, index document or kind of synced copy) is added to `scripts/seeds/helmScale.ts` (`npm run seed:helm-scale`, Morgan) in the same PR, at the volume the lifetime targets say, so every performance check has a list to run against.
