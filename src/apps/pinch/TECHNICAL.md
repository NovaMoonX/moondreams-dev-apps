# Pinch — Technical Design Document

Built against this app's `README.md` and `UX.md`, and against the conventions in `.github/copilot-instructions.md` and `CLAUDE.md`. A-List's `TECHNICAL.md` is the format reference. Like A-List, **every byte of Pinch data is private to one signed-in person**, so there is no invite flow, no roles and no cross-user concurrency. The design weight sits on four things instead: a static ingredient catalog merged with the person's own records, the quantity and cost math, the calendar read model, and an AI reader that fills recipe forms from text, a file or a web page.

## Decisions made in this document

These shape everything below. Each is also listed under [Open questions](#open-questions-for-the-roadmap) where it needs the owner's confirmation.

1. **The catalog is a bundled constant, not a Firestore collection.** About 200 everyday ingredients, their families, nutrition per 100 g, serving sizes and unit conversions ship in the app's own lazy chunk. A person's data refers to them by id (`cat-chicken-breast`). Nothing is copied into a person's account until they change or attach something to a catalog ingredient.
2. **A person's `ingredients` documents are records of what they did, not a copy of the catalog.** A document exists for a custom ingredient, or for a catalog ingredient the person adjusted (nutrition, serving) or attached a product to (the default-product pointer). The client merges catalog and record into one resolved `Ingredient` at read time.
3. **Three layers, one level of grouping.** `family → ingredient → product`. Families don't nest. Recipes and meals refer to an ingredient id only; products carry store, price and size.
4. **Money is integer cents.** Quantities are numbers in a typed unit. Nutrition is stored per 100 g on ingredients and as optional per-serving overrides on recipes. The ingredient form takes it on the label's basis (per an amount of g, oz or servings) and converts before the write, so per 100 g stays the only stored shape; per serving is a display.
5. **Planned meals are copies.** Planning a meal copies its items into a `plannedMeals` document that keeps `sourceMealId`. Editing a saved meal never touches planned days. Items point at recipes and ingredients by id, so a recipe edit reaches every upcoming day.
6. **Past days are frozen once, and priced again only when the person asks.** A planned meal whose day has passed gets a `snapshot` of its cost and nutrition that the app's own freezing writes once and never replaces. Edits to prices, recipes or nutrition first freeze any unfrozen past meals, so history doesn't drift. The one thing that replaces a snapshot is the person's own "Price this day with today's prices" (decision 22).
7. **Anything in use is archived, never removed.** A recipe, custom ingredient or store that anything still points at gets `isArchived: true`. Whether a recipe or ingredient is used on a planned day is answered by one `array-contains` read on `plannedMeals.refIds`, so planned days outside the loaded month window are covered, and whether a recipe still has food in the fridge or freezer by one count of its `IN_STOCK` containers (two equality filters, no index): a recipe whose only use is prepared containers is archived, and the confirm says "is used in 1 prepared container". A saved meal stays always deletable; its containers keep their own title and stay in stock. Products are deleted outright (their ingredient's default pointer is re-pointed or cleared in the same transaction), a saved meal is always deletable, a catalog ingredient is only ever reset to its built-in values, and the MVP has no way to delete a family.
8. **Cost and nutrition are computed, never stored**, except the snapshot of a past meal. Every derived number returns what it left out, so the UI can say so.
9. **Cost exists only for comparable amounts.** A recipe amount and a product size must share a dimension (mass, volume, count) or be bridged by a conversion the catalog or the person has supplied. Anything else is left out of totals and surfaced as a gap.
10. **Gaps are derived, not stored.** A "nudge" is a pure function of the data (a missing price, size, nutrition value or conversion on an ingredient that something uses). Only the person's dismissals are stored.
11. **Dates are date-only.** A planned meal's `date` is UTC midnight of the day picked (`fromDateInputValue`), shown with `formatDateUTC`, and keyed on the calendar by `toDateInputValue`. Weeks run Sunday to Saturday, in the month grid and the week view alike (open question 4).
12. **One shared Cloud Function, no Pinch-specific ones.** The AI reader is the repo's shared client-side Firebase AI setup and store logos use a publishable browser token, so nothing needs a server secret. Reading a recipe from a link needs a server to fetch the page, so it calls the shared, app-neutral `fetchPageContent` callable (`functions/src/linkMetadata/fetchPageContent.ts`, listed in `functions/README.md`), which returns only the kinds of content the caller asks for; nothing under `functions/` is Pinch's own.
13. **Per-person data nests under `apps/pinch/kitchens/{uid}/…`.** A `kitchens` root (not `users/{uid}`) leaves room for a future shared kitchen to be a read grant on this document, not a data move.
14. **How a list looks is a per-device choice, not data.** Each list's View options (show prices, show calories and macros, the nutrition basis of ingredient rows, and on Recipes and Meals the fridge and freezer line) live in the browser's `localStorage` per person and list, so no field, rule or listener is involved and nothing syncs between devices. Prices and the stock line start on and calories and macros start off (`DEFAULT_LIST_VIEW_OPTIONS`); a stored choice always wins over the default, and a stored object missing a key reads that key's default (`parseListViewOptions`, checked by `check:pinch-math`), so a choice stored before the stock switch existed shows the line. Hiding a line only skips drawing it; no total reads these options.
15. **Where a recipe came from is its own small collection, named by `sourceId`.** A `Source` is just an id and a label the person typed ("Serious Eats", "Mom's recipe cards"); a recipe points at one with `sourceId`, which is not `Recipe.source` (that field says how the draft was made: by hand, or read from text, a file or a link). Deleting a source clears the pointer on the recipes that name it and leaves the recipes alone; a recipe that names a source that is gone, or has no `sourceId` key at all, simply reads as having none.
16. **The shopping list is one document per ingredient and unit, merged on add.** A `shoppingItems` document is `{ ingredientId, amount, unit, isChecked }` with the id `{ingredientId}__{unit}`, so adding the same thing again lands on the same document and the list never holds two lines for it. Units are never converted (a cup and a tablespoon of one ingredient are two lines). Lines come from a pure function over recipes, meals, planned meals and days (`utils/shopping.ts`), and nothing is derived from the calendar afterwards: the list is plain data the person edits, and changing a meal later leaves it as it was.
17. **A recipe idea is its own small collection, and becoming a recipe is a link, not a move.** An `Idea` is `{ kind, title, note, url, siteName, imageUrl, photoPath, recipeId, … }`: a link, a photo or a few words. It points at the recipe it became with `recipeId`, set by its own write right after the recipe has committed (a failure there is logged and never loses the recipe); nothing is copied back, and deleting the recipe leaves a dangling `recipeId` that every reader treats as "not made yet". A link idea keeps only what a page gives away in its `meta` (title, site name, picture address), fetched through the shared `fetchPageContent` and never read by the AI model.

18. **Cooking mode is a screen, not data.** It reads a recipe from the store, keeps its step, ticks, servings and timers in component state and memory, and writes nothing of its own: no document, rule, index, Storage path, listener, `localStorage` key or Function. The one write that starts there is the final panel's cook (decision 19) and, after it, a note about that cook (decision 20), and only when the person taps them. It opens as an overlay `Subview` (`{ kind: 'cook', recipeId, servings?, plannedMealId? }`), so the header's back, Escape and the back gesture share one `onBack`, which leaves at once or, while a timer is running or paused, asks "Stop timers and leave?" first; Finish on the last step opens a final panel (§5d) that leaves through the same path. Timers run to a clock deadline, so they are accurate but only while the screen is open: a browser can't ring a web page in the background, and a notification that outlives the page would need push, which this doesn't use.

19. **A cook is a quiet record in its own collection, one document each.** A `Cook` is `{ recipeId, plannedMealId, servings, cookedAt, … }`: that a recipe was made, when (an instant, shown in local time) and at how many servings. It is data the person can correct, so it lives at `apps/pinch/kitchens/{uid}/cooks/{id}` beside `ideas` and `shoppingItems`, owner only. A cook marked from a planned meal has the deterministic id `{plannedMealId}__{recipeId}`, so marking the same meal twice finds the first cook and writes nothing; every other cook (marked from a recipe, from cooking mode started on a recipe, or added by hand for something remembered) has a fresh `generateUuid()` id, and cooking mode started from a planned meal finishes into that meal's own cook. Nothing is derived from cooks beyond a per-recipe count and last date; no total, filter or sort reads them, and a recipe that is deleted takes its cooks with it (§5d).

20. **A recipe's notes are one small collection: what the person wrote, and the record of each edit.** A `RecipeNote` is `{ recipeId, kind: 'NOTE' | 'TWEAK', text, changes, cookId, … }` at `apps/pinch/kitchens/{uid}/notes/{id}`, owner only, one document each, beside `cooks`. A NOTE is the person's own words, optionally tied to the cook it follows up (a cook that is deleted later leaves the note, which then reads as a plain one). A TWEAK is written by the recipe's own save, in the same `writeBatch` as the recipe update, when the recipe has at least one cook and the save changed something the form edits: its `changes` are short plain-word lines computed at save time by the pure `getRecipeChanges` and are never rewritten (the rules hold them), while its `text`, the optional reason, can be edited. `Recipe` gets no new field: "last tweaked" is the newest TWEAK's `createdAt`, read by `selectNoteSummaries`, and a NOTE older than it is labelled "Written before your Oct 20 tweak", so it is always clear which version of the recipe a note was about. Nothing else reads notes; no total, filter or sort does.

21. **A recipe's care is one nullable map on the recipe, not a collection.** `Recipe.care` is `{ fridgeDays, freezerDays, reheat, tip } | null`: how long what the recipe makes keeps in the fridge and the freezer, how to reheat it, and a tip. It is something the recipe form edits, so the recipe's own save writes it (a required key, `null` when all four parts are empty) and a recipe written before it has no key, which reads as no care. Days are whole numbers from 1 to 365; weeks and months exist only in how days are typed and shown (a month is 30 days, so 90 shows as "3 months"), never in the data. `reheat` and `tip` are trimmed text of at most 300 characters. The three readings fill it for review only, from what the source says and nothing else. Only the form, the drawer and cooking mode's last screen read it; no total, filter or sort does, and a change to it never records a tweak.

22. **A past day is priced again by one explicit action that replaces its snapshots, in place.** Decided by the owner: "Price this day with today's prices", one tap and a confirm, with no per-product price history. It keeps every planned meal's `id` (a cook marked from a meal is `{plannedMealId}__{recipeId}`, so a changed id would orphan "Cooked ✓") and every other field, and replaces only `snapshot` and `updatedAt`, in one `runTransaction` per day (§3). The snapshot gains `repricedAt: number | null` (null when the app froze the day, an instant when the person asked) and the rules allow replacing a snapshot only with one that carries it. Rejected: deleting and re-creating each meal in a batch, which needs no rules change but leaves the rules unable to tell a re-price from any other rewrite of a meal, so the marker and the "meal otherwise untouched" checks (criterion 6) would have nothing to hold on to; it was reasoned about and not tried on the emulator.


23. **A prepared container is one document per physical container, owner only, with a short history of its own.** A `Container` at `apps/pinch/kitchens/{uid}/containers/{id}` is food made ahead and kept in the fridge or freezer: `title`, `recipeId` / `mealId` / `cookId` (all `T | null`, immutable; a recipe and a saved meal never both), `servings` (whole portions, 1 to 100), `status` (`IN_STOCK` | `DONE`), `place` (`FRIDGE` | `FREEZER`), `eatBy` and `history`. It is never split: eating some lowers `servings` and adds an EAT; the last portion, or a toss, sets `status: DONE` and the document stays as history (a finished container keeps the portions it ended with, so `servings` is never 0); Delete is a separate action that erases the history and says so. `eatBy` is a date-only value (UTC midnight, written with `fromDateInputValue`, shown through the planned-day helpers that read UTC) meaning "eat by the latest", never an instant; "Eat soon" is the viewer's local today and the next two days against that stored day (`getLocalTodayDate`), "Past its date" is any day before it. Event `at` values are instants shown in local time. `history` holds at most 10 events `{ kind, at, servings, place, note }` of kind MADE, FRIDGE, FREEZE, THAW, EAT, TOSS or EDIT, oldest first: the rules can only walk a list by index and the evaluator stops at 1,000 expressions per request (criterion 13), and past the cap the oldest EAT goes first, then the oldest EDIT, then the oldest move after the first place event that is not a FREEZE (the first place event says how many went in and the FREEZEs are what "Frozen twice" counts, so only a long run of moves reaches the oldest thaws). A cook's container starts with MADE (what the cook made) then where it went; one added by hand starts with where it went alone. Every change that depends on the document's own state is a `runTransaction` that re-reads it and applies a pure plan (`planEat`, `planMove`, `planEatBy`, `planToss`, `planCount`, `planUndoEnd` in `utils/containers.ts`), so another device's change is never written over and an impossible change stops with one plain sentence; "Put some away" is one `writeBatch` of one to twenty containers (a person who puts portions in separate boxes says how many containers; the servings split evenly), accepted locally without waiting for the server. The default eat-by is the person's own call and says so: from the recipe's `care` (`fridgeDays` / `freezerDays`) when it has them, else a named default (`CONTAINER_FRIDGE_DAYS` 4, `CONTAINER_FREEZER_DAYS` 90), and moving out of the freezer starts at the shorter of the thawed window (`CONTAINER_THAWED_DAYS` 2), the recipe's fridge days and the date the container already had; a container already past its date keeps that date through a move, and one that has been frozen before says so; nothing in the app says food is safe. What is in stock is one listener on `status == 'IN_STOCK'` (a lone equality, no index) in the usual optional-extra shape; Finished containers are history, read on demand (§5g). Rejected: a document per portion by default (a bulk meal would be fifteen writes and fifteen rows; a box is the unit, and "how many containers" is there for the person who does portion it), a `Recipe.servingsInStock` counter (no history, and a meal or a typed name would need its own), and keeping every container on one always-open listener (finished ones grow without end).

## Data Schema

Namespace root: `apps/pinch/`

**Per-person private tree.** Everything lives under the person's own kitchen, keyed by their `uid`:

```
apps/pinch/kitchens/{uid}                         → KitchenSettings
apps/pinch/kitchens/{uid}/ingredients/{id}        → IngredientRecord
apps/pinch/kitchens/{uid}/families/{id}           → Family
apps/pinch/kitchens/{uid}/stores/{id}             → Store
apps/pinch/kitchens/{uid}/products/{id}           → Product
apps/pinch/kitchens/{uid}/recipes/{id}            → Recipe
apps/pinch/kitchens/{uid}/sources/{id}            → Source
apps/pinch/kitchens/{uid}/shoppingItems/{id}      → ShoppingItem
apps/pinch/kitchens/{uid}/ideas/{id}              → Idea
apps/pinch/kitchens/{uid}/cooks/{id}              → Cook
apps/pinch/kitchens/{uid}/notes/{id}              → RecipeNote
apps/pinch/kitchens/{uid}/containers/{id}         → Container
apps/pinch/kitchens/{uid}/meals/{id}              → Meal
apps/pinch/kitchens/{uid}/plannedMeals/{id}       → PlannedMeal

Storage:
apps/pinch/kitchens/{uid}/recipes/{recipeId}/cover.webp
apps/pinch/kitchens/{uid}/stores/{storeId}/logo.webp
apps/pinch/kitchens/{uid}/ideas/{ideaId}/photo.webp
```

Why nested under the `uid`: every rule is the same one-line path check, every listener is a plain collection listener, and a mis-scoped query can't leak across people. Products are a top-level collection, not nested under an ingredient, so the Products subview and the cost math read them with one listener.

**Every field is a required key typed `T | null`, never optional.** Absent values are written as explicit `null`; arrays default to `[]`. Timestamps are millisecond numbers. Ids come from `generateUuid` (`@/utils`); catalog ids are the fixed strings `cat-…` and are never generated.

### 1. `KitchenSettings` — `apps/pinch/kitchens/{uid}`

```typescript
interface NutritionSwitches { calories: boolean; protein: boolean; carbs: boolean; fat: boolean }

interface KitchenSettings {
  uid: string;                                  // immutable
  showNutrition: NutritionSwitches;             // all false until switched on
  dismissedNudges: Record<string, number>;      // "NO_PRODUCT:cat-soy-sauce" → dismissedAt (ms)
  createdAt: number;                            // immutable
  updatedAt: number;
}
```

The document is created the first time the person changes a setting or dismisses a nudge; until then the client uses defaults (`isLoaded` with `settings === null` means defaults). There is no setup step. `dismissedNudges` is capped at `MAX_DISMISSED_NUDGES` keys.

### 2. `IngredientRecord` — `…/ingredients/{id}`

```typescript
interface NutritionValues { calories: number | null; protein: number | null; carbs: number | null; fat: number | null }

interface IngredientRecord {
  id: string;                                   // a generated id (custom) or the catalog id (an adjusted catalog ingredient)
  catalogId: string | null;                     // immutable; null for a custom ingredient
  name: string | null;                          // required for custom; null means "use the catalog name"
  familyId: string | null;                      // a catalog family id (cat-fam-…) or a Family id; null = its own family
  iconKey: string | null;                       // @deprecated: icons were removed; old documents may carry one, new writes null
  nutritionPer100g: NutritionValues | null;     // null = the catalog's values (or none, for custom); always per 100 g, whatever basis the form was typed in
  servingAmount: number | null;                 // what "1 serving" of this ingredient is (null = catalog / 100 g)
  servingUnit: Unit | null;
  gramsPerMl: number | null;                    // density, bridges mass <-> volume (user-supplied; editing UI is Next Steps)
  gramsEach: number | null;                     // weight of one, bridges mass <-> count (same)
```

## State Machines & Logic

### 1. Quantity and cost

```
toBase(amount, unit) = { dimension: UNIT_DIMENSION[unit], value: amount × UNIT_TO_BASE[unit] }

bridge(q, targetDimension, ingredient):
  same dimension                → q
  MASS   → VOLUME (needs gramsPerMl)  value ÷ gramsPerMl
  VOLUME → MASS   (needs gramsPerMl)  value × gramsPerMl
  MASS   → COUNT  (needs gramsEach)   value ÷ gramsEach
  COUNT  → MASS   (needs gramsEach)   value × gramsEach
  VOLUME ↔ COUNT                      via MASS (needs both)
  otherwise                     → null   (a NOT_COMPARABLE gap)

lineCost(line, ingredient, product):
  no product                    → gap NO_PRODUCT
  q = bridge(toBase(line.amount, line.unit), productDimension, ingredient)
  q is null                     → gap NOT_COMPARABLE
  cost = q.value × (product.priceCents ÷ toBase(product.sizeAmount, product.sizeUnit).value)
```

Cents are rounded once, at the display edge, never per line, so a recipe's total doesn't drift from the sum of its rounded lines. A product's size of `EACH` ("12 count") compares directly with a recipe line in `EACH`, and with mass or volume only through `gramsEach`.

Worked example: chicken breast, Walmart, $9.48 (948¢) for 3 lb → 3 × 453.592 = 1360.776 g → 0.6967¢/g. A recipe line of 2 lb = 907.18 g → 632¢ ($6.32). A line of `1 cup` chicken breast has no `gramsPerMl` → `NOT_COMPARABLE`, left out and named.

### 2. Nutrition

Per metric, independently. Ingredient values are per 100 g, so a line's grams (bridged as above) give `grams ÷ 100 × per100g[metric]`. A recipe's per-serving value is the sum over lines ÷ `servings`, unless `nutritionOverride[metric]` is non-null. An ingredient with `null` for a metric, or a line that can't be bridged to grams, adds a `NO_NUTRITION` gap for that metric only. Nutrition is computed for all four metrics whether or not they're switched on; the switches only choose what is rendered and which gaps are surfaced (`gaps.ts` takes the `showNutrition` switches). In the recipe form `NutritionReview` shows the calculated per-serving values as `MacroStats` tiles (with a warning tip when some ingredients have no nutrition). Edit turns the four numbers into inputs: in place with Done from `sm` up, and on a phone in a `DetailSheet` drawer ("Nutrition per serving", four compact `Field`s, Done). A changed number is saved as that metric's `nutritionOverride` while a number equal to the calculated one stays derived (`null`), and "Reset to what we calculated" clears the overrides (in the drawer it refills the fields and leaves the drawer open). `MacroStats` and `src/utils/macroUtils.ts` are where calories (whole numbers) and macros (grams) are formatted.

**Typing an ingredient's nutrition on the label's basis.** `IngredientFormSheet` asks "Per [amount] [g | oz | serving]" above the four numbers (starting at 100 g), so what is typed is what the label prints, and `getPer100g` turns it into the stored per-100 g values (`toPer100g` over `getEntryGrams`, see Derived values; a blank metric stays `null`). The serving option needs the form's own serving size to convert to grams through `getServingGrams` (the ingredient's density or weight each): until it does the pill is disabled and a hint says so, with an "Add serving size" link when the size is off. A blank or non-positive amount, or a converted value outside 0 … `MAX_NUTRITION_VALUE`, shows one line and keeps Save disabled; any basis other than exactly 100 g shows "Saved as per 100 g: …" with the converted figures. An existing ingredient reopens in per 100 g, since that is what is stored. `NutritionEntry.isTyped` stays false until a number field is edited: while it is, picking g, oz or serving converts the numbers on screen to the new unit from the per-100 g value (`switchBasis`, per 100 g × grams ÷ 100, two decimals), so what is stored doesn't move; once a number has been typed, picking another unit only relabels the typed numbers and "Saved as per 100 g" shows what that makes. Display never changes a number elsewhere: the ingredient drawer and an ingredient row both read the Kitchen list's View options basis (`useListViewOptions(uid, 'ingredients').basis`, default per 100 g), and the drawer's thin Per serving / Per 100 g `PillGroup` writes that same setting rather than keeping its own. The pills are left out and the drawer shows per 100 g when one serving is exactly 100 g or can't be weighed, and a row falls back to per 100 g when the serving can't be weighed; `scaleNutrition` does the per-serving numbers. Recipes, meals, gaps and stats always compute from per 100 g. `npm run check:pinch-math` covers the conversions.

### 3. Copy-on-plan, edit semantics and the past

| Action | Result |
|---|---|
| Plan a saved meal on a day | one `plannedMeals` document: items copied with new row ids, `sourceMealId` set, `name` set |
| Plan a single recipe or ingredient | the same document with one item, `sourceMealId: null` |
| Edit a saved meal | the `meals` document only; planned days unchanged |
| Edit a planned meal | the planned document only (items, servings, date, type) |
| "Save as a meal" on a planned meal | a new `meals` document from its items; the planned meal's `sourceMealId` is left as it was |
| Edit a recipe | `recipes` document only; every non-frozen planned meal that points at it reads the new values |
| Edit a price, serving size, nutrition or conversion | first `freezePastMeals`, then the write |
| Day passes | its planned meals are frozen the next time the app loads that month (below) |
| "Price this day with today's prices" | every frozen meal of that day gets a fresh snapshot from the current prices and recipes, in one transaction (below) |

`freezePastMeals` runs in two places: `useFreezePastDays` (from `Pinch.tsx`) once the kitchen has loaded and again for any unfrozen past meals a newly loaded month brings (each meal is submitted once per visit, so the list shrinking as writes land never starts another freeze of the same ones; a failed attempt isn't retried until the next visit or the next edit's freeze), and as the first step of every action that changes a value the math reads (`saveProduct`, `setDefaultProduct`, `removeProduct`, `saveRecipe`, `removeRecipe`, `saveIngredient`, `removeIngredient`, through `freezeLoadedPastMeals`). It never runs while a listener has failed, since the numbers it would freeze come from incomplete data. "Past" is the viewer's local day (`getLocalTodayDate`), the same clock the calendar shows as today, so an evening in a time zone behind UTC doesn't freeze the day still being planned. It finds loaded planned meals with `snapshot === null` and `date` before that, computes each snapshot from the current data, and writes `snapshot` field-scoped (no reads), in one `writeBatch` per 100 meals so each batch is one snapshot event for the listeners. A batch is all-or-nothing, so a refused one (a meal another device froze first, or deleted) falls back to one `updateDoc` per meal, settled independently: the refused meal is denied alone and doesn't stop the rest. The rules let the app's own freeze write `snapshot` only from `null` (a snapshot written over an existing one without an instant `repricedAt` is refused), so a stale device can't overwrite history; only the re-price below can. A freeze that fails never blocks the edit that triggered it (`freezeBeforeEdit` swallows it): a person's own price or recipe change isn't held up by bookkeeping, and the cost is only that those meals stay open until the next freeze.

A frozen meal still lists its items from the live recipes and ingredients (so a deleted-then-archived recipe still shows its title), but its totals come from the snapshot. A meal planned on a day that has already passed is frozen at once (`planMeal` writes the snapshot with the meal, unless a listener has failed, in which case it is left open and the load-time freeze takes it). A frozen meal can't be edited: `updatePlannedMeal` is a transaction that refuses it (a snapshot would no longer match its items), the planned meal view hides Modify, and the way to change what it contains is to remove it and add it again; the way to change only its prices is to price the day again. Frozen gaps still count in what a total says it leaves out but are not offered as nudges, since a new price doesn't change a fixed day by itself (`PeriodStats.openLeftOut`); once a price would change it, the drawer offers to price the day again instead. The copy for a frozen day says the same thing: its warning says a price added now counts from today on, its cost tile carries the line "Fixed when the day passed" (or "Priced with today's prices on Oct 14" after a re-price), and a cost or row with no price reads "Wasn't priced" ("Still no price" after a re-price; an open day says "No prices yet" / "No price yet", which invite a fix).

**Pricing a day again (`repriceDay`).** `RepriceDayRow` is the only caller, in the day drawer and the planned meal view, and renders only when the day has passed, the kitchen has loaded without a failure (`hasCompleteCostData`) and `wouldRepriceChange` is true for at least one meal of that day, computed in a `useMemo` over that day's meals and the cost context, so opening a drawer costs one `getMealCost` per meal on that day and nothing else is walked (the 1,100-meal fixture included). The confirm shows the result before anything is written: at the tap, `getRepricePreview(meals, costContext)` (`utils/mealMath.ts`, covered by `npm run check:pinch-math`) runs `buildRepricedSnapshot` over the day's frozen meals from the loaded store and adds up the totals, and `getRepriceLines` (`utils/mealFormat.ts`) words them: "Before" in the cost tile's own words ("Wasn't priced", "Still no price" once the day has been priced again, otherwise the total), "After" as "about $0.66" (about, because the transaction reads the meals again), and "N ingredients still have no price" only above zero. It runs on the tap, not on render. The thunk checks the same preconditions again, takes the day's frozen meals from `selectPlannedByDay`, and runs one `runTransaction`: it re-reads every one of them with `transaction.get`, stops with one sentence and nothing written if any is gone, no longer frozen or no longer on that day, writes nothing when today's prices change none of them, and otherwise writes every meal of the day (a day is never half re-priced, so its label is the same for all its meals): `transaction.update(ref, { snapshot, updatedAt })` where `snapshot = buildRepricedSnapshot(...)`, which is `buildSnapshot` over the meal's own items and the current cost context (the same function the freeze uses, so the numbers equal what a freeze run today would write, nutrition included) with `frozenAt` kept and `repricedAt` set. The prices and recipes come from the loaded store (as for the freeze), the meals from the transaction's own reads. A day is limited to `MAX_REPRICE_MEALS` (400) meals so it stays under Firestore's 500 writes in one transaction; the limit is the action's, not the rules'. A transaction needs a connection, so a write that can't reach the server fails with "Check your connection and try again." (any other Firebase error is logged and shown as one plain sentence). Nothing else is written: not the meals' ids, dates, items, `refIds`, `name` or `sourceMealId`, not their cooks, the shopping list or any note. `useFreezePastDays` and `freezeBeforeEdit` select `snapshot === null`, so a re-priced day (non-null) is never touched by them; the person's own later price edits don't move it either, and they can ask again, which writes nothing and hides the row once today's prices change nothing. The copy never claims more than is known: a snapshot that has `repricedAt` reads "Priced with today's prices on {date of the latest}" (an instant, in local time), one that has none reads "Fixed when the day passed" (a day where only some meals were re-priced says "Some meals priced with today's prices on …"), a line with no price reads "Wasn't priced" before and "Still no price" after, and nothing says "as they were that day".

*Honest limit:* a meal is frozen from the data on the first load after its day, not the data on the day itself. A price changed during a gap in use, in a month the window never loaded, can leak into history for meals that were never frozen. The freeze-before-edit step keeps this to meals older than the loaded window.

### 3a. Archive on delete

```
removeRecipe(id):
  usedInMeals    = meals.some(m => m.items.some(i => i.kind === 'RECIPE' && i.refId === id))
  usedInPlanned  = (await getCountFromServer(query(plannedMeals, where('refIds', 'array-contains', id)))).data().count > 0
  usedInPlanned || usedInMeals  → updateDoc({ isArchived: true, updatedAt })   // hidden from lists and pickers
  otherwise                     → deleteDoc  (+ delete the cover from Storage)
```

The same shape serves custom ingredients (also checked against `recipes.ingredients[].ingredientId`, saved meals and `products`) and stores (against products). Products are deleted outright (the ingredient's `defaultProductId` is re-pointed to another product, or `null`, in the same transaction), and the MVP has no way to delete a family. The confirm names what will happen ("…is used in 3 planned meals and 1 saved meal, so it will be hidden, not erased"), from `getUsage` (the loaded collections plus one server-side count of the planned meals that list the id, so offline it says it can't check instead of undercounting); the remove action asks again itself, so the confirm text can never disagree with what is done to a record that changed in between. The read is a query, so it can't sit inside the delete's transaction: a planned meal added in the instant between the read and the delete is the one race left, and it would show the removed record as "Removed recipe" until the person re-adds it.

Archived items never appear in pickers, search or lists (`selectRecipes`, `selectIngredients`, `selectStores` are the visible lists; the `selectAll…` ones and every `…ById` map still hold them, so `Retired chili` keeps its title and cost on the day that used it). A catalog store the person archived shows as addable again in the store pickers. Selectors that resolve a reference (`resolveIngredient`, recipe titles for planned items) still find them.

### 4. Gaps and nudges

`getGaps(scope)` walks the lines in a scope (one recipe, one meal, a day, the month) and returns `{ kind, ingredientId, detail }[]`:

- `NO_PRODUCT` — a priced ingredient with no product, or a recipe/ingredient item with no price at all
- `NOT_COMPARABLE` — a product exists but the amount and the package size can't be bridged
- `NO_NUTRITION` — only for a metric in `showNutrition`

A gap is **hidden** when `dismissedNudges["{kind}:{ingredientId}"]` exists. Dismissing writes that key; filling the gap makes the gap disappear on its own, and a later regression (a product removed) brings it back only if the key was cleared, so dismissal clearing happens in the same write as the action that fills the gap (`saveProduct` removes the `NO_PRODUCT`/`NOT_COMPARABLE` keys for that ingredient). A nudge renders only when the filtered gap list is non-empty. A total that excludes lines always says so, whether or not its nudge is dismissed. When the note beside it asks about fewer ingredients than the warning counts (a hidden one, or one only in a day that has passed) the warning says so in one line (`NudgeCountNote`, over `countUnaskedIngredients`), so two numbers on one screen are never left unexplained.

The fill-in sheet takes the gap list and renders one row per ingredient with only the fields that gap needs: a product's store, price and size for `NO_PRODUCT`; the size for `NOT_COMPARABLE`; the metric for `NO_NUTRITION`. Saving writes through the same actions as the full forms. A row left blank stays unchanged; a row that is part-filled is not saved and says what it still needs, so Save never writes half a product. A saved row asks to become the ingredient's default product, and a `NOT_COMPARABLE` row rewrites the size of the default product it is about.

### 5. Recipe ingestion (AI)

```
idle → reading(source) → draft(recipe, unread[]) → (person edits) → saved
             │                                           
             └→ failed(message)    (always ends on idle for the next try; nothing was written)
```

- Entry point: a new recipe lists its ways to start through `RecipeStart`, rows only from the shared `MethodPicker` (required `onPick`, one `Method` per `RECIPE_START_METHODS` entry): **From text** (up to `MAX_PASTE_CHARS`), **From a file** (a photo, screenshot or PDF up to `MAX_UPLOAD_BYTES`; JPG, PNG, WebP, HEIC, PDF through `getUploadMimeType`; images through `compressIngestionImage`), **From a link**, and **Write it yourself**, last and quieter. On a phone "New" in Recipes and Calendar's "Add a recipe" raise `RecipeStartDrawer` (a `DetailSheet`), which `Pinch.tsx` renders once at its root from `isStartOpen` in `PinchOverlayContext` (`setIsStartOpen`), so any screen can raise it; picking a way closes the drawer and opens `RecipeSubview` already on that way (`{ kind: 'recipe', id: null, method, fromDrawer: true }`; `fromCalendar` marks one started from the calendar's empty state, carried through the drawer by a ref in `Pinch.tsx` and read by the form's save for its "Added … to your recipes" toast). From `sm` up they open the subview with no `method`, whose first stage is the same list. Editing an existing recipe goes straight to the form.
- `RecipeSubview` stages are `start | text | link | file | form`. Picking text, link or file shows `RecipeReader` for that way (`components/recipes/RecipeReader.tsx` holds the three views), titled with the way's name; "Write it yourself" and each reader's "Write it yourself instead" go to the form, and a finished reading goes to the form with its result. `RecipeSubview` passes `Subview` an `onBack` (`goBack`), which the header's back button, Escape and the browser's or phone's back gesture all call. On a reading screen it raises the phone's start drawer again (`closeSubview()`, then `setIsStartOpen(true)`) when the way came from it (`fromDrawer`) and returns to the start stage otherwise; on the start page and the form it calls `leave`. `leave` closes the subview, first asking `useActionModal().confirm` "Discard this recipe?" (Keep editing / Discard, destructive) while `isDirtyRef` is set. `RecipeForm` sets that ref when its data differs from the initial data, when a cover is chosen or removed, and when it started from a reading; `RecipeSubview` also sets it as a reading lands. Cancel goes through the same `leave` (`onLeave`); saving and deleting close with `closeSubviewIf` and an identity check: the subview showing when the person tapped must still be the one showing, so a save that lands late never closes a screen it didn't start (a slow save, then back and Discard, then Cooking mode: the form is gone and the cooking screen stays).
- Each reader carries its own disclosure line above its input, on screen before anything is sent (text, link and file alike): text and file say an AI reads it and fills in the form and that it isn't saved; link says the page is opened and an AI fills in the form from it, and that it isn't saved. The text and link buttons read "Fill in the recipe". A file is also accepted dropped on the screen; one that isn't an image or PDF, or is over `MAX_UPLOAD_BYTES`, is refused with its own sentence. A link without a scheme gets `https://`, and anything that isn't an http(s) address with a dotted host is refused before any call.

- `lib/extractRecipe.ts` exports `extractRecipeFromText(text)`, `extractRecipeFromFile(file)` and `extractRecipeFromPage(page)`. All call the shared `generativeModel` from `src/lib/firebase/ai.ts` with a response schema: `{ title, servings, prepMinutes, cookMinutes, description, ingredients: [{ name, amount, unit, note }], steps: [string], fridgeDays, freezerDays, reheat, tip, unread: [string] }`, where `unit` is an enum of the app's unit labels and the four storage fields are nullable (see §5f). Files go as `inlineData` base64. Extraction calls are not cached and not Query-backed (non-idempotent), like the other extraction calls in the repo.

- **From a link** fetches the page first through `fetchPageContent` (shared and app-neutral: `src/lib/pageContent/*`; Pinch's `queries/recipePageQueries.ts` asks it for `text`, `meta` and `structuredData` filtered to `schemaTypes: ['Recipe']`, kept ten minutes and never persisted, so a retry of a failed reading doesn't refetch). The callable (`functions/src/linkMetadata/fetchPageContent.ts`) is signed-in only, takes http/https links only, uses the same private-network guard as `fetchLinkMetadata` (`safeFetch.ts`), returns only the kinds asked for and stores nothing. `extractRecipeFromPage` hands the page title and description, the Recipe data (when the page has any, marked as possibly incomplete) and the page text to the model, with a line telling it to ignore ads, comments and related links; the model writes the draft, so a page with partial, missing or odd data reads where a hand-written parser would drop it. The client maps a bad link, an unreadable page and a failed fetch to one warm sentence each.
- The model output is **only a draft for the form.** `matchIngredient(name)` (`utils/ingredientMatch.ts`) resolves each ingredient name to an ingredient id against the person's custom ingredients first, then catalog names and aliases (normalized: lowercase, singular, punctuation stripped). No match → the row is marked "Add as new ingredient" and, if kept, creates a custom ingredient with that name in the same save as the recipe (one `writeBatch`).
- The prompt tells the model the pasted text is data, not instructions, and asks it to list anything it could not read in `unread`; the draft never writes anything itself, so injected text can at most fill form fields the person reviews.
- **The reading becomes the form's starting values.** Because the start list and the reading screens come before the form, `RecipeSubview` holds a finished reading (`{ recipe, source, label }`, the label being "your text", the file's name or the page's host) and hands it to `RecipeForm` as `initialRead`; `getDataFromExtracted` builds the form's initial data from it (a line whose ingredient is already a row is skipped; unmatched names become "Add as new ingredient" rows). The form then says what it was filled from and lists `unread`, and nothing is saved until the person saves. `normalizeExtractedRecipe` also cuts every value to the limits the form and `firestore.rules` enforce (title, description, note, step lengths, servings, minutes, counts), so a long answer can't leave Add disabled or fail the write.
- **The form's basics are summary rows.** `RecipeBasics.tsx` holds `RecipeTitleField`, `RecipeTimesField` and `RecipeDescriptionField`, each a `SummaryEditor` over the same form values as before (`title`, `basics.prep` / `basics.cook` as text, `description`), so dirty tracking, `onTitleChange` and the header's live name work through the editors unchanged; only Servings is still an inline `Stepper`. The title editor opens by itself once (`defaultOpen`) when `recipeId` is null and there is no `initialRead`. Validation is the same predicates as before (`title` required and at most 200 characters, `description` at most 2,000, `isValidMinutes` for prep and cook) and each shows on its row as well as in its editor; the pinned footer's note names the first thing to fix ("Give it a title to save.", "Shorten the title to save.", "Check the prep and cook time to save."). The two minutes fields are `type='number'` with a visible "min" suffix and an `aria-label` of "Prep time in minutes" / "Cook time in minutes"; no label carries "(minutes)".
- **Removals in the form are undoable.** `RecipeForm` owns one `useUndoRemoval` and passes `showUndo` to `RecipeIngredients` and `RecipeSteps` (`onRemoved`), so an ingredient and a step removed in a row can't show two bars at once; `MealBuilder` owns its own for the plate (and puts the saved meal's name back when Undo returns the last item). The `UndoSnackbar` is the form's first child, not its last, because `Subview overlay` stretches its last child to fill the screen. Dreamer UI's toast was not used: its action label is fixed when the toast is added, so it can't show the seconds left without removing and re-adding the toast every second.
- A reading that finds no ingredients at all is a failure ("We couldn't find a recipe in that"). Errors map to one warm sentence each (busy, offline, unavailable, unreadable) and the detail is logged, never shown.
- `source` is saved as `AI_TEXT` / `AI_FILE` / `AI_LINK` when the draft came from the reader, `MANUAL` otherwise (even if the person edited it).

### 5a. Recipe sources

- **Choosing one.** The recipe form's "Where it's from" section is a `PickOrCreate` over the person's sources (existing labels as pills, a dashed "New source" first, nothing preselected for a new recipe), with a text field in its place while there are no sources yet. The form keeps the answer as `{ choice, newText }`: a source id, `NEW_CHOICE` with the label typed, or empty. Saving resolves it in `RecipeForm`: a typed label that matches a loaded source (ignoring case and spaces) reuses its id, otherwise it becomes `draft.newSource` (a new id and the label) that `saveRecipe` writes in the same `writeBatch` as the recipe, like `newIngredients`, so a failed save leaves no orphan source and an offline save is queued whole; a picked id that no longer exists becomes `null`; the result is `draft.sourceId`. While a source is chosen the picker shows a visible "Remove source" link (`PickOrCreate`'s optional `clearLabel`), besides tapping the chosen pill again.
- **Reading from a link.** A recipe read from a link (`source === 'AI_LINK'`) starts on its site: `getReadSourceChoice` takes the reading's label (the page's host), drops a leading `www.`, and picks the existing source whose label matches the host the same way (so "seriouseats.com" matches "www.seriouseats.com"), otherwise starts "New source" with the host as the text (cut to 80 characters). Only the form's starting value is set, so what the person picks or types afterwards is never replaced; the text field takes no focus on its own (`isFocusedOnOpen={false}`) so the keyboard doesn't open over the form. A recipe read from text or a file starts with none.
- **Writing.** `saveRecipe` writes `sourceId` on every save (new or edit), so an edit backfills the key on a recipe that lacked it and the field-scoped `updateDoc` never touches it elsewhere. `renameSource` is a field-scoped `updateDoc` of `label` and `updatedAt` after checking the loaded list for another source with that label. `removeSource` is one `writeBatch` per 400 writes holding the source's delete (in the first batch) and `sourceId: null` on every loaded recipe that names it, archived ones included; a recipe's `updatedAt` is left alone because it versions the cover image. A recipe created on another device at the same moment, or one that wasn't loaded yet, keeps a pointer to a source that no longer exists, which reads as none (above); if a later batch fails the source is already gone and the rest read the same way, and the error is shown.
- **Failure.** The sources listener is an optional enhancement: its error is logged (`console.error`), clears the loaded sources, never reaches the fatal load-error screen (`selectPinchLoadError` leaves it out), and its first answer or error ends the loading gate. While it has failed the form leaves the section out and keeps a recipe's own `sourceId` as it was, so editing never drops a label it couldn't read; the Sources entry, filter and drawer line simply don't appear.
- **Reading and counting.** `selectSources` (alphabetical), `selectSourcesById` and `selectSourceCounts` (`{ bySource: Map<id, count>, withoutSource }` over the listed, non-archived recipes, one pass) feed the Recipes screen. The Source filter is a `FilterBar` with one `FilterSection` holding a pick-one `PillGroup`: "Any", each source used by at least one listed recipe as "Label · count", and "No source · n" only when some recipe has none; a picked source or "No source" stays in the list while picked even if its count falls to zero. The filter exists only while at least one listed recipe has a source (otherwise the screen keeps its plain search-from-8 layout), the picked value falls back to "Any" when its source is gone or the filter disappears, and Meals are never filtered.

### 5b. Shopping list

- **Lines are pure functions** in `utils/shopping.ts`, checked by `npm run check:pinch-math`. `getRecipeLines(recipe, ingredientsById, servings)` scales each recipe line by `servings / recipe.servings`. `getItemsLines(items, recipesById, ingredientsById)` serves a saved meal, a planned meal and a whole day: a recipe item scales its recipe by the item's `servings` over the recipe's own, an ingredient item is its `servings` times the ingredient's serving size. A line whose recipe or ingredient is gone is left out. `mergeLines` adds lines with the same ingredient and unit, rounds once at the end to two decimals (thirds add up to one before rounding), caps an amount at 1,000,000 and drops a line that rounds to nothing. Units are never converted.
- **Adding** is `addToShoppingList({ uid, lines })`: the lines are merged and checked (not empty, at most 200 distinct, an id of 80 characters or fewer, a known unit, an amount above zero), then one `runTransaction` reads every target document first and writes each: a missing item is created (`isChecked: false`), an unchecked one gains the amount (rounded to two decimals, capped), and a checked one restarts with the new amount and `isChecked: false`. That decision is the pure `getMergedFields(existing, line, now, isChecked)` in `utils/shopping.ts`, so `npm run check:pinch-math` covers it (a new line, an open item gaining an amount, a checked item restarting, the 1,000,000 cap, a unit move carrying its flag, an amount that rounds to zero, a recipe with no servings); the transaction code only reads and writes around it. `mergeLines` also drops a non-finite amount, so a recipe whose servings are 0 gives no lines instead of an endless one. Two devices adding at the same time retry on conflict, so neither amount is lost (checked in the browser with two pages adding one recipe at once). The manual "+ Add" uses the same action with one line at the ingredient's serving size. A transaction does not queue offline, so adding needs a connection and says so by failing; ticking, changing and removing are ordinary writes and queue.
- **The other writes are field-scoped.** A tick is `updateDoc({ isChecked, updatedAt })` and touches only its own row (no surface-wide busy state). Changing an amount is `updateDoc({ amount, updatedAt })`. Changing the unit moves the item in one transaction: the old document is deleted and the amount lands on the new unit's document by the same rules as an add, except that a new document takes the moved line's `isChecked`, so a ticked line stays ticked. Moving into an existing document follows the add rules: an open one gains the amount, a ticked one restarts open with only the moved amount, and the row sheet's note says which before saving. Remove is `deleteDoc` after a destructive confirm. "Clear" takes the ids of the checked items from the store when it is dispatched, then deletes them in chunks of 250, one `runTransaction` per chunk: each re-reads its documents and deletes only the ones that are still checked, so an item another device un-ticked or removed in the meantime survives (a stale cache can't delete it), and the thunk resolves to how many were really deleted, which the toast says ("Cleared 15 items", with "The rest changed in the meantime" when fewer than the confirm dialog's count). The confirm dialog's count is the count at tap time. Chunks are independent transactions, so several chunks are not atomic together: one that fails leaves the others cleared and the list shows what is left. A Firebase error is logged and replaced by one plain sentence, "Check your connection and try again." for `unavailable` or when the browser is offline; the app's own sentences ("This is already off your list.") are shown as written.
- **The caps are limits of the app, not of the database.** 200 distinct lines per add and about 300 items on the list are enforced in the action and the screens only: the rules can't count a collection, so another client could pass either. Nothing breaks if it does (the list is longer, and a group still draws 100 rows then "Show N more").
- **Reading.** `selectShoppingRows` groups once per change (`groupShoppingItems`): what is left by ingredient family in Kitchen's family order, "Other" last for an ingredient with no family or one that is gone, rows A to Z by name and unit; what is checked off is grouped the same way into the Got it half (`groupShoppingItems` returns `{ toBuy, got }`); a row in the optional `held` map is placed by its held side instead of its stored flag (see "Holding a ticked row"). `selectShoppingOpenCount` feeds the Shopping tab's badge. `selectShoppingCost` prices the unchecked lines through `getLineCost` with each ingredient's default product, the same way a recipe's lines are priced, so it prices the amount on the list and not whole packages; what it can't price comes back as `Gap`s (`NO_PRODUCT`, `NOT_COMPARABLE`), which the help icon beside the cost line lists by ingredient. The line reads "Amounts on your list: about $3.16" and always has that icon (`HelpTip`, a modal on a phone or touch screen and a tooltip with a mouse): a plain help icon when nothing is left out and a warning when something is. It says that it prices just the amounts from default products, that stores sell whole packages so the till total is usually more, and, for each ingredient it can't price, its name and where to fix it ("Add a price from the ingredient in Kitchen": a fill-in sheet never opens over this screen's own sheets, so the text points the way).
- **Searching.** The list is one thin `PillGroup` of "To buy · n" and "Got it · n" (counts of the rows drawn, which follow the search) that shows one half at a time; the shown half is derived each render by `getShownShoppingView(pick, query, matches)` (the person's pick, unless text typed since the pick matches nothing there and something in the other half), never mirrored with an effect, and with nothing left to buy the To buy half says so and offers "See what you got". From `LIST_SEARCH_THRESHOLD` (8) items the screen shows a `SearchInput` under the switch that filters both halves by ingredient name (`searchByName`, accent-insensitive, every word must match) from the typed text, not a debounced value. Nothing matching says so in one muted line ("Nothing on your list matches “milk”."). The pill counts show the matches; "Clear what you got" (a quiet line in the Got it half, hidden while searching) still clears every ticked item and its confirm counts them all. If the list drops under 8 items the field disappears and the filter with it.
- **Holding a ticked row.** A tick used to move its row into "Got it" within one frame, so the row below jumped up by a row height and a quick second tap landed on the wrong item. Now the stored flag, `selectShoppingOpenCount` (subtitle and tab badge) and `selectShoppingCost` change at once, and only where a row is drawn lags: `useHeldRows` in `ShoppingScreen` records, before the write, the side the row was drawn on (`groupShoppingItems(…, held)` places by that side while the row keeps its stored `isChecked`, so the checkbox and the strike-through are the truth) and lets go after `SHOPPING_MOVE_DELAY_MS` (1.2 s). An un-tick in "Got it" leaves it the same way, a second toggle of one row keeps its first side and restarts only its own timer, and the other rows' timers are untouched. The held map is a ref, read by the selector closure when the store changes: holding renders nothing by itself (the snapshot that follows the write does, once, as before), and only letting go renders, so a tick costs no extra render of the screen. Timers are cleared when the screen unmounts, so leaving the tab mid-hold leaves nothing stuck (the row is simply in "Got it" when you return); a hold that never sees its write land (a failed tick) just lets go. "Clear" and its confirm count what is stored, so a row still drawn in its group is cleared with the rest, and the "Got it · N" label counts what is drawn there, so for a second it can be one behind the subtitle. Checked on the 80-item list of the scale kitchen at 4× CPU: click to next frame for a tick is a median of 86 ms before and 93 ms after (5 interleaved runs each); a tick renders about 30 components and the move after the hold about 48, against about 51 in one go before.
- **Drawing.** Groups after the first three mount when they near the viewport (`LazyMount` with `defer-offscreen`, a row estimated at 57 px), from `lg` they flow in two columns like Kitchen's, a group draws 100 rows then "Show N more" (and nothing is hidden for 103 or fewer), and the "Got it" rows mount only while that group is open. The drawers' add sheets (`AddToShoppingSheet`, titled "Shopping list") live in the screen that owns the drawer (`RecipesScreen`, `CalendarScreen`) and open after the drawer closes, as `FillInSheet` does.
- **Failure.** The shopping listener is an optional extra: its error is logged (`console.error`), clears the loaded items and sets `loadError`, never reaches the load-error screen (`selectPinchLoadError` leaves it out) and is not part of the loading gate (`selectIsPinchLoaded`). The screen then says it couldn't load the list and offers no Add, and the tab's badge shows nothing. While the first snapshot hasn't landed the screen says "Loading your list…". Both add sheets wait for `state.pinch.shopping.isLoaded` (and a null `loadError`) before they let anything through: `AddToShoppingSheet` disables Add, "Tick all" and its checkboxes with "Loading your list…" or "Your list couldn't load, so adding is paused." in the pinned footer, because its defaults (what is already on the list starts unticked) come from the listener's data, and `ShoppingAddSheet` locks its results with the same lines and also checks the 300-item cap when a result is picked, not only when it opens. The gate deliberately leaves the shopping slice out of `selectIsPinchLoaded`, so a shopping failure never reaches the fatal screen.

### 5c. Recipe ideas

- **Adding.** "New idea" opens one `FormSheet` (`IdeaAddSheet`: a drawer on a phone, a modal from `sm`), whose first view is the `MethodPicker` of the three ways (`IDEA_START_METHODS`; *Jot it down* is last and quieter). A way's own view swaps in place with a "Back" link to the list, so there is never a second overlay, and Escape or the back gesture closes the one sheet. Each way is a Dreamer UI `Form` of custom fields with the footer pinned (`Cancel`, "Save idea" disabled until valid); the title of the sheet is "New idea" on the list and "Idea" on a way.
- **From a link.** The field is `type='url'` (the submit button carries `formNoValidate`, since a link with no scheme is fine), `toWebLink` (`@/utils/urlUtils`) trims the ends, picks the one `http(s)://` token out of pasted text that has words around it (two such tokens give no link, and the field says "That looks like more than one link. Paste one at a time."), joins a scheme-less link that wrapped over lines, adds `https://` when no scheme is typed and refuses anything that isn't an http(s) address with a dotted host and no user name or password, and `toIdeaUrl` also caps it at 2,000 characters. A paste into the field that holds more than one word is run through it first (the browser would otherwise glue or drop the line breaks of a pasted line). After `DEBOUNCE_MS.linkDetection` the page is looked up with `fetchPageContent` through a TanStack Query factory (`queries/ideaPageQueries.ts`): `include: ['meta']` only, a callable timeout of `IDEA_LOOKUP_TIMEOUT_MS`, the shared 10-minute stale time for a success, no retry and no `persist`; a failure is a query error, never data, so it is never kept as an empty answer and a repeat of the same link asks again. `meta` carries the page's `<title>` (as `title`), `og:site_name` and `og:image` or `twitter:image` (resolved to an absolute address); the `images` kind is not asked for, because its other entries are the page's own `<img>` tags (logos, ads) and a wrong picture is worse than the tile. The line before anything is sent says what happens ("We fetch the page to find its title and picture. Nothing is saved from it except those."). While the lookup runs the sheet says so; when it returns, the preview shows the picture, a "Looks like" title field the person can change, and the site name; when it fails or times out, a title field with the host as its placeholder and "Couldn't read that page. You can type your own title." (a page that answered with no title gets the same field and "That page didn't give a title. You can type your own."). Saving waits for the lookup to settle (a result, a failure or the `IDEA_LOOKUP_TIMEOUT_MS` limit, plus the debounce): until then Save is disabled and the pinned footer says "Looking up the page. You can save in a moment.", so a link is never saved before its page's title could arrive. Once settled the title is what was typed, else the page's title for this very link, else (in `createIdea`, for a title left blank after a failed or empty lookup) the host, and `siteName` is the page's or the host. A typed title belongs to the link it was typed for (`{ link, title }`), so pasting another link starts again. A link already on the list (`findIdeaByUrl`, comparing `getComparableUrl`: no scheme, `www.`, fragment, trailing slash or tracking parameters, the other parameters in order) shows "That one's already in your ideas" with an "Open it" button, which closes the sheet and opens that idea's drawer, and disables saving; the lookup isn't made for it.
- **From a photo.** `PhotoPicker` (the shared file picker; its new `uploadLabel` and `changeLabel` props and optional `onRemove` are general) and `useImageUpload` (images only, up to 5 MB) feed `createIdea`, which resizes the file with `resizeImageToWebp` to `IDEA_PHOTO_MAX_DIMENSION` (a result that isn't `image/webp` counts as unreadable, and the resize happens before anything is uploaded, so a refused file touches no Storage object), uploads it to `getIdeaPhotoPath(uid, id)` before the document is written, and deletes it again if the write fails. A photo idea needs a title (the field starts empty with "What is it?") and a picture; a failed upload keeps the form and says why through `getStorageErrorMessage` (a neutral "We couldn't save that picture." when the code has no message of its own). A save failure shows as one red line in the footer's `note` slot (the pinned footer, beside the buttons, never below the form where the footer would cover it) and clears when the form changes or a picture is picked.
- **Jot it down.** A title and, shown from the start, a note.
- **The note** is a visible optional second field: "+ Add a note" with a "Remove note" link on the link and photo ways, open from the start on the text way and in the edit sheet.
- **The drawer.** `IdeaDrawer` (a `DetailSheet`, titled "Idea") shows the picture (only if there is one; a failed one leaves nothing), the title, the note (pre-wrapped), a made idea's tinted "Made into <recipe>" row, and for a link a quiet "Open <site name>" row (`Button href`, `target='_blank'`, `rel='noopener noreferrer'`, only for an http(s) address). Its footer is Modify (secondary) beside one primary: "Turn it into a recipe", or "Open the recipe" once made. Modify closes the drawer and opens `IdeaFormSheet`; "Open the recipe" closes it and opens the recipe's own drawer (`RecipesScreen`'s `openId`).
- **Editing.** `IdeaFormSheet` (title, link for a LINK, a photo with "Replace photo" and no remove for a PHOTO, note) saves through `updateIdea`, a field-scoped `updateDoc` of `title`, `note`, `updatedAt`, plus `url` (and `siteName`, `imageUrl: null` when the link names another page; the same page spelled differently keeps its site and picture) and `photoPath` when a replacement was picked, plus the keys an older document lacks. A replaced photo overwrites the same Storage object, and the picture's download URL is keyed on `updatedAt`, so it is fetched again. Delete is a destructive `useActionModal().confirm` ("Delete this idea?" naming it; for a made idea it says the recipe stays), then `removeIdea` (`deleteDoc`, then the photo object, best effort).
- **Turning an idea into a recipe.** The primary opens the recipe subview with `method: getIdeaRecipeMethod(idea)` (`'link'` for a LINK whose address `toWebLink` accepts, `'manual'` for a photo, a few words or an unusable address) and `fromIdeaId`: `{ kind: 'recipe', id: null, method, fromIdeaId }`. `RecipeSubview` reads the idea once, when it mounts. On the link way it hands `RecipeReader` the idea's address as `initialUrl` (the field starts filled; nothing is sent until the button is tapped, so the way's own line is the disclosure) and shows "From your idea: <title>" above it; `RecipeReader` with an `initialUrl` drops "paste its text instead" from its link failure lines, since the text way isn't one tap away from an idea. Back, Escape and the phone's back gesture from that way call `closeSubview` (to the Ideas list, never to the list of ways), "Write it yourself instead" goes to the form below, and a reading goes through `getReadingForIdea` before it reaches the form: the idea's note replaces the page's description when it has one, and the idea's title stays unless it is only the link's host (what a failed lookup saved), which gives way to the recipe's title; everything else (ingredients, steps, servings, times, the website as the source) is the reading's, and `fromIdeaId` still reaches `saveRecipe`, so the idea is marked made whichever way the form was reached. `getDataFromIdea` starts the form with the title, the note as the description and, for a LINK, `getSiteSourceChoice(getWebLinkHost(url), sources)`: the existing source named for the link's host (the `getHostSource` matching, as PR A's link reading does), else "New source" with the host, created with the recipe. It deliberately uses the host and not the page's own site name, so a person with a source called "seriouseats.com" doesn't get a second "Serious Eats". Nothing else is read from the idea: a photo is not copied and stays with the idea (the form says so, and points to "From a file", for a PHOTO idea; the file reading can't take a picture from storage). The form says "From your idea: <title>" and its title editor doesn't open by itself. Leaving an untouched prefilled form asks nothing, and discarding changes nothing. `saveRecipe` takes `fromIdeaId`. The recipe's batch (the recipe, its new source and its new ingredients) is committed on its own, exactly as for any recipe, so an idea that has gone from the server can never take a recipe down with it (a batch queued offline is refused whole if one of its documents is missing). Only after that commit, and only if the idea is still in the loaded store, one field-scoped `updateDoc` writes `{ …missing keys, recipeId, updatedAt }` on the idea; a failure there is caught, logged with `console.error` and not thrown, and `saveRecipe` returns `{ recipeId, linkedIdea }`. The form's toast says "“X” moved to Made into recipes." only when `linkedIdea` is true, and is the plain "Recipe added" otherwise. The recipe's `source` is `MANUAL`.
- **Reading.** `selectIdeas` (newest first), `selectIdeasById` and `selectIdeaGroups` (`{ toTry, made }`: a set of the listed recipes' ids, then two filters over the ideas) feed `IdeasView`, which shows "Still to try" and "Made into recipes" with `SectionDivider` once both have rows, a search (`SearchInput`) from `LIST_SEARCH_THRESHOLD` ideas that looks through title, site name and note, and rows in chunks of twenty (`LazyMount` with `defer-offscreen`, the first chunk eager) that are as tall as their lines: a `IdeaThumbnail` (the link's own picture through `FallbackImage` with the emoji tile as its fallback, a photo's download URL, or the tile), a title of up to three lines, one muted line (`getIdeaDetail`: a link's site, else the first line of the note, and never a line that only repeats the title, as a link saved with its host as the title would) and a chevron pinned to the first line. Two columns from `lg`.
- **The cap.** `SOFT_MAX_IDEAS` (200) is enforced by `createIdea` against the loaded list and by the screens (the "New idea" button is replaced by "Your list holds up to 200 ideas. Delete one to add another."), not by the rules, which can't count a collection.
- **Failure.** The ideas listener is an optional extra: its error is logged (`console.error`), clears the loaded ideas and sets `loadError`, never reaches the load-error screen (`selectPinchLoadError` leaves it out) and is not part of the loading gate. "New idea" is disabled until the first snapshot has landed (the list says "Loading your ideas…") and stays disabled after a failure (the list says it couldn't load them and shows no search), and `createIdea` refuses in both states with a plain sentence, so the guard holds even if the button is reached some other way; Recipes, Meals and Kitchen are unaffected.
- **Not cascaded.** Deleting a recipe never touches the ideas that name it (they read as not made), deleting an idea never touches its recipe, and a recipe made from an idea keeps no pointer back.

### 5c.2. Cooking mode

- **No data of its own.** The screen reads `selectRecipesById` and `selectIngredientsById`, which the app already keeps (and, in `CookNotes`, the notes selectors of §5e), and listens to nothing new. Its writes are the final panel's cook, which belongs to the cooks collection (§5d), and the note typed there about that cook (§5e), neither of them cooking mode's own: with no cooks listener the panel isn't offered, and with no notes listener it has no note link.
- **Entry.** `StartCookingRow` (tinted like "Add to shopping list", subtitle "Steps one at a time, with a timer") sits above that row in the recipe drawer, only when the recipe has a step, and in the planned meal view, only on a day that has not passed and only when a recipe item has steps (`meal.date >= getLocalTodayDate()`, the shopping row's own test). Both close their drawer and call `openSubview({ kind: 'cook', recipeId, servings? })` through `usePinchOverlay`. A planned meal with one such recipe opens it at that item's servings; with several, the row swaps the drawer's content to a short chooser (a "‹ Back" link, one row per item with its servings), never a second overlay. A recipe the day names but that has no steps, an ingredient item and an archived or deleted recipe are not offered. `Pinch.tsx` renders `CookingSubview` keyed on the recipe and keeps the tabs mounted and visible underneath (an overlay, like the recipe form), so closing returns to the same list position.
- **State, all local to `CookingSession`.** `view: 'steps' | 'ingredients'`, `stepIndex`, `servings` (starts at the planned servings, else the recipe's own, and falls back to 1 when the recipe's servings aren't above zero, clamped to `MIN_COOK_SERVINGS` and `MAX_COOK_SERVINGS`, the same two numbers the `Stepper` gets), `gotOut: Record<lineId, boolean>` and the timers sit side by side in the session, not in the view that shows them: the ingredients view and the steps view unmount when the person switches, so state held there would be lost (the ticks were, before this moved up). A tick re-renders the session, which is cheap because the rows are `memo` components with plain-value props and a stable `onToggle`. The index is clamped at render (`Math.min(stepIndex, lastIndex)`), so a recipe edited on another device while open can't leave it past the end; a recipe that loses its steps says "This recipe has no steps yet." and disables Next. `isFinished`, `isMarking`, a `markError` line and `cookedId` (the cook the final panel wrote, read back from the store by id: it is what turns the panel into "Marked as cooked", and what an Undo removes) sit beside them, and `useCookNote` keeps the note typed on that panel (`isOpen`, `text`, `isSaving`, `error`); nothing is kept when the screen closes.
- **A recipe that changes while the screen is open.** `CookingSubview` keeps the last recipe it saw: the store's `selectRecipesById` entry while there is one, and a snapshot that a `store.subscribe` callback updates (state is set from that callback, not during render or in an effect body, and a ref can't be read in render) for when the document goes. An edit shows at once; an archive (the selector includes archived recipes), a delete or a changed `updatedAt` adds one muted `role="status"` line, "This recipe was changed or removed elsewhere."; the session, its timers and its wake lock are never remounted. The "isn't in your kitchen anymore" line is only for a screen opened on a recipe that was never there.
- **Leaving.** The header's back, Escape (`Subview`'s capture-phase handler, which ignores a dialog on top) and the back gesture (`useSubviewHistory`, which restores its history entry before calling `onBack`) all call the same `leave()`, as do the final panel's "Not now" and, through the same `confirmLeave()`, its "I cooked this" (below): it first asks "Discard this note?" (Keep editing / Discard, destructive) while a note is typed and unsaved (`hasDraft`: it counts only while the cook is in the store and the note isn't saving, so Back during a save leaves and lets the write finish, and after an Undo the hidden box is not asked about; the note's own Cancel asks the same question), then, with no live timer, it calls `closeIfStillOpen()` (`closeSubviewIf` for this recipe's cooking screen, like every close after an await); with one or more running or paused (`liveCount`) it awaits `useActionModal().confirm({ title: 'Stop timers and leave?', confirmText: 'Leave', cancelText: 'Stay', destructive: true })` naming the count ("2 timers are still going"). A finished timer and a dismissed one don't count. One Escape or one back gesture over the confirm closes only the confirm (the existing dialog-on-top rule), and Stay is the focused button. Checked from each of the four with a running timer, a paused one, only a finished one and none.
- **Steps view.** `CookStepView` shows "Step n of m" and the step inside one polite, atomic live region (the counter and the text change together, so a screen reader says "Step 3 of 14" and the step after Next, an arrow key or a swipe, and focus stays on Next so Enter can press it again), one dot per step up to `MAX_STEP_DOTS` (12; past that the count alone), and the step in `text-2xl` (`text-xl` over 160 characters or on a screen under 500px tall) with `whitespace-pre-wrap`. Back and Next are 56px tall in a footer pinned by `Subview overlay`'s `.form-footer` rule (44px on a short landscape screen), with the timer strip above them; the one scroller is the overlay layer, so a long step scrolls between the sticky header and footer, and a new step scrolls it back to the top. Swipe is a pointer-event handler on the step area (touch and pen only, at least 60px sideways and 1.5 times the vertical travel, with `touch-action: pan-y pinch-zoom`, so vertical scrolling is the browser's and pinch-zoom still works on the text; only the first finger counts, and a swipe is ignored while `visualViewport.scale` is above 1.05, so zooming a step never turns the page); ArrowLeft and ArrowRight work in the steps view unless a field has focus, a modifier is down or a dialog is open.
- **Ingredients view.** `CookIngredientsView` scales each recipe line with `scaleAmount(amount, servings, recipe.servings)` (`utils/quantity.ts`), the same expression `getRecipeLines` uses for the shopping list, so a line reads identically in both (checked at 6, 2.5 and 0.5 servings against the shopping sheet), and formats it with `formatSize` ("0.25 cup"). Units are never converted, and a recipe whose servings aren't above zero is left unscaled instead of dividing by zero (the shopping list leaves such a recipe out). Rows are `memo` components with plain-value props, so a tick re-renders its own row; the ticks come in as a prop from the session. A line that names an ingredient that is gone reads "Removed ingredient".
- **Timers.** `useCookTimers` (`hooks/useCookTimers.ts`) holds timers as a discriminated union (`running` with `endsAt` and `startedAt`, `paused` with `remainingMs`, `done`) in state mirrored by a ref that every action reads and writes, so a callback never works from a stale list. Only `running` and `paused` timers are live and count toward `MAX_COOK_TIMERS` (4); a finished one waits in the strip for Dismiss, and starting a timer drops the oldest finished ones beyond four so the strip can't grow without end. A countdown runs to a wall-clock deadline and `settle` (mark each ended timer done once and raise its alerts) runs from three places: the nearest `setTimeout` (the deadline plus 20 ms), which re-arms for what is left if it wakes before the deadline (the clock stepped back, so it can't leave a timer at 0:00 unalerted); `useOnTabReturn`, so a timer that ended while the tab was hidden shows done the moment the page returns; and `CookTimerStrip`'s own 250 ms tick, which catches a deadline a forward clock step jumped over. The alerts are a toast ("Timer done", "Step 3 · 25 min", 10 seconds), `navigator.vibrate` when it exists, and three short beeps through one `AudioContext` created and resumed in the tap that started the first timer and resumed again before the beeps (a browser can suspend it in the background), each in its own `try` that logs and carries on. Only `CookTimerStrip` re-renders as the seconds pass (the tick runs only while a timer runs; a timer that has just resumed can't show more than it has left, because the display clamps to `startedAt`); the screen itself re-renders when a timer starts, pauses, resumes, ends or is dismissed. A chip is gone while a timer for the same step and length is in the strip; with four live timers the chips are disabled and "4 timers are going. Dismiss or finish one to start another." shows, and only then (four finished timers leave the chips enabled). The two notices ("Keep this screen open: timers stop if you leave it." from the first start until the strip first empties, shown only while the wake-lock line below isn't, so the two never sit side by side and read as a warning that the timer will die; and the cap line) and the wake-lock line sit in `role="status"` wrappers that are always mounted and `empty:hidden`, so a screen reader hears them appear. When a chip or a Dismiss removes the control that had focus, focus moves to the strip (`tabIndex={-1}`, no ring) or, when the last timer is gone, to the footer's primary button, never the body. Leaving asks first while a timer is live (see Leaving) and then ends every timer (the hook's state and its pending timeout go with the component).
- **Finding the times.** `findDurations` (`src/utils/durationUtils.ts`, no recipe words) reads a text for lengths of time: a number (integer, decimal, `1/2`, `1 1/2`, `½ ¼ ¾` alone or after a whole number) then hours, minutes or seconds in their written forms (`hour`, `hr`, `min`, `sec` and plurals), "an hour" and "half an hour"; a range (`10-15`, `10–15`, `2 to 3`, `1 or 2`) takes its longer end; "1 hr 30 min", "1 hour and 15 minutes" and "2 hours, 30 minutes" join into one length when only a comma or "and" sits between them and the units step down; repeats are dropped, and zero, anything over 24 hours, anything past the first 5,000 characters and any number written with a decimal comma or thousands commas ("1,5 hours" could be 1.5 or 15, "2,500 seconds" 2,500 or 500; a wrong timer is worse than none) is left out. The parser is linear (`joinPieces` builds one array in a single pass) and `findDurations` takes an optional `maxChars` (5,000 by default) so a check can lift the cap. Words like "tablespoons", "minced" and "second batch" without a number in front are not times. It returns `{ seconds, text }`; `formatDuration` ("25 min", "1 h 30 min") labels the chip and `formatClock` ("25:00") the countdown, rounding up so it reads 0:00 only at the deadline. Its cases, including the comma rule, the 5,000-character boundary (a text just under and just over, which fails if the cap is removed) and benchmarks with the cap raised (250,000 repeats of "5 min " and other hostile bodies, each under a second), are in `scripts/checks/durations.ts`, run by `npm run check:pinch-math` with the shopping math (which also covers `scaleAmount`). English only.
- **Keeping the screen on.** `useWakeLock(isActive)` (`src/hooks/useWakeLock.ts`, general) returns `{ isSupported, isHeld, hasFailed }`: it requests `navigator.wakeLock.request('screen')` while active, asks again each time `useOnTabReturn` fires (a browser lets go of the lock when the page is hidden, which also clears `isHeld` through the sentinel's `release` event), releases on unmount, and logs a refusal or a failed release instead of throwing. It mirrors the sentinel carefully: a sentinel whose `released` is true counts as absent, so the next return asks again even if its `release` event hasn't arrived; a `release` event changes `isHeld` only when it comes from the sentinel the hook currently holds, so a late one from an old sentinel can't clear a newer lock; and a request that resolves after the person left is released with its own `catch`, so a refused release is logged and never sets `hasFailed`. Cooking mode shows "This phone may turn the screen off; timers keep running only while this screen is open." (one sentence that also says what to do about timers, instead of a second line beside theirs) only when `!isSupported || hasFailed`, and nothing when it works. Wake Lock needs a secure context, so a plain-HTTP LAN page shows the line. It was checked with a stub (one request on open, none before, released when hidden, requested again on return, released on close, a refusal shows the line, a late `release` event from an old sentinel, a released sentinel without its event, and a refused release after leaving, each of which fails on the earlier hook); headless Chromium refuses the real call, which showed the line.
- **Limits.** Timers end when the screen closes and don't ring in the background or on a locked phone. Vibration is Android only and needs a recent tap. Sound is prepared in the tap that starts a timer, because a browser won't start audio on its own later. A step's text is never read for ingredients, so amounts written in it don't scale (the ingredients view says so once the servings differ).

### 5d. Marking a recipe cooked

- **Where it happens.** (1) The recipe drawer's tinted "I cooked this" row ("Keeps a record. You can change the date later."; disabled until its write settles, so a double tap or a tap during the acknowledgement lag can't make two cooks and two Undo toasts, which offline means it waits for the write to be acknowledged) adds a cook at the recipe's own servings (`getCookServings`: kept inside 0 to 1,000) with `cookedAt` now, and a toast says "Marked Adobo chicken as cooked" with an Undo that deletes exactly that cook's id. (2) Cooking mode's Finish (last step) opens a final panel inside the same screen, "Nice work. Mark it as cooked?", showing the session's servings (the ingredients view's stepper, so a person who scaled the recipe records what they made): its primary "I cooked this" awaits `confirmLeave()` (the "Stop timers and leave?" question while a timer is running or paused; Stay keeps the panel and writes nothing), then `markRecipeCooked(recipe, servings)` (it resolves with the new cook's id and raises the toast with Undo), then, only once the write has succeeded, ends the timers (`useCookTimers().clear()`) and swaps the panel in place to "Marked as cooked" with the servings, a quiet "Add a note about it" (§5e) and a primary **Done**; the panel reads the cook from the store by id, so an Undo from the toast puts the question back. Started from a planned meal (`DayDrawer`'s `onStartCooking` puts `plannedMealId` on the subview state; a recipe's own drawer leaves it out), the primary calls `markPlannedRecipeCooked` instead (it resolves with the cook's id, `getPlannedCookId`), with `cookedAt: Date.now()`, the session's servings and `isToasted: true` (the meal's row holds its own Undo, so only this caller asks for the toast): the same transaction and id as the row's "Mark as cooked", so one dinner can never be two cooks, and the panel then reads that cook by its fixed id exactly as above. A plan's cook already in the store before this session wrote one (`selectCooksById`, ignored while the write is in flight) turns the question into "Already marked as cooked" with a single "Done", no note and no write, and the transaction's own `isNew: false` covers a mark that lands from another device in between (the "Already marked" toast). The meal can be a day ahead ("Start cooking" is offered on any day that hasn't passed), so the cook is written at once and the row shows it from that day; a transaction needs a connection, so offline the thunk's "Check your connection and try again." shows in the panel, which stays open for another try. Every close that follows an awaited step (Done, a saved note, `leave()`) is `closeSubviewIf(...)`, which closes only if the screen showing is still this recipe's (and this plan's) cooking screen (a functional `setSubview` update in `Pinch.tsx`). The write can sit queued offline and resolve long after the person has left: that used to call a bare `closeSubview()` and close whatever was open by then (a half-typed recipe form: reproduced with a delayed write, then fixed), and now a late resolution only updates the state of a session that is gone. The header's back is not disabled while the write is in flight, since offline that would trap the person on the screen: it leaves, the write carries on and its toast arrives when it is acknowledged; a failed write shows its sentence in the panel and keeps it open. "Not now", the header's back, Escape and the back gesture call `leave()` and write nothing (with a typed, unsaved note `leave()` and the note's Cancel first ask "Discard this note?" with Keep editing / Discard, but not while the note is saving or once an Undo has hidden the box); a quiet "Back to the last step" link (and ArrowLeft) returns to the step. Finish only opens the panel when `canMark` holds (the cooks listener has landed without error and the recipe is still in the store); otherwise it leaves as before, and a full history (500) disables "I cooked this" with a line saying why. (3) A planned meal on today or a day that has passed (`meal.date <= getLocalTodayDate()`) has one "Mark as cooked" row per recipe in it, in the same block as "Start cooking" and "Add to shopping list", turning into "Cooked ✓" with an Undo once its cook exists (the toast's Undo covers the seconds after a mark, but this standing one can come weeks later and takes a cook out of the history, so it asks first with the history's "Delete this cook?", naming the day). A recipe listed twice in one meal is one row with the servings added up (`getMarkableRecipes`), because the deterministic id can only hold one cook; an ingredient item has no row. (4) The Recipes list says "Cooked 3×" in the row's detail line when the count is above zero, and the drawer says "✅ Cooked 3 times · last Oct 12" (or "Cooked once · Oct 12") under its facts.
- **Writes.** `addCook({ uid, recipeId, servings, cookedAt })` is a plain `setDoc` of a new document with a fresh id and returns the id (the toast's Undo and the history's "+ Add an earlier cook" both use it); `markPlannedCooked(...)` is one `runTransaction` that reads the deterministic document and writes nothing when it exists, resolving `{ id, isNew }` (the decision is the pure `mergePlannedCook`, which `npm run check:pinch-math` covers with the other cook helpers), and a `false` shows an "Already marked" toast; `updateCook` is a field-scoped `updateDoc` of `servings`, `cookedAt`, `updatedAt` (plus a missing `plannedMealId` as `null`) and never writes a cached cook back; `removeCook` is `deleteDoc` of one id (Undo, and the history's Delete after a destructive confirm). The two adds refuse, with a sentence the screen shows, when the cooks listener hasn't landed or has failed, when the recipe isn't in the loaded recipes, when the servings aren't above zero and up to 1,000, when `cookedAt` is more than a minute ahead, and when the recipe already has `SOFT_MAX_COOKS_PER_RECIPE` (500) cooks. The screens gate the same predicate: the rows are disabled while the history loads, say "This recipe's history is full" at 500, and are left out when the listener failed. A transaction doesn't queue offline, so marking from a planned meal needs a connection: every cook write logs a Firebase error and shows "Check your connection and try again." for `unavailable` or an offline browser (the shopping list's rule), else one plain sentence; the recipe drawer's mark is an ordinary write whose toast arrives when the write is acknowledged, while the fact line already shows it from the local cache.
- **The history sheet.** "Cooking history" (a row in the recipe drawer, only when the recipe has a cook: for a recipe never cooked the action block stays at three rows, Start cooking, I cooked this and Add to shopping list, and a fourth was judged too heavy at 390×844, so the way in is to mark it and change the date in its history) closes the drawer and opens `CookingHistorySheet`, a `FormSheet` titled "Cooking history" (a drawer on a phone, a modal from `sm`), from `RecipesScreen`. It lists the recipe's cooks newest first as "Oct 12 · 6 servings" (with "From your plan for Sun, Oct 11" when `plannedMealId` is set), the first `COOK_HISTORY_FIRST_PAGE` (8) and then "Show N more" in pages of 50, but only while more than three would be hidden (so 11 cooks show all of them, 12 show 8 and "Show 4 more"), with "Show fewer" once everything is open. A fixed header (the recipe's name and "+ Add an earlier cook") sits over the one scrolling list, capped with `max-h-[max(12rem,calc(92dvh-14rem))]`. A row, or the add link, swaps the sheet's content in place for `CookEditView` with a "Back" link (never a second overlay; each opening carries a token, and the close after a save or delete is `closeView(token)`, a functional update that does nothing once another view is showing, so a write that resolves late can't close the wrong one): a day and a time (`Input type='date'` with `max` today, stacked below a narrow container), a servings `Stepper`, Cancel and Save, and for an existing cook an icon-only `DeleteIconButton` on the left of the footer whose destructive confirm ("Delete this cook?") names the day and time. Save is disabled until something changed and says "Pick a time that has already passed." for a moment in the future (compared against `useNow()`, the 15-second clock); a save that leaves the day and time untouched keeps the stored `cookedAt` to the millisecond instead of the minute the inputs show. The edited cook is read from the live store by id, so one deleted on another device returns to the list. When the listener failed the sheet says so and offers no add; a full history says so and hides "+ Add an earlier cook". Under each row a quiet "Add a note" (and an "Add a note" row in a cook's own view, disabled with "Save your changes first" while that view is dirty) swaps the same content for `NoteForm` (§5e), carrying a token too, so its close is the same functional update; both rows are left out when the notes listener hasn't landed or failed.
- **Reading.** `selectCooksByRecipe` (a `Map<recipeId, Cook[]>`, newest first by `cookedAt`, then `createdAt`, then id), `selectCookSummaries` (a `Map<recipeId, { count, lastCookedAt }>` built in one pass over every cook), `selectCooksById` and `selectCooksLoadError`, all `createSelector`s over the slice's items. A recipe row reads only its own count (`useAppSelector((state) => selectCookSummaries(state).get(id)?.count ?? 0)`, a number), so marking one cook re-renders that recipe's row and no other; a planned meal's row reads its own `Cook` object and whether the recipe's history is full, so every visible row re-renders when the cooks change, which costs nothing next to the few rows of one meal. Cooks whose `recipeId` names no recipe are never read by anything, so they are neither shown nor counted.
- **Listener and failure.** `startCooksListener` (a plain collection `onSnapshot`) is started from `usePinchSync` with the other optional collections. Its error is logged (`console.error`), clears the loaded cooks and sets `loadError`; it never reaches the load-error screen (`selectPinchLoadError` leaves it out) and is not part of the loading gate (`selectIsPinchLoaded`). Without cooks the app is unchanged except that the drawer shows no fact line, "I cooked this" or "Cooking history" row, the Recipes list shows no "Cooked N×", and a planned meal shows no cook rows.
- **Archive and delete.** Archiving a recipe (it is on a planned day or in a saved meal) leaves its cooks alone, so the history is still there; a hard delete takes the recipe's cooks with it in `deleteCooks`, which reads them from Firestore (`getDocs` with `where('recipeId', '==', id)`, one equality, so no index) instead of the store, because the store is empty while the cooks load or after the listener fails, and deletes them in `writeBatch`es of `COOK_BATCH_SIZE` (400), after the recipe's own delete has succeeded and as best effort (a failure is logged and the delete still finishes). Anything that survives (a cook written after the read, a failed batch) names a recipe that no longer exists and is ignored by every reader. Cooks made from a planned meal are not removed when the planned meal is: the person did make it.
- **The cap.** `SOFT_MAX_COOKS_PER_RECIPE` (500) is checked by the add actions against the loaded cooks and by the screens, not by the rules, which can't count a collection. It is per recipe, so the whole collection is not bounded.
- **A toast over a drawer.** The Undo toast is raised while the recipe drawer is open, so the toast stack sits above the drawers' overlay (`z-index: 110` on the notifications region in `index.css`; drawers and modals are `z-[100]`): below it the toast showed dimmed behind the overlay and its button couldn't be tapped. It is a central rule and reaches every mini-app's toasts.


### 5e. Recipe notes and tweaks

- **Where a person writes one.** (1) The recipe drawer's "Notes" section, under the steps: with no notes it is one tinted `ActionRow`, "Add a note" ("Something to remember next time"); with notes it is the header "Notes 12", that row, then the entries newest first. (2) "Add a note" under each time in the cooking history, and in a cook's edit view. (3) "Add a note about it" on cooking mode's last screen, once the cook it follows up has been written. A note on a cook carries that cook's id as `cookId`; any other note writes `null`.
- **The drawer's journal.** `RecipeNotes` reads `selectNotesByRecipe` and `selectNoteSummaries` for its recipe and `selectCooksById`, and draws `NoteEntry` rows: the date (`formatDateShort`, with a year when it isn't this one), written "Changed on Oct 6" with a small "Tweak" tag for a TWEAK so it reads as a point in time, and an **Edit** button (named "Edit the note from Oct 6", so its accessible name holds its visible word) in a 40px band, then "After cooking on Oct 12" when `cookId` names a cook that still exists, a TWEAK's "You changed:" list, the text (`whitespace-pre-wrap`, four lines and "More" only when it overflows, measured by a `ResizeObserver` callback, so the state is set from a subscription and not an effect body) and, for a NOTE written before the recipe's latest tweak, "Written before your Oct 20 tweak". The first 5 entries show and "Show N more" (pages of 50) only when more than three would be hidden, with "Show fewer" once everything is open. The facts line gains "✏️ Last tweaked Oct 20" only when a TWEAK exists. A failed notes listener turns the section into "Your notes couldn't load just now." with no add row.
- **In cooking mode.** `CookNotes` (`components/cooking`) sits under the Steps / Ingredients switch on both views (not on the final panel) and reads the same selectors as the drawer (`selectNotesByRecipe`, `selectNoteSummaries`, `selectCooksById`, plus `notes.isLoaded` and `loadError`) in its own component, so the session doesn't re-render for a note. It renders nothing until the notes have loaded without error and the recipe has one. Collapsed it is one tappable block of the newest entry, whatever its kind, from the pure `getNoteSummary` (`utils/notes.ts`, checked by `npm run check:pinch-math`): "Your latest note (Oct 12):" and the words for a NOTE, "Changed on Oct 6:" and the change lines joined with "; " (then the reason, after a full stop) for a TWEAK, runs of white space squeezed to one so a note's line breaks don't take the lines, clamped to two (one on a short landscape screen). Tapping it opens the journal in place, newest first through `NoteEntry` with `onEdit={null}` (no Edit button: editing is a sheet, and no sheet opens over an overlay subview), `RECIPE_NOTES_FIRST_PAGE` (5) at a time with "Show N more" only when more than three would hide. It is a quiet `bg-muted/50` block with a hairline, so a late-landing listener adds it once and a step change never moves it.
- **The note sheet.** `NoteSheet` is a `FormSheet` ("Note": a drawer on a phone, a modal from `sm`) mounted by `RecipesScreen` after the drawer closes, like the cooking history; `NoteForm` is its body and is also what the history sheet swaps in place (with a "Back" link, never a second overlay). One textarea (`NoteTextField`, focused once the sheet has settled, with a counter from 1,800 characters) and, for a new note on a recipe that has cooks, "Is this about a time you cooked it?" as a `PillGroup`: "No, just a note" first, then the latest four cooks (`getNoteCookChoices`, plus an older cook that was preselected), each labelled with its day, and with its time when two share a day (`getCookChoiceLabels`). Save is disabled until the text has a character (a blank paste stays disabled) and the footer says why. Editing offers only the words (the date and the cook show as a muted line, a TWEAK's "You changed:" list is read-only), an icon-only `DeleteIconButton` asks "Delete this note?" first, and a saved NOTE has "Update the recipe from this note" (disabled with "Save your changes first" while its text is edited). Saving a new note toasts "Added a note to Adobo chicken". A sheet whose notes haven't loaded says so and offers nothing.
- **Writes.** `addNote({ uid, recipeId, text, cookId })` is a plain `setDoc` of a NOTE with a fresh `generateUuid()` id and every key; it refuses, with a sentence the sheet shows, until the notes listener has landed, when it failed, for a recipe that isn't in the store, for text that is blank or over 2,000 characters, for a `cookId` that isn't one of that recipe's loaded cooks and at 300 notes for the recipe. `updateNote({ uid, id, text })` is a field-scoped `updateDoc` of `text` (blank is refused for a NOTE and becomes `null` for a TWEAK) and `updatedAt`, plus any key an older document lacks; it never writes a cached note back. `removeNote` is `deleteDoc` after a destructive confirm. The 300 is the app's cap, not the rules'.
- **A tweak is part of the recipe's save.** `saveRecipe` builds the TWEAK itself and puts it in the recipe's own `writeBatch`, beside the recipe update, any new source and any new ingredients, so the note exists exactly when the recipe write did (a failed save leaves no orphan note, and an offline save is queued whole). It does so only for an edit (never a new recipe), only when `selectTweakTracking` is `'on'` (the recipe has a cook, the cooks and notes listeners have landed without error and the journal has room), and only when `getRecipeChanges(existing, draft, …)` returns a line. A cover-only edit has none, neither has one that changes only the recipe's care (§5f), and neither has one that changes nothing the person could see (stray spaces, an empty step or description). The note's `createdAt` is the recipe's own `updatedAt` for that save, its `text` is the form's "What changed and why?" line trimmed (or `null`) and its `cookId` is `null`. The lines are compared with the recipe as this device last had it, so an edit made on another device in between can show in them.
- **`getRecipeChanges(before, after, ingredientsById, sourcesById?)`** (`utils/recipeChanges.ts`, pure, covered by `npm run check:pinch-math`) compares the two recipes after trimming and ignoring blank steps, a blank description and a blank line note, and returns plain-word lines in the form's order: "Renamed to “…”", "Serves 4 → 6", "Prep time 10 → 15 min" (or "Cook time 35 min → 1 h 10 min" past an hour, "set to", "removed"), a source change, then ingredients by line id ("Garlic 4 → 6 each", "Garlic 4 each → 0.5 cup", "Added Lime juice, 2 tbsp", "Removed Salt", "Swapped Butter for Olive oil", a line note added, changed or removed, and "Ingredients reordered" when the lines both lists share changed their order), steps ("Step 2 now reads: “…”" quoted to 80 characters, "Added step 4: …", "Removed step 2: …", or "Steps reordered" when the same steps changed order; a shared start and end are skipped, so a step put in the middle is one line, not a rewrite of what follows) and nutrition per typed metric ("Calories 520 → 600", "Fat set to 20 g", "Protein back to the calculated number"). There is no line when nothing changed, and a safety line ("The recipe changed") covers any difference the rest missed, so "no lines" always means "no change". At most 8 lines, the rules' cap: past eight the first seven stay and the eighth says "and N more", and a line over 200 characters is cut at a whole character and ends in "…", counting 16-bit units the way the rules' `size()` does (tried in the emulator: 100 emoji pass, 101 are refused; counting code points would let a line of emoji through the check and fail the save). Amounts are compared as they print (rounded to two places), so 4 → 4.001 is not a line of its own and a save whose only change is one of those reads "The recipe changed". A check runs 600 random pairs of edits (lines exactly when the recipe changed, never more than eight).
- **Updating from a note.** "Update the recipe from this note" closes the sheet and opens the recipe form (`{ kind: 'recipe', id, fromNoteId }`). The form shows the note as a read-only quote at the top ("Updating from your note") and starts "What changed and why?" with the note's words, which the person may change. Saving writes a new TWEAK as above; the NOTE it came from is never written. A recipe with no cooks records nothing, as for any edit, and shows no "What changed and why?" line; the quote is still shown.
- **The form's field.** "What changed and why?" is a one-line input after Nutrition (Enter doesn't submit), with a line under it saying that saving adds a note with today's date and what changed. It shows only while tracking is `'on'`; at `'full'` a muted line says this change won't be noted and to delete an older note; otherwise nothing shows, so a recipe never cooked, a failed listener and a new recipe say nothing false. There is no way to save an edit without its tweak (open question 19).
- **Listener and failure.** `startNotesListener` (a plain collection `onSnapshot`) is started from `usePinchSync` with the other optional collections. Its error is logged (`console.error`), clears the loaded notes and sets `loadError`; it never reaches the load-error screen (`selectPinchLoadError` leaves it out) and is not part of the loading gate (`selectIsPinchLoaded`). Without notes the app is unchanged except that the drawer says "Your notes couldn't load just now.", no "Add a note" or "Last tweaked" shows, the history and cooking mode offer no note, and a recipe edit saves without a tweak.
- **Archive and delete.** Archiving a recipe leaves its notes alone. A hard delete reads the recipe's notes from Firestore (`getDocs` with `where('recipeId', '==', id)`, one equality, so no index) instead of the store, which is empty while the notes load or after they fail to and would leave them orphaned, and removes them with `deleteNotes`, `writeBatch`es of 400, after the recipe's own delete and as best effort (a failure is logged and the delete still finishes), like cooks; anything that survives names a recipe that no longer exists and is ignored by every reader. Deleting a cook leaves its notes, which then read as plain notes while the dangling `cookId` stays in the document.
- **Reading and performance.** The grouping and the summary are `createSelector`s over the slice's items (the summary in one pass). `RecipeRow` is a `memo` component, so opening a drawer or a sheet, or adding a note, re-renders no row of the list (it re-rendered every mounted row before); a recipe save still re-renders them, as every recipe snapshot does, because each snapshot gives every recipe a new object.


### 5f. Recipe care

- **Where a person writes it.** The recipe form's "Keeping it" row (`RecipeCareField`, a `SummaryEditor`) sits between Steps and Nutrition: a caption, the summary (`getCareSummary`, or "Add how to keep it" in muted text while empty, two lines at most) and a chevron. Its editor is a drawer on a phone or a short screen and a popover from `sm`, ending in Done like the others, and holds two compact number fields, "Fridge" and "Freezer", each with a visible "days" suffix (`type='number'`, 1 to 365, labelled "Fridge in days" and "Freezer in days"), the line "Whole days. A month is 30, so 90 shows as 3 months.", a "Reheat" and a "Tip" textarea (two lines from `sm`, three on a phone, since each can hold 300 characters). Captions are small and inputs 40px (`Field isCompact`, `COMPACT_INPUT_CLASS`); Enter in a days field closes the editor, and in Reheat or Tip it adds a line break (the drawer keeps it) and never saves the recipe. Days are typed as days and stored as one number, not chosen as weeks or months: a unit picker would double the editor's height for the one case that wants it (a freezer measured in months), so the summary does the converting on the way out ("90" reads back as "3 months", as 90 minutes reads back as "1 h 30 min" in Prep and cook). A bad value (days that aren't whole numbers from 1 to 365, a reheating line or tip over 300) shows in red under the row and in the editor, and the pinned footer says "Check the fridge and freezer days to save."
- **Form data.** `RecipeFormData.care` is a `CareText` (four strings), so typing never loses a half-typed number. `getInitialData` builds it from `getRecipeCare(recipe)`, `getDataFromExtracted` from the reading, and the save turns it into `RecipeCare | null` with `parseCareText` (blank parts null, all blank null). The form's dirty check includes it like any field, so Keep editing and Discard behave as before.
- **Writing.** `saveRecipe` puts `care` in the same field-scoped `updateDoc` (or the new recipe's `set`) as every other field the form owns, on every save: a recipe written before care gains the key the first time it is saved from the form, as `null` unless the person filled the row in, and a save that never opens the row can't lose what was there. A change to care never records a tweak (§5e): `getRecipeChanges` leaves it out of what it compares and of the check that guards its "no lines means nothing changed", so a save that touches only care writes no note and leaves "Last tweaked" and every older note's "Written before your … tweak" label as they were, and one that also changes something the tweak does cover records only that line (open question 20).
- **The drawer.** `RecipeCareSection` is a `FormSection` titled "Keeping it" between Steps and Notes, drawn only when `getRecipeCare` isn't null. Each part is a line with a fixed 20px emoji column: 🧊 "Keeps 3 days in the fridge", ❄️ "Keeps 3 months in the freezer", ♨️ "Reheat: …" (not 🔥, which means calories everywhere in Pinch) and 💡 the tip in the person's words (pre-wrapped, long words break); a part that is null is left out.
- **Cooking mode.** `CookingSession` takes `getLeftoversLine(getRecipeCare(recipe))` from the recipe it already holds and shows it as one muted line (`text-balance`, so it never ends on a lone word) under the servings and "Keeps a record…" lines on both states of the last panel ("Nice work. Mark it as cooked?" and "Marked as cooked"), so it doesn't move when the cook is written. Reheating and a tip alone give no line. Nothing else on the panel or in cooking mode changes.
- **The readers.** The text, photo and link readings share one schema and one prompt, extended with `fridgeDays`, `freezerDays`, `reheat` and `tip` (all nullable) and the rule "fill in … only when the recipe itself says how long leftovers keep, how to reheat them or how to store them. Never estimate how long food keeps, and use null when the recipe is silent." The schema tells the model to answer in whole days (a week is 7, a month is 30) and, for a range, with its shorter end: a storage time is a safety figure, so the cautious end is read, where cooking mode's timers take the longer end of a cooking range. `normalizeExtractedRecipe` turns the answer into `ExtractedRecipe.care` with `toCare`: days are rounded down (a storage time is a safety figure, so 36 hours is 1 day and half a day is dropped) and must then be 1 to 365 (anything else is null, so "2 years" is dropped and never cut to a year), text is trimmed and cut to 300, a wrong type is null, and a record with nothing in it is null. A reading that finds no storage lines leaves `care` null, so the form's row stays quiet and nothing is listed under "We couldn't read"; the disclosure lines ("An AI reads it and fills in the form. It isn't saved.") are unchanged and still true. The form is filled for review only, as before, and the storage text a person typed is never sent to the AI.
- **Copy.** Pinch has no copy-out of recipes (the Markdown copy was removed in the redesign), so nothing carries care out of the app. If a copy returns it should add the lines under the steps with the person's text escaped for the format, and add nothing for a recipe without care.
- **Legacy and rules.** `isRecipe` reads `care` with `data.get('care', null)`, so a document without the key is valid on create and on update, and `isRecipeCare` allows null or a map with exactly `fridgeDays`, `freezerDays`, `reheat` and `tip` (days null or an int from 1 to 365, text null or 1 to 300 characters). It doesn't insist that the map holds something (the client writes null for that) and it changes nobody's access: the owner writes recipes as before, another signed-in user and a signed-out visitor are refused as before.
- **No new listener, function, secret or index.** `care` rides on the recipes the app already listens to; `firestore.indexes.json` and `functions/` are unchanged.

### 5g. Prepared containers

- **Where one is made.** (1) Cooking mode's Finish panel, after "I cooked this" (below); (2) a recipe's or saved meal's drawer, "Put some away" (a `ContainerFormSheet` mounted by `RecipesScreen` after the drawer closes, opened on that recipe or meal with nothing to pick); (3) Kitchen's Prepared meals subview, "Add" (the same sheet, which first asks what it is: a search over the listed recipes and saved meals capped at four rows, ending in "Call it “text”" for a typed name, with a "Change" link once picked, and its search box focused once the drawer has settled). The sheet's title is "Container", its fields are how many servings (a whole-number stepper, 1 to 100), where (Fridge or Freezer, a `PillGroup`), **how many containers** (a stepper from 1 to `MAX_CONTAINERS_PER_PUT_AWAY` (20), never more than the servings, with a line that says how the servings split: "2 servings in each", or "3 servings in the first, 2 in each of the others", the extra servings going one each to the first) and eat by (a date input with the hint "Your call. …"), inside the repo's `Form` as one `custom` field. It writes through `useContainerActions().putAway(inputs)`, which checks the cap (the soft cap counts containers: what is in stock plus what is added), builds the containers (`buildContainer`, a fresh `generateUuid()` each, one shared `createdAt`) and dispatches `writeContainers`: **one `writeBatch`**, so N containers land whole or not at all. Each document is an ordinary create, so the rules did not change; a batch of 20 was written against the emulator with the real rules. The batch is not awaited: the write is accepted locally at once, the sheet and the Finish panel answer from the store, and only a refusal that arrives later raises a toast ("That didn't save"), so a slow or offline network never holds the screen (offline, the toast adds that it was saved on this phone and will sync). The saved toast ("Saved 4 containers (4 servings) in the freezer, eat by Jan 12, 2027", or "Saved 2 servings in the freezer, …" for one) carries an Undo (`undoContainerBatch`): one transaction that re-reads each document and deletes the batch only while none of them has changed since (`updatedAt` still equals `createdAt`), else it leaves them and says so. It reads server state, so an Undo tapped within one round trip of a save that is still in flight can find nothing to delete, and the put-away then lands anyway (narrow, accepted).
- **Eat-by defaults (`getEatByDefault`, `getEatByHint`, `getMoveNotes`).** Counted from the viewer's local today. Fridge: the recipe's `care.fridgeDays`, else `CONTAINER_FRIDGE_DAYS`. Freezer: `care.freezerDays`, else `CONTAINER_FREEZER_DAYS`. Thawing out of the freezer: the shorter of `CONTAINER_THAWED_DAYS` and `care.fridgeDays`. **A move starts from what the container already has** (`currentEatBy`): a container already past its date keeps that date through any move (the hint reads "Your call. The date stays as it was." and the move view says "It was past its date before it was moved."); a thaw starts at the earlier of its current eat-by and today plus the thaw days ("Your call. It already had an earlier date, so that stays." when the current date wins); moving an in-date fridge container into the freezer starts fresh from today. A container that has been frozen before says so in the freeze view ("It's been frozen once before.", "twice", "N times"), with "Your call." kept and no claim about safety. The hint otherwise starts "Your call." and says where the number came from ("Taken from the freezer time in your recipe.", "Once thawed, this starts at 2 days.", "A starting point of 4 days; change it to what you trust."; none says how long food keeps or that it is safe, and pinchMath guards that). The sheet's move rows read "Sets a new eat-by date" in every state. Until the person types a date, the date follows the default for the place; a typed date is kept when the place changes and never replaced by a default. A saved meal and a typed name have no care, so they use the named defaults.
- **The list.** `ContainersSubview` (`{ kind: 'containers'; recipeId?; mealId? }`, a `Subview`, so its back button and the back gesture close it, and a sheet over it takes the gesture first) shows one place at a time through a thin `PillGroup` of Fridge · n, Freezer · n and Finished (counts of what matches the scope chip and the search; the shown place is derived each render, never mirrored with an effect: the person's pick, or the other place while a search matches only there, and before any pick the fridge, falling to the freezer while the fridge is empty; both places keep their option when empty, so the switch never reshuffles), soonest eat-by first (`selectContainers` sorts once), drawing `CONTAINER_LIST_PAGE_SIZE` rows per place and "Show N more" only when more than three would hide, and a search from `LIST_SEARCH_THRESHOLD` rows. A row says how many servings, "Eat by tomorrow" (today, tomorrow or the date read in UTC) and a soft tint with a word: "Eat soon" (today and the next `CONTAINER_EAT_SOON_DAYS` days) or "Past its date" (before today), never a solid fill. Two rows with the same title in a place are told apart by a "Made Oct 14" tag, and a thawed one always says "Thawed". Opened from a drawer it shows the containers of one recipe, or of one saved meal and of the recipes in it (the same roll-up as the Meals row), with an "Only Adobo chicken ×" chip that clears the filter. - **Changes are transactions over pure plans.** `eatContainer({ id, count: number | 'ALL' })` (`ALL` means whatever is left when the transaction reads it), `tossContainer`, `moveContainer({ id, to, eatBy })`, `changeContainerEatBy`, `correctContainerCount({ id, count })`, `undoContainerEnd({ id, at })` each run `applyPlan`: `runTransaction`, `transaction.get`, the plan for that action over the fresh document, then `transaction.update` of only the fields the plan owns (`servings` and/or `status`, `place`, `eatBy`, always `history` and `updatedAt`). A plan refuses with one sentence when the container is already finished ("This one is already finished."), when a count is more than is left ("Only 1 serving is left, so nothing was changed."), when it is already in that place, or when an eat-by isn't a whole UTC day; the thunks never let `servings` leave 1 to 100. A transaction needs a connection, so a failed write says "Check your connection and try again." It resolves to the ids and instants it really cleared, so the toast's number is the number written; a chunk that already committed stays committed when a later one fails (the sentence says how many were cleared before it stopped); nothing cleared is a plain sentence and no toast. `CLEAR` is an end event like `EAT` and `TOSS` (`END_KINDS`): it is the last event, so `appendEvent` never drops it, `planUndoEnd` reverses it only while it is still the last event at that instant, `getFinishedText` reads "Cleared out 3 of 6" and `getEventText` "Cleared out · 3 servings". The Undo toast (once per toast, the in-flight guard keyed on the place so a double confirm cannot clear twice) is `undoClearOut`, chunked the same way, putting back only what is still as the clear-out left it and saying how many came back ("Put back 7 of 9; the rest changed since"). A container another device put away after the tap is not cleared; deleting a container (which erases its history) stays a separate, per-container action. The rules' `kind` allowlist gained `CLEAR`; documents written before it are untouched.
- **History and the cap.** `appendEvent` keeps at most `MAX_CONTAINER_HISTORY` (10) events: the first is never dropped, the oldest EAT goes next, then the oldest EDIT, then the oldest move after the first place event that is not a FREEZE, and only then the event right after the first place event (out of reach while moves alternate between freezing and thawing); the event just added is never the one dropped. The first place event and the FREEZEs go last because they are what `getStartServings` (what went in), `getFreezeLine` ("Frozen twice", "Frozen 3 times", nothing for one) and the sheet's "Thawed in the fridge on …" line (`getLastPlaceEvent`, the last event that says where it went, so the line survives an eat or an edit) read: a cook's container (MADE, FREEZE) with many eat-by edits keeps its FREEZE, and a freeze-heavy history (a thaw and a refreeze past the cap) still counts every freeze. A long run of moves can still drop the oldest thaws after the first place event (`pinchMath` puts away 8, then freezes and thaws it five times: the 8 that went in and all five freezes stay, the oldest thaw goes). A cook's container starts with MADE, which records what the cook made (a whole number from 1 to 100, whatever the cook's half servings), and then where it went with its own servings; a container added by hand starts with where it went alone (no MADE: one event, so it spends no slot and doesn't read twice). What went in is the first place event (`getStartServings`). The cap of 10 is set by the rules evaluator's budget of 1,000 expressions a request, not only by the length checks (criterion 13).
- **Cooking mode (the Finish panel).** `usePutAway` (`hooks/usePutAway.ts`) lives in `CookingSession` beside the note hook and `CookPutAway` draws it in the "Marked as cooked" and "Already marked as cooked" panels once the containers listener has landed without error. **What a bulk meal is** (`isBulkMeal`, no new field on `Recipe`): the session's servings are `BULK_MEAL_SERVINGS` (5) or more, or the recipe says it freezes (`care.freezerDays` set; fridge days alone are not a signal), or something of that recipe is already in stock. **The question is asked only when something is left**: the servings cooked, minus what is eaten tonight (the servings planned when it was started from a planned meal, else `CONTAINER_SITTING_SERVINGS`), minus what this cook already put away (the first place event of its saved containers), is above zero; otherwise only the quiet link shows, so a recipe that says it freezes, cooked at exactly the planned servings, no longer asks about a serving that doesn't exist. An answer opens the details in place on the panel (no overlay over the overlay): servings (default `getPutAwayServings(left)`: what is left, whole, from 1 to 100, so "Put another away" never re-offers the whole batch), a line that works the default out (`getPutAwayBasis`: "6 left after the 2 you're eating tonight", "6 left after about 2 for tonight", "Nothing left after … and the 6 already put away"), one quiet line when the total put away from this cook would pass what it made ("That's more than the 8 you made.", never blocking), the place when the link asked for it, how many containers (above) and the eat-by from the recipe's care; the pinned footer swaps to Skip and Save. Each line this screen wrote carries its own quiet "Undo" (`undoSaved`, the same guarded `undoContainerBatch` the toast used, run once: the button gives way to "Undoing…", a second tap finds nothing, and it deletes exactly the ids that save wrote, or none if one has changed since); a refused Undo or a rejected write is one plain red line inside the card, and a line from an earlier visit has no Undo. **Layout:** the panel is one centered column (`max-w-md`, never stretched at 1280): the centered title lines, then each stage in one hairline card (`bg-muted/50`, `border-border`, `rounded-2xl`, no card inside it): the question (centered heading, the fridge and freezer pills, then "Not this time"), the details as settings rows (`ContainerFields layout='rows'`: caption left and control right, the helper line under both, "Where" as a full-width `PillGroup` row, the over-note and any error at the card's foot; the drawer form keeps `stack`), the saved lines, and the note box; "Put some away" / "Put another away" and "Add a note about it" are `ActionRow`s in one `ActionGroup` under them. **Nothing here blocks leaving:** Save disables only its own button while the write is accepted (it isn't awaited from the server), and the header's back, Escape and the back gesture all use `leave()`, which asks "Keep editing / Discard" only when something was changed (`hasDraft`). A held write channel was used to check it: the panel showed the save at once and leaving worked. **A note no longer ends the question:** saving one returns to the panel with "Note saved." and the question still there while it is unanswered, and closes the screen as before only when nothing is left to ask; the note box and the details are never open together. The "Leftovers keep …" line is hidden on a 320px phone while the details are open (the eat-by hint carries it). ## Security Rules Design Criteria

1. **Everything is owner-only.** Every path under `apps/pinch/kitchens/{uid}` (the kitchen document and all thirteen subcollections) allows read and write only when `request.auth.uid == uid`. There is no member-of, admin or role branch.
2. **`isAdmin()` and `isDevUser()` get no access to kitchen data.** They gate only the app *catalog* document `apps/pinch`, as for every other app. A dev fixture signs in as its own `uid` like anyone else.
3. **The app document** `apps/pinch` follows the existing four-read-predicate shape (admin/dev, unrestricted-public, uid-allowed, email-allowed) with admin-only writes, in alphabetical position among the app blocks.
4. **Shape validation covers every field on every type**, with the `T | null` convention (the key must exist; `null` is legal only where the type says so). Enums use `in [...]` against the same strings as `constants.ts`. Bounds: `priceCents` is an int `0 … 100_000_000`; `sizeAmount`, `amount` and `servings` are numbers `> 0` and `≤ 1_000_000`; nutrition values are null or numbers `0 … 100_000`; `gramsPerMl` and `gramsEach` are null or `> 0`; `date` is an int and a multiple of `86_400_000`; strings have length caps (names 120, titles 200, steps 2,000, notes 200, a source's label and a recipe's `sourceId` 80); arrays are capped at `MAX_RECIPE_INGREDIENTS`, `MAX_RECIPE_STEPS`, `MAX_MEAL_ITEMS` and `refIds` (rules can't loop, so a recipe's ingredient lines and steps are the client's to enforce, while a meal's at most 10 items are each checked by index: keys, kind, string ids and `servings > 0`; adding length caps on those ids or checking the elements of `refIds` exceeds the evaluator's budget, so those stay the client's); `url` is null or an `https://` string; `dismissedNudges` has at most 500 keys. A shopping item (`isShoppingItem`) has exactly its seven keys, `ingredientId` of 1 to 80 characters, an `amount` that is a number above zero and at most 1,000,000, a known `unit`, a boolean `isChecked`, integer timestamps, and its document id equal to both `data.id` and `ingredientId + '__' + unit`. An idea (`isIdea`) has exactly the eleven keys of the type, every one required (there is no older shape), `kind` one of `LINK`, `PHOTO`, `TEXT`, a `title` of 1 to 120 characters, a `note` of 1 to 1,000 or null, a `url` of at most 2,000 characters that starts `http://` or `https://` (lower case, so `JavaScript:` and `//x` fail) and has no user name or password before the host, or is null, a `siteName` of 1 to 120 or null, an `imageUrl` of at most 2,000 characters that starts `https://` with no user name or password, or is null, a `photoPath` that is null or exactly `apps/pinch/kitchens/{uid}/ideas/{id}/photo.webp`, a `recipeId` of 1 to 80 characters or null, integer timestamps, and its document id equal to `data.id`; that `kind` fits the other fields (a link has a `url`) is the client's. A cook's `servings` is a number `> 0` and `≤ 1,000`, its `cookedAt` an int at most a day (`86_400_000` ms) after `request.time` (clock skew, so a moment that has not happened can't be recorded), `recipeId` and a set `plannedMealId` are 1 to 80 characters, and a set `plannedMealId` means the id is `plannedMealId + '__' + recipeId`. A recipe's `care` (`isRecipeCare`) is null or a map with exactly its four keys, `fridgeDays` and `freezerDays` null or ints from 1 to 365 and `reheat` and `tip` null or 1 to 300 characters. A note (`isRecipeNote`) has exactly its eight keys, every one required (there is no older shape), `kind` `NOTE` or `TWEAK` (a NOTE's `text` must be a string), `text` null or 1 to 2,000 characters, `recipeId` 1 to 80, `cookId` null or 1 to 80, integer timestamps, its document id equal to `data.id`, and `changes` a list of at most 8 strings of 1 to 200 characters, empty for a NOTE; rules can't loop, so each of the eight is checked by index (`isNoteChangeAt`), like a meal's items.
5. **Immutable fields only:** `id`, `uid`, `createdAt` (a source's and an idea's too), an idea's `kind`, an ingredient record's `catalogId`, a store's `catalogStoreId`, a product's `ingredientId`, a shopping item's `ingredientId` and `unit` (its id is built from them), a note's `recipeId`, `kind`, `cookId`, `changes` and `createdAt` (a tweak's record of what changed is history while its `text` stays editable; the stored `cookId` and `changes` are read with `.get(key, null)` and `.get(key, [])`, so a document made without them can still be edited with the backfill), a cook's `recipeId` and `plannedMealId` (a planned meal's cook has its id built from them; `plannedMealId` is compared with `resource.data.get('plannedMealId', null)`, so an older document lacking the key can still be edited), a container's `recipeId`, `mealId` and `cookId` (what it was made from never changes; the collection is new, so they are compared directly). Everything the UI edits stays editable, including a planned meal's `date`, `mealType` and `items`.

6. **A snapshot is written once and replaced only by a marked re-price.** A planned meal's `snapshot` may be written while the stored value is `null`; after that an update must leave it equal, or be a re-price (`isRepricedFrom`): the new snapshot carries an integer `repricedAt`, keeps the stored `frozenAt`, and the rest of the meal (`date`, `mealType`, `name`, `sourceMealId`, `items`, `refIds`) is unchanged, so its id and anything that points at it stay (a re-price that also changes any of those, or that writes the snapshot back without an instant `repricedAt`, as the freeze does, is refused). Only the owner can update, as for everything under the kitchen, so access for another signed-in user and a signed-out visitor is unchanged. `frozenAt` and `repricedAt` must each be `≤ request.time.toMillis()` plus five minutes of clock skew, so a phone whose clock runs a little fast can still freeze. The shape is checked (the five original keys required, `repricedAt` allowed and, being new, optional on a stored snapshot: `value.get('repricedAt', null)` is null or an int; bounded totals, at most 10 lines); the elements of `leftOut` and `lines` are the client's, since rules can't loop. (Rule check: `resource.data.get('snapshot', null) == null || request.resource.data.snapshot == resource.data.snapshot || isRepricedFrom(resource.data, request.resource.data)`.) The client writes `repricedAt: null` in every snapshot it freezes, so a rules deploy has to reach the backend before a client that freezes is exercised against it.
7. **Cross-field integrity the client could get wrong:** a planned meal's `refIds` equal the distinct `refId`s of its `items` is **not** enforced in rules (it's a derived index the client writes; a wrong value only affects the "in use" check, and the owner is the only writer), and the rules say so. A recipe has at least one ingredient; a meal and a planned meal have at least one item; `defaultProductId` shape is a string or null.
8. **Deletes:** the owner may delete any document in their kitchen, including the kitchen document. The client archives anything in use (Logic §3a), and the rules deliberately don't, since the data is the owner's own and a stale client must not be locked out of cleaning up.
9. **No cross-document rules.** No `get()`/`exists()`: a product doesn't have to point at an existing ingredient or store at the rules level, and a recipe's `sourceId` doesn't have to name an existing source and an idea's `recipeId` doesn't have to name an existing recipe and neither does a cook's (nor its `plannedMealId` an existing planned meal) or a note's `recipeId` (nor its `cookId` an existing cook); the client resolves a dangling reference to "Removed …" (a source to none, an idea's recipe to "not made yet").
10. **Rules tolerate older documents.** A field added later is validated on the incoming document with `request.resource.data.get('field', default)` and compared against the stored value with `resource.data.get('field', default)`; every edit action backfills the new key with its empty value in the same write. A recipe's `sourceId` is the example in use: `isRecipe` lists it in `hasOnly` but not `hasAll`, reads it with `data.get('sourceId', null)` and accepts it missing, `null` or a string of 1 to 80 characters. `care` is the second: listed in `hasOnly`, not in `hasAll`, read with `data.get('care', null)`, and written by every save of the recipe, so an older recipe gains the key. An idea has no older shape: the collection is new, `isIdea` requires all eleven keys, and `createIdea`, `updateIdea` and the recipe link still write back any key a document lacks (so a document made outside the app can be edited through the client). (Reserved, not yet written: `plannedMeals.madeAt`, the `stock` collection.)
11. **No server-only collections; one new index.** (Containers, criterion 13, add the one index below.) The only queries are a single-field range on `plannedMeals.date` and an `array-contains` on `plannedMeals.refIds`, neither of which needs a composite index. The sources, the shopping items, the ideas, the cooks and the notes are listened to as plain collections (the lists are grouped and sorted in the client), so they add no query and no index (a cook is read with one plain collection listener, and a recipe's cooks and notes are found in the loaded store, not by a `where`); `firestore.indexes.json` is unchanged by everything before containers. A source and a shopping item have no file, so only an idea adds to the Storage rules (criterion 12).

12. **Storage rules.** `apps/pinch/kitchens/{uid}/recipes/{recipeId}/{file}` and `…/stores/{storeId}/{file}` are readable and writable only by `request.auth.uid == uid`, only `image/*` content types, at most 5 MB, and the file name is `cover.webp` or `logo.webp`. Storage paths mirror the Firestore document paths. A recipe idea's photo is `apps/pinch/kitchens/{uid}/ideas/{ideaId}/photo.webp`, the cover's rule over again: owner only, `image/*`, under 5 MB, and the file name must be `photo.webp`; a PHOTO idea's `photoPath` is checked against that path in `firestore.rules`.

13. **Containers (`isContainer`).** Exactly its twelve keys (`data.size() == 12` and `hasOnly`, so a missing or extra key is refused), `id` equal to the path, `title` 1 to 120 characters, `recipeId`, `mealId` and `cookId` null or 1 to 80 characters with `recipeId` and `mealId` never both set, `servings` an int 1 to 100, `status` in `['IN_STOCK', 'DONE']` (a create must be `IN_STOCK`), `place` in `['FRIDGE', 'FREEZER']`, `eatBy` an int that is a multiple of 86,400,000 (UTC midnight) from 946,684,800,000 to 4,102,444,800,000 (2000-01-01 to 2100-01-01), integer timestamps, and `history` a list of 1 to 10 events. **The history is the part that is cheap on purpose.** Rules can't loop and the evaluator stops at 1,000 expressions per request (measured on the emulator, which enforces it: a first draft that shaped each event with `hasOnly`/`hasAll`/`in [...]` failed at 5 events), so each event is checked by index (`n <= i || isContainerEvent(history[i])`, with `let n = history.size()`) with the leanest shape that still holds: `size() == 5`, `kind.matches('MADE|FRIDGE|FREEZE|THAW|EAT|TOSS|EDIT')` (a whole-string match), `at is int`, `servings` null or an int 1 to 100, `place` null or `matches('FRIDGE|FREEZER')`, `note` null or a string of at most 80. Ten events with servings and place on every one (the busiest history the app writes) pass, on create and on update, in the rules matrix, so the cap of 10 is set by this budget and not only by the length checks; an event with servings, place and a note all set at once, which no action writes, can fail at ten (it is denied, not allowed). An update keeps `id` in the path and compares `recipeId`, `mealId`, `cookId` and `createdAt` with the stored ones; everything else the actions change (`servings`, `status`, `place`, `eatBy`, `history`, `updatedAt`, and `title`, which no screen edits yet) stays editable, a reopen (`DONE` back to `IN_STOCK`) is allowed, and nothing locks a history to appends only (the owner is the only writer and the client's plans are the only code that writes it). A key added later raises that count in the same PR and is read with `get(key, default)`, so a document written without it still passes. A put-away of several containers is one `writeBatch` of ordinary creates, each checked by this same rule (a batch of 20, each with a two-event history, was accepted by the emulator with these rules), so batches needed no rule change; a cook's MADE event carries a whole number from 1 to 100 (the client rounds and clamps it), and a container added by hand has a one-event history. The one index is `containers (status ASC, updatedAt DESC)`, for the Finished view; the stock listener is a lone equality and `where('recipeId', '==', id)` is one equality, so neither needs one.

Every slice resets on the shared `resetAllState`, so a user switch never shows the previous person's data.

**Two listener tiers**, both under `store/listeners/` and started from `usePinchSync(uid, shownMonth)` called once from `Pinch.tsx` (the only orchestrator); no tab, panel or drawer has an `onSnapshot` of its own:

1. **Signed-in user:** `startSettingsListener`, `startIngredientsListener`, `startFamiliesListener`, `startStoresListener`, `startProductsListener`, `startRecipesListener`, `startSourcesListener`, `startShoppingListener`, `startIdeasListener`, `startCooksListener`, `startNotesListener`, `startContainersListener` (`where('status', '==', 'IN_STOCK')`), `startMealsListener` — `onSnapshot` of the kitchen document and each collection under `kitchens/{uid}`. One effect keyed on `uid`.
2. **Visible window:** `startPlannedMealsListener(uid, windowKey, onChange)` — `onSnapshot` of `plannedMeals` where `date >= windowStart` and `date < windowEnd`, a single-field range covering the window's centre month (`shownMonth`) plus `MONTH_WINDOW_PADDING` (2) months on each side. The effect is keyed on that month (`"2026-10"`). `CalendarScreen` moves the centre only when the view being shown reaches past the window (`isRangeInWindow`), so month paging restarts the listener about every second page and week or day paging seldom does. The slice keeps the previous window's documents until the new snapshot lands, so the grid never blanks while paging.

**Loading gate.** `Pinch.tsx` shows a skeleton until every user-tier slice `isLoaded` and the first window has landed. An empty kitchen (no settings document, no records) is a valid state that renders the empty Calendar; there is no first-run blocking modal. The settings listener is waited for but its failure never shows the error screen (the nudges simply stay open), since it only carries an enhancement, and the sources listener is the same: its first snapshot or error ends the gate (so the filter doesn't pop in after the list), and its error is left out of `selectPinchLoadError`. The shopping listener is left out of the gate as well as of the error (a screen that needs it shows nothing until it lands), and its error is logged and clears its items. The ideas listener is the same: it is left out of the gate and of the error, the Ideas list shows nothing until it lands, and its error is logged and clears its items.

The recipe form's own editors (the title, prep and cook and description drawers of `SummaryEditor`, and on a phone the ingredient, step and nutrition drawers, one level deep) are the drawers allowed over a subview, and so is a `HelpTip`'s small modal, which is how the meal builder's cost warning opens on a phone or any touch screen. A drawer's action that opens a form closes the drawer first; the in-place "swap" screens (planned meal, save-as-meal) are components inside the drawer, not new overlays.

## Security & Privacy Requirements

- **Private by construction.** A person's kitchen is readable and writable only by them. There is no admin read; Security Rules Design Criteria #1–2 are the whole access model. Nothing in the MVP is shared or public.
- **What leaves the app, and where it goes.** (1) Pasted text or a chosen file (a photo, screenshot or PDF) goes to Firebase AI (Gemini) to be read into a recipe draft; the provider sees the content, not the person's identity beyond Firebase's own request metadata, and the screen says an AI reads it and that it isn't saved. A reading can hand back how long a dish keeps and how to reheat it, but only what the source says, and a recipe's own storage notes are never sent anywhere. (2) A recipe link goes to our `fetchPageContent` function, which fetches the page from Google's servers (signed-in only, http/https only, private networks refused) and returns only what Pinch asks for (the page's text, its title and meta description, and any Recipe data) without storing anything; the client then sends that content, not the link, to the AI reader. (3) Store logos are requested by the person's browser from the logo service by domain only (well-known stores only); a person's own stores never leave Storage. (4) A recipe idea's link goes to the same `fetchPageContent`, asking for the page's `meta` only (its title, site name and picture address); nothing is read by an AI model, and what is stored from the answer is those three. When the idea is shown, the person's browser requests its picture address from that website (through `FallbackImage`, with no referrer). Nothing else leaves: there is no copy-out of recipes, plans, ingredient lists, ideas or the shopping list.
- **The logo service's key is a publishable browser token**, domain-restricted at the provider, kept in an env var (`VITE_STORE_LOGO_TOKEN`) and sent only in the logo request's URL. It is not a secret and is not a Functions secret; if the chosen provider needs a secret, the logo route moves behind an `onCall` instead.
- **Stored data minimization.** No location, no payment details, no card data, no receipt images are stored. A product stores store, price, size and an optional link; a recipe stores what the person typed or reviewed (its storage notes are days and two lines of their own words, at most 300 characters each), and a source is only a label they typed and a cook only which recipe, when and how many servings, and a note only the person's own words (up to 2,000 characters), the cook it follows and, for a tweak, up to eight short lines naming what they changed in the recipe (ingredient names, amounts, and the first words of a step).
- **Persistence on the device.** Nothing from the person's own documents is persisted beyond Firestore's own cache. No TanStack query in the MVP carries `meta: { persist: true }`; the catalog is code, not a query. The one thing Pinch writes to the browser itself is each list's View options, as small JSON in `localStorage` under `pinch:{uid}:view:{recipes|ingredients}` (Recipes and Meals share the first): four display choices and no kitchen data; the key carries the uid, so someone else signing in on the same browser doesn't read it, and nothing clears it on sign-out. Cooking mode writes nothing to the browser at all.
- **Links are https-only** and shown with `ExternalLinkText`. An idea's link may be http (a page that has no https version still opens) but only http(s), never `javascript:` or `data:` (the field, `createIdea` and the rules all check), and it opens in a new tab with `rel='noopener noreferrer'`; its picture address must be https, in the client and in the rules, and only `FallbackImage` ever loads it.
- **What a recipe idea stores, field by field.** `title`, `note` (the person's own words, shown only to them), `kind`, `url` (the link they pasted, without sign-in details), `siteName`, `imageUrl` and the page's title when the person kept it, `photoPath` (their own picture in their own Storage folder), `recipeId` and the two times. No location, no author, no page text or description. Nothing about an idea is shared, public or copied out.
- **What a note stores, field by field.** `recipeId`, `kind`, `text` (the person's own words, shown only to them), `changes` (lines the app composed from the recipe the person already owns: an ingredient's name, an amount, a time, a step's first 80 characters), `cookId` and the two times. No location, no author, no photo. A note is never shared, sent to an AI or put in a link, and it isn't in any push or email; deleting a recipe deletes its notes.
- **What a container stores, field by field.** `title` (a recipe's or meal's name, or what the person typed), `recipeId`, `mealId` and `cookId` (ids of their own documents), `servings`, `place`, `status`, `eatBy` (a day), `history` (what happened and when: kinds, instants, portions, a place, and an EDIT's note of two dates) and the timestamps. Nothing about money, location, other people or food safety, and nothing leaves the app: there is no copy, export, share or push of a container.
- **Rules are verified against the emulator, not by the UI hiding a control:** the owner allowed and a second signed-in user denied on every path (kitchen document and all thirteen collections, plus Storage), signed-out denied; and one denied write per integrity rule (snapshot overwrite without the re-price marker, a re-price that also changes the meal, `priceCents` negative, `sizeAmount` 0, an unknown `unit`, a `date` that isn't UTC midnight, a 61st recipe ingredient, an `http://` link).

- **Terms and licences are a gate, not a footnote:** the USDA FoodData Central licence (catalog nutrition) and the logo service's terms and attribution are each confirmed in the PR that ships the thing that uses them, with the logo credit in an About row in Settings. Pinch vendors no other third-party asset since the ingredient icons were removed.

## Performance

- **Scale fixture.** `npm run seed:pinch-scale` (`scripts/seeds/pinchScale.ts`, opt-in) fills Taylor's kitchen: 300 custom ingredients across 40 families, 150 products across 12 stores, 200 recipes of 3–8 ingredients (about seven in eight with one of 12 sources, one of them with a 73-character label, the first holding the most), 60 saved meals, about 1,100 planned meals from 45 days back to 45 days ahead (12 a day), a shopping list of 80 items across 25 families (every fifth one checked off) and 80 recipe ideas (two links in three, a third of the links with a picture address that can't load offline, every seventh title over 100 characters, every ninth with a long note, every tenth already made into a recipe). Every list or per-row feature is opened on it at phone width (390×844) with the dev build throttled 4× (CDP `Emulation.setCPUThrottlingRate`), switching Calendar ↔ Recipes ↔ Kitchen. The numbers from the last pass are in the PR that added it.
- **Never freeze the same meal twice, and freeze in batches.** The load-time freeze of ~540 past meals took the page down twice over: a write per meal raised one snapshot event each, and the list of unfrozen meals shrinking after each event started another freeze of the rest (quadratic writes, most of them refused by the write-once rule). It is now one batch per 100 meals (one event each), each meal is submitted once per visit (`useFreezePastDays`), and a refused batch falls back to a write per meal.
- **One pass, then lookups.** `selectRecipeCosts`, `selectPeriodStats` and `selectSourceCounts` compute in one walk and are memoized; no cell, row or drawer recomputes a recipe's cost. The Calendar's `renderCell` reads `selectPlannedByDay` only.
- **Build once.** The currency `Intl.NumberFormat` and the catalog lookup maps are module-level; the search index is built on first use.
- **Cap what's drawn.** A row is as tall as its lines (no `min-h` under a lone title), so the height estimates follow the lines: `FamilyGroup` adds up 20px for the divider plus 45px per ingredient (44 plus its rule) and 16px for each of the price and nutrition lines that will show, and the recipe list adds a price and a macro line to its cover-sized rows; both are within about 1% of what is measured at 390px. Kitchen mounts a family group near the viewport and the recipe list mounts twenty rows at a time (`LazyMount`); recipe and ingredient pickers show the top matches; the breakdown caps at one month of rows. A calendar cell builds a hover popover only on a computer, and the card's content only while it is open; a phone's cell is just its chips. A week's agenda shows six rows a day then "Show N more", and Products caps its rows at 60 with "Show 60 more". The `LazyMount` height estimates follow the View options (a row with its price, macro or stock line hidden is shorter, and a hidden stock line adds nothing).
- **What is not ours.** A cold first load of the fixture (about 1,900 documents) spends most of its time inside the Firestore SDK ingesting them into its local cache, and so does paging far enough to move the planned-meals window (the listener restarts). Both are measured in the PR and left alone; later visits read the persistent cache.
- **Keep the tap instant.** Each tab renders in a transition and is wrapped in `memo`; the day and recipe drawers read from the store at open, not from their parents.
- **The shopping list at scale.** Groups are built in one pass by a selector, a row is a `memo` component with plain-value props and a group is a `memo` component with a comparison over its rows' ids, names, amounts, units and checked flags, so a tick re-renders the group the row left and its own row and not the other groups, groups after the first three mount near the viewport, and a group draws 100 rows. The count left to buy is read by the tab's badge and by the Shopping screen only, through their own selectors, so Calendar, Recipes and Kitchen never subscribe to a tick (a tab mounts only while it is the active one). The sheets that add a recipe's or a day's lines are measured with the fixture's busiest day (77 lines): one scrolling list, the header and footer fixed. Checked on the fixture at 390×844 with the dev build at 4× CPU: opening the list, a tick, opening the add sheet from a recipe and from a busy day, and the three tab switches; the numbers are in the PR.
- **A tick was slow, and why.** Measured on a kitchen with 300 ingredients, 150 products and an 80-item list (16 ticked) at 390×844 with the dev build at 4× CPU, click event to the next painted frame: a tick took a median of about 595 ms over 24 ticks (435 to 785 ms) while the list was a subview. A count of component renders per tick (a stub React DevTools hook that tallies the fibers that rendered) found 294 to 319: Kitchen re-rendered with its 40 family groups because it read the open count and stays mounted under a subview, and every shopping group re-rendered because the selector builds new group objects on each snapshot, so `memo` on the group never held. The fixes: the count lives in the tab's badge and the list screen only, the list is a tab (so nothing else is mounted while it is open), and a group compares its rows. Now a tick renders 24 to 51 components and takes a median of about 150 ms (87 to 240 ms, 8 ticks). Switching into the Shopping tab takes 360 to 700 ms at 4× and back to Kitchen 660 to 900 ms, which is mounting the 300-ingredient Kitchen and was not changed here.
- **Recipe ideas at scale.** The list draws rows in chunks of twenty that mount near the viewport (`LazyMount` with `defer-offscreen`; the first chunk is eager), a row is as tall as its lines, and each row's picture is a lazy `FallbackImage` (a photo idea adds one cached download-URL query), so 80 ideas mount 20 rows at first and the rest as they are scrolled to (checked on the fixture: 20 at first, all 80 reachable, 8 under "Made into recipes"). Measured on the fixture at 390×844 with the dev build at 4× CPU, loaded unthrottled and throttled only after the page had rendered, median of 5 rounds: the Ideas pill 317 ms until its first rows are drawn, "New idea" 267 ms until the list of ways shows, tapping an idea 204 ms until its drawer shows, back to the Recipes pill 482 ms. The three tab switches, median of 7 rounds each against the same data: Recipes to Kitchen 866 ms (base 922), Kitchen to Calendar 568 ms (base 588), Calendar to Recipes 607 ms (base 661), so they are unchanged within the noise of the throttled run (single rounds reach 1.0 to 1.5 s on the base too). No "Maximum update depth" or render storm in the console.
- **Cooking mode adds nothing to the screens that stay mounted.** It mounts only while open, reads three existing selectors (recipes, ingredients and the per-recipe cook counts) and, through `CookNotes`, the notes selectors and starts no listener (one `store.subscribe` callback runs the memoized recipe selector on a store change, to remember the last recipe); the overlay context value gained no field, so no tab re-renders for it. At 4× CPU on Alex's kitchen with a 40-ingredient recipe, click to the next painted frame took a median of 268 ms to open it, 72 ms for Next, 382 ms for the ingredients view with its 40 rows and 86 ms to come back to the steps (six rounds each), and the three tab switches stayed at 45 to 63 ms. The scale fixture wasn't loaded when this was measured, so the tabs were not checked against it.
- **Notes at scale.** Taylor's fixture holds about 310 notes across 160 of the 200 recipes (one has twelve, for "Show N more"; a fifth are tweaks, one with the eight-line maximum, and some follow one of the recipe's cooks), generated by their own random sequence so the rest of the fixture is unchanged. The summary and grouping are built once per snapshot, the drawer draws five entries and mounts the rest on "Show N more", `RecipeRow` is now `memo`, and a note's own text measures its clamp once per entry. Measured at 4× CPU on that fixture at 390px, click to the next change on screen (medians): the recipe drawer with twelve notes opened in about 260 ms and the note sheet from it in about 180 ms (five rounds); a save of a cooked recipe that records a tweak took about 1.3 s, and about 1.8 s against 1.5 s for the same edit to a recipe with no cook on another pair of recipes (both include `freezeBeforeEdit` and the write's round trip to the emulator; five rounds); the four tab switches took 0.47 to 0.82 s over eight rounds, the largest single one 1.24 s (0.56 to 0.9 s was normal before). Un-throttled, with the whole list unfiltered, opening and closing a drawer or adding a note re-rendered no `RecipeRow` (40 before the `memo`), and a recipe save re-rendered the 20 mounted rows once or twice, as it does without notes, because each recipe snapshot gives every recipe a new object. No render storm or "Maximum update depth" appeared in any run.
- **Care at scale.** Half of Taylor's 200 fixture recipes carry care (a fifth with all four parts, a fifth fridge-only, the rest in the older shape with no key), generated without a random draw so the rest of the fixture is unchanged. Care adds no listener, selector or per-row work: the drawer reads `getRecipeCare` for its one recipe when it renders, a list row never reads it, and cooking mode derives one string from the recipe it already holds. Measured at 390×844 with the dev build at 4× CPU (throttled only after the page had rendered), click to the next change on screen, medians of 7 rounds, two passes alternating the notes branch (before) and this branch (after) on the same data: opening a recipe drawer took 162 and 226 ms before and 160 and 204 ms after for a recipe with all four parts, 208 and 240 before and 206 and 235 after for a fridge-only one, and 182 and 214 before and 227 and 223 after for one in the older shape; the four tab switches took 415 to 969 ms before and 331 to 883 ms after, so everything is level within the noise of a shared machine (single rounds reach 1.0 to 1.6 s on both builds). No "Maximum update depth" or render storm appeared in any run. The fixture's other agents were using the same emulator while this ran, so absolute numbers are loose and only the before and after of one pass are comparable.
- **Prepared meals at scale.** Taylor's fixture holds about 300 containers in stock (a recipe with twelve, to see its row, drawer rows and put-away history with many containers at once; three in ten recipes with none, ten saved meals with one; some fridge ones past their date) and 260 finished ones, from their own random sequence so the rest of the fixture is unchanged. `getStockMaps` is one pass into two `Map`s per snapshot, each row subscribes to its own entry with `shallowEqual` (a container changing re-renders only the rows whose numbers moved), `RecipesScreen`'s chunk height estimate adds a line only for rows that have stock, and the stock subview draws 20 rows per place with "Show N more". Finished containers are never held live: the page is read when the view opens. Measured numbers are in the PR.
- **Cooks at scale.** Taylor's fixture holds about 330 cooks across 200 recipes (one recipe has twelve, for "Show N more"), generated by their own random sequence so the rest of the fixture is unchanged. The summary map is built once per snapshot, a recipe row subscribes to its own count (a number) so marking a cook re-renders one row, a planned meal's rows re-render with any cook change (a meal has a handful of them), and the history sheet mounts only while open and draws eight rows first. The numbers are in the PR.

## Open questions for the roadmap

Each has a default the design already assumes; the owner can change any of them cheaply now and expensively later.

1. **Store logo source: Logo.dev is wired, its terms are still to be confirmed.** Logos load by domain in the person's browser behind `storeLogoUrl` (enabled only when `VITE_STORE_LOGO_TOKEN` is set, with a letter-tile fallback and `no-referrer`). Logo.dev's Community plan needs attribution for commercial projects and says logos remain their owners' trademarks; the plan's terms and the attribution wording were not verified from the sandbox. Gate before launch: read them, then keep or reword the attribution line already in Settings' About row, or leave the token unset. Fallback if it doesn't fit: letter tiles plus person-uploaded images only.
2. **USDA FoodData Central licence** is to be confirmed before the catalog ships (the page could not be read while planning). Defaults to FDC's public-domain/CC0 position; the generated catalog keeps a `source` note per row.
3. **Ingredient icons: removed, approach to be decided.** The vendored Twemoji set, `IngredientIcon` and the icon picker are gone, and `IngredientRecord.iconKey` stays in the type as `@deprecated` (existing documents may carry one; new writes null). Whatever returns (a licensed set, emoji per family, or nothing) will need its own notice if vendored.
4. **The calendar grid's first weekday: decided.** Dreamer UI's `Calendar` has no week-start option (its weekday header and `daysInView` are Sunday-first), so the month grid starts on Sunday, and the week view and `getWeekStartKey` start on Sunday to match it. Building a Monday-first grid by hand would give up the shared `Calendar`; it is cheap to revisit if the owner wants it.
5. **Freezing from first-load data.** A past meal is frozen from the data at the first load after its day, with a freeze-before-edit step. Prices changed while the app wasn't opened for a month or more can leak into meals that were never frozen. Alternative: a daily scheduled Function that freezes by UTC day (adds a Function, a secret-free deploy, and a time-zone approximation).
6. **"Average per meal" excludes meals with no priced item**, and "price per gram of protein" uses only meals where both are known. Both are my choices; the screens say what was left out.
7. **Pinch uses `kitchens/{uid}` private data only.** If "cook with someone" means a live shared kitchen (not a one-time copy of a plan), the shared data needs its own tree; the `kitchens` root makes that a read grant on this document for the first version but not a co-edited kitchen.
8. **`refIds` is a client-written index** the rules don't verify. A wrong value can only make a delete hard-delete something still in use; the single writer is the owner. The defence is the client derive-on-write and the emulator checks.
9. **No test runner exists in the repo.** The quantity bridge, cost and stats math are pure and the most valuable thing to test. Default: `npm run check:pinch-math` (`scripts/checks/pinchMath.ts`, plain `node:assert`) runs the cost worked examples above and the nutrition-basis conversions (per 28 g, per ounce, per serving, an unweighable serving), plus the timezone-pinned browser pass the repo already requires. CI runs it, and `npm run check:pinch-ingest` (the recipe-reading normalizer and ingredient matching), on every pull request that touches `src`, `scripts/checks` or `scripts/seeds`: the `check_pinch` job in `.github/workflows/check-firestore-indexes.yml`, on Node 20, with the math checks under UTC, America/Los_Angeles and Pacific/Auckland. It sits beside `check_indexes` so that job's name is unchanged.
10. **Unit conversions beyond the catalog are Next Steps.** A custom ingredient has no density or per-piece weight in the MVP, so a recipe line in cups of a custom ingredient priced by the pound is left out and named, and its nutrition can be typed per gram or ounce but not per a serving that can't be weighed (a cup, "each"). The `gramsPerMl` and `gramsEach` fields are in the schema already so adding the UI later needs no migration.
11. **Ingredient serving sizes.** An ingredient item in a meal ("1 serving of Doritos") needs a serving size; the catalog supplies one and a custom ingredient defaults to 100 g until the person sets one. Doritos-style items need that field in the ingredient form's extras, where it also lets the label's nutrition be typed per serving.
12. **The AI text cap** (20,000 characters) and the 10 MB upload cap are reasonable limits set here, not provider limits.
13. **Reading a recipe from a link is unverified from the sandbox.** A real page fetch through `fetchPageContent` and the AI step over a page's recipe data had no outside network to run against; the contract (schema.org nodes in `structuredData`, capped `text`, the page's `meta`) is from the code and docs, so check a few real recipe sites before relying on it, including how well the model reads a page that publishes no Recipe data and only has text. The page reader itself was exercised on sample and hostile HTML, and the callable's validation and private-address refusals against the emulator.
14. **Recipe sources: a few things are unverified or accepted.** (a) A recipe read from a link starting on its site's source was checked in the browser with an injected reading and against the host-matching helper, but a real link reading (the page fetch and the AI step) had no outside network to run against. (b) A source's label is unique only as far as the loaded list: two devices adding the same label at once can leave two, which the person can rename or delete; making it strict would need the normalized label as the document id. (c) Deleting a source with more than 400 recipes commits several batches that are not atomic with one another; the source goes with the first, and any recipe left pointing at it reads as having none. (d) The rules and the client ship together: a client that writes `sourceId` against rules that haven't deployed is refused (`hasOnly`), so the rules go out first, as for every field added to a recipe.
15. **The shopping list: a few things are unverified or accepted.** (a) The cost line prices the amount on the list from each ingredient's default product, not whole packages, so it is lower than a till total; the line says "Amounts on your list", its help icon says that stores sell whole packages and the till total is usually more, and what it can't price is named with where to fix it. (b) 200 lines per add and 300 items on the list are limits of the action and the screens, not of the rules (§5b). (c) A change of unit is one transaction, but a unit that the same ingredient already has adds to it when it is open and restarts it when it is ticked, and the sheet says which before saving. (d) The rules and the client ship together: a client that writes a shopping item against rules that haven't deployed is refused, and the list then says it couldn't load; the preview build talks to the production backend, so it can't exercise the list until the rules are deployed (CI deploys them on merge). (e) Adding needs a connection because a transaction does not queue offline; this was reasoned from how Firestore transactions work and not tried on a device with the network off.
16. **Recipe ideas: a few things are unverified or accepted.** (a) A real page lookup had no outside network to run against. The success path was driven in the browser with the callable's answer stubbed (including a picture on an http address, 300-character titles and a response slower than the timeout), the failure path against the Functions emulator (which cannot reach a site), and the contract (`title` from the page's `<title>`, `meta.siteName` from `og:site_name`, `meta.image` from `og:image` or `twitter:image`) from reading `fetchPageContent`; check a few real recipe sites. A page's `<title>` often ends in the site's name ("Lemon bars | Serious Eats"); it is kept as it is and the person can change it. (b) A picture that only has an http address is not kept (the rules and the client want https). (c) Offline, a lookup fails and the idea saves with the host as its title, and the final `setDoc` queues. A Storage upload doesn't queue: it retries up to the SDK's limit and then fails with the "timed out, check your connection" sentence, and any other Storage error (permission, quota, an unknown code) gets its own plain sentence from `getStorageErrorMessage` and never says to check the connection. How long the upload retries offline is reasoned from the SDK, not verified. (d) The 200-idea cap is the action's and the screens', not the rules'. (e) A photo idea's picture is not copied to a recipe made from it, so that recipe has no cover; the idea keeps its photo. (f) Changing an idea's link doesn't look the new page up: the old picture is dropped and the site becomes the host. (g) The rules and the client ship together: a client that writes an idea against rules that haven't deployed is refused and the Ideas list says it couldn't load them; the preview build talks to the production backend, so it can't exercise ideas until the rules are deployed (CI deploys them on merge, and `storage.rules` carries the photo path). (h) The idea's link to its recipe is its own write after the recipe commits, so an idea deleted on another device a moment earlier costs only the link: the recipe saves, the toast is the plain "Recipe added", and the failed write is logged (§5c). The batch queued offline therefore never holds an idea document. If the idea was already gone from the loaded store the link is not even tried. (i) Photo objects can be left behind, and are accepted: when an idea is deleted offline its Storage delete is best effort and may not run, and when replacing a photo the object is overwritten before the document write, so a failed write leaves the new picture under the old document. Both are private to the person, at most 5 MB, one object per idea path, and nothing points at a stray one. (j) `toWebLink` picks the one link out of pasted text that has words around it, but a link with no scheme and spaces among other words ("Lemon bars example.com/x") is joined into one host, because a link wrapped over lines has the same shape; a link that starts with a scheme and wrapped over lines is cut at the first break. (k) One Escape closes a destructive confirm and the edit sheet under it together, as it does for every existing form sheet, and the browser's back gesture leaves Pinch while a drawer or sheet is open, as it does for every existing drawer.
17. **Cooking mode: a few things are unverified or accepted.** (a) Wake Lock was checked against a stub that mimics a browser releasing the lock when the page is hidden; headless Chromium refuses the real request, and iOS Safari's support (and its behaviour in an installed web app) was not tried. (b) Vibration, the beep and the toast were checked by counting calls with stubs and with Playwright's clock; no real device buzzed or beeped, and iOS plays sound from a page only after a tap, which the first timer's start provides. (c) Timers were fast-forwarded with a fake clock and a hidden-then-visible tab; a real phone put to sleep or a real backgrounded tab was not tried, and a locked phone will not ring. (d) The time reader is English only and reads a range as its longer end, an unusual phrasing ("a couple of minutes", "overnight", "until golden") gets no chip, and a person can still use their own timer. (e) The ingredients view shows the recipe's lines as written, so two lines for the same ingredient and unit stay two (the shopping list merges them). (f) Amounts inside a step's text are not scaled. (g) Announcements and focus were checked by reading the live region's text and `document.activeElement` after each action; no screen reader (VoiceOver, TalkBack, NVDA) was used. (h) A backwards or forwards clock step was simulated with Playwright's `page.clock.setSystemTime`, which keeps timers on their own clock like a browser does; a real device clock change was not tried. (i) Pinch and zoom were checked through CDP touch events and `Emulation.setPageScaleFactor` (the computed `touch-action` is `pan-y pinch-zoom` and a swipe is ignored while zoomed), not with a real two-finger pinch on a phone.
18. **Cooking history: a few things are unverified or accepted.** (a) The 500-cook cap is the app's, not the rules' (§5d). (b) A transaction doesn't queue offline, so marking from a planned meal needs a connection; marking from the recipe is an ordinary write and queues. Both were reasoned from how the SDK behaves, not tried with the network off. (c) Deleting a recipe removes its cooks (read from Firestore, not the store) in batches, as best effort; a failed or partial cascade leaves unreachable documents, which readers ignore and nothing cleans up later. (d) `cookedAt` is checked against the server's clock plus a day in the rules and against `useNow()` (a 15-second clock) in the form, so a time within the same quarter-minute can be refused for a moment. (e) A planned day marked cooked on another device keeps its cook if the planned meal is removed; nothing links them after that. (f) The rules and the client ship together: a client that writes a cook against rules that haven't deployed is refused, and the drawer then offers no "I cooked this"; the preview build talks to the production backend, so it can't exercise cooks until the rules are deployed (CI deploys them on merge). (g) One Escape closes a destructive confirm and the history sheet under it together, as it does for every existing form sheet, and the browser's back gesture leaves Pinch while a drawer or sheet is open, as it does for every existing drawer. (h) A reminder toast's "View" only dismisses while a dialog is open (`hasDialogOnTop()` in `useReminderToasts`), so tapping it can't navigate away from a form modal holding typed text; with no dialog it navigates as before. The toast stack's `z-index` is a central rule that reaches every mini-app: toasts now sit above an open drawer or modal instead of behind it, readable and tappable (checked with Waypoint's reminder toasts over its New trip modal: behind the overlay and untappable before, on top and tappable after). The cost is that on a phone a stack of toasts can cover the top of a modal, its close button included, until they time out or are swiped away.
19. **Recipe notes: a few things are unverified or accepted.** (a) There is no way to save an edit without its tweak: fixing a typo in a cooked recipe still adds a note ("Description changed"). An opt-out on the form's "What changed and why?" line would cost one more control and a way to say it; the default keeps the history complete. (b) A tweak's lines are compared with the recipe as this device last had it, because the save is a batch (as specified), not a transaction that re-reads the recipe; two devices editing the same recipe at once can each record lines that don't match the other's. (c) The 300-note cap is the app's, not the rules'; at 300 an edit saves without a tweak and the form says so. (d) A recipe with no cooks records nothing, even when the form was opened from a note; the quote shows, the "What changed and why?" line doesn't, and the note's words are not carried over. (e) One Escape over the delete confirm closes the confirm and the sheet beneath it, as it does over the cooking history's delete (the drawer and the confirm both listen for the key), and the back gesture with a sheet open leaves the page, as it does for every sheet in the app; neither is new, and both were checked, not fixed. (f) The tweak lines are English only and name ingredients as they are called when the recipe is saved, so renaming an ingredient later does not rewrite an old tweak. (g) A device with a far-future clock writes a tweak whose `createdAt` is in the future, and later notes then read "Written before your <that day> tweak". The rules do not bound `createdAt` or `updatedAt`, because bounding the tweak would fail the whole recipe save on a skewed device (cooks do bound `cookedAt`).

20. **Recipe care: a few things are unverified or accepted.** (a) The three readers were exercised with the model's answer stubbed (the request's schema and prompt, a reading with storage lines, one without, one with out-of-range and wrong-typed values, 84 days as "12 weeks", and all three ways); how well the real model reads a storage line, and converts weeks and months, on a real page, photo or PDF had no outside network to run against, so check a few real recipes, including a page that gives a range ("3 to 4 days", read as 3) or only a freezer time. (b) Care is days only, 1 to 365: a freezer measured in years is not representable, and a month is fixed at 30 days (so "3 months" is 90, not 91 or 92). (c) A range reads as its shorter end; this is a product choice for a food-safety figure and the opposite of cooking mode's timers, which take the longer end of a cooking range. (d) Care edits never record a tweak, even on a cooked recipe: storage advice isn't a change to what is cooked or how, and a tweak sets "Last tweaked <today>" and makes every older note read "Written before your <today> tweak" although the dish didn't change. The cost is that a care edit leaves no dated trace. The alternative is to compare care in `getRecipeChanges` (lines such as "Fridge storage 3 days → 5 days") so it is recorded like any other edit; it is a product decision and reversing it means adding that comparison back, with its lines in form order after Steps. (e) Cooking mode's line speaks only of days; reheating and a tip show on the recipe's drawer, not on the last screen. (f) There is no copy-out of recipes in Pinch to carry care, so the "copy" part of the idea has nothing to attach to until one returns. (g) Rules and client ship together, as for every field added to a recipe: the recipe's save writes `care` on every save, so a new client against rules that haven't deployed is refused for every recipe save, not only for ones that use care; the preview build talks to the production backend, so it can't save a recipe until the rules are deployed (CI deploys them on merge), and the rules should go out first. (h) The editor opens as a popover from `sm` and is about 370px tall, taller than the other row editors: near the bottom of a short window the popover runs past the screen edge and the form has to be scrolled to reach Done, as Description's would in the same place, and it stays reachable because the popover scrolls with the form. (i) The reader is told not to estimate, but nothing in the app can tell a stated time from a guessed one; the person reviews every filled value before saving.

21. **Pricing a past day again: a few things are unverified or accepted.** (a) The owner chose a one-tap re-price of a whole day from today's prices over per-product price history; the day's nutrition is read again with its prices (it is one `buildSnapshot`), so a recipe edited since the day passed can move a day's calories along with its cost, and the row can appear on a day that used such a recipe with no price changed. The screen says so: the row's subtitle ("Redoes this day's cost and nutrition from today"), the help text ("prices and recipes … calories can change too") and the confirm, which previews the total before and after, all hold for a day that had no prices too. (b) A day is re-priced as a whole: a meal whose own numbers wouldn't change is still given a fresh snapshot, so a day never reads as part re-priced; a day with more than 400 meals refuses with a sentence, and the row isn't offered for one. A re-price that finds nothing to change (another device did it a moment earlier) writes nothing and toasts "already uses today's prices". (c) A priced line can become unpriced if what it named has been removed since (ingredients and recipes in use are archived rather than removed, so this needs data that was deleted from outside the app), and the confirm already says the old prices are replaced. (d) The prices and recipes come from the loaded store, the meals from the transaction's own reads, so a price changed on another device a moment earlier and not yet synced isn't in the numbers. (e) A transaction needs a connection, so re-pricing offline fails with a sentence and writes nothing; this was reasoned from how transactions work and not tried with the network off. (f) The rules and the client ship together: the client writes `repricedAt: null` in every snapshot it freezes or plans onto a past day, which a deployed backend that predates the key refuses (`hasOnly`), and the freeze then fails without showing anything (`freezeBeforeEdit` swallows it), so a price or recipe edit made in that window leaves past days unfixed and reading live prices until a freeze succeeds after the rules deploy; the preview build talks to the production backend, so it can't freeze or re-price until the rules are deployed (CI deploys them on merge), and the rules go out first.

22. **Prepared containers: a few things are decided here, and a few are unverified or accepted.** (a) The history holds ten events, not more, because the rules evaluator allows 1,000 expressions per request and a list can only be walked by index (criterion 13); past ten the oldest eats go first, then the oldest eat-by edits, then the oldest move after the first place event that is not a freeze, so a container eaten a portion at a time over more than ten sittings loses its earliest EATs while its place events stay (and the count of portions is the document's own `servings`); only a long run of moves (frozen and thawed five times over) can drop the oldest thaws, never the first place event or a freeze. Merging eats of the same day into one event would stretch this and would need the undo to subtract a count; not done. (b) What a bulk meal is (five or more servings, a recipe that says it freezes, or one already in stock) is a rule of mine, stated in one place (`isBulkMeal`); the owner may want a different threshold. (c) The default eat-by days (4 for the fridge, 90 for the freezer, 2 once thawed) are named starting points, not advice, and the copy says "Your call". (d) The Finished view's composite index is declared but the emulator never enforces indexes, so it is unverified until the index is built after deploy; until then the Finished view shows one line saying it couldn't load. (e) OWNER DECISION, thawing portions: real meal prep puts portions in separate boxes and thaws one the night before, but a put-away used to be one container of N servings, so "Thaw in the fridge" moved all N. It is solved by containers × servings each: "How many containers?" (1 to 20) splits the servings evenly (the extra ones go one each to the first) into that many containers in one batch, each its own history, with the same cook and eat-by, so one box thaws alone. A container already put away whole is not split after the fact; whether the owner wants "split this container" is open. (f) OWNER DECISION, planning from stock: food eaten from a container still counts its cost in the plan's tiles, the shopping sheet still pre-ticks a recipe's ingredients (it only says you already have servings), a plan can't be built from stock ("use the lentil soup on Tuesday"), and there is no reminder and no Calendar surface for what to eat soon. These are follow-ups for the owner to decide, not part of this change. (g) The rules were proven on the emulator against the previous rules: the 37 new-behaviour rows are owner-only and every other row, for every existing collection, is identical (432 cases a file); the evaluator's expression limit in production is assumed to be the emulator's, since it is the same engine. (h) Deploy order: the client writes only the new collection, so against rules that predate this PR the listener is denied and the container UI says so in one line while nothing else changes; the preview channel builds against the production rules and CI deploys rules only on merge, so it can't exercise containers until they are deployed. (i) No push or reminder for an approaching eat-by date, no automatic eating and no subtracting from the shopping list: follow-ups. (j) A saved meal's container counts plates and is eaten one at a time from a plan; a recipe's counts its own servings. (k) A typo in a typed container's name can only be fixed by deleting it; `title` is editable in the rules but no screen edits it yet.
