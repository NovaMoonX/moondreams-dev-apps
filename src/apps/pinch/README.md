# Pinch 🧂

## The Short

Pinch is a calm, personal kitchen planner. It comes stocked with a catalog of everyday ingredients and seasonings, keeps a library of your recipes and the meals you put together from them, and puts what you plan to eat on a calendar. Add the products you buy and what you pay for them, and every recipe, meal, and week knows what it costs. Nutrition is there too, whenever you want to see it.

## The Story

Planning meals with an AI chat is easy to start and hard to keep going. Every conversation begins from scratch: it can't see what's in your kitchen, doesn't remember what you cooked last week, and has no idea what any of it costs. The recipes are good, but the context is gone by tomorrow. Pinch keeps that context: your ingredients, the products you buy, your recipes and your plan, with a recipe from any chat, photo or web page easy to bring in.

## How It Works

1. **Start with a stocked kitchen.** A ready-made catalog of everyday ingredients and seasonings is waiting, each with its nutrition.
2. **Add what's missing.** Create your own ingredients, from a variant of an existing one (Chicken → Chicken breast) to a ready-to-eat food like Doritos. A name is all it takes to start.
3. **Add the products you buy.** Attach a store, a price, and a package size to any ingredient, like chicken breast from Walmart, whenever you have them handy. Pick the store from a list of well-known ones with their logos, or add your own.
4. **Build recipes.** Write one by hand, or start from text, a file (a photo, screenshot or PDF) or a link to a recipe page, and the AI fills in the recipe for you to review.
5. **Bring recipes in from anywhere.** Paste a recipe copied from any chat, snap a photo of a cookbook page, or hand it a link.
6. **Put meals together.** A meal is a list of items, each a recipe or an ingredient, each with its own serving count. Save the ones you eat again.
7. **Plan your days.** Put a meal, a single recipe, or a single ingredient on the calendar under breakfast, lunch, dinner, snack, dessert, or drink.
8. **Tweak any day.** Skip the peppers on Tuesday or double the rice on Friday without touching the saved meal.
9. **See your week at a glance.** Switch between a month, a week and a single day. Days show a soft-tinted chip per meal (the meal's title on a wide screen), a Today button brings you back, and a stats row describes the period you're looking at.
10. **Fill in the details as you go.** A missing price, package size, or nutrition value shows up as a quiet line right where it matters ("2 ingredients need a price · Add prices"), so nothing is demanded up front and a tap fills it in.
11. **Turn on nutrition if you want it.** Calories, protein, carbs, and fat each stay hidden until you switch them on.
12. **Lists stay short, and you choose what they show.** Rows start with prices and leave out calories and macros; an eye button on Kitchen and Recipes turns either one on or off, whenever you like.
13. **Remember where a recipe came from.** Tag a recipe with a source, like a favorite site or a stack of cookbooks, then find your recipes by it.
14. **Make a shopping list.** From a recipe, a saved meal, a planned meal or a whole day, pick what you need and it goes on one list, the Shopping tab, grouped by ingredient family. Tick things off at the store.

### Finding Your Way, In Detail

- **Calendar** (first): what you plan to eat, by month, week or day, with the period's stats above it.
- **Recipes** (second): your recipes and saved meals, and the sources your recipes came from.
- **Kitchen** (third): everything you buy. Ingredients, their products and your stores live here. Later it will also hold how much you have on hand and the servings you've prepared.
- **Shopping** (fourth): what you still need to buy, grouped by ingredient family, with a count on the tab while anything is left.

### Filling In As You Go, In Detail

- **Starting takes almost nothing.** A new ingredient needs a name. A new product needs a store and a price. A new recipe needs a title and its ingredients.
- **Everything else is invited, in context.** A price for an ingredient you use, a package size, a nutrition value, a conversion Pinch needs to compare two amounts: each appears as one quiet line exactly where the gap affects a number. A recipe's cost area says "2 ingredients need a price · Add prices", and tapping it opens a short form for just those.
- **One tap fills several.** When a recipe arrives with a dozen unknown prices, the line is still one line, and its form walks through them together.
- **Totals say what they leave out.** A cost with gaps carries a small warning that names what the total leaves out, so a number is never quietly low.
- **Only what you use, only what you've switched on.** Nudges cover ingredients that are in a recipe or on the calendar, and nutrition nudges appear only for the metrics you've turned on.
- **Always easy to wave off.** Every nudge has a small close button; closing one keeps that line away for that ingredient. Nudges live only where the gap is, in the moment you're looking at the number.

### Recipes and Meals, In Detail

- **A recipe is how to make one thing.** Adobo chicken, cilantro lime rice, your house sauce. Recipes live in your library and have no date.
- **A meal is what you eat in a sitting.** It's a list of items with a serving count on each: adobo chicken, cilantro lime rice, black beans, salsa, sour cream, peppers.
- **An item is a recipe or an ingredient.** Anything in your catalog can sit in a meal next to your recipes. Doritos is an ingredient: its nutrition and price live on it once, and follow it into every meal.
- **A meal can be a single item.** A one-pot chicken pan is just a recipe on the calendar by itself.
- **A recipe can say where it came from.** "Serious Eats", "Mom's recipe cards", "NYT Cooking": a source is just a name you type or pick in the recipe form, under the description, and it's optional. The recipe's details say "From Serious Eats", and once any recipe has one, the Recipes tab gets a filter by source (with how many recipes each has) and a Sources list where you can rename a source or delete it. Deleting a source never deletes recipes; they just lose that label. A recipe read from a link starts on its website's name, which you can change.
- **Save a meal to reuse it.** Saved meals sit with your recipes in the Recipes tab.
- **Every day has its own copy of a meal.** Putting a meal on the calendar copies its items onto that day. Changing one day's items leaves the saved meal and every other day as they were, and editing the saved meal later leaves days already planned as they were. "Save as a new meal" turns a tweaked day into a meal of its own.
- **Recipes are shared by everything that uses them.** Edit a recipe and every meal still ahead of today that includes it shows the change.
- **Deleting something in use archives it.** A recipe, custom ingredient, or store that's part of planned days or saved meals disappears from lists and pickers, and the days and totals that used it stay whole.

### Ingredients, Variants, and Products, In Detail

- **An ingredient is anything you buy.** Chicken breast, paprika, a bag of Doritos.
- **Three layers, kept separate.** A *family* groups related ingredients (Chicken). An *ingredient* is the specific thing recipes and meals use (Chicken breast). A *product* is what you actually buy (Chicken breast, Walmart, $9.48 for 3 lb).
- **Recipes only ever name the ingredient.** A recipe says "chicken breast", never "Walmart chicken breast", so the same recipe works wherever you shop and reads correctly if it's ever shared. Products feed your own numbers.
- **Prices live on products.** A product's price and size give an ingredient its cost per unit, and that rolls up into recipe cost, meal cost, and the calendar totals.
- **One level of grouping, on purpose.** Families don't nest, so the catalog can grow to hundreds of ingredients without becoming a tree to maintain.
- **Your own ingredients sit beside the built-in ones.** A custom ingredient can join an existing family (Chicken → Chicken feet) or start its own.

### Stores, In Detail

- **A list of well-known stores is ready.** Pinch includes the grocery stores and warehouse clubs most people shop at, each with its logo, so adding "Walmart" is a tap, not typing.
- **Add your own.** The corner market, a farm stand, an online shop: give it a name and, if you like, a photo or logo. Without an image it gets a tile with its first letter.
- **Pick a store when you add a product.** The picker shows your stores first, then the well-known ones, with a search once the list grows.
- **Stores are for you.** They group your products. They aren't part of anything you share.

### Cost and Nutrition, In Detail

- **Cost is always on.** A recipe shows its total and cost per serving, a meal shows the sum of its items, and the calendar's stats row shows its cost and average cost per meal for the period you're viewing. Tapping it breaks the numbers down by day and by meal.
- **Cost appears when amounts can be compared.** A recipe's amount and a product's package size line up when they measure the same kind of thing (pounds with ounces, cups with tablespoons), or when the catalog knows the conversion (a cup of flour to grams). Anything that can't be compared is called out by name with a nudge to fix it and is left out of the totals, so every number shown is one the app can stand behind.
- **Past days keep their numbers.** Once a day has passed, its cost and nutrition are fixed as they were that day. Updating a price or editing a recipe changes what's ahead, not last month's totals.
- **Nutrition is yours to enter, and yours to show.** Built-in ingredients arrive with calories, protein, carbs, and fat. You can add or adjust them on any ingredient. A recipe's nutrition adds up from its ingredients, and you can type your own numbers over it, handy when a recipe comes with its own from the web.
- **Type nutrition the way the label prints it.** Per 28 g, per ounce or per serving: Pinch converts it to per 100 g and says what it saved. Switching between g, oz and serving before you've typed a number converts the ones already there; once you've typed, it only changes what your numbers are for. A serving needs a size Pinch can weigh, and a blank or impossible number is called out before you save.
- **See it per serving or per 100 g.** An ingredient's drawer and the Kitchen list's rows share one choice, which starts at per 100 g. Flip it in the drawer (the switch is left out when a serving is exactly 100 g or can't be weighed) or in the list's view options.
- **Showing nutrition is opt-in.** In settings, calories, protein, carbs, and fat are four separate switches, all off to start. Turning one on shows it per serving on recipes and meals and as a daily average on the calendar's stats row. With protein on, the stats row also shows your average price per gram of protein.
- **View options are for the rows only.** The eye button turns prices and calories and macros on or off for a list's rows (prices start on, calories and macros start off), and picks per serving or per 100 g for ingredients. Totals, drawers and nudges don't change, and Reset puts the list back. Each list's choice is kept on this device, for you. A row is only as tall as the lines it shows.
- **A recipe's basics open one at a time.** The title, the prep and cook time and the description are one-line summaries; tap one to change it in a drawer on a phone or a small popover on a bigger screen, and press Enter or Done to close it. A recipe written from scratch opens its title for you.
- **Removing something from a recipe can be taken back.** Taking out an ingredient or a step (or an item from a meal's plate) shows "Removed Salt · Undo" with a few seconds counting down; Undo puts it back where it was.
- **Blanks are named.** An ingredient with no nutrition entered is called out with a nudge and left out of the totals while that metric is on.
- **A week runs Sunday to Saturday.** The month grid and the week view both start on Sunday.
- **A meal holds up to 10 items.** Planning more than that is a recipe's job.

### Shopping List, In Detail

- **Add from where you are.** A recipe, a saved meal, a planned meal and a day each have an "Add to shopping list" row in their drawer (not on days that have passed). It opens a sheet with every ingredient ticked, so you untick what you already have. A recipe also asks how many servings you're cooking, and every amount scales to that.
- **One line per ingredient and unit.** Adding chicken thigh twice makes one line with both amounts added. A line that is already on the list starts unticked and says how much is there ("On your list · 2 lb"), since ticking it adds more. Units are never converted: a cup and a tablespoon of the same ingredient are two lines.
- **A day adds up.** An ingredient on its own counts as its serving size times the servings; a recipe counts its ingredient lines scaled by the servings planned.
- **Tick off what you got.** A tick strikes the line through where it is and, a moment later, moves it to "Got it", a collapsed group at the bottom, so the lines below don't jump if you tick the next one right away. Add the same thing again and it starts over, unticked. "Clear" takes everything in "Got it" off the list, after asking, and says how many it cleared. If another device un-ticked one in the meantime, that one stays.
- **Change a line.** Tap a row to change how much and which unit, or take it off. A ticked line that changes unit stays ticked. Moving a line into a unit that is already on the list adds to it, or, if that one is ticked, puts it back on the list at the moved amount, and the sheet says so before you save. "+ Add" at the top finds any ingredient and puts it on at its usual serving.
- **Find a line.** From 8 things on, a search field sits above the list and narrows it by name as you type.
- **An approximate cost.** A line under the title says "Amounts on your list: about $12.40", worked out from each ingredient's default product and the amount on the list. Stores sell whole packages, so the till total is usually higher, and the help icon beside it says so. The icon turns into a warning when something is left out, like an ingredient with no price yet, and names it and where to add the price.
- **A toast takes you there.** After adding, "View" switches to the Shopping tab, unless you have already opened a full-page screen such as a recipe form, which it never replaces.
- **It's just a list.** It doesn't follow the calendar: changing a meal later leaves the list as it was. Nothing on it is shared, copied out or sent anywhere.
- **Limits.** One add takes up to 200 lines, and the list holds about 300 things before it asks you to clear what you've got. Both are limits in the app, not in the database.

### Bringing Recipes In, In Detail

- **Four ways to start:** a new recipe begins with a short list: From text, From a file, From a link, or Write it yourself. On a phone it's a drawer; on a wider screen it's the first page. Picking one opens that way's own screen, which says what the AI will do, and each has "Write it yourself instead"; back from it returns to the list. The form is never the first thing you face, and leaving it with something typed or read in asks before the work is thrown away.
- **From text:** a paste box accepts up to 20,000 characters. The AI reads it and fills in a new recipe for you to review.
- **From a file:** a photo, screenshot, or PDF (up to 10 MB) works the same way.
- **From a link:** Pinch fetches the page, and the AI reads its text, along with any recipe data the page publishes, and fills in the form from that, so a page with partial or no recipe data still reads. The link goes to a shared function that reads the page from Google's servers; nothing is stored.
- **Always reviewed:** the AI fills in the recipe form and tells you what it couldn't read, but nothing is saved until you confirm.
- **Unknown ingredients:** if a recipe mentions something that isn't in the catalog, it's offered as a new custom ingredient during review, so you decide what joins your catalog.

## How It Feels

- **The metaphor is a home cook's counter.** Warm, hands-on, and a little playful, like measuring by the pinch. Paper-cream surfaces and an espresso-ink primary, with peach tints for chips, groups and the selected option, and warm orange as the accent (today, the active tab, the default product's star). Dark mode keeps the same warmth.
- **Personal.** Built around your kitchen, your stores, and your prices.
- **Mobile-first.** Everything works one-handed on a phone, standing at the counter.
- **Details come later.** Pinch asks for a name and little else to get going. The rest waits behind quiet nudges until you're ready.
- **Zero pressure.** Nothing nags you to plan every meal or fill in every price. An empty day is fine, and a missing price just means a number is left out.
- **Numbers when you want them.** Cost is a quiet companion; nutrition stays out of sight until you turn it on.
- **AI as a helper.** Every feature works fully by hand; AI just saves typing.

## What Makes This Different From a Generic Recipe App

- **A stocked starting catalog, including seasonings.** The everyday staples and the spice rack already exist, with nutrition, so a recipe can be built in seconds and ingredients stay consistent from one recipe to the next.
- **Cuts and varieties are first-class.** Chicken breast and chicken thigh are different ingredients with different prices and protein, grouped under one family so they're easy to find and swap.
- **Meals are compositions, not recipes.** A plate is an entree, a side, and a bag of chips. Recipes and plain ingredients sit side by side in one meal, and any day can bend it.
- **What you pay is part of the app.** Products tie real prices at real stores to recipes and meals, so costs are calculated from your own shopping.
- **The calendar reads like a food diary at a glance.** Days show a soft-tinted chip per meal type (the meal's title on a wide screen), with the period's numbers above.

## Under the Hood

- **Frontend:** React 19, TypeScript, Vite, TailwindCSS 4, DreamerUI (including its `Calendar` for the month view)
- **State:** Redux Toolkit, following the repo's standard per-app store pattern; one-off lookups through TanStack Query
- **Backend:** Firebase Auth, Firestore for ingredients, products, recipes, recipe sources, meals, calendar entries, the shopping list and per-user settings (adding to the list merges into existing lines in one transaction, so two devices adding at once never lose an amount); Firebase Storage for recipe and store images
- **AI:** the repo's shared Firebase AI (Gemini) setup to read pasted text, photos, and PDFs (JPG, PNG, WebP, HEIC, and PDF up to 10 MB) and return drafts for review; for links, the shared `fetchPageContent` Cloud Function fetches the page's text and any recipe data first, and the AI model writes the draft from them
- **On the device:** a list's view options are kept in the browser's local storage, per person; nothing else of yours is kept there beyond Firestore's own cache
- **Catalog data:** built-in nutrition values drawn from USDA FoodData Central, which publishes its data for free reuse (licence not yet confirmed from here: to be checked before launch), stored per 100 g alongside common unit conversions; money is stored as whole cents
- **Store logos:** a curated list of well-known stores (names and web domains) whose logos load from a logo service in the person's browser, with a letter tile whenever one isn't available; stores you add keep their own uploaded image in Firebase Storage. The logo service (Logo.dev) and its terms are to be confirmed before launch.
