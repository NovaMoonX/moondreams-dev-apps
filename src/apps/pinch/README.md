# Pinch 🧂

## The Short

Pinch is a calm, personal kitchen planner. It comes stocked with a catalog of everyday ingredients and seasonings, keeps a library of your recipes and the meals you put together from them, and puts what you plan to eat on a calendar. Add what you pay for things where you buy them, and every recipe, meal, and week knows what it costs. Nutrition is there too, whenever you want to see it.

## The Story

Planning meals with an AI chat is easy to start and hard to keep going. Every conversation begins from scratch: it can't see what's in your kitchen, doesn't remember what you cooked last week, and has no idea what any of it costs. The recipes are good, but the context is gone by tomorrow. Pinch keeps that context: your ingredients, what you pay for them, your recipes and your plan, with a recipe from any chat, photo or web page easy to bring in.

## How It Works

1. **Start with a stocked kitchen.** A ready-made catalog of everyday ingredients and seasonings is waiting, each with its nutrition.
2. **Add what's missing.** Create your own ingredients, from a variant of an existing one (Chicken → Chicken breast) to a ready-to-eat food like Doritos. A name is all it takes to start.
3. **Add what you pay.** Attach a store, a price, and a package size to any ingredient, like chicken breast from Walmart, whenever you have them handy, and see what it costs per pound beside each place. Pick the store from a list of well-known ones with their logos, or add your own.
4. **Build recipes.** Write one by hand, or start from text, a file (a photo, screenshot or PDF) or a link to a recipe page, and the AI fills in the recipe for you to review.
5. **Bring recipes in from anywhere.** Paste a recipe copied from any chat, snap a photo of a cookbook page, or hand it a link.
6. **Put meals together.** A meal is a list of items, each a recipe or an ingredient, each with its own serving count. Save the ones you eat again.
7. **Plan your days.** Put a meal, a single recipe, or a single ingredient on the calendar under breakfast, lunch, dinner, snack, dessert, or drink. Your recent recipes and saved meals sit under the search as rows with their cost and what's in the fridge, one tap each; a "When" row at the top holds the day and meal, leaving a half-built plate asks before it drops it, and the toast's "View" shows where it landed.
8. **Tweak any day.** Skip the peppers on Tuesday or double the rice on Friday without touching the saved meal.
9. **See your week at a glance.** On a phone the calendar opens on the week ahead, starting today, with the days already gone this week folded under "Earlier, Oct 11–14"; a month grid is one tap away (and the default on a wide screen). Days show a soft-tinted chip per meal (dinner first, the meal's title on a wide screen), a past meal you cooked shows a round tick, a Today button brings you back, and one line says what the period's meals cost (View options' Show stats turns it into a row of tiles: the total, the average per fully priced meal and the daily nutrition). Until you have a recipe or a saved meal the header is empty and the one button is "Add your first recipe"; Plan and View options arrive with the first.
10. **Fill in the details as you go.** A missing price, package size, or nutrition value shows up as a quiet line right where it matters ("Chicken thigh and 4 more need a price · Add prices"), so nothing is demanded up front and a tap fills it in.
11. **Turn on nutrition if you want it.** Calories, protein, carbs, and fat each stay hidden until you switch them on from the eye (View options).
12. **Lists stay short, and you choose what they show.** Rows start with prices and leave out calories and macros; an eye button on Kitchen and Recipes turns either one on or off, whenever you like.
13. **Remember where a recipe came from.** Tag a recipe with a source, like a favorite site or a stack of cookbooks, then find your recipes by it.
14. **Make a shopping list.** From a recipe, a saved meal or a whole day, pick what you need and it goes on one list, the Shopping tab, in the order you walk a shop. The Shopping tab can also pull the next week of planned meals in one go, each line says which meals it is for, and a meal you're no longer cooking comes off in one tap. Tap a line to tick it off at the store.
15. **Keep recipe ideas.** A link to a page you like, a photo, or a few words about something you'd like to make someday. When you're ready, an idea turns into a recipe with its title, note and site already filled in.
16. **Cook it step by step.** Start cooking from a recipe, or from a meal you planned (a dinner of two or three recipes cooks as one, switching between them without losing your place or your timers): one step at a time in big type, a timer ready to start for any time a step mentions (once started, the timer is the screen while you wait), the ingredients each step names with their amounts, and the screen kept on where your browser allows it.
17. **Keep a record of what you cooked.** One tap on a recipe, or on a meal you planned, notes that you made it, when, and for how many. It's a quiet history you can correct later, and a recipe shows when you last made it.
18. **Keep notes on a recipe, and see when you changed it.** Write down what you'd do differently, after a cook or any time. When you edit a recipe you've cooked, Pinch adds a note with the date and what changed, so you can always tell which version a note was about.
19. **Say how to keep it.** A recipe can say how many days it keeps in the fridge and the freezer, how to reheat it, and a tip such as "keep the sauce separate". It shows on the recipe, and cooking mode's last screen puts them on the put-away buttons ("Fridge · keeps 3 days").
20. **Keep track of your fridge and freezer.** Put what you made ahead in the fridge or freezer as a box: how many servings, when to eat it by, and what happened to it. Put portions in separate boxes and thaw one at a time, mark one eaten in a tap, correct a count that went wrong, and see a meal's own recipes' stock on its row. Kitchen's Fridge & freezer lists what is in each, soonest first, one row per batch, and a recipe or meal says when some is put away. The screen opens on the place with more boxes that want a look. After a holiday, a tinted row above the list says how many boxes are past their date, in both places, and tosses them in one tap (asking first, and naming them, with their place when they are in both), and Select ticks several boxes to eat, toss or move to the other place together, each act with an Undo; a move is offered only when every ticked box is in one place. What you finished is a clock icon in the header.
21. **Sort recipes your own way, and star the ones you make again.** Put a recipe in as many collections as you like ("Bulk", "High-protein", "Weeknight"). The recipe form offers a few from the recipe's own numbers (High-protein from 25 g of protein a serving, Quick at 30 minutes or less, Freezer-friendly when it says it freezes) and adds nothing until you tap. Filter Recipes by a collection, and to file many recipes at once, Select ticks them and "Add to a collection" puts them all in the ones you pick, with an Undo. Star a recipe or a saved meal to keep it at the top of its list (and, for a saved meal, of the plan builder's recents).
22. **Keep track of your pantry.** Say what you brought home as a package: how much, where it lives (pantry, fridge or freezer) and when it expires. Two cartons of milk or three bags of rice are separate packages, each with its own amount and date, so the one to use first is the one that shows. Kitchen's Pantry lists each ingredient with what is on hand, says which are past their date (and tosses them in a tap) or due soon (and finds your recipes that would use them up), and Select acts on several packages at once. Recipes, the shopping list and cooking mode know what you have: a recipe marks the exceptions, an add sheet leaves out what you have enough of, and cooking offers to take out what it used.

### Finding Your Way, In Detail

- **Calendar** (first): what you plan to eat, by month or week (a phone opens on the week ahead), with one line about the period above it.
- **Recipes** (second): your recipes, saved meals and recipe ideas; a search finds a recipe by its name, an ingredient, its source, a collection it is in or its description.
- **Kitchen** (third): your ingredients, your pantry, fridge and freezer, and prices. Ingredients are the list; **Fridge & freezer** (what you've put away, one row per batch, with what to eat first) and **Pantry** (what you have of each ingredient, package by package, with the date on each) are two tiles under the search once they hold something, with what wants a look said in words. **Stores & prices** (each price under the store it's at) is a storefront icon in the header once a store or a price exists, and a gear beside it opens **Settings** (what Pinch reminds you about and how long a new box keeps) once there is a recipe, a box or a package to set up. On the narrowest phones "+ New ingredient" shrinks to its icon so the header stays one line.

- **Shopping** (fourth): what you still need to buy, in shop sections (Produce first), with a count on the tab while anything is left.
- **Back does what you expect.** The phone's back gesture (or Escape) closes the drawer or step on top, one at a time; from any tab it returns to the Calendar, and from the Calendar it leaves Pinch. Each tab keeps its place, search and pill while you hop between them.

### Filling In As You Go, In Detail

- **Starting takes almost nothing.** A new ingredient needs a name. A new price needs a store, an amount and a size. A new recipe needs a title and its ingredients.
- **Everything else is invited, in context.** A price for an ingredient you use, a package size, a nutrition value, a conversion Pinch needs to compare two amounts: each appears as one quiet line exactly where the gap affects a number. A recipe's cost area says "Chicken thigh and 4 more need a price · Add prices", and tapping it opens a short form for just those.
- **One tap fills several.** When a recipe arrives with a dozen unknown prices, the line is still one line, and its form walks through them together.
- **Totals say what they leave out.** A cost with gaps carries a small warning that names what the total leaves out, so a number is never quietly low. A total that leaves something out says so in the figure too ("$5.20 so far"), and a recipe marks each ingredient line that has no price yet.
- **Only what you use, only what you've switched on.** Nudges cover ingredients that are in a recipe or on the calendar, and nutrition nudges appear only for the metrics you've turned on.
- **Always easy to wave off.** A nudge names the ingredient that matters most and has a "Not now" that quiets it until you reload; nothing is hidden for good by a stray tap. Nudges live only where the gap is owned: a recipe, and the period on the Calendar.
- **Prices in a few taps.** "Add prices" steps through the ingredients that weigh most first, says where each is used and how much is planned, keeps the store you picked, and starts each package size in the unit the recipe uses.

### Recipes and Meals, In Detail

- **A recipe is how to make one thing.** Adobo chicken, cilantro lime rice, your house sauce. Recipes live in your library and have no date.
- **A meal is what you eat in a sitting.** It's a list of items with a serving count on each: adobo chicken, cilantro lime rice, black beans, salsa, sour cream, peppers.
- **An item is a recipe or an ingredient.** Anything in your catalog can sit in a meal next to your recipes. Doritos is an ingredient: its nutrition and price live on it once, and follow it into every meal.
- **A meal can be a single item.** A one-pot chicken pan is just a recipe on the calendar by itself.
- **A recipe's drawer is the recipe.** The description, one line of servings, time and cost per serving, then the ingredients and the steps. The rest is a tap away and opens in place: a 🕘 icon beside the name for your notes and how often you cooked it, and ⋯ More beside Modify for what's in the fridge and freezer and the other things you can do (start cooking, mark it cooked, add it to the shopping list, put some away). Back and Escape close one of those at a time.
- **A recipe can say where it came from.** "Serious Eats", "Mom's recipe cards", "NYT Cooking": a source is just a name you type or pick in the recipe form, after the steps, and it's optional. Once you have one, the Recipes tab gets a filter by source (with how many recipes each has), so you can see everything from Serious Eats at once, and a 📖 Sources button beside "New recipe". It opens your sources with how many recipes each has: tap one to open it, and the sheet has a row that shows its recipes, its name to change and a trash to delete it. A source no recipe uses any more says so there and can be deleted in one tap, and it stops being offered in the recipe form. Open a recipe and its source is a pill under the title ("From Serious Eats ›"): tap it to see the recipes from there (when the source holds only that recipe, the pill is just a label, with nothing to open). Deleting a source never deletes recipes; they just lose that label. A recipe read from a link starts on its website's name, which you can change.
- **A collection is a set of recipes you name.** "Bulk", "High-protein", "Weeknight": a recipe can be in as many as you like, and you make them in the recipe form, in its Collections row (a dashed "New collection" first, then your collections as pills, with a search once there are many). For a recipe whose numbers say so, a few are suggested under the pills: High-protein (25 g of protein a serving or more, only when every ingredient's protein is known or you typed it), Quick (prep plus cook of 30 minutes or less, both given) and Freezer-friendly (its storage notes say it freezes); tapping one puts the recipe in it, making the collection if you don't have it yet, and nothing is ever added for you. Once you have one, Recipes' filter gets a Collections question (each collection that holds a recipe with its count, and "No collection") with a pencil that opens your collections to rename or delete them; deleting one takes it off its recipes and never deletes a recipe. A recipe's drawer puts where it is from and its collection on one line: a recipe in one collection shows it by name, and tapping it shows that collection's recipes; a recipe in several shows "In 3 collections", which opens their names in the drawer, each a door to that collection's recipes. The search finds a recipe by a collection's name too. Renaming a collection renames it everywhere.
- **File many recipes at once.** On Recipes a Select icon (from two recipes) ticks recipes, "All shown" ticks whatever the search and filters show, and "Add to a collection" opens the same picker as the form: pick collections or name a new one, then "Add 12 recipes". It writes 200 recipes at a time, adds only what a recipe doesn't have yet and never goes past 20 collections on a recipe (the toast says how many were at the limit), and its Undo takes off only what that act added, and the collection it made if nothing else is in it.
- **Star the recipes and saved meals you make again.** A star on a row, or on the title line of its drawer, keeps it at the top of its list; a starred saved meal also stays at the top of the plan builder's recent picks. With eight or more recipes (or saved meals) the filter has a Favorites pill once one is starred.
- **A recipe can keep its how-to video and other links.** In the recipe form's "Links" row: one how-to video (a YouTube link shows its picture) and other links, eight in all, each labelled Article, Original recipe, Technique or your own words. They sit at the bottom of the recipe, after the cost: a "How to" card for the video and a "More links" list, each opening the other site in a new tab. A recipe with no links shows nothing. Pinch only links out: there is no embedded player and nothing plays on its own.
- **A new recipe can suggest photos.** Open the Photo row of a new recipe and, under the upload buttons, "Photos from the web" shows six pictures found by searching the web with the recipe's title and nothing else; a line there says so. Tap one and it becomes the cover, with the site it came from named under it. Pinch keeps the picture's web address, not a copy, so it can stop showing if that site takes it down (the recipe then shows the pot). An upload and a web photo replace each other, Remove goes back to none, and editing a recipe never searches. A "Turn off" chip in the block, and a switch in Settings, stop it.
- **Ingredients can sit under headings.** "For the chicken" and "For the sauce": a group is a name you type or pick in an ingredient's editor, and one ingredient can be in two groups with its own amount in each (soy sauce in the marinade and in the sauce). The recipe, cooking mode and a reading of a recipe that has its own headings keep them, a step names which soy sauce it means, and the shopping list adds the two amounts into one line. A recipe without groups looks the same as before.
- **Save a meal to reuse it.** Saved meals sit with your recipes in the Recipes tab.
- **Every day has its own copy of a meal.** Putting a meal on the calendar copies its items onto that day. Changing one day's items leaves the saved meal and every other day as they were, and editing the saved meal later leaves days already planned as they were. "Save to Saved meals" in a planned meal's ⋯ More turns a tweaked day into a saved meal of its own.
- **A planned meal shows what's next.** It keeps the name you planned it with ("Dinner · Taco night") and opens on what's on the plate, with each dish's "Mark as cooked" on its own row and the cost after it. Tonight its main button is Start cooking, or Eat from fridge when a box of it is due today or tomorrow; a day ahead, a cooked dinner and a past one just offer Modify. Everything else (cooking ahead, the other box, saving the plate, taking it off the day) is under ⋯ More.
- **Recipes are shared by everything that uses them.** Edit a recipe and every meal still ahead of today that includes it shows the change.
- **Deleting something in use archives it.** A recipe, custom ingredient, or store that's part of planned days or saved meals disappears from lists and pickers, and the days and totals that used it stay whole.
- **Find tonight's recipe by what's in it.** The search looks at names, ingredients (and their other names, like scallion), sources and descriptions. When your search finds nothing among recipes but a saved meal or an idea matches, it says so and takes you there.
- **Deleting says what goes.** Deleting a recipe names what goes with it: its cooking history (how many times) and its notes.

### Ingredients, Variants, and Products, In Detail

- **An ingredient is anything you buy.** Chicken breast, paprika, a bag of Doritos.
- **Three layers, kept separate.** A *family* groups related ingredients (Chicken). An *ingredient* is the specific thing recipes and meals use (Chicken breast). A *product* is what you actually buy (Chicken breast, Walmart, $9.48 for 3 lb); the app calls it a price.
- **Recipes only ever name the ingredient.** A recipe says "chicken breast", never "Walmart chicken breast", so the same recipe works wherever you shop and reads correctly if it's ever shared. Products feed your own numbers.
- **Prices live on products.** A product's price and size give an ingredient its cost per unit, and that rolls up into recipe cost, meal cost, and the calendar totals.
- **One level of grouping, on purpose.** Families don't nest, so the catalog can grow to hundreds of ingredients without becoming a tree to maintain.
- **Your own ingredients sit beside the built-in ones.** A custom ingredient can join an existing family (Chicken → Chicken feet) or start its own.

### Stores, In Detail

- **A list of well-known stores is ready.** Pinch includes the grocery stores and warehouse clubs most people shop at, each with its logo, so adding "Walmart" is a tap, not typing.
- **Add your own.** The corner market, a farm stand, an online shop: give it a name and, if you like, a photo or logo. Without an image it gets a tile with its first letter.
- **Pick a store when you add a price.** The picker shows your stores first, then the well-known ones, with a search once the list grows.
- **Stores are for you.** Stores & prices lists every price under its store, and a store's heading opens its name and logo. They aren't part of anything you share.

### Cost and Nutrition, In Detail

- **Cost is always on.** A recipe shows its total and cost per serving, a meal shows the sum of its items, and the calendar's summary line shows what the period you're viewing costs ("$4.10 so far" while some ingredients have no price). Turn on Show stats in the Calendar's View options and the line gives the total to a row of tiles under it: Total ("so far" while some ingredients have no price, "+" once the gaps are all on days that have passed), Avg per fully priced meal (only the meals with every ingredient priced count, from three of them, so it is never an average of partial costs) and, for the numbers you've switched on, the price per gram of protein (the same meals) and the daily nutrition. Tapping the line or the tiles shows how much is priced and each day's cost (a day opens from there); the averages show there, under the total, while the tiles are off. A day you open has its own total, and its other stats follow Show stats: on, the same tiles for that day sit under its title ("That day" for the nutrition); off, the nutrition you switched on still sits under the total ("That day"), and a "Stats for this day" row opens the cost averages in place, drawn only when the day has them (three fully priced meals).
- **Cost appears when amounts can be compared.** A recipe's amount and a price's package size line up when they measure the same kind of thing (pounds with ounces, cups with tablespoons), or when the catalog knows the conversion (a cup of flour to grams). Anything that can't be compared is called out by name with a nudge to fix it and is left out of the totals, so every number shown is one the app can stand behind. A line with no amount ("salt to taste", "a handful of parsley") is left out of the cost and the nutrition, and Pinch never asks you to price it.
- **Past days keep their numbers, until you say otherwise.** Once a day has passed, its cost and nutrition are fixed. Updating a price or editing a recipe changes what's ahead, not last month's totals. If you added a price afterwards and want that day to count it, "Price this day with today's prices" in the day's drawer redoes that one day. It redoes the day's cost and nutrition from today's prices and recipes, so a recipe you changed since can move it too. It asks first and shows the day's total before and after (the after as "about"), can't be undone, shows only when today's prices would change something, and the day then says when it was priced.
- **Nutrition is yours to enter, and yours to show.** Built-in ingredients arrive with calories, protein, carbs, and fat. You can add or adjust them on any ingredient. A recipe's nutrition adds up from its ingredients, and you can type your own numbers over it, handy when a recipe comes with its own from the web.
- **Type nutrition the way the label prints it.** Per 28 g, per ounce or per serving: Pinch converts it to per 100 g and says what it saved. Switching between g, oz and serving before you've typed a number converts the ones already there; once you've typed, it only changes what your numbers are for. A serving needs a size Pinch can weigh, and a blank or impossible number is called out before you save.
- **See it per serving or per 100 g.** An ingredient's drawer and the Kitchen list's rows share one choice, which starts at per 100 g. Flip it in the drawer (the switch is left out when a serving is exactly 100 g or can't be weighed) or in the list's view options.
- **Showing nutrition is opt-in.** In View options (the eye on the Calendar, Recipes and Kitchen), "Nutrition, everywhere" has calories, protein, carbs, and fat as four separate switches, all off to start. Turning one on shows it per serving on recipes and meals and as a daily average under the calendar's summary line and in its summary. With protein on, the summary also shows your average price per gram of protein.
- **View options are for the rows only.** The eye button turns prices and calories and macros on or off for a list's rows (prices start on, calories and macros start off), and picks per serving or per 100 g for ingredients. Totals, drawers and nudges don't change, and Reset puts the list back. Each list's choice is kept on this device, for you; on the Calendar the eye has Show stats, for the tiles under the summary line (off to start), and Show prices, for the Week's meal rows. A row is only as tall as the lines it shows.
- **A recipe's basics open one at a time.** The title, the prep and cook time and the description are one-line summaries; tap one to change it in a drawer on a phone or a small popover on a bigger screen, and press Enter or Done to close it. A recipe written from scratch opens its title for you.
- **Removing something from a recipe can be taken back.** Taking out an ingredient or a step (or an item from a meal's plate) shows "Removed Salt · Undo" with a few seconds counting down; Undo puts it back where it was.
- **Blanks are named.** An ingredient with no nutrition entered is called out with a nudge and left out of the totals while that metric is on.
- **A week starts today on a phone or tablet.** There the week shows the seven days from today; the month grid, and the week's columns on a wide screen, run Sunday to Saturday.
- **A meal holds up to 10 items.** Planning more than that is a recipe's job.

### Shopping List, In Detail

- **Add from where you are, or from your plan.** The Shopping tab's "+ Add" has "From your plan": the next seven days of planned meals in one sheet, merged per ingredient, with a "Days" row for other days on a calendar (days that have passed are left out). A recipe, a saved meal and a day each have an "Add to shopping list" row in their drawer (a day's says how much of it is already on your list, or "On your list ✓" once every meal of it has put its lines there; not on days that have passed). It opens a sheet that says "Untick what you already have" (or "Tick what you need" when nothing starts ticked), with every ingredient ticked except the ones this same recipe, meal or day already put on your list and the staples most kitchens have (spices, oils, vinegar, salt, water), which wait at the bottom under "Usually at home". A recipe also asks how many servings you're cooking, and every amount scales to that.
- **One line per ingredient and unit.** Adding chicken thigh twice makes one line with both amounts added. Each line remembers what put it on the list. In an add sheet, a line starts unticked only when this same recipe, meal or day already put it there ("Already on your list for Thu dinner"). A line something else put there starts ticked and says how much is there ("On your list · 2 lb"). When a day already on the list shares a line with the rest of the week, the sheet adds only the rest, so the weekly shop neither under-buys nor doubles up. Each line says which meals it is for by name ("2 lb · for Thu dinner and Adobo chicken"; past three, two and "and 5 more"), and its arrow lists every one with how much it wants. A recipe line with no amount, like salt to taste, goes on as just its name, and a line with an amount of the same ingredient takes it in. Units are never converted: a cup and a tablespoon of the same ingredient are two lines. On the list, one ingredient's weights (or volumes) in two units read as one line ("Doritos · 5.49 oz"), and ticking it ticks both.
- **See what the list is for, and stop shopping for a meal.** Once the list holds something a meal, recipe or saved meal put there, "Meals on your list" sits under the search and names the soonest ones. It opens every meal the list is for, soonest first, with how much of it is still to buy ("3 of 8 to buy", "Got everything"). "Remove" takes off what that meal added: what only it needed comes off, a line another meal also needs keeps that meal's share, what you already ticked stays, and what you typed on the list yourself is never touched. A recipe comes off at the servings you added it at, however many times you added it. A note says what happened, counted the way the list shows it, and an Undo puts it back for a few seconds. It needs a connection.
- **A day adds up.** An ingredient on its own counts as its serving size times the servings; a recipe counts its ingredient lines scaled by the servings planned.
- **See that a day is covered.** Once every meal of a day has put its lines on the list, the day's drawer says "On your list ✓", and still opens the sheet to add anything that's missing.
- **Tick off what you got.** Tap a line to tick it: it strikes through where it is and, a moment later, moves to "Got it", so the lines below don't jump if you tick the next one right away. The switch under the header shows To buy, Got it, or All: everything left, then what you got under a "Got it" line. Every tick is a round checkbox. Add the same thing again and it starts over, unticked. "Clear what you got" (in Got it, and on that line in All) takes everything you got off the list at once, with an Undo, and says how many it cleared. If another device un-ticked one in the meantime, that one stays.
- **Change a line.** The arrow on a line changes how much and which unit, or takes it off (with an Undo). A ticked line that changes unit stays ticked. Moving a line into a unit that is already on the list adds to it, or, if that one is ticked, puts it back on the list at the moved amount, and the sheet says so before you save. "+ Add" at the top finds any ingredient (an exact name or alias first, so "milk" finds whole milk, and a family's name finds its members) and puts it on at its usual serving; the sheet stays open for the next one.
- **Amounts you can pick up.** Counts round up to whole ones (3 lemons, not 2.5), and a spice or oil you need less than a tablespoon of shows just its name. The stored amounts don't change.
- **Find a line.** From 8 things on, a search field sits above the list and narrows it by name as you type.
- **An approximate cost.** A line under the search says what it covers, "About $12.40 for 6 of 7 things", worked out from each ingredient's default price and the amount on the list; it only shows once it has a figure (with fewer than half the lines priced there is no line). Stores sell whole packages, so the till total is usually higher, and the help icon beside it says so. The icon turns into a warning when something is left out, like an ingredient with no price yet, and names it.
- **A toast takes you there.** After adding, "View" switches to the Shopping tab, unless you have already opened a full-page screen such as a recipe form, which it never replaces.
- **It's just a list.** It doesn't follow the calendar: changing a meal later leaves the list as it was. Nothing on it is shared, copied out or sent anywhere.
- **Limits.** One add takes up to 200 lines, and the list holds about 300 things before it asks you to clear what you've got. Both are limits in the app, not in the database.

### Recipe Ideas, In Detail

- **A place for "I'd like to make something like this".** Ideas sit beside Recipes and Saved meals as a third pill, once you have one; until then "Keep it as an idea", the last row of New recipe, opens the same sheet. An idea is a link, a photo, or a few words, with a title and an optional note.
- **Three ways to add one.** "New idea" lists them: *From a link* (paste a recipe page), *From a photo* (a picture or screenshot of a dish or recipe) and *Jot it down* (just a few words). Each opens on its own screen, and back returns to the list. A link or text you started as a recipe can end as an idea instead: the "From a link" and "From text" screens have "Save as an idea" under "Fill in the recipe".
- **A link finds its own title and picture.** Pinch fetches the page to read its title, site name and picture address, and shows what it found, with the title ready to change. Nothing else from the page is kept. "Save idea" waits until the lookup has finished (six seconds at most), so the idea is named after its page. If the page can't be read, takes too long or has no title, you're asked for one; left blank, the site's address is used. Saving a link or a few words closes the sheet at once, even with no signal; the idea's row says "Saves when you're back online" until it reaches the server.
- **No duplicates.** Pasting a page that is already on the list ("That one's already in your ideas") offers to open it instead, however the link was spelled (with or without `www.`, a trailing slash or tracking parameters).
- **A note is optional.** An "Add a note" chip on a link or a photo; the jotted-down way shows it from the start.
- **Open an idea to see it whole.** The picture, the title, the note, "Open <site>" for a link (it opens the page in a new tab), and "Modify" and "Turn it into a recipe" in the footer.
- **Turn it into a recipe.** An idea that holds a link opens "From a link" with its address already filled in, so one tap on "Fill in the recipe" reads the page into the form (the idea's own title and note are kept, and its website is "Where it's from"); nothing is sent until you tap it. If the page can't be read, the dashed "Write it yourself" row opens the form with the idea's title, its note as the description and its website as "Where it's from". A photo or a few words open that form directly; a photo stays with the idea and isn't copied to the recipe (the form says so and points to "From a file"). Nothing is saved until you look it over and save; back or leaving the form changes nothing. When the recipe is saved, Pinch says "Added … to your recipes" with Plan it, shows you the new recipe in your list, and the idea moves to "Made into recipes" (its button becomes "Open the recipe"). If the idea can't be updated (say it was deleted on another device a moment earlier), the recipe is still saved.
- **Deleting asks first.** Deleting an idea asks first, and for one that became a recipe says the recipe stays. Deleting the recipe later puts the idea back among the ones still to try, and the delete confirm says so.
- **Limits.** 120 characters for a title, 1,000 for a note, and about 200 ideas before the list asks you to delete one; the 200 is a limit in the app, not in the database.
- **Private.** Ideas are yours alone. A link idea's picture is loaded from the website it came from when you look at it.

### Marking a Recipe Cooked, In Detail

- **A quiet record, not a to-do.** "Mark as cooked" is in a recipe drawer's ⋯ More (it keeps a record you can change later). One tap adds an entry at the recipe's own servings, for now, and a note says "Marked Adobo chicken as cooked" with **Undo**, which takes back exactly that entry. Tap again and you've cooked it twice.
- **From the calendar.** A meal planned for today or a day that has passed has a "Mark as cooked" button on each recipe in it (not for a plain ingredient, and not for a day still ahead). Today counts as now; a day that has passed counts as noon that day, since that's when you remember. The row then shows a round tick and "Cooked", with "Not cooked" beside it, which asks first, naming the recipe, since it takes the entry out of your history. Once every recipe in a meal is cooked, the day's drawer shows a round tick beside it, and the meal stops offering to start cooking or to eat from the fridge. Marking the same meal twice never makes two entries: the second says "Already marked".
- **You see it where you look.** A recipe that has been cooked says "Cooked 4 times, last Oct 12" beside a round tick at the top of its drawer's notes and history, above your notes (the Recipes list itself stays quiet). When a recipe is on today's plan, its drawer's main button is "Start cooking", which opens cooking at the recipe's own servings and, when you finish, marks that dinner.
- **Cooking history.** The "Cooked 4 times, last Oct 12" row in the drawer's notes and history opens the list of each time, newest first ("Oct 12 · 6 servings", with "From your plan for Sun, Oct 11" when it came from the calendar). Tap one to change its day, time or servings, or to delete it, and "+ Add an earlier cook" adds one you forgot (last Sunday's dinner). That row appears once a recipe has been cooked, so for one that hasn't, mark it now and change the date in its history. It shows the first 8 and "Show N more" past that, and a day in the future is refused.
- **Removing a recipe.** Taking a recipe off your lists because a planned day or saved meal still uses it keeps its history. Deleting a recipe outright deletes its history with it.
- **From cooking mode.** Finish on the last step records the cook at the servings you were making and says "Nice work · Marked as cooked", with **Undo** right there until you tap **Done** (it takes exactly that entry back) and a "Servings made" stepper if the number was different. A plate finishes once: every recipe is marked cooked together, each with its own servings, one Undo takes them all back, and each bulk recipe gets its own "Putting any away?". The same screen offers putting some away and "Add a note" (Cancel and the back arrow ask before they throw away what you typed). A quiet "Back to the last step" returns to the step. Started from a planned meal, it marks that meal's own entry (the meal then shows a round tick and "Cooked", so one dinner is one entry); if it was already marked, the screen says "Already in your cooking history" and saves the servings you made on it. Leaving before Finish (the back arrow, Escape, the back gesture) never adds an entry. If a timer is still going, Done asks "Stop timers and leave?" first, and if your history can't load, Finish just leaves.
- **Limits.** Up to 1,000 servings per entry, and about 500 entries for one recipe before it asks you to delete an older one; the 500 is a limit in the app, not in the database.
- **Private, and prices ignore it.** The history is yours alone. Prices, totals and your shopping list ignore it. Notes can follow a cook, and a change to a recipe is only recorded as a tweak once the recipe has been cooked.

### Recipe Notes, In Detail

- **A notes list on every recipe.** Behind the drawer's 🕘 icon (notes and history) is what you wrote and every change you made, newest first, each with its day (a change you made says "Changed on Oct 6"). Tap **Edit** on one to change its words or delete it. With no notes yet, the section is one row: "Add a note" ("Something to remember next time").
- **Notes after a cook.** In the note sheet, a recipe you've cooked asks "Is this about a time you cooked it?" with your last few cooks as answers ("Oct 12", "Today") and "No, just a note"; a cook from today is picked to start with. A note that follows a cook says "After cooking on Oct 12". The cooking history has an "Add a note" under each time that has none (it doesn't ask which cook again), and cooking mode's last screen offers "Add a note". If you later delete that cook, the note stays as a plain note.
- **Changes are recorded with the day they happened.** When you save changes to a recipe you've cooked, Pinch adds a "Tweak" note to its list in the same save, with a short line for each change ("Garlic 4 → 6 each", "Added Lime juice, 2 tbsp", "Removed Salt", "Step 2 now reads: …", "Cook time 35 → 45 min", "Serves 4 → 6") and, if you typed one, why. The form has an optional "What changed and why?" line for that. The drawer's status line adds "· changed Oct 20" when that change is newer than your last cook.
- **Your latest note is in cooking mode, on the first step.** Below the step, a tinted card says "📝 Last time · Oct 12" with your note (starting at its "Next time…" sentence when it has one) and adds "You've changed the recipe since." when you edited the recipe after that note. "Got it" puts it away for this cook, and "Apply to the recipe" opens the recipe's form with the note quoted. With more than one note, tapping the top of the card opens your notes right there, newest first; nothing opens over the cooking screen. Later steps don't carry it: your notes are at the foot of the Ingredients view. It isn't there while your notes load, if they can't, or when the recipe has none.
- **Which version a note was about.** A note you wrote before the recipe's latest tweak says "Written before your Oct 20 tweak", so an old "needs more garlic" doesn't read as if it were about today's recipe.
- **Only a recipe you've cooked is tracked.** A recipe you haven't cooked yet, a brand new recipe, and a save that changes nothing you could see (a new cover picture, or only its storage notes) add no tweak. Changing the order of the ingredients or steps counts ("Ingredients reordered").
- **Apply a note to the recipe.** Each note in the drawer's Notes (and in cooking mode's open notes) has "Apply to the recipe". It opens the recipe with your note quoted at the top, and saving records a new tweak with your note's words as the reason. The note itself stays as you wrote it.
- **The record of a change can't be rewritten.** You can edit the words of any note or the reason on a tweak, or delete one (it asks first), but the list of changes on a tweak is history and stays as it was saved.
- **Removing a recipe.** Taking a recipe off your lists because a planned day or saved meal still uses it keeps its notes. Deleting a recipe outright deletes its notes with it.
- **Limits.** A note holds up to 2,000 characters, a tweak lists up to eight changes (the last may say "and 3 more"), and about 300 notes for one recipe before it asks you to delete an older one; the 300 is a limit in the app, not in the database.
- **Private.** Your notes are yours alone and are never shared or sent anywhere. A tweak's lines can carry your ingredient names and the first words of a step you changed.

### Keeping Leftovers, In Detail

- **One row in the recipe form.** "Keeping it" is a row of the card that ends the form (after the nutrition), with Where it's from, Collections and Links, each with its own icon, and What changed and why on a recipe you have cooked. It reads "Add how to keep it" while it is empty and "Fridge 3 days, freezer 3 months, reheating, tip" once it isn't. Tap it to type days for the fridge and the freezer, a line on how to reheat it and a tip. Every part is optional.
- **Days, said the way you'd say them.** You type whole days, 1 to 365. A month is 30 days, so 90 shows as "3 months", 14 as "2 weeks" and 3 as "3 days".
- **On the recipe.** The drawer gets a "Keeping it" section under the steps: "Keeps 3 days in the fridge", "Keeps 3 months in the freezer", "Reheat: …" and your tip. It only shows when there is something to say, and sits above the notes.
- **On cooking mode's last screen.** The put-away buttons say how long it keeps ("🥛 Fridge · keeps 3 days", "❄️ Freezer · keeps 3 months").
- **Read from the source.** A text, photo or link reading fills the row in when the source itself says how long it keeps, how to reheat it or how to store it ("keeps 4 days in the fridge, freezes up to 3 months"). A range uses the shorter end, weeks and months become days, a fraction of a day rounds down, and the reader is told never to guess a time the source doesn't give. Like everything a reading finds, it is for you to review, and nothing is saved until you save the recipe.
- **Changing it isn't a tweak.** Editing a recipe's storage notes never adds a tweak note, even for a recipe you've cooked: how long it keeps isn't a change to what you cook or how, so the "changed" date and the "Written before your … tweak" labels on older notes stay as they were.
- **Older recipes keep working.** A recipe written before this simply shows no storage notes. Saving it from the form writes it as having none (`care: null`), and nothing else about it changes.
- **Private, and short.** Your storage notes are yours alone, like the rest of the recipe. Days go up to 365; the reheating line and the tip hold up to 300 characters each.

### Your Pantry, In Detail

- **One package, one record.** Each bag, carton, jar or box you add is its own package: which ingredient, how much and in what unit, where it is (Pantry, Fridge or Freezer) and when it expires. Two cartons of whole milk with different dates are two packages under one "Whole milk" row, each with its own amount and its own date.
- **Adding asks one question.** The sheet starts from the ingredient's usual pack when it has one (its default product's size), else the unit your recipes use, and puts it where it usually lives (meat, dairy and produce in the fridge, frozen food in the freezer, the rest in the pantry). "Use by" is asked every time and starts with nothing chosen: 3 days, 1 week, 1 month, "Pick a date" or "No date", so a date is a decision and the button says what is missing until it is made. "Add another" saves this package and keeps the sheet open for the next of the same thing, with the date cleared, and says what it just added with an Undo.
- **Three places to start.** Kitchen's Pantry header has Add, an ingredient's drawer has "Add a package" under "In your kitchen" (which starts from your last package of it), and the Shopping tab's "Add to pantry" steps through what you ticked (below). Pantry shows in Kitchen with your first package, not before; an ingredient a recipe uses offers "Add a package" on its drawer before that, so the first package doesn't need a shopping trip.
- **A date says what to do.** Past its date reads "Past its date" and offers Toss. Inside your window, which is the days ahead you chose in Settings (one to start), it reads "Use by tomorrow" or "Use by Fri" in the same pill the fridge uses, and offers "Use it up". Further out it is a quiet "Use by Jan 1, 2027", and a package with no date never flags. A date is a calendar day and reads the same wherever you are.
- **Totals add only what measures alike.** "3.2 lb in all" for two chicken packages (with one past its date it says "2.2 lb in date", since the row flags the other); a mix of weight and volume shows each ("2 lb and 1 cup"), never one number made by converting between them. Pinch compares a recipe's cup of rice with a bag in pounds only through that ingredient's own grams per cup or per each, and says "about" when it does. A recipe amount like "to taste" has no number and is never taken out of anything.
- **The row for each ingredient.** Rows list soonest date first, with no date last. A row says how many packages and the total, and carries one flag only when something there needs a decision ("1 past its date", "Use by tomorrow"). From eight ingredients a search field appears, and a switch for Pantry, Fridge and Freezer shows only when you use more than one place. Up to four ingredients a short intro explains packages; from eight rows on, the ones that need a look come first under their own divider, and the rest follow under "Everything else".
- **A package's own sheet.** Tap an ingredient to see its packages, then one to: say how much you used (type it, or Half or All of it), toss it, move it to the fridge or freezer (it keeps its date unless you change it), change its date, or read its history ("Added to the fridge", "Used 1 cup", "Moved to the freezer"). Each act has an Undo that puts back only what is still as the act left it. A package keeps its last ten events; when there are more, used and edited entries go first so how it came in and where it ended stay readable.
- **Many at once.** After a holiday, a tinted row above the list says how many packages are past their date and tosses them in a tap, asking first and naming them. Select ticks several packages, an ingredient's checkbox ticks all of its shown ones, "All past their date" ticks those, and Toss, Move or Used it up act on them together, with an Undo that takes back only what is still as the act left it. Each package is re-read just before it changes, so one a second device already changed is skipped and counted.
- **Use it up.** On a package that is due soon, "Use it up" lists your own recipes that use that ingredient, those that also use something else due soon first, and what else they would use up. It opens the recipe; nothing is written, planned or put on a list for you.
- **Recipes know what you have.** In a recipe's ingredient list a line only says something when it is the exception: "Past its date" always, otherwise whichever of "You have it" and "Not in your pantry" there are fewer of, with the share said once above the list ("1 of 9 in your pantry"). Salt, oil, vinegar and water, the staples most kitchens have, are never marked "Not in your pantry" and are left out of the share. "Add to shopping list" starts a line unticked when you already have enough, with "You have it in your pantry. Tick to add it anyway", and never hides a line you might still want.
- **From the shopping list.** Under "Got it", "Add to pantry" steps through the lines you ticked one at a time ("1 of 2"): how much, where and until when, from the ingredient's usual pack when it has one; with no pack it asks "How much did you buy?" and Save waits for an answer, since what the list says is what you meant to buy, not what came home (it stays as a hint, "On your list: ⅔ cup"). "Skip" or "Save and next" moves on, nothing is required, and the line stays as it was. Once a line has gone into your pantry on this device it is not offered again.
- **When you finish cooking.** Cooking mode's last screen asks "Take it out of your pantry?" with a tick for each ingredient it can compare with what you have. "Take out" (which works offline like the cook itself) draws each from its soonest-dated package, leaves any past its date alone, says how much it really took ("about 0.41 lb") and holds an Undo until you tap Done. If a package can't cover it, it takes what there is and says so.
- **A morning note.** Settings has "Ingredients about to expire" (off to start), which adds the pantry to the morning note you may already have for the fridge: "Check your kitchen", naming the ingredients and how soon, and opening Pantry. It follows the same days ahead and hour as the fridge note and sends nothing on a day with nothing to say.
- **Works offline, mostly.** Adding a package works with no signal and syncs later. Using, moving, editing and taking out need a connection, because each re-reads the package first, and say so instead of guessing.
- **Older kitchens keep working.** A kitchen with no packages shows no Pantry, no markers, no nudges and no new line anywhere; nothing is created until you add a first package.
- **Private, and short.** Your packages are yours alone, like the rest of your kitchen. A package holds up to a million of its unit, up to 500 packages are kept, and both are limits in the app, not in the database.

### Settings and Reminders, In Detail

- **One place, one door.** The gear icon in Kitchen's header opens Settings. It starts with a short card, then three groups; every switch and pill saves as you touch it, so there is no Save button.
- **Reminders are on a device first.** "Reminders on this device" asks your browser for permission only when you tap its switch, and says why beside it. If your browser has blocked the site, it says where to allow it; on an iPhone or iPad it says to add the site to your Home Screen. Turning it off stops every Moondreams reminder on that device, and stays off when you reload. The kinds under it wait, dimmed, until the device is on.
- **A morning note about food.** "Food to eat soon or past its date" sends one note a day, at the hour you pick (9:00 am to start, in your time zone), naming the dishes in your fridge and freezer that are due within one, two or three days or went past their date yesterday: "Adobo chicken is due tomorrow", "Chicken stock and Chili are past their date". It sends nothing on a day with nothing to say, never more than one, and the dish names show on your lock screen.
- **Ingredients in the same note.** "Ingredients about to expire" is its own switch under it, off to start, and adds the pantry's packages to that note instead of sending a second one: one line, "Check your kitchen", names up to two ingredients and counts the rest. It uses the same days ahead and hour.
- **A push when a timer ends.** "Timers when the screen is off" sends a push a few seconds after a cooking timer ends, for when the screen is off or you are in another app. It is cancelled if you pause, reset or leave the timer, or if the timer ends while Pinch is open in front of you (Pinch rings by itself then). When it ends while the screen is off, you get the push and not a second notice inside Pinch. Pick a cook up again after a reload and the same push stays with its timer; "Not now" cancels it. It can't reach a phone that is offline. You don't have to find it in Settings first: under a running timer a quiet "Buzz me if the screen is off" row has a Turn on button that opens a small sheet, asks your browser for permission on that tap and switches this reminder on; blocked or unsupported says so there, and once it is on the row is gone.
- **How long new boxes keep.** "We start cooked food at 3 days in the fridge" is the app's number, and you can change it, for the fridge (2, 3, 4, 5 days or a week, or any number from 1 to 14) and the freezer (2 weeks to a year, or any number of weeks). Every new box starts there when you put something away, from the form, from cooking mode or when a box moves between the fridge and the freezer; a recipe's own days still come first, and boxes you've already put away keep their dates. Above 4 fridge days a quiet line says most food-safety guidance gives three to four days for cooked leftovers.
- **Photo suggestions.** "Suggest photos for new recipes" is on to start: a new recipe's Photo drawer searches the web with its title. Turn it off here (or from the block itself, with an Undo) and the title is never sent.
- **Nothing is stored until you change something.** Without a change you have the defaults and no settings document; your choices are yours alone, like the rest of your kitchen.

### Cooking Mode, In Detail

- **Start from a recipe or a planned meal.** "Start cooking" is a button in a recipe's drawer (when it has steps) and in a planned meal's drawer (the main button tonight, and under ⋯ More on a day ahead or when a box due to be eaten is the main button; not on a day that has passed, nor once that recipe is cooked). A meal with several recipes opens them all at once, as one slim line of dishes under the header (the ones you're not on say which step they're at); each keeps its own step, ticks and servings, and the last step of one moves on to the next with "Next: Cilantro lime rice". Cooking opens at the recipe's own servings (or the plan's, when the plan asks for more), and says how many you planned for tonight.
- **One step at a time.** The step in big type, "Step 2 of 6" with a thin progress bar, and big Back and Next buttons at the bottom; Next becomes Finish on the last step, which records that you cooked it (with Undo). Swipe left or right, or use the arrow keys. The step has room around it, about 32px above and below on a phone, and a long step scrolls while the buttons stay where they are.
- **The amounts are one tap away, under the step.** A tray above Back and Next says "For this step · 4 items" with the first three ("Soy sauce ¼ cup, Garlic 4, Black pepper 1 tsp, and 1 more"); tapping it opens every line in place, so the step keeps the room and nothing is left out. The "Ingredients" button beside the step count opens the full list, and Back to the steps (or the back arrow) returns. On a wide screen the ingredient list sits beside the steps. On a phone held sideways the dishes and the step count share one line, the step sits on the left and the tray and your note on the right.
- **Ingredients for the servings you're making.** The Ingredients view starts at the recipe's own servings and moves a whole serving a tap (a half only below one). Change them and every amount scales, the same way the shopping list scales it, then reads the way you measure: ⅓ cup, 5 tbsp instead of 0.31 cup, "about 8" instead of 7.5. Tick what you've got out; Reset clears the ticks. A line with no amount shows its words ("to taste") and stays as it is. Amounts written inside the steps stay as the recipe wrote them, and a scaled step that has one says so.
- **Timers for the times in a step.** A step that says "25 minutes", "1 hr 30 min" or "10-15 min" shows a timer card right under it, reading "25:00" with a Start button (a range uses its longer end). Nothing starts until you tap Start.
- **While you wait, the timer is the screen.** Once a timer starts you have done the step and are waiting, so the step makes way for the timer: the time left in big digits you can read from across the kitchen, a progress bar, what it is for ("Marinate the chicken · 30 min"), your other timers under it, and "Up next" with the first sentence of the next step so you can get ready. Pause is the one big button (and a running timer's only control, so a slip can't throw it away); once paused it reads Resume, with Reset under the timer. "Show this step" opens the step again under the timer if you need to re-read it. On a plate, a quiet "Meanwhile" line under "Up next" takes you to the other dish while this one waits. Next (or back, or a swipe) leaves the timer running in a slim row of pills above the buttons, each just its time and the step's first words (its length first when two timers share a step); tap one to bring it back, and if you are further along its footer offers "Back to step 3". Up to four timers run (or wait, paused) at once, shared by the whole plate when you cook several recipes (where each timer also says which recipe it's for). When one ends it comes to the front by itself with "Time's up", a buzz and a short beep where your device allows them (a note on screen only if the screen was covered or hidden), says how long ago it rang ("Rang 2 min ago") and buzzes again about once a minute, up to five more times, until you dismiss it; Dismiss takes you back to where you were. A second timer that ends meanwhile waits behind the first, so you never dismiss one you haven't seen. A finished timer waits for Dismiss and doesn't count toward the four, and its step then reads "Done · 30 min" with a quiet "Start again". A time written with a comma ("1,5 hours", "2,500 seconds") gets no timer, because it could mean two things.
- **Timers keep time while the screen is off.** Each one runs to a clock time, so after the screen turned off, or you went to another tab or app, it is exact when you come back; one that finished while you were away comes to the front and says how long ago ("Rang 4 min ago"). A browser can't ring in the background, so leaving cooking mode ends them. The screen says so under a running timer ("Keep this screen open: timers stop if you leave it."), and if a timer is still going (running or paused) when you leave, a "Stop timers and leave?" question asks first, with Stay as the safe answer. The back arrow, Escape, the back gesture and the last screen's Done all ask the same way. With no timer going, or only finished ones, they leave at once. If the page reloads or your phone throws the tab away, Pinch offers to pick the cook up again (below), with the timers still counting from the clock.
- **Pick up where you left off.** While you cook, Pinch keeps a small copy on this device: which recipes, how many servings, the dish and step you're on, and each timer (when it ends, or how much was left if you paused it). If the page reloads or the browser throws the tab away while the chicken marinates, the first screen you land on (any tab) has a tinted row: "Pick up Adobo chicken, step 3?" with "Timer 12:30 left" (or "paused with 12:30 left", or "A timer rang 8 min ago") under it, a "Keep cooking" button and a hairline "Not now". Nothing opens by itself. "Keep cooking" opens cooking mode on the same dish and step, with the timers still running to their clock times (one that ran out while you were away comes to the front, "Rang 8 min ago"). The copy is cleared when you finish, when you leave on purpose (including the "Stop timers and leave?" question), when you tap "Not now" (which also cancels the push of a timer still running) and when you sign out; it expires after 12 hours; a recipe you deleted in the meantime is dropped; it isn't offered to another person signing in on the same browser, or in a second tab while the first is still cooking. A browser that won't store it just cooks without the row.
- **The screen stays on where it can.** Pinch asks your browser to keep it awake. If the browser can't or won't (an older one, or a page that isn't secure), the timer still keeps exact time and rings when you come back; the "Buzz me if the screen is off" row under a running timer is how to have it tell you with the screen off, and a recipe with no times never hears about it
- **Switching views keeps your place.** Going to the ingredient list and back to the steps keeps the step you're on, the servings you chose and what you've ticked (and the same for each recipe of a plate).
- **A recipe that changes while you cook doesn't end it.** If the recipe is edited, archived or deleted on another device, cooking carries on (with what it last had, if it's gone), keeps your timers and the screen awake, and a quiet line says "This recipe was changed or removed elsewhere."
- **Nothing is saved unless you say so.** Not the step you're on, the ticks or the timers; they end when you leave. The only things cooking mode adds are the cooking-history entry when you tap Finish (Undo takes it back), what you put away, and a note, when you write one.
- **Hands-free, if you want it.** Where the browser can listen (Chrome, Safari, Edge and others that offer it, on a secure page; not Firefox), a small mic sits at the end of the title row. The first tap says in two lines what you can say and that your browser's speech service hears it (Pinch keeps nothing), asks **how it should listen**, and keeps the rest under How it works (Google in Chrome, Apple in Safari, Microsoft in Edge; people nearby too), with Turn on and Not now; after that, one tap starts it, every time you open cooking mode, and it never starts by itself. **By default it listens in short, deliberate stretches:** a tap opens a ten-second command window that closes by itself (each command you give keeps it open another ten seconds), and a tap on the lit mic ends it early. The status chip above the buttons (it names the state: Listening, Waiting, or the time left) opens a sheet where you can change that: **Tap to talk** (the default), **Say “Hey Pinch”** (the mic stays on and waits; the phrase alone, or with a command in the same breath ("Hey Pinch, next step"), opens the same window; a recipe's "a pinch of salt" never wakes it; it turns itself off after ten quiet minutes and says so; the minutes don't count while a timer runs or for two minutes after one rings, and it never waits more than an hour) and **Keep listening for 15 minutes** (no phrase needed; a countdown on the chip and a ring round the mic, and it ends at that time whatever you say). Tap to talk and Hey Pinch are remembered per person on the device; the fifteen minutes never are. The first-use sheet and the rows say plainly that waiting for "Hey Pinch" keeps the mic on, so your browser's speech service hears the room until you turn it off, and that Pinch only acts after the phrase. While a window is open the mic is lit and filled with a soft pulse and the chip's bar empties over its ten seconds; while it waits the mic is tinted with a dot. A line beside the chip suggests what to say, then shows what it heard ("Heard “next”") or the answer ("Garlic: 6"), which is also read aloud; in a window, something that is not a command is quoted back ("Not a command: …") and does nothing. Say "next", "back", "repeat" (it reads the step aloud), "ingredients" or "steps", "start the timer" (or "start the ten minute timer" when a step has two; like Start, it makes that timer the screen, and "next step" moves on while it keeps running, as the footer's Next does), "pause" and "resume" (the timer on screen, else the one that ends soonest; not while a Time's up shows), "dismiss" for a timer that rang, "stop the timer" (it does what the footer offers: a ringing timer is dismissed, one at a time and the one that rang first, a running one is paused, since Pause is its only control, and a paused one is reset), "how much time is left" (the timer on screen, then the next one to end), "how much garlic" or "how many cups of rice", "switch to rice" or "next recipe" on a plate, and "stop listening". Only the commands it lists count, so talk in the kitchen ("go on", "again", "cook the chicken") doesn't move the screen, and one that can't apply says so ("No timer on this step"). On the ingredient list "back" goes back to the steps, like its button. "Next" on the last step, "finish" and "I'm done" never finish: they say "Last step: tap Finish yourself." Voice never marks anything cooked, puts anything away or saves a note. The sheet has two panes: how it listens (the three ways as rows, and **Read steps aloud**, off to start, which reads each step as it appears while the mic is on) and what to say (the five commands of a step, the rest under More commands). Pinch can't hear you while it reads, so the line says "Reading aloud… tap Next to skip". It stops when you leave, when the Finish panel opens, when its time runs out (a window, ten quiet minutes of waiting, the fifteen minutes) and when you switch away from the page (and picks up again when you come back, if it was waiting or kept open), and a refused microphone is said once, with what to do behind a tap on the mic. English commands for now.

### Bringing Recipes In, In Detail

- **Four ways to start:** a new recipe begins with a short list: From text, From a file, From a link, or Write it yourself. On a phone it's a drawer; on a wider screen it's the first page. Picking one opens that way's own screen: a card says what the AI will do, the text, link or file sits in its own box, and a footer holds Back and the one button that reads it ("Fill in the recipe" or "Choose a file"). Each has the same quiet "Write it yourself" row as the list; leaving a pasted link or text that way, or by going back to the list, asks first. A link that won't open or read also offers "Paste its text instead". When a reading fails, the line under the box says what went wrong: a lost connection or a busy reader is never put down to the link, the text or the file. The last row of the list, "Keep it as an idea", keeps it for later, and the text and link screens have a "Save as an idea" chip (no AI involved). The form is never the first thing you face, and leaving it with something typed or read in asks before the work is thrown away.
- **From text:** a paste box accepts up to 20,000 characters. The AI reads it and fills in a new recipe for you to review.
- **From a file:** a photo, screenshot, or PDF (up to 10 MB) works the same way; on a phone its dashed box grows to the height the screen has left.
- **From a link:** Pinch fetches the page, and the AI reads its text, along with any recipe data the page publishes, and fills in the form from that, so a page with partial or no recipe data still reads. Under the box, "Links from your ideas" lists the links you saved as ideas (three, then Show more); one tap puts a link in the box, and nothing is read until you tap Fill in the recipe. The link goes to a shared function that reads the page from Google's servers; nothing is stored.
- **Always reviewed:** the AI fills in the recipe form and tells you what it couldn't read, but nothing is saved until you confirm.
- **Unknown ingredients:** if a recipe mentions something that isn't in the catalog, it comes with up to three close names to pick from ("shredded lettuce" offers Iceberg lettuce and Romaine lettuce) and is otherwise offered as a new custom ingredient during review, so you decide what joins your catalog.
- **Lines with no amount stay that way.** "Salt to taste" or "a handful of parsley" come in with no amount and the words as the note, and never stop you saving. In the form, "No amount (to taste, a handful…)" under an amount does the same by hand.

## How It Feels

- **The metaphor is a home cook's counter.** Warm, hands-on, and a little playful, like measuring by the pinch. Paper-cream surfaces and an espresso-ink primary, with peach tints for chips, groups and the selected option, and warm orange as the accent (today, the active tab, the default price's star). Dark mode keeps the same warmth.
- **Personal.** Built around your kitchen, your stores, and your prices.
- **Mobile-first.** Everything works one-handed on a phone, standing at the counter.
- **Details come later.** Pinch asks for a name and little else to get going. The rest waits behind quiet nudges until you're ready.
- **Zero pressure.** Nothing nags you to plan every meal or fill in every price. An empty day is fine, and a missing price just means a number is left out.
- **Numbers when you want them.** Cost is a quiet companion; nutrition stays out of sight until you turn it on.
- **AI as a helper.** Every feature works fully by hand; AI just saves typing.

## What Makes This Different From a Generic Recipe App

- **A stocked starting catalog, including seasonings.** The everyday staples and the spice rack already exist, with nutrition, so a recipe can be built in seconds and ingredients stay consistent from one recipe to the next.
- **Cuts and varieties are first-class.** Chicken breast and chicken thigh are different ingredients with different prices and protein, grouped under one family so they're easy to find and swap.
- **Meals are compositions, not recipes.** A plate is an entree, a side, and a bag of chips. Recipes and plain ingredients sit side by side in one meal, and any day can bend it.
- **What you pay is part of the app.** A price ties what you pay at a real store to recipes and meals, so costs are calculated from your own shopping.
- **The calendar reads like a food diary at a glance.** Days show a soft-tinted chip per meal type (the meal's title on a wide screen), with one line of the period's numbers above.

## Under the Hood

- **Frontend:** React 19, TypeScript, Vite, TailwindCSS 4, DreamerUI (including its `Calendar` for the month view)
- **State:** Redux Toolkit, following the repo's standard per-app store pattern; one-off lookups through TanStack Query
- **Backend:** Firebase Auth, Firestore for ingredients, products, recipes, recipe sources, recipe collections, recipe ideas, cooking history, recipe notes, meals, calendar entries, the shopping list, pantry packages, per-user settings and reminder choices (adding to the list merges into existing lines in one transaction, so two devices adding at once never lose an amount); Firebase Storage for recipe, store and idea images
- **Notifications:** Firebase Cloud Messaging through the repo's shared push sender. A scheduled Cloud Function (every 30 minutes) sends each person who turned it on the morning note about food (dishes in the fridge and freezer, and pantry ingredients when that kind is on), once a day in their own hour; the shared reminder function (every minute) delivers a cooking timer's push. Dish, ingredient and step names are the only content, and only your own devices receive them.
- **Photo search:** a callable Cloud Function (`searchRecipeImages`) looks pictures up for a new recipe's title through DuckDuckGo first and Google Programmable Search as the fallback (one env switch orders them), with a shared cache, a daily budget and a per-person limit; it sends the title and nothing else, and a chosen photo is kept as its web address.
- **AI:** the repo's shared Firebase AI (Gemini) setup to read pasted text, photos, and PDFs (JPG, PNG, WebP, HEIC, and PDF up to 10 MB) and return drafts for review (including how long a dish keeps and how to reheat it, only when the source says so); for links, the shared `fetchPageContent` Cloud Function fetches the page's text and any recipe data first, and the AI model writes the draft from them; the same function gives a recipe idea its link's title, site name and picture address (no AI involved)
- **On the device:** a list's view options, the calendar's last view, cooking mode's three voice choices and whether you turned reminders off on this device are kept in the browser's local storage, per person, and each tab's place, search and pill are kept only in memory while Pinch is open; nothing else of yours is kept there beyond Firestore's own cache. Cooking mode adds the cooking-history entry (one per recipe on a plate) when you tap Finish, what you put away and a note about it, and keeps one small copy of where you are on this device (`pinch:<uid>:cooking-session`: recipe ids, servings, the step in view and the timers; for the pick-up row, under 12 hours, cleared when you finish, leave, tap Not now or sign out): it asks the browser to keep the screen awake and plays a short beep when a timer ends, and nothing about either is stored or sent. Voice commands use the browser's own speech recognition and speech synthesis (no Pinch server and no AI): while the mic is on the browser's speech service hears what is said (Chrome's sends it to Google, Safari's to Apple), and Pinch keeps nothing of it; the three voice choices are that the explainer was answered with Turn on, Read steps aloud, and whether it listens for "Hey Pinch" (the other way, tap to talk, is the default; the fifteen minutes are never kept)
- **Catalog data:** built-in nutrition values drawn from USDA FoodData Central, which publishes its data for free reuse (licence not yet confirmed from here: to be checked before launch), stored per 100 g alongside common unit conversions; money is stored as whole cents
- **Store logos:** a curated list of well-known stores (names and web domains) whose logos load from a logo service in the person's browser, with a letter tile whenever one isn't available; stores you add keep their own uploaded image in Firebase Storage. The logo service (Logo.dev) and its terms are to be confirmed before launch.
