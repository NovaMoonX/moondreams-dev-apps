# Pinch 🧂

## The Short

Pinch is a calm, personal kitchen planner. It comes stocked with a catalog of everyday ingredients and seasonings, keeps a library of your recipes and the meals you put together from them, and puts what you plan to eat on a calendar. Add what you pay for things where you buy them, and every recipe, meal, and week knows what it costs. Nutrition is there too, whenever you want to see it.

## The Story

Planning meals with an AI chat is easy to start and hard to keep going. Every conversation begins from scratch: it can't see what's in your kitchen, doesn't remember what you cooked last week, and has no idea what any of it costs. The recipes are good, but the context is gone by tomorrow. Pinch keeps that context: your ingredients, what you pay for them, your recipes and your plan, with a recipe from any chat, photo or web page easy to bring in.

## How It Works

1. **Start with a stocked kitchen.** A ready-made catalog of everyday ingredients and seasonings is waiting, each with its nutrition.
2. **Add what's missing.** Create your own ingredients, from a variant of an existing one (Chicken → Chicken breast) to a ready-to-eat food like Doritos. A name is all it takes to start.
3. **Add what you pay.** Attach a store, a price, and a package size to any ingredient, like chicken breast from Walmart, whenever you have them handy, and see what it costs per pound beside each place. Pick the store from a list of well-known ones with their logos, or add your own.
4. **Build recipes.** Write one by hand, or start from text, a file (a photo, screenshot or PDF) or a link to a recipe page, and the AI fills in the recipe for you to review.
5. **Bring recipes in from anywhere.** Paste a recipe copied from any chat, snap a photo of a cookbook page, or hand it a link.
6. **Put meals together.** A meal is a list of items, each a recipe or an ingredient, each with its own serving count. Save the ones you eat again.
7. **Plan your days.** Put a meal, a single recipe, or a single ingredient on the calendar under breakfast, lunch, dinner, snack, dessert, or drink. Your recent recipes and saved meals are one tap away, a row of days picks the day, and the toast's "View" shows where it landed.
8. **Tweak any day.** Skip the peppers on Tuesday or double the rice on Friday without touching the saved meal.
9. **See your week at a glance.** On a phone the calendar opens on the week ahead, starting today, with this week's earlier days folded away; a month grid is one tap away (and the default on a wide screen). Days show a soft-tinted chip per meal (dinner first, the meal's title on a wide screen), a past meal you cooked shows ✅, a Today button brings you back, and one line says what the period's meals cost.
10. **Fill in the details as you go.** A missing price, package size, or nutrition value shows up as a quiet line right where it matters ("Chicken thigh and 4 more need a price · Add prices"), so nothing is demanded up front and a tap fills it in.
11. **Turn on nutrition if you want it.** Calories, protein, carbs, and fat each stay hidden until you switch them on from the eye (View options).
12. **Lists stay short, and you choose what they show.** Rows start with prices and leave out calories and macros; an eye button on Kitchen and Recipes turns either one on or off, whenever you like.
13. **Remember where a recipe came from.** Tag a recipe with a source, like a favorite site or a stack of cookbooks, then find your recipes by it.
14. **Make a shopping list.** From a recipe, a saved meal or a whole day, pick what you need and it goes on one list, the Shopping tab, in the order you walk a shop. The Shopping tab can also pull the next week of planned meals in one go, and each line remembers what it is for. Tap a line to tick it off at the store.
15. **Keep recipe ideas.** A link to a page you like, a photo, or a few words about something you'd like to make someday. When you're ready, an idea turns into a recipe with its title, note and site already filled in.
16. **Cook it step by step.** Start cooking from a recipe, or from a meal you planned (a dinner of two or three recipes cooks as one, switching between them without losing your place or your timers): one step at a time in big type, the ingredients each step names with their amounts, a timer for any time a step mentions, and the screen kept on where your browser allows it.
17. **Keep a record of what you cooked.** One tap on a recipe, or on a meal you planned, notes that you made it, when, and for how many. It's a quiet history you can correct later, and a recipe shows when you last made it.
18. **Keep notes on a recipe, and see when you changed it.** Write down what you'd do differently, after a cook or any time. When you edit a recipe you've cooked, Pinch adds a note with the date and what changed, so you can always tell which version a note was about.
19. **Say how to keep it.** A recipe can say how many days it keeps in the fridge and the freezer, how to reheat it, and a tip such as "keep the sauce separate". It shows on the recipe, and cooking mode's last screen puts them on the put-away buttons ("Fridge · keeps 3 days").
20. **Keep track of your fridge and freezer.** Put what you made ahead in the fridge or freezer as a box: how many servings, when to eat it by, and what happened to it. Put portions in separate boxes and thaw one at a time, mark one eaten in a tap, correct a count that went wrong, and see a meal's own recipes' stock on its row. Kitchen's Fridge & freezer lists what is in each, soonest first, one row per batch, and a recipe or meal says when some is put away.

### Finding Your Way, In Detail

- **Calendar** (first): what you plan to eat, by month or week (a phone opens on the week ahead), with one line about the period above it.
- **Recipes** (second): your recipes, saved meals and recipe ideas; a search finds a recipe by its name, an ingredient, its source or its description.
- **Kitchen** (third): your ingredients, your fridge and freezer, and prices. Ingredients are the list; **Fridge & freezer** (what you've put away, one row per batch, with what to eat first) and **Stores & prices** (each price under the store it's at) are a tap away once they hold something. Later it will also hold how much of each ingredient you have on hand.

- **Shopping** (fourth): what you still need to buy, in shop sections (Produce first), with a count on the tab while anything is left.
- **Back does what you expect.** The phone's back gesture closes the drawer or step on top, one at a time; from any tab it returns to the Calendar, and from the Calendar it leaves Pinch. Each tab keeps its place, search and pill while you hop between them.

### Filling In As You Go, In Detail

- **Starting takes almost nothing.** A new ingredient needs a name. A new price needs a store, an amount and a size. A new recipe needs a title and its ingredients.
- **Everything else is invited, in context.** A price for an ingredient you use, a package size, a nutrition value, a conversion Pinch needs to compare two amounts: each appears as one quiet line exactly where the gap affects a number. A recipe's cost area says "Chicken thigh and 4 more need a price · Add prices", and tapping it opens a short form for just those.
- **One tap fills several.** When a recipe arrives with a dozen unknown prices, the line is still one line, and its form walks through them together.
- **Totals say what they leave out.** A cost with gaps carries a small warning that names what the total leaves out, so a number is never quietly low. A total that leaves something out says so in the figure too ("$5.20 so far"), and a recipe marks each ingredient line that has no price yet.
- **Only what you use, only what you've switched on.** Nudges cover ingredients that are in a recipe or on the calendar, and nutrition nudges appear only for the metrics you've turned on.
- **Always easy to wave off.** A nudge names the ingredient that matters most and has a "Not now" that quiets it until you reload; nothing is hidden for good by a stray tap. Nudges live only where the gap is owned: a recipe, and the period on the Calendar.
- **Prices in a few taps.** "Add prices" steps through the ingredients that weigh most first, says where each is used and how much is planned, keeps the store you picked, and starts each package size in the unit the recipe uses.

### Recipes and Meals, In Detail

- **A recipe is how to make one thing.** Adobo chicken, cilantro lime rice, your house sauce. Recipes live in your library and have no date.
- **A meal is what you eat in a sitting.** It's a list of items with a serving count on each: adobo chicken, cilantro lime rice, black beans, salsa, sour cream, peppers.
- **An item is a recipe or an ingredient.** Anything in your catalog can sit in a meal next to your recipes. Doritos is an ingredient: its nutrition and price live on it once, and follow it into every meal.
- **A meal can be a single item.** A one-pot chicken pan is just a recipe on the calendar by itself.
- **A recipe's drawer reads in three groups.** Its source as a link under the name and the description; one line of servings, time and cost per serving; and a block with how often you cooked it, what is in the fridge and freezer and your latest note, each a row that opens what it sums up. Then the actions. A recipe with nothing to say in the block has none.
- **A recipe can say where it came from.** "Serious Eats", "Mom's recipe cards", "NYT Cooking": a source is just a name you type or pick in the recipe form, after the steps, and it's optional. The recipe's details say "From Serious Eats", and once you have one, the Recipes tab gets a filter by source (with how many recipes each has), and tapping "From Serious Eats" on a recipe shows the others from there. The filter's "Edit sources" opens a list where you can rename a source or delete it; a source no recipe uses any more says so there and can be deleted in one tap, and it stops being offered in the recipe form. Deleting a source never deletes recipes; they just lose that label. A recipe read from a link starts on its website's name, which you can change.
- **Save a meal to reuse it.** Saved meals sit with your recipes in the Recipes tab.
- **Every day has its own copy of a meal.** Putting a meal on the calendar copies its items onto that day. Changing one day's items leaves the saved meal and every other day as they were, and editing the saved meal later leaves days already planned as they were. The "Save as a meal" chip on a planned meal turns a tweaked day into a meal of its own.
- **A planned meal shows what's next.** It keeps the name you planned it with ("Dinner · Taco night"). Tonight its main button is Start cooking, or Eat from fridge when a box of it is due today or tomorrow; a day ahead, a cooked dinner and a past one just offer Modify.
- **Recipes are shared by everything that uses them.** Edit a recipe and every meal still ahead of today that includes it shows the change.
- **Deleting something in use archives it.** A recipe, custom ingredient, or store that's part of planned days or saved meals disappears from lists and pickers, and the days and totals that used it stay whole.
- **Find tonight's recipe by what's in it.** The search looks at names, ingredients (and their other names, like scallion), sources and descriptions. When your search finds nothing among recipes but a saved meal or an idea matches, it says so and takes you there.
- **Deleting says what goes.** Deleting a recipe names what goes with it: its cooking history (how many times) and its notes.

### Ingredients, Variants, and Products, In Detail

- **An ingredient is anything you buy.** Chicken breast, paprika, a bag of Doritos.
- **Three layers, kept separate.** A *family* groups related ingredients (Chicken). An *ingredient* is the specific thing recipes and meals use (Chicken breast). A *product* is what you actually buy (Chicken breast, Walmart, $9.48 for 3 lb); the app calls it a price.
- **Recipes only ever name the ingredient.** A recipe says "chicken breast", never "Walmart chicken breast", so the same recipe works wherever you shop and reads correctly if it's ever shared. Products feed your own numbers.
- **Prices live on products.** A product's price and size give an ingredient its cost per unit, and that rolls up into recipe cost, meal cost, and the calendar totals.
- **One level of grouping, on purpose.** Families don't nest, so the catalog can grow to hundreds of ingredients without becoming a tree to maintain.
- **Your own ingredients sit beside the built-in ones.** A custom ingredient can join an existing family (Chicken → Chicken feet) or start its own.

### Stores, In Detail

- **A list of well-known stores is ready.** Pinch includes the grocery stores and warehouse clubs most people shop at, each with its logo, so adding "Walmart" is a tap, not typing.
- **Add your own.** The corner market, a farm stand, an online shop: give it a name and, if you like, a photo or logo. Without an image it gets a tile with its first letter.
- **Pick a store when you add a price.** The picker shows your stores first, then the well-known ones, with a search once the list grows.
- **Stores are for you.** Stores & prices lists every price under its store, and a store's heading opens its name and logo. They aren't part of anything you share.

### Cost and Nutrition, In Detail

- **Cost is always on.** A recipe shows its total and cost per serving, a meal shows the sum of its items, and the calendar's summary line shows what the period you're viewing costs ("$4.10 so far" while some ingredients have no price). Tapping it shows how much is priced, each day's cost (a day opens from there) and the average per meal.
- **Cost appears when amounts can be compared.** A recipe's amount and a price's package size line up when they measure the same kind of thing (pounds with ounces, cups with tablespoons), or when the catalog knows the conversion (a cup of flour to grams). Anything that can't be compared is called out by name with a nudge to fix it and is left out of the totals, so every number shown is one the app can stand behind. A line with no amount ("salt to taste", "a handful of parsley") is left out of the cost and the nutrition, and Pinch never asks you to price it.
- **Past days keep their numbers, until you say otherwise.** Once a day has passed, its cost and nutrition are fixed. Updating a price or editing a recipe changes what's ahead, not last month's totals. If you added a price afterwards and want that day to count it, "Price this day with today's prices" in the day's drawer redoes that one day. It redoes the day's cost and nutrition from today's prices and recipes, so a recipe you changed since can move it too. It asks first and shows the day's total before and after (the after as "about"), can't be undone, shows only when today's prices would change something, and the day then says when it was priced.
- **Nutrition is yours to enter, and yours to show.** Built-in ingredients arrive with calories, protein, carbs, and fat. You can add or adjust them on any ingredient. A recipe's nutrition adds up from its ingredients, and you can type your own numbers over it, handy when a recipe comes with its own from the web.
- **Type nutrition the way the label prints it.** Per 28 g, per ounce or per serving: Pinch converts it to per 100 g and says what it saved. Switching between g, oz and serving before you've typed a number converts the ones already there; once you've typed, it only changes what your numbers are for. A serving needs a size Pinch can weigh, and a blank or impossible number is called out before you save.
- **See it per serving or per 100 g.** An ingredient's drawer and the Kitchen list's rows share one choice, which starts at per 100 g. Flip it in the drawer (the switch is left out when a serving is exactly 100 g or can't be weighed) or in the list's view options.
- **Showing nutrition is opt-in.** In View options (the eye on the Calendar, Recipes and Kitchen), "Nutrition, everywhere" has calories, protein, carbs, and fat as four separate switches, all off to start. Turning one on shows it per serving on recipes and meals and as a daily average in the calendar's summary. With protein on, the summary also shows your average price per gram of protein.
- **View options are for the rows only.** The eye button turns prices and calories and macros on or off for a list's rows (prices start on, calories and macros start off), and picks per serving or per 100 g for ingredients. Totals, drawers and nudges don't change, and Reset puts the list back. Each list's choice is kept on this device, for you. A row is only as tall as the lines it shows.
- **A recipe's basics open one at a time.** The title, the prep and cook time and the description are one-line summaries; tap one to change it in a drawer on a phone or a small popover on a bigger screen, and press Enter or Done to close it. A recipe written from scratch opens its title for you.
- **Removing something from a recipe can be taken back.** Taking out an ingredient or a step (or an item from a meal's plate) shows "Removed Salt · Undo" with a few seconds counting down; Undo puts it back where it was.
- **Blanks are named.** An ingredient with no nutrition entered is called out with a nudge and left out of the totals while that metric is on.
- **A week starts today on a phone or tablet.** There the week shows the seven days from today; the month grid, and the week's columns on a wide screen, run Sunday to Saturday.
- **A meal holds up to 10 items.** Planning more than that is a recipe's job.

### Shopping List, In Detail

- **Add from where you are, or from your plan.** The Shopping tab's "+ Add" has "From your plan": the next seven days of planned meals in one sheet, merged per ingredient, with "Change" for other days on a calendar (days that have passed are left out). A recipe, a saved meal and a day each have an "Add to shopping list" row in their drawer (a day's says how much of it is already on your list, or "On your list ✓" once every meal of it has put its lines there; not on days that have passed). It opens a sheet that says "Untick what you already have" (or "Tick what you need" when nothing starts ticked), with every ingredient ticked except the ones this same recipe, meal or day already put on your list and the staples most kitchens have (spices, oils, vinegar, salt, water), which wait at the bottom under "Usually at home". A recipe also asks how many servings you're cooking, and every amount scales to that.
- **One line per ingredient and unit.** Adding chicken thigh twice makes one line with both amounts added. Each line remembers what put it on the list. In an add sheet, a line starts unticked only when this same recipe, meal or day already put it there ("Already on your list for Thu dinner"). A line something else put there starts ticked and says how much is there ("On your list · 2 lb"). When a day already on the list shares a line with the rest of the week, the sheet adds only the rest, so the weekly shop neither under-buys nor doubles up. Tap a line's arrow to see what it's for ("For Adobo chicken (Thu)"). A recipe line with no amount, like salt to taste, goes on as just its name, and a line with an amount of the same ingredient takes it in. Units are never converted: a cup and a tablespoon of the same ingredient are two lines. On the list, one ingredient's weights (or volumes) in two units read as one line ("Doritos · 5.49 oz"), and ticking it ticks both.
- **A day adds up.** An ingredient on its own counts as its serving size times the servings; a recipe counts its ingredient lines scaled by the servings planned.
- **See that a day is covered.** Once every meal of a day has put its lines on the list, the day's drawer says "On your list ✓", and still opens the sheet to add anything that's missing.
- **Tick off what you got.** Tap a line to tick it: it strikes through where it is and, a moment later, moves to "Got it", so the lines below don't jump if you tick the next one right away. The switch under the header shows To buy, Got it, or All: everything left, then what you got under a "Got it" line. Every tick is a round checkbox. Add the same thing again and it starts over, unticked. "Clear what you got" (in Got it, and on that line in All) takes everything you got off the list at once, with an Undo, and says how many it cleared. If another device un-ticked one in the meantime, that one stays.
- **Change a line.** The arrow on a line changes how much and which unit, or takes it off (with an Undo). A ticked line that changes unit stays ticked. Moving a line into a unit that is already on the list adds to it, or, if that one is ticked, puts it back on the list at the moved amount, and the sheet says so before you save. "+ Add" at the top finds any ingredient (an exact name or alias first, so "milk" finds whole milk, and a family's name finds its members) and puts it on at its usual serving; the sheet stays open for the next one.
- **Amounts you can pick up.** Counts round up to whole ones (3 lemons, not 2.5), and a spice or oil you need less than a tablespoon of shows just its name. The stored amounts don't change.
- **Find a line.** From 8 things on, a search field sits above the list and narrows it by name as you type.
- **An approximate cost.** A line under the title says what it covers, "About $12.40 for 6 of 7 things", worked out from each ingredient's default price and the amount on the list; with fewer than half the lines priced it says "Not enough prices yet". Stores sell whole packages, so the till total is usually higher, and the help icon beside it says so. The icon turns into a warning when something is left out, like an ingredient with no price yet, and names it.
- **A toast takes you there.** After adding, "View" switches to the Shopping tab, unless you have already opened a full-page screen such as a recipe form, which it never replaces.
- **It's just a list.** It doesn't follow the calendar: changing a meal later leaves the list as it was. Nothing on it is shared, copied out or sent anywhere.
- **Limits.** One add takes up to 200 lines, and the list holds about 300 things before it asks you to clear what you've got. Both are limits in the app, not in the database.

### Recipe Ideas, In Detail

- **A place for "I'd like to make something like this".** Ideas sit beside Recipes and Saved meals as a third pill, once you have one; until then "Not now? Keep it as an idea" at the bottom of New recipe opens the same sheet. An idea is a link, a photo, or a few words, with a title and an optional note.
- **Three ways to add one.** "New idea" lists them: *From a link* (paste a recipe page), *From a photo* (a picture or screenshot of a dish or recipe) and *Jot it down* (just a few words). Each opens on its own screen, and back returns to the list. A link or text you started as a recipe can end as an idea instead: the "From a link" and "From text" screens have "Save as an idea" under "Fill in the recipe".
- **A link finds its own title and picture.** Pinch fetches the page to read its title, site name and picture address, and shows what it found, with the title ready to change. Nothing else from the page is kept. "Save idea" waits until the lookup has finished (six seconds at most), so the idea is named after its page. If the page can't be read, takes too long or has no title, you're asked for one; left blank, the site's address is used. Saving a link or a few words closes the sheet at once, even with no signal; the idea's row says "Saves when you're back online" until it reaches the server.
- **No duplicates.** Pasting a page that is already on the list ("That one's already in your ideas") offers to open it instead, however the link was spelled (with or without `www.`, a trailing slash or tracking parameters).
- **A note is optional.** "+ Add a note" on a link or a photo; the jotted-down way shows it from the start.
- **Open an idea to see it whole.** The picture, the title, the note, "Open <site>" for a link (it opens the page in a new tab), and "Modify" and "Turn it into a recipe" in the footer.
- **Turn it into a recipe.** An idea that holds a link opens "From a link" with its address already filled in, so one tap on "Fill in the recipe" reads the page into the form (the idea's own title and note are kept, and its website is "Where it's from"); nothing is sent until you tap it. If the page can't be read, "Write it yourself instead" opens the form with the idea's title, its note as the description and its website as "Where it's from". A photo or a few words open that form directly; a photo stays with the idea and isn't copied to the recipe (the form says so and points to "From a file"). Nothing is saved until you look it over and save; back or leaving the form changes nothing. When the recipe is saved, Pinch says "Added … to your recipes" with Plan it, shows you the new recipe in your list, and the idea moves to "Made into recipes" (its button becomes "Open the recipe"). If the idea can't be updated (say it was deleted on another device a moment earlier), the recipe is still saved.
- **Deleting asks first.** Deleting an idea asks first, and for one that became a recipe says the recipe stays. Deleting the recipe later puts the idea back among the ones still to try, and the delete confirm says so.
- **Limits.** 120 characters for a title, 1,000 for a note, and about 200 ideas before the list asks you to delete one; the 200 is a limit in the app, not in the database.
- **Private.** Ideas are yours alone. A link idea's picture is loaded from the website it came from when you look at it.

### Marking a Recipe Cooked, In Detail

- **A quiet record, not a to-do.** "I cooked this" is one of the buttons under a recipe's facts in its drawer (it keeps a record you can change later). One tap adds an entry at the recipe's own servings, for now, and a note says "Marked Adobo chicken as cooked" with **Undo**, which takes back exactly that entry. Tap again and you've cooked it twice.
- **From the calendar.** A meal planned for today or a day that has passed has a "Mark as cooked" button on each recipe in it (not for a plain ingredient, and not for a day still ahead). Today counts as now; a day that has passed counts as noon that day, since that's when you remember. The row then reads "✅ Cooked · Not cooked", and Not cooked asks first, naming the recipe, since it takes the entry out of your history. Once every recipe in a meal is cooked, the day's drawer shows ✅ beside it, and the meal stops offering to start cooking or to eat from the fridge. Marking the same meal twice never makes two entries: the second says "Already marked".
- **You see it where you look.** A recipe that has been cooked says "✅ Cooked 4 times, last Oct 12" in its drawer, in the same block as the fridge and freezer and your latest note (the Recipes list itself stays quiet). When a recipe is on today's plan, its drawer's main button is "Start cooking", which opens cooking at the recipe's own servings and, when you finish, marks that dinner.
- **Cooking history.** The "Cooked 4 times, last Oct 12" row in the drawer opens the list of each time, newest first ("Oct 12 · 6 servings", with "From your plan for Sun, Oct 11" when it came from the calendar). Tap one to change its day, time or servings, or to delete it, and "+ Add an earlier cook" adds one you forgot (last Sunday's dinner). That row appears once a recipe has been cooked, so for one that hasn't, mark it now and change the date in its history. It shows the first 8 and "Show N more" past that, and a day in the future is refused.
- **Removing a recipe.** Taking a recipe off your lists because a planned day or saved meal still uses it keeps its history. Deleting a recipe outright deletes its history with it.
- **From cooking mode.** Finish on the last step records the cook at the servings you were making and says "Nice work · Marked as cooked", with **Undo** right there until you tap **Done** (it takes exactly that entry back) and a "Servings made" stepper if the number was different. A plate finishes once: every recipe is marked cooked together, each with its own servings, one Undo takes them all back, and each bulk recipe gets its own "Putting any away?". The same screen offers putting some away and "Add a note" (Cancel and the back arrow ask before they throw away what you typed). A quiet "Back to the last step" returns to the step. Started from a planned meal, it marks that meal's own entry (the meal then reads "✅ Cooked", so one dinner is one entry); if it was already marked, the screen says "Already in your cooking history" and saves the servings you made on it. Leaving before Finish (the back arrow, Escape, the back gesture) never adds an entry. If a timer is still going, Done asks "Stop timers and leave?" first, and if your history can't load, Finish just leaves.
- **Limits.** Up to 1,000 servings per entry, and about 500 entries for one recipe before it asks you to delete an older one; the 500 is a limit in the app, not in the database.
- **Private, and prices ignore it.** The history is yours alone. Prices, totals and your shopping list ignore it. Notes can follow a cook, and a change to a recipe is only recorded as a tweak once the recipe has been cooked.

### Recipe Notes, In Detail

- **A notes list on every recipe.** Under the steps, the drawer shows what you wrote and every change you made, newest first, each with its day (a change you made says "Changed on Oct 6"). Tap **Edit** on one to change its words or delete it. With no notes yet, the section is one row: "Add a note" ("Something to remember next time").
- **Notes after a cook.** In the note sheet, a recipe you've cooked asks "Is this about a time you cooked it?" with your last few cooks as answers ("Oct 12", "Today") and "No, just a note"; a cook from today is picked to start with. A note that follows a cook says "After cooking on Oct 12". The cooking history has an "Add a note" under each time that has none (it doesn't ask which cook again), and cooking mode's last screen offers "Add a note". If you later delete that cook, the note stays as a plain note.
- **Changes are recorded with the day they happened.** When you save changes to a recipe you've cooked, Pinch adds a "Tweak" note to its list in the same save, with a short line for each change ("Garlic 4 → 6 each", "Added Lime juice, 2 tbsp", "Removed Salt", "Step 2 now reads: …", "Cook time 35 → 45 min", "Serves 4 → 6") and, if you typed one, why. The form has an optional "What changed and why?" line for that. The drawer's status line adds "· changed Oct 20" when that change is newer than your last cook.
- **Your latest note is in cooking mode.** Under the Steps / Ingredients switch, a quiet block says "Your latest note (Oct 12): …" (two lines, starting at its "Next time…" sentence when it has one), and adds "You've changed the recipe since." when you edited the recipe after that note. A change you made never takes the block: the recipe already shows it. Tap it to open your notes right there, newest first, five at a time; nothing opens over the cooking screen. It isn't there while your notes load, if they can't, or when the recipe has none.
- **Which version a note was about.** A note you wrote before the recipe's latest tweak says "Written before your Oct 20 tweak", so an old "needs more garlic" doesn't read as if it were about today's recipe.
- **Only a recipe you've cooked is tracked.** A recipe you haven't cooked yet, a brand new recipe, and a save that changes nothing you could see (a new cover picture, or only its storage notes) add no tweak. Changing the order of the ingredients or steps counts ("Ingredients reordered").
- **Apply a note to the recipe.** Each note in the drawer's Notes (and in cooking mode's open notes) has "Apply to the recipe". It opens the recipe with your note quoted at the top, and saving records a new tweak with your note's words as the reason. The note itself stays as you wrote it.
- **The record of a change can't be rewritten.** You can edit the words of any note or the reason on a tweak, or delete one (it asks first), but the list of changes on a tweak is history and stays as it was saved.
- **Removing a recipe.** Taking a recipe off your lists because a planned day or saved meal still uses it keeps its notes. Deleting a recipe outright deletes its notes with it.
- **Limits.** A note holds up to 2,000 characters, a tweak lists up to eight changes (the last may say "and 3 more"), and about 300 notes for one recipe before it asks you to delete an older one; the 300 is a limit in the app, not in the database.
- **Private.** Your notes are yours alone and are never shared or sent anywhere. A tweak's lines can carry your ingredient names and the first words of a step you changed.

### Keeping Leftovers, In Detail

- **One row in the recipe form.** "Keeping it" sits between the steps and the nutrition. It reads "Add how to keep it" while it is empty and "Fridge 3 days, freezer 3 months, reheating, tip" once it isn't. Tap it to type days for the fridge and the freezer, a line on how to reheat it and a tip. Every part is optional.
- **Days, said the way you'd say them.** You type whole days, 1 to 365. A month is 30 days, so 90 shows as "3 months", 14 as "2 weeks" and 3 as "3 days".
- **On the recipe.** The drawer gets a "Keeping it" section under the steps: "Keeps 3 days in the fridge", "Keeps 3 months in the freezer", "Reheat: …" and your tip. It only shows when there is something to say, and sits above the notes.
- **On cooking mode's last screen.** The put-away buttons say how long it keeps ("🥛 Fridge · keeps 3 days", "❄️ Freezer · keeps 3 months").
- **Read from the source.** A text, photo or link reading fills the row in when the source itself says how long it keeps, how to reheat it or how to store it ("keeps 4 days in the fridge, freezes up to 3 months"). A range uses the shorter end, weeks and months become days, a fraction of a day rounds down, and the reader is told never to guess a time the source doesn't give. Like everything a reading finds, it is for you to review, and nothing is saved until you save the recipe.
- **Changing it isn't a tweak.** Editing a recipe's storage notes never adds a tweak note, even for a recipe you've cooked: how long it keeps isn't a change to what you cook or how, so the "changed" date and the "Written before your … tweak" labels on older notes stay as they were.
- **Older recipes keep working.** A recipe written before this simply shows no storage notes. Saving it from the form writes it as having none (`care: null`), and nothing else about it changes.
- **Private, and short.** Your storage notes are yours alone, like the rest of the recipe. Days go up to 365; the reheating line and the tip hold up to 300 characters each.

### Cooking Mode, In Detail

- **Start from a recipe or a planned meal.** "Start cooking" is a button in a recipe's drawer (when it has steps) and in a planned meal's drawer (the main button tonight, a quiet chip on a day ahead; not on a day that has passed, nor once that recipe is cooked). A meal with several recipes opens them all at once, as pills under the header; each keeps its own step, ticks and servings, and the last step of one moves on to the next with "Next: Cilantro lime rice". Cooking opens at the recipe's own servings (or the plan's, when the plan asks for more), and says how many you planned for tonight.
- **One step at a time.** The step in big type, "Step 2 of 6" with a dot per step (just the count past twelve), and big Back and Next buttons at the bottom; Next becomes Finish on the last step, which records that you cooked it (with Undo). Swipe left or right, or use the arrow keys. A long step scrolls while the buttons stay where they are.
- **The amounts are on the step.** Each step lists the ingredients it is the first to name, with their amounts ("Soy sauce ¼ cup", "Garlic 6"), so you rarely need the Ingredients view while cooking. On a wide screen the ingredient list sits beside the steps.
- **Ingredients for the servings you're making.** The Ingredients view starts at the recipe's own servings and moves a whole serving a tap (a half only below one). Change them and every amount scales, the same way the shopping list scales it, then reads the way you measure: ⅓ cup, 5 tbsp instead of 0.31 cup, "about 8" instead of 7.5. Tick what you've got out; Reset clears the ticks. A line with no amount shows its words ("to taste") and stays as it is. Amounts written inside the steps stay as the recipe wrote them, and a scaled step that has one says so.
- **Timers for the times in a step.** A step that says "25 minutes", "1 hr 30 min" or "10-15 min" gets a "Start 25 min timer" chip (a range uses its longer end). Up to four timers run (or wait, paused) at once above the buttons, each with Pause and a close button. Each timer is named for its step ("Brown the beef"); cooking a plate, each timer also says which recipe it's for ("Adobo chicken · Marinate the chicken"), the four are shared by the whole plate, and tapping a timer takes you back to its recipe and step. When one ends the strip lights up with Dismiss, with a buzz and a short beep where your device allows them (a note on screen only if the screen was covered or hidden). A finished timer waits for Dismiss and doesn't count toward the four. A time written with a comma ("1,5 hours", "2,500 seconds") gets no chip, because it could mean two things.
- **Timers only run while the screen is open.** A browser can't ring in the background, so leaving cooking mode ends them. The screen says so the first time you start one ("Keep this screen open: timers stop if you leave it."), and if a timer is still going (running or paused) when you leave, a "Stop timers and leave?" question asks first, with Stay as the safe answer. The back arrow, Escape, the back gesture and the last screen's Done all ask the same way. With no timer going, or only finished ones, they leave at once.
- **The screen stays on where it can.** Pinch asks your browser to keep it awake. If the browser can't or won't (an older one, or a page that isn't secure), one line says "This phone may turn the screen off; timers keep running only while this screen is open." in place of the timer line, so there is never a pair of them
- **Switching views keeps your place.** Going to Ingredients and back to Steps (or the other way) keeps the step you're on, the servings you chose and what you've ticked (and the same for each recipe of a plate).
- **A recipe that changes while you cook doesn't end it.** If the recipe is edited, archived or deleted on another device, cooking carries on (with what it last had, if it's gone), keeps your timers and the screen awake, and a quiet line says "This recipe was changed or removed elsewhere."
- **Nothing is saved unless you say so.** Not the step you're on, the ticks or the timers; they end when you leave. The only things cooking mode adds are the cooking-history entry when you tap Finish (Undo takes it back), what you put away, and a note, when you write one.

### Bringing Recipes In, In Detail

- **Four ways to start:** a new recipe begins with a short list: From text, From a file, From a link, or Write it yourself. On a phone it's a drawer; on a wider screen it's the first page. Picking one opens that way's own screen, which says what the AI will do, and each has "Write it yourself instead"; back from it returns to the list. Under the list, "Not now? Keep it as an idea" keeps it for later, and the text and link screens end in "Save as an idea" (no AI involved). The form is never the first thing you face, and leaving it with something typed or read in asks before the work is thrown away.
- **From text:** a paste box accepts up to 20,000 characters. The AI reads it and fills in a new recipe for you to review.
- **From a file:** a photo, screenshot, or PDF (up to 10 MB) works the same way.
- **From a link:** Pinch fetches the page, and the AI reads its text, along with any recipe data the page publishes, and fills in the form from that, so a page with partial or no recipe data still reads. The link goes to a shared function that reads the page from Google's servers; nothing is stored.
- **Always reviewed:** the AI fills in the recipe form and tells you what it couldn't read, but nothing is saved until you confirm.
- **Unknown ingredients:** if a recipe mentions something that isn't in the catalog, it comes with up to three close names to pick from ("shredded lettuce" offers Iceberg lettuce and Romaine lettuce) and is otherwise offered as a new custom ingredient during review, so you decide what joins your catalog.
- **Lines with no amount stay that way.** "Salt to taste" or "a handful of parsley" come in with no amount and the words as the note, and never stop you saving. In the form, "No amount (to taste, a handful…)" under an amount does the same by hand.

## How It Feels

- **The metaphor is a home cook's counter.** Warm, hands-on, and a little playful, like measuring by the pinch. Paper-cream surfaces and an espresso-ink primary, with peach tints for chips, groups and the selected option, and warm orange as the accent (today, the active tab, the default price's star). Dark mode keeps the same warmth.
- **Personal.** Built around your kitchen, your stores, and your prices.
- **Mobile-first.** Everything works one-handed on a phone, standing at the counter.
- **Details come later.** Pinch asks for a name and little else to get going. The rest waits behind quiet nudges until you're ready.
- **Zero pressure.** Nothing nags you to plan every meal or fill in every price. An empty day is fine, and a missing price just means a number is left out.
- **Numbers when you want them.** Cost is a quiet companion; nutrition stays out of sight until you turn it on.
- **AI as a helper.** Every feature works fully by hand; AI just saves typing.

## What Makes This Different From a Generic Recipe App

- **A stocked starting catalog, including seasonings.** The everyday staples and the spice rack already exist, with nutrition, so a recipe can be built in seconds and ingredients stay consistent from one recipe to the next.
- **Cuts and varieties are first-class.** Chicken breast and chicken thigh are different ingredients with different prices and protein, grouped under one family so they're easy to find and swap.
- **Meals are compositions, not recipes.** A plate is an entree, a side, and a bag of chips. Recipes and plain ingredients sit side by side in one meal, and any day can bend it.
- **What you pay is part of the app.** A price ties what you pay at a real store to recipes and meals, so costs are calculated from your own shopping.
- **The calendar reads like a food diary at a glance.** Days show a soft-tinted chip per meal type (the meal's title on a wide screen), with one line of the period's numbers above.

## Under the Hood

- **Frontend:** React 19, TypeScript, Vite, TailwindCSS 4, DreamerUI (including its `Calendar` for the month view)
- **State:** Redux Toolkit, following the repo's standard per-app store pattern; one-off lookups through TanStack Query
- **Backend:** Firebase Auth, Firestore for ingredients, products, recipes, recipe sources, recipe ideas, cooking history, recipe notes, meals, calendar entries, the shopping list and per-user settings (adding to the list merges into existing lines in one transaction, so two devices adding at once never lose an amount); Firebase Storage for recipe, store and idea images
- **AI:** the repo's shared Firebase AI (Gemini) setup to read pasted text, photos, and PDFs (JPG, PNG, WebP, HEIC, and PDF up to 10 MB) and return drafts for review (including how long a dish keeps and how to reheat it, only when the source says so); for links, the shared `fetchPageContent` Cloud Function fetches the page's text and any recipe data first, and the AI model writes the draft from them; the same function gives a recipe idea its link's title, site name and picture address (no AI involved)
- **On the device:** a list's view options and the calendar's last view are kept in the browser's local storage, per person, and each tab's place, search and pill are kept only in memory while Pinch is open; nothing else of yours is kept there beyond Firestore's own cache. Cooking mode keeps nothing of its own (the only things it adds are the cooking-history entry (one per recipe on a plate) when you tap Finish, what you put away and a note about it): it asks the browser to keep the screen awake and plays a short beep when a timer ends, and nothing about either is stored or sent
- **Catalog data:** built-in nutrition values drawn from USDA FoodData Central, which publishes its data for free reuse (licence not yet confirmed from here: to be checked before launch), stored per 100 g alongside common unit conversions; money is stored as whole cents
- **Store logos:** a curated list of well-known stores (names and web domains) whose logos load from a logo service in the person's browser, with a letter tile whenever one isn't available; stores you add keep their own uploaded image in Firebase Storage. The logo service (Logo.dev) and its terms are to be confirmed before launch.
