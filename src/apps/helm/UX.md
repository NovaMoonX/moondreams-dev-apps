# Helm UX

How Helm is laid out and how a person moves through it. The product vision is in `PRODUCT.md`. The screens are drawn on the **Helm UX** canvas (a Design artifact, link added once published; its board specs are `canvas.md`); this file holds what a picture cannot: the rules behind each screen, the sitemap, the components, the journeys and the form fields.

The canvas has one clickable shell: home is the conversation with {Name}, Talk walks listening, thinking and speaking, Browse opens every view, and Settings sits behind the gear. Its Tweaks: **State** (lived in, first run), **Assistant name** (Nomos, Ellery, Bartholomew) and **Theme** (light, dark). Static boards follow, one row per journey. The older rows keep their journeys, re-drawn on the new home where they changed; round 6 adds B11 and B12 (questions in every shape), row 11, talking to {Name} (V1 to V9, N2, N3, X1, X2), and row 12, your life (P2 to P11: a pet, a place, Google Contacts, How {Name} sees you, a procedure and its run, media on a record, a key date). Round 6d redraws the reply (words first, then a compact card), adds back-to-back messages (V9), the context bar on every screen (retired in 6e), and turns Knowledge into topic documents (K1 to K8). Round 6e takes the dock off every content page: home keeps Browse · Talk · Type, and every content page ends in one built-in **ask bar** (Y1 compares three placements, Y2 is the conversation it opens, Y3 is getting around without the dock); key dates split into dates that come back and moments (E2, E9 Moments, E10 Coming up, P11).

**`{Name}` in this file and on the canvas is the assistant's name the person chose (default "Nomos").** No screen, string, constant or stored summary hard-codes "Nomos".

## 1. What is specific to Helm

| Rule | What it means on screen |
|---|---|
| Home is the conversation | Helm opens on {Name}, not on a calendar (owner, round 6: Helm runs a life, not a schedule). Home is a voice-first surface, not a chat log: at rest it shows the assistant's name, at most two quiet lines (the next fixed thing today, and "2 need you") and the big Talk button; a turn shows what you said, what {Name} says back in large type, and what it did as a card with Undo. Earlier turns fold under "Earlier today · 4" and leave home overnight; Activity keeps everything. There is no capture sheet and no footer tab bar. |
| One way to talk, in one place per kind of screen (round 6e) | On home, Talk is the big button of the dock (a 68px ink button, labelled), with Type beside it and Attach while listening. On every content page the dock is gone and the page ends in the **ask bar**, one strip in the same place on every content page: "Ask about Grandpa", with the mic and the keyboard (1c). Utility screens (Settings, Connected apps, Activity, forms, Search) have neither. "Hey {Name}" works while Helm is open on screen and starts a turn about the page shown, so "move this to Friday" needs no name. No view has its own add-a-task button; Projects' "New project" and People's "Add from Google Contacts" are the only other create doors. |
| Every view is close at hand, and Browse stays calm | Home's dock is Browse · Talk · Type (owner, round 6); Browse lives on home only (round 6e). Browse pulls up one sheet of tiles sized by importance (owner, round 6b: "not all tiles need to have the same size"): "Search everything" on top; **Today** as a wide tile carrying its state ("Next: 11:00 AM · Brunch with Maya's parents", "2 events · 1 planned"); **Needs you** as a tall tile while questions wait (the verdigris dot, the count in the serif, the first question, "and 1 more"), shrinking to a thin full-width tile "Nothing waiting" when empty; **People** (three faces, "14 people · 2 pets") and **Projects** (the next deadline, "3 active"); then **small tiles** two to a row, each an icon, its count and its name: Places, Knowledge, Procedures, How {Name} sees you. Connected apps · Settings · Activity sit in a quiet foot row. **Only these four views get a big tile; a section added later is a small tile, never a big one** (the small tiles grow by rows of two; past 8 the last becomes "More · 3", opening the rest in place). A small tile shows only once its view has something. Sizes are fixed by Helm, never by the person; ordering by use is Next Steps. Every view is one tap from Browse; a content page has Back and a Home button in its app bar (the Home button is left out when Back already says "Home"), so any view is two taps from any page. No tab bar. |
| Done by {Name}, said by {Name} | In a reply, {Name}'s words come first beside its mark (the first letter of its name in the title serif, in a pewter disc), then a compact card of what it did with one Undo (round 6d). Anywhere else, what {Name} did is one line led by the mark, in the past tense, with its Undo on the same line where it was done. What {Name} only suggests (a plan slot, a procedure, a link) has a dashed verdigris edge until the person keeps it. A dashed edge means "suggested, not yours yet" and nothing else is dashed. Verdigris means "your call": the dashed edge, the "Suggested" word and the Needs you dot, nothing else. |
| Guessed is never dressed as known, and the world is never dressed as yours | A fact, a link or a match {Name} inferred carries a hollow ring and "{Name} inferred"; one the person said or confirmed carries a filled ring and "You said" or "You confirmed". Both rings are ink; the shape and the words carry the meaning, the same everywhere a fact appears. A note in the person's Knowledge carries the book mark (`BookMarked`), never a ring. An answer puts what comes from the person's records first, in the pewter result card with the assistant's mark; anything from general knowledge sits below a `SectionDivider` "Not from your records", in its own outlined white card led by a globe and "General knowledge", with a caution line (a health question: a bold caution first). The two never share a card. |
| Fixed is solid, flexible is Helm's | A Google Calendar event is a plain card with a small calendar mark and its calendar's name; it has no actions in Helm. A thing read from another app (a Waypoint flight, a Nine Lives vet visit) is the same solid card with that app's logo (`AppLogo`, 20px) in place of the calendar mark and the app's name in its meta: Helm reads it and never changes it (round 5). A planned task is a card with a round tick. A suggested task is the dashed card. Three looks, three meanings, on every day. Mail and files an answer looked up are the person's own, so they sit in the records card with a mail or file icon, never a ring. |
| No made-up numbers | No percentages, progress bars, health colours or scores. A project's state is an evidence line ("2 overdue · Final guest list in 9 days"); only "overdue" takes the warning colour. A task's steps say "Steps 2 of 7"; a procedure's run says "5 of 15". |

**Three words, three things (round 3).** A **fact** is about the person's own people, places and projects ("Dana Ruiz is the florist"). A **note** is general knowledge the person keeps ("Cake keeps 3 days refrigerated"), in **Knowledge**; since round 6d notes feed **topics**, documents {Name} writes from them (a note in no topic is a **loose note**). A **capture** is what they told {Name}; copy calls it "what you said" (never "your note", which now means a Knowledge note).

**Four more words (round 6).** A **who** is anyone in the person's life, drawn by its kind: a person, a pet or an organization (a vet, a bakery), each with an optional photo; copy never says "who record", it says the name. A **procedure** is a reusable routine ("Packing for a trip"); a **run** is one go at it, ticked for one trip, project or event. A task stays one-off, and may have **steps**. A **topic** (round 6d, replacing "doc") is one document {Name} keeps up from the person's notes ("Car care › Washing"), nested up to three levels, with sources and history; on screen it is always a "topic", never a "doc". **Media** is a photo, screenshot, PDF, voice note or Markdown file the person sent that {Name} kept on a record.

### Look: "the household's private secretary"

The owner's words: cool colours that inspire confidence, "an experienced butler for a family with lineage". Helm should feel like good stationery and a quiet, assured person who has run the house for years: deep ink navy, pewter and silver greys, white paper, one restrained cool accent (verdigris, the green-grey of old copper), and a serif face for titles over the site's clean sans. No nautical words, no crests in copy, no flourish. The full token table, type scale, radii, logo and mark are in `canvas.md` "Look and tokens"; they go into `helm.css` on `html.helm-theme` and into `.claude/rules/helm.md`.

- **Palette roles.** Pewter page, white cards, ink navy for the one filled button per screen and the Talk button; a pale pewter tint for the assistant's lines, the reply card, the context chip and selected pills; verdigris only for "your call" (above). Overdue is the one warm colour (amber text); destructive is red text, never a fill except in its confirm.
- **Type.** Titles (page title, a sheet's title, the name in an identity header) are the serif at 600; everything else is the sans. Figures are tabular. Sentence case everywhere; at most one eyebrow per screen (none planned).
- **Shapes.** Corners between formal and playful: cards 14, fields 10, pills and every tick round (`TickMark`, a round checkbox rule in `helm.css`). Hairlines, no shadows on cards; the subview card keeps the site's soft shadow.
- **Tone of fixed copy.** Plain, short and assured, an exception to "Playful by default" like Runway's: past-tense reports ("Made a task and linked it to Wedding"), no cheerleading, streaks or emoji in copy. Emoji appear only where the person chose one (a project's 💍). The person's chosen tone (section 1a) changes what {Name} writes freely, not these lines (see "For the owner").

### 1a. The assistant's name and tone

- **Name.** One line of text, 1 to 16 characters, trimmed; empty means "Nomos" (the field's placeholder says so). It shows in: home's title, the wake phrase ("Hey {Name}"), every "{Name} inferred", the mark's letter, the result card's lines' accessible names, Activity's actor, empty states, Settings and the first-capture AI line. It is never written into a stored sentence: a record says *who* acted (`actor`), and the screen supplies the current name, so renaming changes every old line too. Copy is written so the name works as a subject without a possessive ("{Name} inferred", not "{Name}'s guess") and without a pronoun that assumes a gender (the assistant is "it" in help text, and help text avoids pronouns where it can).
- **Long names.** Home's serif title ends in an ellipsis before the search icon; "Hey {Name}" in hints wraps, never clips. A name the recognizer hears badly (a made-up word) still works typed; the rename field checks it (below). The mark always shows one letter (the first letter of the name, upper case); a name that starts with a digit or a symbol shows the `Feather` icon instead.
- **Tone.** Four choices, one picked: **Composed** (default: plain, complete sentences), **Warm** (friendlier, a few more words), **Brief** (the fewest words, fragments allowed), **Formal** (the butler: "Your cake tasting is this afternoon…"). Each choice shows one sample line, the same answer in that voice, using the current name. Tone shapes the sentences {Name} writes itself: answers to questions, the "why" line on a suggestion when the model writes one, and drafted steps, procedures and docs. Record lines, buttons, labels, questions ("Which Sam?") and errors read the same in every tone.
- **Where.** Settings' first group, "Your assistant": one summary row "{Name} · Composed" opening the "Your assistant" sheet (board S2). It is not asked on first run (the nurse never wants setup before the first capture); the first result card ends once with a quiet door "You can rename {Name} and choose how it sounds" → S2. Under the name field: "Say "Hey {Name}" to try it" with a mic; it answers "I heard you" or "I didn't catch that. Try a name that's easier to say, or keep typing to me." (round 6).

### 1b. Connected apps (round 5)

Helm reads from the person's other apps on this site and from Google, and changes nothing in them. One screen in Settings holds every connection.

- **Two kinds of connection, one list.** The site's own apps (A-List, Going Once, Nine Lives, Pinch, Runway, Waypoint, Worth the Wait, and any app added later) and outside services (a Google account, with Calendar, Gmail and Drive inside it). Only apps the person can open are listed; an app they can't open is hidden, never greyed. Several Google accounts are allowed; the email is the row's title, since it is what tells two apart.
- **Grants are per category.** Each app offers categories in the person's words ("Vet visits and vaccines due", not "event"). Connecting shows them with round ticks, since nothing is read until Connect; on the connection's page they become `AppToggle`s that apply at once. Turning one off takes its copy out of Helm, with Undo on the line.
- **What starts on.** Names and dates Helm can plan around (trips, plans and stays, meal plans, vet visits and vaccines due, movie nights, pickups). Off until ticked: money detail, health notes and medicines, free text the person wrote and other people's names (owner, round 5). Runway offers "Spending by category" (on, kept) and beside it "Transactions" (off, and looked up only when a question needs it, with only what an answer quotes kept, like Gmail), whose line reads "Lets Helm answer things like "what did I spend at Costco?" Looked up when you ask, never kept." (owner, round 5).
- **Worth the Wait shares one category, "Box counts", which starts off (owner, round 5).** Its line: "Your boxes, how many items each holds, and how many are revealed. Never what's inside." Item contents never leave Worth the Wait, revealed or not; there is no other category to tick.
- **Kept or looked up, said on every category.** Another app's categories are kept in Helm as a sealed copy, updated every hour ("Updated today 9:30 AM"). Gmail and Drive are searched only when a question needs them, and only what an answer quotes is kept, sealed with the answer ("Searched when you ask. Only what an answer quotes is kept."); the answer says where it looked ("Looked in Gmail (you@gmail.com) just now. Only what this answer quotes is kept."). Calendar stays as planned in round 2.
- **Read only, said plainly.** Every connect screen and connection page has one line: "Helm reads these and changes nothing in Waypoint." Google's screen adds that Helm can't send mail, change events or edit files.
- **Every record keeps its source.** A record from another app opens a sheet with a muted "From Waypoint" block: the app's logo, "Updated today 9:30 AM · read only in Helm", "Change it in Waypoint and Helm catches up within the hour", and "Open in Waypoint" (same tab; Back returns). A copy kept after Disconnect says "From Waypoint · no longer updated". A fact {Name} noted from an email carries a hollow ring and "{Name} inferred · from an email, Nov 5". A person {Name} met in an email shows that email in Mentions, with the account.
- **One source wins for each field, and a disagreement shows.** The calendar wins for an event's time; the app that owns a thing wins for it (Waypoint for a flight); an email never wins. When two disagree, Helm plans by the winner and says so: the card carries one line ("Two times: an email says 2:00 PM"), the sheet shows both with "Helm plans by this" on the winner, "Helm can't change your calendar" and two actions ("The calendar is right", "Remind me to ask the bakery"). A calendar event and an app's item that are the same thing (same start within 15 minutes, matching title) draw as one card with both marks. **As built (issue 33):** the calendar's mark with the other source's small logo on its corner; when the app's time differs, a muted line "A-List Tracker says 7:00 PM" on the card and in the sheet; the sheet lists each source's block, and the app's place shows when the calendar has none.
- **Trouble says what and the way out.** A Google account that stopped sharing (Google's testing limit, about weekly) shows "Stopped sharing" with the triangle icon on its row, an `InlineNotice` with "Last worked …" on its page, and Reconnect as the page's primary; answers say "Couldn't look in Gmail (you@company.com): Google stopped sharing it" with a Reconnect chip. An app that can't be reached is retried on its own and shows "Couldn't update since Thu · Try now" on its row; past a day, Today adds one muted line naming it. An app the person lost access to says "You can't open Nine Lives any more" with Disconnect. No new colour: the triangle and the words carry it, as amber stays "overdue" only.
- **Disconnect asks once.** A drawer: what stops, that nothing in the app changes, and one round tick "Also delete what came from Waypoint" naming the count ("2 trips and 14 plans. Your tasks and notes that mention them stay."), **ticked** (owner, round 5). Under the box, one muted line: "Untick to keep them, marked "no longer updated". Helm deletes kept copies after 90 days." For a Google account that only looked things up it names what {Name} noted from it, and the lines answers quoted from your mail and files, which go with it. Disconnecting Google withdraws the permission at Google too.
- **One site-wide place, as an index (design's call, light).** Grants belong to the site, not to Helm: any app may read another later, and the person needs one place to see who reads what. The account page (`/account`) gets a "Connected apps" section, shown once any grant exists, listing each reader app with what it reads and its status; a row opens that grant's page inside the reader app (Helm's L4), where it is changed. The list is never a second editor, so the two can't drift, and it costs one shared list component (`ConnectedSourcesList`, no app's words) that Helm's Settings screen uses too. Source apps get no section of their own in the MVP.
- **Gmail and Drive for others.** Their read permissions need Google's review before anyone but test users can grant them, so the Gmail and Drive ticks show only for an account Helm allows; everyone else sees Calendar alone.

### 1c. Talking to {Name} (round 6, ask bar round 6e)

- **Opening Helm is silent.** No greeting is spoken and the mic is closed until the person taps Talk or says "Hey {Name}". The wake phrase listens only while Helm is open on screen, through the site's speech layer (`useSpeechCommands`, `@/lib/speech`), never in the background; Settings → Voice turns it off.
- **A turn, in four states, one button.** Talk (idle) → **Listening** (the button rings, the words appear in large type as heard, a wave under them, Cancel and Attach beside it) → **Thinking** ("Working on it…", the button becomes Stop, a short "Got it" spoken at once in this phone's voice, and a status line naming what {Name} is looking at) → **Speaking** (the reply in large type, its words turning ink as they are said, Stop to cut it off). Listening ends after 8 seconds of quiet, a tap on the button, or "that's all". Nothing is sent while the person can still see the words being heard; Cancel throws them away. **As built (issue 12):** Talk rings while listening, thinking and speaking; Cancel takes Browse's place while listening, and Send is the Talk button's own tap; the words are in large type above the dock, a long sentence keeping its newest words in view, and the field steps aside while the voice is in use; thinking is the spinning ring and Stop, with the turn's own "Reading…" line in the thread as the one status; a turn that is sorted says its short "Got it" line in this phone's voice, and a turn that needs the person or fails says nothing yet (its written reply stands); Stop while thinking ends the wait, not the filing ("It's still being added. I just won't read it out."); a Send with nothing heard says "I didn't catch anything. Tap to try again."; a turn still unsorted offline stops the wait with "Saved. I'll file it when you're back online."; "undo that" takes back the latest turn filed in this session and says so ("Nothing to undo yet." when there is none, "Still working on that one. Try again in a moment." while a turn is still being read). Leaving home or closing the conversation over a page drops words being heard and cuts off a line being said; a reply that arrives on a page with no voice controls stays written. Attach, the status line under thinking, barge-in and the Stopped mark come in later issues.
- **Short spoken, whole on screen.** Spoken replies stay under about 40 words, in the chosen tone; the card under the words holds the whole answer, every record line and its Undo. "Tell me more" (said or tapped) reads on from where the voice stopped. Past 15 seconds of thinking a line joins: "Still working. You can leave; the answer waits here."
- **Barge-in.** Talking while {Name} speaks stops the voice at once and starts listening; what was cut off stays on screen, marked "Stopped · you cut in", and a tap on it reads it again.
- **Typing is always there, and a typed turn is answered in writing.** Type opens a compose bar over the dock (Attach, the field, Talk instead, Send); Enter sends. Only a spoken turn gets a spoken reply (owner, round 6). While {Name} speaks, "Mute" sits in home's header: replies stay written until Helm is closed. **As built (issue 12):** Mute sits under the line being spoken (on home and in the conversation over a page), where the eye is while the reply plays; once muted, "Muted" sits in the same place (above the dock's buttons, or beside Stop in the conversation) and a tap turns it back on. It is never saved.
- **Asking from a content page: the ask bar (round 6e, Y1 to Y3).** Home keeps its dock unchanged. Every content page (a person, pet or place, a project, a task, a topic or note, a procedure, Today, Needs you, the People and Projects lists, Knowledge, How {Name} sees you, an answer's source) drops the dock and ends in the ask bar: a full-width strip that is part of the layout, never floating, the page's content ending above it. It holds one field-like row: {Name}'s mark, the words "Ask about Grandpa" ("Ask about this topic", "Ask about today", "Ask about these 3 questions", "Ask about these 24 people", "Ask about what's coming up", "Ask about this task"; a long name is cut with an ellipsis and the chip shows it whole), then a mic and a keyboard button. The shell renders it once from the open page's context; no page places its own. **It does not move when the page scrolls**: the page scrolls above it, and it stays put at the bottom, under the thumb, the same on every content page. A tap on the words listens at once if the person last spoke and opens the field if they last typed; the mic and keyboard choose directly; "Hey {Name}" does the same as a tap. The conversation opens as a sheet over the page with the chip on top ("About Grandpa" with his face, "About this topic: Washing", "About today"); its ✕ turns it into "About anything" and that turn carries nothing from the page. "Back to Grandpa" at the sheet's top (or a swipe down) closes it, and the page comes back scrolled where it was, changed if the turn changed it. Utility screens (Settings, Connected apps, Activity, forms, Search) have no ask bar. The context is the ids of the records the page shows; the server re-reads them under the person's own account. Asking with a topic open offers "Add to this topic" on a general answer. This replaces round 6d's context bar over the dock and its two treatments (X1, X2); the other two placements (a slot in the app bar, a row under the title) stay on Y1 as Tweak options for the owner. **As built (issue 9):** Today, Tasks and an open task's sheet publish what they show and have the bar ("Ask about today", "Ask about these 9 tasks", "Ask about this task"); the bar is fixed at the page's foot, with the page padded so the last row clears it, and an open task sheet draws its own copy at its foot (one bar on screen at a time). The bar holds the mark and the words, then a mic (opens the conversation and starts listening, the field left unfocused so the keyboard doesn't cover the words) and a keyboard (opens it with the field focused), each with a 40px hit area (issue 12); a tap on the words opens the conversation with the field focused. The conversation is an overlay `Subview` over the page, opened by `?ask=page` with one history entry; its header reads "Back to Today", and the chip sits just above the field, where its ✕ turns it into "About anything" for that opening. A "this", "it" or "the fifth one" with nothing to point at files nothing and waits in Needs you. It shows the open session's turns (home shows them too) and has a draft of its own. Inbox has no bar until its records are tasks; Home and utility screens never have one.
- **Questions are asked in the conversation.** What needs the person ("Which Sam?", "Which is true now?") is asked on home, one at a time, spoken and answered by voice or a tap; "Skip" puts it back ("Skipped. It's waiting in Needs you."). Questions come in seven shapes (1e). "2 need you" on home starts them; the Needs you view lists them for whoever prefers a list. Nothing carries a badge.
- **A reply reads as {Name} answering (round 6d).** What you said is a right-aligned ink bubble with its time under it. The reply is {Name}'s words first, beside its mark ("Done. I'll remind you tomorrow at 9, and I've noted Maya's birthday."), then a compact card of what it did, then any question. The card (`DidCard`) is indented under the words: one row per change, its icon, the record's name and one muted line ("Task · Tomorrow, 9:00 AM", "Key date · November 12 · on Maya, new"), each row a door to its record; the foot holds one Undo for the turn ("Undo all of this" when there is more than one line) and the time. After Undo the rows strike through and the card says what was taken back and what was kept (V8; the partial rule is B5's). The card never repeats the words: the words say it in a sentence, the card lists it. It replaces the round 6 log of "Made a task: …" lines.
- **Back-to-back messages make one turn while {Name} thinks (round 6d).** Bubbles sent before the reply arrives join the turn: they stack under one time, thinking says "Reading all three…", and one reply answers them all ("Got all three.") with one card and one Undo. A bubble sent after a reply starts a new turn, with its own thinking and reply. The hint under thinking says so: "Keep typing: anything you send before the reply joins this turn." Spoken turns behave the same: talking again while thinking adds to the turn; after the reply it is a new one.
- **Attach.** The paperclip (beside Talk while listening, and in the compose bar) offers: Take a photo · Choose a photo · Choose a file (PDF or Markdown, `.md`) · Record a voice note. A file shows as a thumbnail on what you said. Markdown is read as text. {Name} decides what to keep and says it in the card: "Kept the order form on Cake tasting" (a line that opens it), or "Didn't keep the screenshot" with "Keep it". A kept Markdown file is filed into a topic ("Added to Car care › Washing", 1f), kept as a loose note, or kept as media on the record it was about. Video waits for a later version (owner, round 6).
- **Offline, the phone hears and speaks (owner, round 6).** Helm bundles an opt-in offline voice: an on-device recognition model (about 60 MB, downloaded on request after a check that the phone can run it) with the site's natural or system voice for replies, so talking works offline on every phone once it is downloaded. Cutting in by voice works offline too. What you said is "Saved on this phone. {Name} will sort it once you're back online.", with the chips Helm read; questions wait under "Waiting for a connection". Before the download, or on a browser that can't run it, Talk offline shows its lock (`LockedButton`): "Download the offline voice in Settings to talk without a connection. Type for now." On screen the feature is named "Hear me offline"; its exact words are in PRODUCT.md "Speaking and Listening".
- **The mic refused.** Talk shows its lock; a tap says how to allow the microphone in this browser, and Type stays. **As built (issue 12):** a browser with no recognition or a refused microphone leaves Talk as it is; the tap opens the composer with the cursor in it and one line above the field ("Allow the microphone for this site to talk. Type for now.", or "This browser can't listen. Type for now."), which goes when the person types.
- **Voice has its own limit.** Past the day's cloud listening and speech (60 minutes and 30,000 characters a person), {Name} switches to this phone's voice, with one line saying so; the reasoning limit is A10's. **As built (issue 15):** past the speech allowance answers are read by this phone's voice with nothing said yet; the one line comes with spoken answers, and "Got it" lines are always this phone's voice.
- **`^` names a procedure or a note** in typed text (`@` a who, `#` a place, `!` urgent), as the shorthand row offers above the keyboard.

### 1d. Your life: who, places, procedures, docs and media (round 6, key dates round 6e)

- **One kind of record for anyone.** People, pets and organizations share one page (photo or initial, serif name, how they relate; tabs Facts · Tasks · Mentions · Media). A pet's page says its kind and age and offers its vet; a pet that also lives in Nine Lives offers "Connect Nine Lives" there. An organization (Greenwood Vet, Sweet Layer Bakery) is the same page with a building mark.
- **Not everyone is equal.** Each who has a tier: **Close**, **Regular** or **Passing**, set by {Name} from how often they come up and shown as "In your life · Close" (a row the person can change). The People view groups by tier under `SectionDivider`s, Close first; Passing people fold behind "Passing · 9" and stay out of pickers until they matter. In a project each linked who has a role: **key person**, **involved** or **mentioned**; the project's People tab shows key people as rows and the rest behind one door ("Involved · 6 · mentioned · 9").
  **As built (issues 34, 35 and 40):** People is a small tile in Browse and a content page (Back "Home"): Close then Regular, A to Z inside each, the person themself first with "You", a pet's species or an organization's kind under its name, then "Others" folded behind "Show others · 2" (passing ones), with `SearchInput` from 8. A who's page (Back "People") has its mark, name and kind or relation, then Facts (a quiet empty line until issue 40), Tasks (open linked tasks, rows open the task sheet) and Mentions (the turns that named them, newest first, from what is loaded). A place's page lists its tasks. ## 5. Where the screen set stops
