# Helm 🧭

> Everything below is the product vision; the UX doc and the technical design come after this is approved.

> **The assistant's name.** Helm's assistant is called Nomos until the person renames it. This document says "the assistant" in prose; in quoted screen copy and spoken lines, `[name]` stands for whatever name the person chose, and the wake phrase is "Hey [name]" (see Your Assistant, In Detail). No screen or spoken line ever hard-codes "Nomos": it is only the default value of the name.

## Who It Is For



Every change names the person it serves and is walked as that person. Each person uses Helm alone: one person per account, their own assistant, nothing shared with anyone else. A change for one never breaks another's journey.

### The owner, carrying a wedding, a job and a full life

- **Situation.** A full-time job, their own wedding to Maya at Ridgeline Barn eight months out, a friend's wedding they officiate first (Priya Park and Sam Whitaker's, Sat, Dec 12 at Cascade Orchard in Leavenworth, with a video call with the couple on Monday, Nov 16 and six questions for them), a weekend in Portland flying home Sunday morning, two cats, a car they care for, a move planned for March with things to sell on Going Once before it, and the people around all of it: a florist and a baker who matter until June, Priya and Sam until December, a sister who matters always, a coworker mentioned once. Their life is spread over two Google accounts (calendar, mail, Drive), the site's other apps (the honeymoon and the Portland weekend in Waypoint, the wedding fund in Runway, meals in Pinch, the cats in Nine Lives, the moving sale in Going Once), photos of quotes on their phone and a long chat elsewhere where they keep refining how they wash the car.
- **Journey they repeat.** Open Helm and talk: "Dana's quote came in at twenty-one hundred, and remind me to call her Friday" while walking to the car; send a photo of the quote; hear "Got it" and see what changed, with Undo. On a person's page, "she's key for the wedding" and "Maya's birthday is March 3"; see "Maya's birthday · Sat, Mar 3" on Today a few days before, and ask "what did I get her last year?"; on the Car care › Washing topic, "what did I decide about the ceramic coat?" and "I switched to two buckets", then tap a paragraph to see it came from a file sent last week. Ask "what's on today?" and hear a two-sentence answer. Glance at "How [name] sees you" now and then and fix what's wrong. Later (Next Steps): say "took my pills" and "another glass of water" without opening anything or naming which pills, and see the line ticked in the reply, get a nudge for pills while the habit builds and choose fewer once it holds, take Floss off Today once it is formed, keep a guitar goal whose why they rambled once and Helm tidied on their yes, whose practice Helm offers to move off the evenings that keep not working, or make smaller after they said "too tired" three times in two weeks, say "the wedding matters most, then work" and have Helm hold to it, see "hold Italian until after Nov 30?" when a fourth goal would crowd the wedding in a busy month, ask "could I drive to Leavenworth on Sunday after I land?" with their hands on the wheel and answer each follow-up by voice, and save "AWD or 4WD?" for a quiet evening later; read "How [name] sees you" for what Helm thinks works for them and strike a line that's wrong, and tell the assistant to be blunt and honest.
- **Level.** Everything in the MVP: the voice conversation with the wake phrase on, attachments, people with tiers, roles and photos, pets, Google Contacts picked in, preferences, living notes, procedures, projects, Today, and Connected apps on both Google accounts and most of the site's apps. In Next Steps, hands-free nearly always, a few habits (pills, water, floss) with reminders while they build, one or two goals (guitar), an order of what matters most said in their own words, what Helm learns about when things work, and its Pause or Hold Off lines.
- **Never wants to see.** The assistant speaking first (beyond the habit reminders they turned on), sending or changing anything outside Helm, or keeping a photo without showing where it went. A guess about a person, a preference or who they are shown as if they had said it. Helm treated as a calendar with extras, a project or a goal shown as "62% done", a streak to protect or a habit tracker's red day, a long spoken answer when a sentence would do, a pattern Helm noticed acted on in their own plans without asking, a reminder about a habit they already formed, a priority picked from a fixed scale, Helm deciding on its own what matters to them, their own words replaced without asking, or something they said about feeling tired turned into a verdict about them.

### A nurse on rotating shifts, who talks and forgets

- **Situation.** Works twelve-hour shifts that change week to week, posted in a Google Calendar called "Shifts". Has a steady stream of small things (renew the license, return the parcel, book the dentist) that get lost between shifts, and is too tired to type after one.
- **Journey they repeat.** Sitting in the car after a shift, tap once and say three things in a row; hear "Got it, three things" and drive home. Before a night shift, run their "Before a night shift" procedure and tick it. On a day off, ask "anything for today?" and hear one or two things suggested around rest, and nothing at all on the day a night shift ends. Later (Next Steps): a "stretch before bed" habit whose "before bed", and its nudge, follow the shift and keep coming through a week of nights when Helm is never opened, "too tired, skipping it" said three times in a week of nights and answered with a five-minute version, and Helm noticing that mornings on a day off after a run of shifts never get anything done, planning those days from noon and saying why, until they say "mornings work again" when the rota changes.
- **Level.** The voice conversation by tap (never the wake phrase), tasks, one procedure, and Today with only Google Calendar connected and the Shifts calendar marked. No projects or goals, no contacts, no settings opened; in Next Steps one or two habits, and Helm's learning left as it comes.
- **Never wants to see.** Setup before the first sentence, a list of apps to connect, a required syntax, a spoken reply blaring in the break room when they typed, a plan that treats a rest day as free time to fill, streaks, "you missed" or "you're behind", the same suggestion offered again the week after they said no, or a lecture about taking on too much after a run of shifts.

### Someone handling a parent's care from a distance

- **Situation.** Coordinates their mom's appointments, medicines and paperwork from another city: two doctors, a pharmacy, an insurer, a care coordinator. Gets letters as photos and discharge summaries as PDFs. Does not use Google Calendar and does not want to connect anything. Cares a great deal that this stays private.
- **Journey they repeat.** After each call, send a voice note or say what was said ("Dr. Patel moved the cardiology follow-up to Mar 12, keep the metoprolol at 25 mg"); send the insurer's letter as a photo and see what was kept from it; before each appointment, start a run of "Before Mom's appointment" for that visit; check Mom's page for what is known, where each fact came from and who is key for her care. Later (Next Steps): a 9 PM reminder to call about Mom's evening pills that says only "Your evening habit is up" on the locked screen, "Mom took her pills" after the call (the evening ones, since it's evening), a note such as "she felt dizzy after" kept on that day as said, and on Mom's metoprolol habit see which days were ticked and when.
- **Level.** The conversation (mostly typed in waiting rooms, voice at home), attachments, one project (Mom's care) with people in roles, Mom as a close person, facts with sources, a few notes, one procedure; in Next Steps, Mom's medicines as habits kept for her. No goals. Nothing connected, no browser trusted.
- **Never wants to see.** An inferred dose or date shown as confirmed, a newer fact silently replacing an older one, general knowledge passed off as a doctor's words, Mom's health read aloud when they typed, a letter kept without saying where, anything about Mom readable by anyone else or handed to another app, a nudge to connect anything, a dose time Helm moved or a new time it proposed, an unticked dose shown in red as if it were a verdict, Mom's medicine named on a locked phone screen unless they chose it, a note about how Mom felt turned into a guess about her health, or a reminder for Mom's doses that quietly stops.

**Each person sees only what they use.** Projects, goals, habits, procedures, Knowledge, pets and contacts stay out of the way until the person makes one, "What I've noticed" shows only once Helm has noticed something, Today works without a calendar, reminders appear only once a habit exists, "What matters most" only once the person has said something matters, and Pause or Hold Off lines only once there is something to pause (Next Steps), the wake phrase is off until chosen, and the assistant's name, tone and "How [name] sees you" are never pushed at anyone. Connections are offered only where one would help (Today's calendar row, a person's page for Google Contacts) and otherwise wait in Settings → Connected apps.

## The Short

Helm is a private companion for one person's whole life, and you mostly talk to it. Its assistant (Nomos, or whatever you rename it) answers in a natural voice, briefly, and keeps what matters: the people in your life and how much each matters, your pets, places, projects and tasks, what you like and who you are, the routines you repeat, and the things you've learned, kept as living notes you can ask about and refine. Send it a photo, a PDF, a voice note or a Markdown file and it keeps what's useful and shows where it went. Wherever you are in Helm, it knows what's on the screen, so "move this to Friday" just works. Connect Google Calendar, Gmail, Drive, Google Contacts and the site's other apps and it can read them, saying where each thing came from, but never change them. Your day and calendar are one view among several, not the centre. It organizes inside Helm on its own, shows what it did with Undo, never speaks first (the one push is a habit reminder you turn on, later), and keeps everything you tell it encrypted.

## The Story

Most of the effort of a full life is not the doing but the keeping track: who said what, who matters for what, what you prefer, what you worked out last time, which deadline is real. To-do apps hold the list but not the context; chat assistants hold the context for one conversation and forget it, and what they help you work out ends up in a chat you have to go back and find. Helm is the owner's attempt at a calm chief of staff you can simply talk to: something that remembers meaningfully, keeps what you learn in a form you can use, plans realistically, acts responsibly and asks before anything leaves the house. It started as a planner and grew, in the owner's review, into a companion for the whole of a life; this document cuts that to what the people above need first.

## Words We Use



The owner: "I have a tendency to conflate things." These words mean one thing each, in this document, in the assistant's replies and on screen. When a sentence could be two of them, the assistant asks once.

| Word | What it is | The owner's examples | Where it is planned |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Task** | A one-off thing to do, with a date or none. Done once, then gone. | "Go shopping in two weeks"; "buy the cat's refill" | MVP (Tasks, In Detail) |
| **Repeating task** | A job on a cadence that has to get done whether or not it ever feels automatic. It falls due, can be late, and waits until done. No stages. | "Clean the car every weekend", or "every 3 weeks"; the cat's flea medicine once a month | Next Steps, Issue 70 |
| **Habit** | Something in a daily or weekly window you want to become part of you. Never overdue; it moves through stages and steps back once it holds. | "Practise piano"; "water through the day"; floss | Next Steps (Habits, In Detail) |
| **Care or health habit** | A habit about a medicine, a dose or someone's care. Kept exactly as said, never formed, never moved by Helm. Always private, like every health and care thing. | Pills morning and evening; the cat's daily pill; Mom's metoprolol at 9 PM | Next Steps (Habits, In Detail) |
| **Private** | Not a kind of thing but something any thing can be: never named outside Helm (a lock screen, a copy, an export) unless you turn that on for that one thing. Helm marks health and care things private on its own; you can mark or unmark anything else. | Mom's metoprolol (private on its own); "keep Dana's surprise party private" | Next Steps (Private Things, In Detail) |
| **Procedure** | A set of steps kept once and run each time, each run ticked on its own. | The steps for moving out of the apartment; packing for a trip | MVP, built (Procedures, In Detail; Issues 54, 55) |
| **Scheduled action** | Something the assistant does for you at a set time, without being asked each time. | "Put next week's groceries on the calendar every Sunday" | Next Steps, Issue 115 (its outbound kinds on Issues 67 and 93) |
| **Goal** | A project about becoming: why it matters, milestones and a small regular step. | "Get back into guitar" | Next Steps (Goals, In Detail) |
| **Project** | An objective with tasks, milestones and people. | The wedding; the move | MVP (Projects and Health, In Detail) |

- **"Routine" is not a kind of thing.** In copy it only ever means a repeating task or a procedure ("your packing routine" is a procedure); the assistant never makes "a routine".
- **The test when it isn't clear.** Does it need to become automatic (a habit), or just get done (a repeating task)? The pet's medicine shows both: a daily pill at a set time is a care habit; a flea dose once a month is a repeating task. When Helm can't tell, it asks once, as a pick-one with what each means ("A habit · On Today at its time, counted like 5 of 7. Never overdue." or "A task that comes back · Due each month. If it's missed, it waits until done."), and the answer can be changed later with Undo.
- **Reminders belong to the word.** A habit's reminders are the opt-in ones that fade as it holds (Habits, In Detail); a repeating task simply falls due on Today; a scheduled action never pushes: what it did shows in Helm the next time it is opened (Scheduled Actions, In Detail).

## How It Works



1. **Open Helm and talk.** Home is a conversation: tap once and speak, or type. The assistant answers briefly in a natural voice and keeps listening for your reply until you stop.
2. **It keeps what you said.** One sentence can hold several things; each becomes a task, a fact, a person, a place, a preference or a note, shown right in the conversation with one Undo.
3. **Show it things.** Send a photo, a screenshot, a PDF, a voice note or a Markdown file; it keeps what's useful on the right record, or reads it and lets it go, and says which.
4. **Ask about what you're looking at.** Every page with content has the same "Ask about this" control in the same place ("Ask about Grandpa"); tap it, or say "Hey [name]" if you turned it on, and the assistant knows what's on the page: "move this to Friday", "who's this?", "add a step".
5. **Answer what it can't settle.** When it truly needs you, the assistant asks one question at a time, in the shape that fits (pick one, pick several, yes or no, a few words, a date, a person or a number), answered by tap or voice; skip it and it waits in Needs you.
6. **Know your people.** People, pets and organizations each have a page, a photo if you like, how much they matter to you, their role in each project, and key dates like birthdays. Pick people from Google Contacts to bring them in.
7. **See how it sees you.** "How [name] sees you" lists who you are, what you like, how you write and talk, and (Next Steps) what tends to work for you and what doesn't, each line saying what it came from; change or remove anything.
8. **Keep what you learn.** Knowledge keeps topics as living documents the assistant writes from what you tell it, nested up to three deep (Car care › Washing): read one like a page, see where each part came from, ask about it, change it, and undo any version.
9. **Repeat what works.** A procedure is a routine you run again and again (packing for a trip, before Mom's appointment); each run is ticked off on its own and attached to its trip, event or project.
10. **Group work into projects.** A project has an objective, milestones, tasks, its people in their roles and its procedures, with health stated from evidence, never a percentage.
11. **Connect your apps, if you like.** The assistant can read Google Calendar, Gmail, Drive and the site's other apps, each sharing only what you switch on; Helm changes nothing in them.
12. **Look at your day when you want to.** Today shows your fixed events and a few tasks suggested for real gaps; it is one view among the others, first in Browse, with a quiet line for the week's birthdays and anniversaries.
13. **Ask anything.** Answers come from your own records first, with sources, and from general knowledge only when they don't say, marked "Not from your records".
14. **Undo anything it did, and make it yours.** Activity lists every change; rename the assistant, pick its tone and (Next Steps) the traits you want in it ("blunt and honest", "kind, brief"), and trust your browser so Helm opens offline.
15. **Keep up the small things (Next Steps).** A habit (pills morning and evening, water through the day) is a quiet line on Today, ticked by a tap or a word ("took my pills" ticks the one that's due, no need to say which), and reads as "11 of 13 this week", never a streak.
16. **Form it, then take it off Today (Next Steps).** Turn on reminders while a habit builds; they keep coming whether or not you open Helm, fade as it holds, and stop once it is formed, and a week later Helm offers to take it off Today, keeping its weeks.
17. **Work toward what you want (Next Steps).** A goal is a project about becoming: why it matters (kept in your words, tidied only if you say yes), milestones, and a small regular step kept up as a habit; it reads as showing up and milestones reached.
18. **It learns what works, in the open (Next Steps).** Helm notices when things keep not happening and listens to how you say they went, both ways ("too tired tonight", "that felt easy"); it always answers what you said, then acts at once or says it's noted; it shows what it learned under "What I've noticed" with its evidence, changes what it planned itself with Undo, asks before touching what you set, never moves anything about health, and waits before offering again after a no. What it learns is kept with your things, so a new phone loses nothing.
19. **Say what matters most (Next Steps).** "The wedding matters most, then work"; "Italian can wait." Helm keeps the order in your words, shows it, plans by it, and asks "which comes first?" when too much is tied at the top.
20. **It says when to pause or hold off (Next Steps).** When at least two different reasons, or a deadline that can't fit, say so, a line suggests pausing something or starting it later, with its reasons; you decide.
21. **Ask what it would take (Next Steps).** "Could I drive to Leavenworth on Sunday after I land?" gets what has to be done first and what it would move, from everything Helm knows and reads, including where you and your people are and how each meeting happens; a question you'd rather learn about later is saved and brought back on a quiet evening; a dated project gets its research offered in time.
22. **Hands busy, still answered (Next Steps).** Every question it asks can be answered aloud, "Which one?" included; after asking it listens for a moment, you can talk over it, and if it stopped listening too soon, "Hey [name], the evening ones" answers. Taps always work and are never needed.

### Home: a Conversation, In Detail

The owner's words: "the main view to be a communication interface. Not necessarily a chat UI, but one which leans into voice-first communication."

- **What home is.** One calm surface for talking with the assistant: the current conversation above the voice dock. There is no tab bar.
- **The voice dock, on home only.** Three controls at the bottom of home: **Browse** (opens the views, below), **Talk** (the large centre control: starts listening) and **Type** (opens the field). The dock belongs to home and appears nowhere else. Every other page has Back and a way home; a content page asks through its own built-in "Ask about this" control instead (What's on Screen, In Detail).
- **Browse is tiles, sized by how much they matter.** Browse opens a sheet of tiles pulled up from the dock of mixed sizes, so the views used most are the biggest and the rest stay small:
  - Search across the top.
  - **Big tiles:** **Today** (the widest, with its one-line state, "2 events · 1 planned"), **Needs you** (grows while it has questions, showing the first one; shrinks to a small tile saying "Nothing waiting" when empty), **People** (a few faces and a count) and **Projects** (the next deadline and a count).
  - **Small tiles or a compact row:** Places, Knowledge, Procedures, Coming up and How [name] sees you, each with its count.
  - **The foot row:** Connected apps · Settings · Activity.
  A small tile shows only once its view has something in it. Sizes are fixed by Helm in the MVP, never by the person; a section added later is a small tile, never a big one, so Browse stays calm as Helm grows. Ordering by the person's own use is Next Steps. The exact layout is the design track's.
- **What it shows when idle.** The assistant's mark, a one-line hint ("Tap to talk, or type"), and at most two quiet lines when they are true: the next fixed thing today ("Cake tasting at 3:00 PM") and "2 need you", a door to the Inbox. Nothing else competes with the conversation, and an empty Helm shows only the hint.
- **The conversation, not a log.** Home shows this sitting's turns: what the person said (their words, as heard or typed), what the assistant said, and what it did. Turns from an earlier visit fold under "Earlier today" and then leave home; every turn stays findable in Activity, with what it changed. There is no endless thread to scroll.
- **A reply reads as an answer.** The assistant's words come first ("Added it for Friday.", in the tone the person chose and the way they write), then what it did as one compact card under them, then any question. A turn never reads as a system log.
- **Several messages in a row.** Each message the person sends shows on its own. Messages sent while the assistant is still thinking join the same turn and get one reply that covers them all ("Got all three." then one card with everything it did); a message sent after a reply starts a new turn. Spoken, a pause long enough to end a turn followed by more speech before the reply begins joins the same turn too.
- **What it did, in the conversation.** Each change is one line in the past tense on the reply's card ("Made a task: Call Dana · Fri, Nov 13", "Added Dana to Wedding as key", "Kept the photo on Dana's quote"), each opening its record, with one Undo for the turn. Past 5 lines it shows 5 and "Show 4 more". A question the assistant needs answered shows as the last line ("1 needs you: Which Sam?"), answerable right there.
- **Opening Helm is silent.** The assistant never greets aloud, never speaks first and never starts listening on load (the wake phrase, once chosen, is the one exception, and it only listens). The first thing that happens is the person's tap or words.
- **Typing is always there.** Every turn can be typed; the voice is the easy way in, never the only one. A typed turn gets a written reply (Speaking and Listening, In Detail).
- **Shorthand, dates and limits** work in a typed turn as before (Capture, In Detail).
- **Empty, first time.** "Tell [name] anything: a task, someone's name, something to remember." with Talk and Type in the dock. The first reply ends once with a quiet door: "You can rename [name] and choose how it sounds."

### Speaking and Listening, In Detail

The owner chose a hybrid voice: natural cloud voices online, the phone's own when offline, behind one voice service that can swap providers, with its own budget apart from the assistant's thinking.

- **Tap, then hands-free.** One tap on Talk starts listening (with the soft rising chime the site uses). The person talks; a pause ends their turn; the reply plays; and the assistant listens again for an answer, without another tap. After 8 seconds with nothing said, listening ends on its own; a tap on the lit control ends it at once; and saying so ends it (next).
- **Ending it by saying so.** The owner: "If they say like never mind or forget it… we should be able to detect that and then just close it out. If hands off, just close out the audio." Two kinds of phrase end the conversation, typed or spoken (close variants count too). **Finishers** ("that's all", "that's it", "thanks", "I'm done") send what was said and end it after the reply. **Dismissals** ("never mind", "forget it", "forget about it", "cancel", "stop", "nothing", "no thanks", "leave it") drop what is open and end it at once.
  - **Only when it is the whole turn, or how it ends.** A dismissal at the end ("Actually, never mind") drops what came before it. A finisher after something said ("eggs and milk, that's all") sends the turn as said and ends the conversation once it is answered: listening doesn't reopen. One-word closers ("cancel", "stop", "nothing", "thanks") end it only when they are the whole turn. "Stop suggesting that", "cancel the dentist" or "leave it till Friday" are requests like any other. Helm hears the list on the phone, at once and offline; when the words aren't on it but plainly mean the same ("ugh, doesn't matter"), the assistant reads it as an ending too.
  - **An answer comes first.** While a question is open, a phrase that answers it is its answer: "nothing" or "none" to a pick-several is "None of these", "no thanks" to a yes or no is "No". "Cancel it" with a task's page in view and nothing pending is a request about that task.
  - **"Forget it" never deletes.** Forgetting something needs the thing named ("forget that I like olives", Change It or Look It Up, In Detail).
  - **What closes.** Listening stops and the audio closes: no more chimes, the microphone let go. What was still being made is dropped and nothing is written: a question about a request not yet done, a half-made draft, a confirm waiting for a yes. What was already done in this conversation stays, with its Undo on screen. A question about something already filed (which Sam goes on a task already made) is not dropped: it waits in Needs you, as a skipped one does (Questions, In Detail), since dropping it would leave the task half-filed without a word.
  - **What it says.** Nothing aloud. Hands-free, one soft falling chime, the opposite of the rising one listening starts with, so a person who isn't looking knows the microphone is off. On screen, one quiet line: "Okay, left it", with Undo when a draft was dropped (Undo brings it back as it was); typed, an open question's card folds to "Left it".
  - **"Stop" while it talks.** Stops its voice at once, as before; it also ends the conversation when nothing is waiting on an answer. With a question open, the voice stops, the question stays on screen and listening carries on for the answer.
  - **After it ends.** The wake phrase works as before ("Hey [name]" starts a new conversation); the listening window does not reopen on its own, and a dropped question can't be answered late.
- **Barge-in.** Speaking while the assistant talks stops its voice at once and takes what the person says as the next turn. Whatever it hadn't said yet stays on screen.
- **"Hey [name]", while Helm is open.** A switch in the Voice sheet, off until the person turns it on (the one-time offer on the third spoken conversation, "Say 'Hey [name]' instead of tapping Talk, while Helm is open on your screen · Turn on", is not built yet). On, Helm listens for the phrase only while it is open and on screen, never with the screen off or the tab hidden, and says so in the switch's own words. The phrase counts only at the start of what was heard. Until it is heard, nothing is sent to Helm's voice service: Hear me offline listens for it on the phone once downloaded, and the browser's own recognition until then.
- **Concise by default.** A spoken reply is a sentence or two (about 40 words at most): the answer, not the reasoning. A longer answer is spoken as its short version and shown in full on screen, ending "The rest is on your screen." "Tell me more" reads on. A confirmation is short and says what happened, the way a person would: "Added it for Friday." or "All three are in."
- **Spoken when you spoke.** A turn the person spoke gets a spoken reply and a written one; a typed turn gets a written reply only. Mute, shown at the top while the assistant speaks, silences spoken replies until the person leaves Helm (replies are still written): the control reads "Mute", and while on, "Muted", with one line in the conversation: "Replies muted until you leave Helm. Tap Muted to hear them." Coming back to Helm starts unmuted. While a spoken conversation is going, everything the assistant shows is also said: what it did ("Made a task: call Dana Friday"), questions ("Which Sam: work, or your cousin?") and refusals.
- **Voice follows the turn, on every page.** The owner: "If you speak to it, it speaks to you." It should feel like talking to a person, not a chatty one. Whatever page a turn starts on, the same rule holds: a turn spoken on home, through "Ask about this" on a person's, project's or habit's page, or after "Hey [name]" anywhere in Helm is answered aloud and in writing, on that page, without opening home; a typed turn, wherever it is typed, is answered in writing only. Each turn is judged on its own, so speaking after typing gets a spoken reply and typing after speaking gets a written one. While a spoken conversation is open, tapping one of the assistant's choices ("Work Sam") counts as an answer in that conversation and is answered aloud. Spoken replies keep the same brevity everywhere (a sentence or two, the rest on screen), and Mute holds across pages until the person leaves Helm.
- **It knows your names.** The owner: "if I know someone who probably has an odd name, initially it may be misunderstood, but the agent can see like, oh, okay, this person exists in your contacts." A name the person has told Helm (a person, pet, organization, place or project, and the other names under "Also called") is heard as that name, in three steps, cheapest first:
  - **Told before it listens.** Each time listening starts, the speech recognizer is handed the words it is most likely to get wrong: the person's own names for things, never everyday words. That means people and their other names, pets, places, organizations (an employer, a bakery), the titles of projects, goals, habits, lists, topics and procedures, the wake phrase, the names of connected apps and what they hold that a turn can name (a Pinch recipe or store, a Waypoint trip's city, a Runway group, only while that connection is on), and words the person corrected before. Dates, numbers, units and common words are left out, since recognizers already hear them well. It can take about 100 words, so the list starts with what is on the page in view, then names used in the last 2 weeks, then the ones used most, then the rest, and stops when it is full.
  - **Never a private name.** Only names that aren't private go (the owner's choice). A private person, place or thing and their other names never do, and so no health or care word ever does: medicine names, a clinic marked private. Those are where a mishearing hurts most, so they are matched on the phone first and then by the assistant (Private Things, In Detail), and a reply that ticks a medicine always names it (Habits, In Detail), so a wrong one is seen at once.
  - **Fixed on the phone.** A heard word that sounds like exactly one known name, clearly closer than any other, is shown as that name, with a dotted underline in the words as they appear; a tap shows what was heard, "Heard 'Shivon' · Use what I said".
  - **Settled by the assistant, or asked.** The assistant gets the same names with the turn and settles what is left. When two fit, it asks one question and never picks silently: "Which Siobhan: Kelly or Doyle?" (Questions, In Detail).
  - **A correction is remembered.** "No, Siobhan, S-I-O-B-H-A-N" fixes the turn and keeps how the person said it on Siobhan's record, so it is heard right next time (People, Pets and Organizations, In Detail, "Matching").
  - **Typed too.** A misspelt name in a typed turn ("Shivon") is matched the same way, on the phone and then by the assistant; the reply simply uses the right name, with no underline.
- **Undo by voice.** "Undo that" or "take that back" undoes the last turn's changes, and says what it took back.
- **One voice.** Helm has one voice identity: warm, a natural pace, never over-cheerful, urgent without alarm, concise. Whichever provider speaks, it is set to match. Tone (Your Assistant, In Detail) shapes the words; the voice is the same in every tone. Choosing among voices is Next Steps.
- **"Got it" is instant.** Quick confirmations are spoken by the phone's own voice the moment the change is saved, so nothing waits on the network; answers use the natural cloud voice.
- **Voice offline, on every phone.** "Hear me offline" is a one-time download of a speech recognizer that runs on the phone (about 40–75 MB until it is measured), off until the person turns it on. It is offered once, the first time Talk is tapped with no connection or with the cloud voice unavailable, and always sits in Settings → Voice; it is kept per phone, removable, and offered only where the phone can run it. Once downloaded, Talk works offline, interrupting included, and replies use the site's natural voice (if downloaded too) or the phone's system voice. What needs the assistant's thinking is kept on the device and done once back online: "Saved on this device. [name] will sort it once you're back online."
- **"Hear me offline", word for word.**
  - Row (Settings → Voice): "Hear me offline" · "Talk to [name] with no connection. Runs on this phone."
  - Offer (first Talk with no connection): "Talk without a connection? A one-time download, about 40–75 MB. · Download"
  - Turn-on sheet, title: "Hear me offline"
  - Sheet, what it does: "Your phone turns your speech into words itself, so Talk works with no connection."
  - Sheet, size and where: "A one-time download of about 40–75 MB from [host], kept on this phone only."
  - Sheet, what leaves: "What you say offline stays on this phone until [name] reads it online."
  - Sheet, licence: "Uses [recognizer], open source under [licence]." (plus, if it carries copyleft: "It includes [part], under the GPL.")
  - Sheet, buttons: "Download" · "Not now"
  - Downloading: "Downloading… 23 of 60 MB" (the real size once known)
  - Ready: "Ready. Talk works offline on this phone."
  - Failed: "The download stopped. · Try again"
  - Can't run: "This phone can't run offline listening. Talk needs a connection here."
  - Remove: "Remove from this phone" → "Removed. Talk needs a connection again on this phone. · Undo" (Undo downloads it again)
- **Before the download.** Offline, Talk uses the browser's own recognition where it works without a connection; where it doesn't, Talk offers the download and Type carries on (recording a voice note needs a connection, since its words are written out in the cloud): "Voice needs a connection on this phone until you turn on Hear me offline." Nothing is ever lost.
- **The microphone, on a tap.** The first tap on Talk opens a short sheet before the browser's prompt: what is heard, by whom ("Your speech goes to [voice provider] to be turned into text, with the names you're likely to say so it hears them right, and the replies you hear come from it too. It keeps neither." once the provider's terms say so), and that Helm keeps the words, not the sound. Denied: "The microphone is off for Helm in your browser's settings. You can still type." Asked once, never in a loop.
- **When the voice runs short.** Each person has a daily cloud allowance: 60 minutes of listening and 30,000 characters of speech (a few hundred short replies); the whole site's voice spend is capped at $5 a day. Past either, Helm keeps talking in the phone's own voice and recognition (the offline model if downloaded) and says so once, in the conversation: "Using your phone's voice for the rest of today. It may sound less natural." (Issue 14 builds the listening half: "Listening with this browser for the rest of today." Issue 15 builds the speaking side without the line: past the allowance answers are read by the phone's voice silently; the line arrives with spoken answers, issue 27.) Nothing stops working, and the allowance starts again at midnight in the person's time zone.
- **What is kept.** The words of each turn, sealed, as the source of what they made. The sound of the person's voice is never stored (a voice note they send is the one exception, Sending a Photo, File or Voice Note, In Detail).

### What's on Screen, In Detail

- **Content pages, and the rest.** A content page is one that shows something the person might ask about: a person, pet or organization, a place, a project, a task, a Knowledge topic or note, a procedure or a run, Today, Needs you, the People and Projects lists, Knowledge, "How [name] sees you", Coming up, and an answer's sources. Utility screens (Settings, Connected apps, Activity, Search, and any form or sheet that edits something) have no ask control. Home has the dock.
- **One "Ask about this" control, the same everywhere.** Every content page has the same built-in control, always in the same place on the page and part of its layout, never floating over it, and drawn so it reads as asking the assistant, not as a generic action. Where it sits is the design track's choice; that it never moves between pages is the rule.
- **Its words, per page, exactly:**
  - A person, pet, organization or place: "Ask about Grandpa", "Ask about Miso", "Ask about Sweet Layer Bakery"
  - A project: "Ask about Wedding"
  - A task: "Ask about this task"
  - A Knowledge topic or note: "Ask about this topic", "Ask about this note"
  - A procedure or a run: "Ask about Packing for a trip", "Ask about this run"
  - Today, or another day: "Ask about today", "Ask about Sat, Mar 3"
  - Needs you: "Ask about these 3 questions"
  - A list (People, Projects, a filtered task list, Coming up): "Ask about these 24 people", "Ask about these 6 projects", "Ask about these 12 tasks", "Ask about what's coming up"
  - Knowledge: "Ask about your knowledge"
  - How [name] sees you: "Ask about how [name] sees you"
  - An answer's sources: "Ask about these sources"
  A name too long for the line is cut with an ellipsis ("Ask about Sweet Layer Bak…"); the full name is on the chip.
- **What a tap does.** It opens the conversation about this page, listening at once if the person last talked by voice and with the field open if they last typed (both are always one tap apart). At the top of the conversation sits a chip naming what it is about: "About Grandpa", "About this topic: Washing", "About today", "About 12 tasks". The chip's × removes it ("About anything"), and that turn then carries nothing from the page. "Hey [name]" on a content page opens the same conversation with the same chip.
- **The way back.** Closing the conversation returns to the page, scrolled where it was, with any change already showing there. The close control reads "Back to Grandpa" ("Back to today", "Back to this topic"); swiping the conversation down does the same. The turns stay on home's conversation too.
- **The assistant knows what you're looking at.** Every screen tells the assistant which records it shows: the open task, person, place, project, note or procedure (or the rows in view). Helm's server reads those records itself, under the person's own account, so what the assistant knows is what is really saved. "Move this to Friday" on a task moves that task; "who's this?" on a person's page answers from what Helm knows about them; "add a step" on a procedure adds it to that procedure, with Undo.
- **"This" means what's in view.** On a list, "this" is the row the person last opened or the one on screen if there is only one; when two could be meant: "Which one: Call Dana or Order flowers?" The thing in view is searched first for any question, then the rest of the person's records.
- **Only what the screen shows.** The screen names only the records on it, a few at most, and nothing from another screen; it never sends its own words or anything typed but unsaved. A copied thing from a connected app can be asked about but not changed: "move this to Friday" on a Waypoint activity says "Helm can't change Waypoint. Change it there?" with Open Waypoint.
- **One conversation everywhere.** Asking from a page and talking on home are one conversation: the reply and what changed show where they were asked, with Undo, and the same turns show on home.
- **Asking from a topic.** While a topic is open, a question is answered from that topic first, then the rest of the person's records, then general knowledge marked as such (Knowledge, In Detail).

### Sending a Photo, File or Voice Note, In Detail

The owner's words: "Nomos would know if these should be stored and, if so, how so a user can view them as it relates to any changes. After all, life exists in mediums."

- **What can be sent.** Photos and screenshots, PDFs, voice notes recorded in Helm, and Markdown (`.md`) files, from home or from the conversation on any screen. Video is Next Steps. Up to 5 at once.
- **Said before anything is sent.** The attach control says, before the picker opens: "[name] reads what you send to sort it, and keeps only what's useful. You'll see what it kept."
- **Real input.** A phone photo of any size is shrunk on the device first. A PDF can be up to 20 pages and 10 MB; a longer one is refused when it is picked, with a line naming the cap. A voice note can be up to 5 minutes. A Markdown file is read as text, up to 20,000 characters, like a note. Several files at once are read together as one turn, so a quote's photo and "this is Dana's" make one change.
- **Kept or let go, and said either way.** The assistant decides for each file. Kept: it is stored sealed, on the record it changed ("Kept the photo on Dana's quote"), shown there with "From what you sent on Nov 9", and in the conversation. Let go: "Read the letter. Didn't keep it · Keep it", with a muted line under it, "Deleted in 24 hours unless you keep it." After 24 hours the file is deleted and the line reads "Read the letter. Didn't keep it" with no button. Keep it: "Kept the letter on Mom's care · Undo". What it read always leaves its trace in what it made (a fact's source says "From the letter you sent on Nov 9"). *As built (issue 52):* removing a kept photo, or deleting the task or fact it was on, lets it go for 24 hours like a photo the assistant didn't keep, and Undo keeps it again.
- **What each kind becomes.** A photo of a quote or a letter: facts and tasks, with the photo kept when the person would want to see it again. A photo the person says is of someone ("this is Dana") or of a place: offered as that record's photo, set with Undo. A PDF: its facts and dates, the file kept on the record or project when it is a document worth having (a contract, a discharge summary). A Markdown file: filed into a Knowledge topic (or kept as a loose note), or attached to a record, when it is something to keep; otherwise read like a long typed message. A voice note: turned into words, which become its turn; the sound is let go unless the person taps "Keep the recording".
- **Never recognizes faces.** The assistant never works out who is in a photo from their face; a person's photo is set only when the person says who it is, picks it, or brings it from Google Contacts.
- **Kept media is the person's.** Each kept file shows on its record with Remove (with Undo); "Media" on a record lists all of them. Delete all my Helm data removes every file.
- **When it can't read one.** The reply says "Couldn't read miso-vet-summary.pdf. It may be protected or scanned too faintly.", and the file is let go like any other, so its "Keep it" keeps it as it is, unread. A failed upload keeps the file in the field with Try again.

### Capture, In Detail

A capture is any turn the assistant files: spoken, typed, or sent as a file. These rules hold however it arrives.

- **What a capture really is.** Real input is messy: a run-on spoken sentence, three things in one line ("Mom's birthday is June 3, buy the rings, call Dana Friday"), a pasted vendor email, a half-thought with no verb, a question with a task tacked on. The assistant splits it into as many items as it holds and files each one; the original words stay attached to everything they produced, as their source. A typed capture can be up to 4,000 characters (a long pasted email fits).
- **Shorthand is exact, when typed.** `@`, `#`, `^` and `!` are read by Helm itself, not guessed by the AI: `@Dana` always means a person, pet or organization, `#Sweet Layer Bakery` always a place, `^Packing for a trip` always a procedure or note by that name, `!` always urgent. A project is named in words ("for the wedding"), never by a symbol. Speaking never needs shorthand.
- **Dates are worked out by Helm.** "Friday", "next Tue", "Mar 12", "tomorrow at 3" are read by plain code from today in the person's time zone, never by the AI. "Next Friday" on a Wednesday means the Friday of next week, always the same rule.
- **What Helm read shows before Send.** As the person types, a chip line under the field shows what Helm read for certain (Dana · Fri, Nov 13 · Urgent). Tapping a date chip opens a calendar to fix it; tapping any chip offers "Read as text". The AI only ever adds to this; it never overrides a chip. A spoken turn is sent when the person stops talking; its words show as heard, and what Helm read is in what it did, with Undo.
- **Addressing the assistant is fine.** A turn that opens with the assistant's name ("Nomos, call Dana Friday", or the new name after a rename) drops the address. The name is never read as a person.
- **Reading.** While the assistant works, the turn shows "Thinking…" under the person's words; more messages sent meanwhile join the turn and are answered together (Home: a Conversation, In Detail), each still the source of what it made. A typical turn takes a few seconds; past 15 seconds it adds "Still working. You can leave this; it'll be in your Inbox." Leaving never loses anything.
- **Too much for one turn.** A capture is filed as at most 20 changes. One that holds more files the first 20 and leaves the rest as "Too much to file at once" in Needs you, with "Sort the rest".
- **"Remember that…"** Something true in general rather than about the person's own life ("remember that cakes keep 3 days in the fridge") is filed into the topic it belongs to, or asks where the first time (Knowledge, In Detail), with Undo.
- **Too long.** From 3,600 typed characters a quiet count shows what is left; a paste longer than 4,000 stays in the field with "Too long by 312 characters. Split it into two." "Save as a note" keeps the whole text as a note as written (up to 20,000 characters, read by no AI), so nothing is cut silently.
- **A lot in a short time.** Quick turns ("add milk", "done with the dentist", "thanks") use a lighter model and have their own allowance: 60 in 10 minutes and 400 a day. A question, and turns that plan or file several things, use the fuller model: 20 in 10 minutes and 150 a day. Helm's whole AI spend is capped at $10 a day. Past a 10-minute allowance, a turn is saved as written with "Saved. [name] will sort this in a few minutes." Past a day's allowance (or the site's cap), "Saved. [name] will sort this tomorrow." Spoken, the same words are said. Neither loses anything, and both are sorted on their own when the wait is over and Helm is open. No screen shows a count of what is left.
- **Without AI.** If the AI service is unreachable, the capture is saved as written, with its chips applied: "Saved as written. [name] will sort it when it's back." It waits in the Inbox and is read again the next time Helm opens. After three failed tries it says why in a sentence that names the cause and offers Try again, "Make it a task as written" and Delete.
- **Offline.** A capture made with no connection is kept encrypted on this device: "Saved on this device. [name] will sort it once you're back online." A question asked offline is answered once Helm is back online, and the answer waits in "New since you looked". Signing out with captures still on the device asks first: "2 things you told [name] haven't been saved yet. Sign out anyway?", with Stay as the safe choice.
- **An unsent turn survives.** Text typed but not sent is still in the field when the person comes back, even after the phone closes the app.
- **Couldn't read it.** Words the assistant can't make sense of (a bare link, a fragment, a mumble) are never filed on a guess. Spoken, it asks once: "Sorry, I didn't catch that." Typed, it waits in Needs you as "Couldn't read this", with "Make it a task as written", "Add more and try again" and Delete.

### The Inbox, In Detail

- **Two parts.** "Needs you" (questions the assistant won't guess) at the top, newest first, then "New since you looked" (turns whose results the person hasn't seen, such as one sorted after being offline). Home's "2 need you" line counts Needs you only and opens the Inbox.
- **Asked in the conversation too.** A question a turn raises is asked right there, aloud if the turn was spoken; left unanswered, it waits in Needs you. "What do you need from me?" reads the waiting questions one at a time. The assistant never brings one up on its own in a later conversation.
- **The questions.** Any of the shapes in Questions, In Detail; the ones Helm builds itself keep their fixed wording:
  - *Which person?* "Lunch with Sam Thursday" when Memory has two Sams: each candidate with the one detail that tells them apart ("Work · Design team", "Cousin"), then "Someone new" and "Don't link anyone".
  - *Which is true now?* A new capture contradicts a fact ("Dana says centerpieces are $2,100 now" against $1,800): both with their sources and dates, nothing preselected. The one not picked is kept as "Was $1,800 until Nov 8". "Neither" opens Correct.
  - *Which day?* Only when the words hold two dates Helm can't choose between ("the 3rd or the 10th"): the two as choices, plus a calendar.
  - *Couldn't read this*, *Couldn't sort this*, *Too much to file at once*: as in Capture, In Detail.
- **Answering.** One tap or one spoken answer (or Done, for several) answers and files, with Undo, and the question leaves Needs you. Questions never expire and are never answered by default.
- **Seen.** A turn leaves "New since you looked" once its result was seen or the person taps "Mark all seen" (shown at 2 or more). It stays in Activity and on each record it made.
- **Empty.** "Nothing waiting. [name] asks here when it isn't sure."

### Questions, In Detail

The owner's words: "Agent should be able to ask different types of questions, like most chats today. For example, multi-select or free response."

- **When it asks, and when it acts.** Inside Helm the assistant organizes freely and shows what it did with Undo; anything that would leave Helm is proposed, never done. A question is only for what it can't settle itself, where a guess would file something wrong: which of two people, which of two facts is true now, which day, which of several things the words point to, or a preference it needs to make what was asked for ("Which of these should the packing procedure include?"). It never asks to confirm something it can just do and undo, never asks what Helm's records already say, and never asks to make conversation.
- **One at a time when spoken.** In a spoken conversation it asks one question, waits for the answer, then asks the next. On screen a turn shows at most 3 questions; any more wait in Needs you with "2 more questions in Needs you".
- **The shapes:**
  - **Pick one** (2 to 6 options), with "Something else" opening a short answer when the options may not cover it.
  - **Pick several** (2 to 8 options), with "None of these" and Done.
  - **Yes or no.**
  - **A few words** (a short answer, typed or spoken, up to 500 characters).
  - **A date or a time**, with a calendar or time picker; a spoken "next Friday" is read by Helm's code like any date.
  - **A person, place or project**, from the person's own records, with "Someone new" (or "New place", "New project") at the end.
  - **A number**, with its unit when there is one ("How many guests? · people").
- **Who writes the words.** The assistant may write a question and its options, which Helm checks and caps (6 or 8 options, 80 characters each) before showing. Questions Helm builds itself ("Which Sam?", "Which is true now?", "Which day?") keep their fixed wording in every tone.
- **Word for word.**
  - None of these (pick several): "None of these". Picking it clears the other ticks.
  - Something else (pick one): "Something else", which opens a field: "Tell [name] what you mean".
  - Answer later (every shape): "Answer later". After it: "Saved for later. It's waiting in Needs you." Spoken the same way.
  - Done (pick several): "Done", reading "Done · 2" once two are ticked; with nothing ticked it reads "Pick at least one, or None of these" when tapped.
  - Spoken, pick several with two options: "Which of these? Cake tasting, venue walk, or both?"
  - Spoken, pick several with more: "Which of these? Cake tasting, venue walk or florist call. You can say more than one."
  - Spoken, pick one: "Which one: Cake tasting or venue walk?"
  - Spoken, more than 4 options: the first 3, then "or one of 3 more on your screen."
  - Spoken, yes or no: the question as written; "Yes or no?" is added only when the question doesn't already make it plain.
  - Spoken, a few words: the question, then listening, with no prompt word.
- **Answering by voice.** An option's name, "the first", "the second one", "the first two", "both", "all of them", "none of these", "yes", "no", a date, a number, "skip". An answer that fits none of the options is asked once more ("Sorry, which one? Cake tasting or venue walk?"), then the question waits on screen and in Needs you.
- **Skipped or left unanswered.** It waits in Needs you, never expires and is never answered by default; nothing it would have changed is done in the meantime. Ending the conversation ("never mind", Speaking and Listening, In Detail) is not a skip: a question about a request not yet done is dropped with the request, and one about something already filed waits here like a skipped one.
- **Recorded, with Undo.** Each answer is kept with its question in Activity ("Asked: Which of these? · You picked Cake tasting and Venue walk"), and what the answer changed has one Undo, like any turn.

### Asking a Question, In Detail

- **Your records first.** "When is the cake tasting?" is answered from Helm's tasks, facts, people, preferences, notes, procedures and what your connected apps share, with each source shown (a filled ring for what you said, a hollow one for what was inferred, the book mark for a note, with "not checked" on one saved from a general answer, a connected app's logo for what came from it) and a door to the record. An inferred source is named in the answer itself ("[name] inferred this from what you told it on Nov 3"). When two of your records disagree, both are shown with their dates and neither is picked. On a screen, the thing in view is looked at first (What's on Screen, In Detail).
- **Spoken answers are short.** Spoken, the answer is its first sentence or two and its main source in a few words ("Today at 3, at Sweet Layer Bakery. From your calendar."); the full answer and every source show on screen.
- **Then your mail and documents.** With Gmail or Drive connected, a question Helm's records don't fully answer and that mail or a document plausibly could ("what did Dana quote?") is also searched there. Each message or file used is cited by sender or name, date and account, with "Open in Gmail" or "Open in Drive", and the answer ends with what was searched: "Searched your records and your mail (personal)." A search that finds nothing says so, then falls through to general knowledge only for questions about the world.
- **General knowledge only when your records don't say.** A question about the world ("how long does a cake keep?") that nothing in Helm or your connected apps answers gets a general answer in its own block under the label **Not from your records**, with one line of caution: "From general knowledge, not checked against anything you've told Helm. It may be out of date." Spoken, the answer begins "This isn't from your records, and it may be out of date." and then gives the answer; the full caution stays on screen. Helm doesn't search the web.
- **Never in one sentence.** Records, connected apps, mail and documents are all sourced, so one sentence may draw on several of them, each cited. General knowledge is the one kept apart: when records answer part of a question and general knowledge the rest, the answer is two blocks, and no sentence draws on both. A general block that differs from one of the person's notes says so on its own line ("Your note 'Cake storage' says 5 days.").
- **Health and medicine carry a stronger caution.** A general answer about symptoms, medicines, doses or treatment says instead: "General information, not medical advice. Check with a doctor or pharmacist before acting on it." Spoken, it begins "This isn't from your records, and it isn't medical advice." and ends "Check with a doctor or pharmacist before acting on it." Neither caution changes with tone, and neither names a person from Memory.
- **Questions about your own things never fall back.** "What's Dana's number?" or "when is Mom's follow-up?" can only be answered from records and connected apps; with nothing there it says "Nothing in Helm says when Mom's follow-up is." and never guesses.
- **An answer is not a record.** A general answer is never stored as a fact or a note on its own. Under it, "Save to my knowledge" keeps it (Knowledge, In Detail); otherwise it lives only in Activity.
- **A question writes almost nothing.** An answer has no Undo, with one exception: when a mail or Drive search finds something that disagrees with an event, the answer notes it on the event as one fact, with Undo ("Noted the 2:30 PM on Cake tasting"). A turn that mixes a question and things to file gets both.
- **When it can't answer yet.** With the AI unreachable, the question is kept ("Saved. [name] will answer when it's back.") and its answer joins "New since you looked" once it comes.

### What the Assistant Does on Its Own, In Detail

- **Inside Helm, it acts and tells.** Creating, linking, tagging and moving tasks, people, pets, organizations, places, facts, preferences, projects, roles and procedures, adding to a note, setting a photo the person named, and keeping or letting go of a sent file happen straight away and show as one line with Undo, in the conversation and in Activity.
- **Nothing leaves Helm in the MVP.** Helm only reads its connections and changes nothing in them; the only thing that leaves is what the person copies with their own tap. Proposals that would leave Helm, approved one by one, start in Next Steps. Acting outside Helm without asking (levels 3 and 4) is a Stretch goal, opt-in per kind of action.
- **Never speaks first, never interrupts.** The assistant answers when spoken or typed to. It may offer a plan, a procedure or a Pause or Hold Off line, but only where the person will look (Today, a task, an event, a project); it opens no conversation and says nothing aloud unprompted. The one push is a habit reminder the person turned on (Next Steps), which names no habit unless the person chose to show that habit's name, and fades as the habit holds (Habits, In Detail).
- **Never shows an attempt as done.** A change is shown (and spoken) as done only after it is saved. A failed one says so in words and keeps what was said.
- **Undo is honest about later changes.** Undo re-reads what is there now. A single record changed since ("You edited this task after [name] made it") is kept and the Undo says so. Undo for a whole turn goes on past the records changed since and reports them: "Took back 1 task and 2 links. Kept 1 task you changed since." Each Undo adds its own line to Activity; the original is marked undone, never removed. An undone change can't be undone again.
- **Every offer can be declined, and a no is remembered.** A suggested plan slot, procedure, link or role has "Not now", and declined for one thing (this event's run, this role for Dana) it is not offered again for that thing in the MVP. In Next Steps every offer Helm makes, from a plan slot to a pattern, the offer to take a formed habit off Today or a Pause or Hold Off line, follows one back-off: a no waits 4 weeks, a second no 8, a third no ends it unless the person asks, and once a wait is over it comes back only on new evidence gathered after the no and as strong as before; "Stop suggesting that" ends it at once (What Helm Learns, In Detail). A Not now given before then counts as one no.

- **Tone.** Four choices, each shown (playing it aloud is not built yet) with the same sample answer before saving (a fixed sample, no AI call):
  - **Composed** (the default): plain, complete sentences. "Your cake tasting is today at 3:00 PM at Sweet Layer Bakery."
  - **Warm**: friendlier, a few more words. "Your cake tasting is today at 3:00 PM at Sweet Layer Bakery. Hope it's a good one."
  - **Brief**: the fewest words. "Cake tasting: today, 3:00 PM, Sweet Layer Bakery."
  - **Formal**: measured and courteous, like a seasoned butler. "The cake tasting is this afternoon at 3:00 PM, at Sweet Layer Bakery."
- **What tone changes.** Only what the assistant says or writes freely: answers (spoken and written; a general answer's label and caution stay fixed), and the intro line on a procedure or note it drafts. Every tone is concise when spoken.
- **What tone never changes.** Facts, names, dates, times and amounts; the words of anything it files; result lines, sources and "You said" / "[name] inferred"; questions; Undo, failure and confirm wording; buttons, chips, labels and empty states; the voice itself; what it does, how much it suggests and what it asks before acting. No tone adds emoji, exclamation marks, cheerleading or praise.
- **Personality (Next Steps).** The owner's words: the person gives the assistant its characteristics, including how honest it may be. On top of the tone, the person picks or says up to 5 traits: a closed set shown as chips (Blunt, Gentle, Kind, Warm, Playful, Dry, Brief, Detailed, Formal, Casual, Encouraging, Curious) and their own words, read into the nearest of them ("like a coach who doesn't sugar-coat it" reads as Blunt and Encouraging, said back so it can be changed). Said in a turn ("be more blunt with me") it is set the same way, said back with Undo.
  - **How honest.** One line of its own, since it matters most, with three levels: "Tactful · honest, but softened" · "Straight · plainly, without cushioning" · "Unvarnished · even when it's not what I want to hear". It shapes how plainly the assistant says a plan won't fit, a habit isn't holding or an idea has a problem; it never changes a fact, a count or a source, and never hides something the person should know.
  - **When two pull against each other.** Picking traits that clash (Blunt with Gentle, Brief with Detailed, Playful or Casual with Formal, Dry with Warm, and Unvarnished honesty with Gentle) shows one line under them, never a refusal: "Blunt and Gentle pull against each other. When they clash, which wins?" with each, and the choice is kept ("Blunt, gently put"). Saved anyway without choosing, the later trait wins and the line stays. Said in a turn, the same question comes as a pick-one, answerable aloud.
  - **See it before you keep it.** The sheet shows the same fixed sample as the tones, rewritten for the traits chosen, and one harder one (a plan that won't fit) so honesty shows: "Saturday won't hold all three. The ceremony draft is the one to move." Changes apply from the next reply.
  - **What it never changes.** Everything listed under "What tone never changes", and the rules about health (the strong caution stays), about what it does and asks before acting, and about praise: no trait adds cheerleading, emoji or exclamation marks. A trait that would need them ("hype me up") is kept as "Encouraging" and says so.
  - **Seen with the rest.** Traits are listed in "How [name] sees you" under "How you like answers", said by the person (filled ring), and sync with everything else.
- **From now on.** A new name or tone applies to what is said next; an answer already given keeps its words.

### People, Pets and Organizations, In Detail

The owner's words: "not all are equal… there'll be key people for a project and others which just come up", and "how do we store pets and other identities which aren't necessarily people".

- **One kind of record for "who".** A person, a pet or an organization (an insurer, an employer, a clinic you deal with) is one record with its kind, a name, how it relates to the person ("sister", "florist", "our cat", "Mom's insurer"), how to reach it, an optional photo, its facts and its projects. `@` finds any of them. Groups and households are Next Steps.
- **Also called.** The owner: "give people nicknames… or based on family relationships." A who can have other names ("Bubs", "Grandpa Joe", "Dr. P"), shown as an "Also called" row on their page with Add and Remove on each. Every place a name is found uses them: Search, `@`, the matching below, and listening (Speaking and Listening, In Detail, "It knows your names"). A name the person uses that Helm doesn't know yet, which the assistant thinks means someone, is asked once and saved only on a yes: "Is Bubs Eli?" · "Yes" · "No" · "Someone new"; No leaves the turn as written and doesn't ask about that name for that person again.
- **Family words.** Beside the "how they relate" words, a who can have a family word from a fixed list: mother, father, parent, sister, brother, sibling, son, daughter, child, partner, spouse, grandmother, grandfather, grandparent, grandchild, aunt, uncle, cousin, niece, nephew, the in-law and step forms of these, and, where it fits, older, younger, oldest, youngest or twin. It is filled when the person says it ("my brother Eli") or picks it on the page ("Family · Younger brother", Change and Remove), and the order is kept as said ("my younger brother Eli" needs no dates). Older and younger mean compared with the person; oldest and youngest mean among those brothers or sisters. Only when nothing was said does Helm work it out from birthdays it already holds: older or younger needs the person's own birthday with a year (said, "I was born March 3, 1992", or set on their own page) and that relative's; oldest or youngest needs only the siblings' years. It never asks for a birthday just to work out an order. The assistant suggests the word; Helm's code accepts only words on the list.
- **Pets are who, not what.** A pet has a name, species and birthday, and its facts (its vet, its food, its medicine) like anyone. A Nine Lives cat, once connected, shows its app's details on the same page when the names match for sure, with both sources; the Nine Lives part stays read only.
- **An organization or a place.** A place is somewhere the person goes (`#Sweet Layer Bakery`); an organization is someone they deal with that isn't a person (`@Blue Shield`). One may link to the other ("Sweet Layer Bakery · place" on the organization's page); the assistant doesn't turn one into the other.
- **How much each matters.** Each who has one of three levels:
  - **Close**: the people who matter always (family, partner, closest friends, a pet). Listed first everywhere, and the first match when a name is ambiguous.
  - **Regular**: people the person deals with (a florist, a doctor, a coworker). Listed normally.
  - **Passing**: someone who just came up ("the guy at the tire shop"). Kept with their facts and findable in Search and answers, but left out of People lists, folded under "Passing (12)", until they matter.
  - New people start Passing when only mentioned, and Regular when the person says how they relate or gives a way to reach them; the assistant may suggest Close from family words ("my sister"), shown as inferred until the person confirms it. A Passing person becomes Regular, with a line and Undo, when any one of these happens: they come up three times on at least two different days, a task is linked to them, they get a Key or Involved role in a project (a Mentioned role leaves them Passing), or a date that comes back (a birthday, an anniversary) is kept for them. The person can change any level on the page or by saying so ("Maya is close").
- **Roles in a project.** On a project, each who has a role: **Key** (the people the project depends on: Dana the florist for the Wedding), **Involved** (part of it), **Mentioned** (came up once). A project's page lists Key first with their photos. "She's key for the wedding" on Dana's page sets it. A who can be in many projects, with a different role in each.
- **Photos.** A person, pet, organization or place can have a photo (several for a place): picked from the phone or camera, kept from something sent ("this is Dana"), or brought from Google Contacts. Kept sealed like everything else. A who without one shows their initials.
- **Matching.** `@Dana` or "Dana" links to an existing who only when there is exactly one sure match (a Close one wins a tie with a Passing one only when the others are Passing); otherwise the Inbox asks. Other names count as names ("Bubs" finds Eli). A family word finds people too, typed or spoken: "this is for my younger brother" finds the brother who is younger, uses him and names him in the reply ("Added for Eli: …"); when two fit it asks one question naming them, "Which brother: Eli or Marcus?"; when it can't tell, it asks the order itself once ("Is Eli younger than you?"), or, with several brothers whose order it doesn't know, one pick-several question ("Which of your brothers are younger than you?", everyone not picked kept as older), and keeps the answer; with no brother known it asks who, as a search with "Add your brother" (it asks for the name). A private who is found the same way. A name heard or typed a little wrong ("Shivon") still finds Siobhan when she is clearly the closest by sound and spelling (Speaking and Listening, In Detail, "It knows your names"). A correction the person makes is kept on the record as how the name sounds, sealed like the rest, and shows in the ⋯ name sheet as "Also heard as: Shivon", each with Remove; Forget removes them with the record.
- **Where they live, and where they are (Next Steps).** Each who can have a home (a city, or a place Helm knows) and a temporary location with dates ("Priya is in Portland until Sunday"); see Where You and Your People Are, In Detail.
- **Forgetting.** The confirm names what goes: "Forget Dana Ruiz? The 6 facts and 1 photo go too; 3 tasks stay, with no person." A who with care habits kept for them names those too, and forgetting ends them with their reminders: "Forget Mom? Her 2 care habits end and their reminders stop; 4 facts go too." An ended habit folds under "Ended" with its ticks. A thing that was private because it belonged to them stays private (Private Things, In Detail). Merging two records is Next Steps; a duplicate is fixed by Undo on the turn that made it.

### Where You and Your People Are, In Detail



**Next Steps.** The owner's words: each person, the owner and everyone in People, has a home and, now and then, somewhere else for a while; "nearby" is worked out from these.

- **A home.** The person's own ("I live in Seattle") and each who's ("Priya and Sam live in Leavenworth"), said once or set on the page: a city, or a place Helm already knows. Kept as a fact with its source like any other; a different one said later is a contradiction question. The cities and places in homes and times away are among the names listening expects, so "Leavenworth" or "Cascade Orchard" said aloud is heard as itself (Speaking and Listening, In Detail, "It knows your names").
- **Somewhere else for a while.** A temporary location with dates: "I'm in Portland this weekend", "Mom is at my sister's until the 20th". It ends on its last day on its own; "I'm back" ends it early.
- **Away in another time zone.** Habits, reminders, the day turning over and anything Helm does at a set time keep to Your day's time zone. When the person says they'll be somewhere in another zone for a span ("I'm in Lisbon Dec 2 to 9"), the reply carries one offer row, once for that span: "Use Lisbon time Dec 2–9" (those days follow Lisbon's clock, then home time comes back on its own; Undo) and, when something runs at home on a schedule, "Pause what runs at home (groceries on Sunday)" (Scheduled Actions, In Detail), which pauses every run that falls in those days, an action made while away included. Either can be said. It is not a travel mode: only that span changes, and only what was chosen; nothing chosen leaves home time.
- **Read from what Helm already has.** Where the person will be also comes from their calendar (an event's place, once Google Calendar shares it) and their trips (a Waypoint trip's places and days), each named as the source: "In Portland until Sun, Nov 15 · from Waypoint". A Helm event's place counts the same (How and Where a Meeting Happens, In Detail). When two disagree, the newer thing the person said wins, and the other is shown beside it.
- **"Nearby".** Two cities are near when they are within 50 km of each other, or the person has said one is close ("the orchard is just outside Leavenworth"). Helm says why it thinks so: "You'll be in Leavenworth on Monday, where Priya and Sam live." With Precise on, it can name a straight-line distance to a place the person names ("You're about 2 km from the pharmacy"); it never claims a drive time.
- **The phone's location, the person's choice, offered only when a request needs it.** Three settings, Off by default: **Off** (only what you tell me, your calendar and your trips), **City only** (the city you're in today, nothing finer) or **Precise** (where you are, so "nearby" can mean a few streets). Helm never asks up front. The assistant knows which setting is on and whether the phone has said no, and when something the person asks would be answered better with a finer location than they allow, or they say they'd like it used ("use where I am"), its reply answers with what it has and carries one button for the setting that would help: "Use this phone's location · City only" or "· Precise", with a line on what it keeps. Only a tap turns it on, since the phone asks its own permission only then; the assistant can't turn it on itself. Not now hides that button for 4 weeks unless the person asks again. When the phone has said no, the line says how to allow it in the phone's settings instead of a button. City only keeps the city as a temporary location for the day; Precise keeps the position, sealed, for at most 6 hours, and neither is kept as a history of where the person went. Changed any time in Settings → Helm on its own.
- **Seen and changed.** On each page, "Lives in Leavenworth" and "In Portland · until Sun, Nov 15", each with its source, Change and Remove; the person's own on "How [name] sees you" under Who you are.

### Key Dates, In Detail

The owner's words: "in order to have a timeline for the user and for their people, I think we should store key dates", then in round 6d: "I don't think they're going to scale for a user's lifetime." So key dates come in two kinds, kept and shown differently: a few dates that come back every year, and an open-ended run of moments that belong to a timeline.

- **Dates that come back.** **Birthday**, **Anniversary** and **Custom yearly** ("Gotcha day", "Sobriety date"). A who has a few at most. They feed Today and Coming up, and answers ("When is Maya's birthday?"). The year may be unknown: a birthday can be just "March 3".
- **Moments.** **Moved**, **Started a job**, **Left a job**, **Graduated**, **Adopted**, **Met**, **Passed away** and **Custom one-time** ("Ran the Chicago marathon"). There is no limit to how many a life holds. They never show on Today or in Coming up; they are the record a life timeline will read (Next Steps). A moment can be a full date, a month and year, or a year alone ("Moved to Denver · 2009").
- **Who has them.** People, pets and organizations ("Sweet Layer Bakery · Opened · 2015"), and the person themself, whose own show on "How [name] sees you" under Who you are, in the same shape as a who page.
- **How they get in.** Said in conversation ("Maya's birthday is March 3", "we adopted Miso in June 2023", "Grandpa moved to Tucson in the 80s"), filed with Undo: "Noted Maya's birthday: March 3", "Noted a moment for Miso: Adopted · June 2023". A relative date is worked out by Helm's code, as other dates are; one that can only be pinned to a month, a year or a decade is kept that way ("the 80s" is kept as 1980s) and shown that way, never made more exact. Picked people from Google Contacts bring their birthday. Each date keeps its source and is said or inferred like any fact; a date that disagrees with one already kept is the usual "Which is true now?" question.
- **On a who page, word for word.** One "Key dates" section:
  - Heading: "Key dates"
  - The dates that come back, soonest first:
    - "Birthday · March 3, 1994 · turns 32 on Tue"
    - "Birthday · March 3" with a muted "Year not known · Add year"
    - "Anniversary · June 20 · 1 year on Jun 20"
    - "Gotcha day · June 4"
  - A muted divider "Moments", then the latest 3, newest first:
    - "Started a job · Jan 2025 · Northwind"
    - "Moved · Aug 2024 · to Denver"
    - "Graduated · 2016"
  - Then "See all 14 moments" when there are more than 3.
  - Add: "Add a date"
  - Empty: one "Add a key date" row (tell [name] a birthday or a moment, or add one there)
  - A row opens a small sheet with its source, Edit, Remove (with Undo) and, for a date that comes back, "Show on Today" (on by default for Birthday, Anniversary and Custom yearly). Adding or changing a date that comes back by hand needs a connection, and the sheet says "Needs a connection to save"; a date said in a turn while offline waits with the turn.
- **All moments, word for word.** "See all 14 moments" opens a page titled "Grandpa's moments", grouped by decade, then year, newest first:
  - Decade heading: "2020s", "2010s", "1980s"
  - Year heading under it: "2024", "2016"; a moment known only to its decade sits under the decade with no year heading, last ("Sometime in the 1980s")
  - Row: "Aug 12 · Moved to Denver", "Jan · Started at Northwind", "Graduated" (year only)
  - Each decade shows its count and folds after the newest one: "1990s · 3" opens on a tap.
  - Empty: "No moments yet. Tell [name] about a move, a new job or another milestone."
  - It has "Ask about Grandpa's moments" like any content page.
- **Coming up, word for word.** One view for everyone's dates that come back, reached from Today's "+2 more this week" and from a small tile in Browse:
  - Title: "Coming up"
  - Subtitle: "Birthdays, anniversaries and other dates that come back, for the next 12 months."
  - Month headings: "November", "December", then "January 2027" once the year turns.
  - Row: "Thu, Nov 12 · Maya's birthday · turns 32", "Fri, Nov 20 · Your anniversary with Maya · 5 years", "Thu, Dec 3 · Miso's gotcha day · 3 years", "Tue, Dec 8 · Dana Ruiz's birthday" (no year known, so no age)
  - Today and tomorrow say so: "Today · Maya's birthday · turns 32"
  - Close people first within a day, then regular; passing people's dates show only behind "Show everyone" at the top.
  - Search from 8 rows; a month with more than 20 dates shows 20 and "Show 14 more in March".
  - Empty: "No birthdays or anniversaries yet. Tell [name] one, or add it on someone's page."
- **On Today, word for word.** Under the summary, at most 3 lines for dates that come back in the next 7 days, a date today or tomorrow first whoever it belongs to, then close people (and pets and the person themself), then regular, soonest first within each; passing people never:
  - "Maya's birthday · Sat, Nov 14", "· tomorrow", "· today"
  - With a known year: "Maya turns 32 · Thu, Nov 12"
  - "Your anniversary with Maya · Fri, Nov 20 · 5 years"
  - "Miso's gotcha day · Thu, Dec 3"
  - More: "+2 more this week", opening Coming up at this week.
  It is a view: never spoken, never a push, and the assistant never brings one up on its own; "anything coming up this week?" reads them.
- **In answers.** "When is Maya's birthday?" gets "Saturday, November 14." with its source; "how old is Grandpa?" uses a known year or says "Nothing in Helm says what year Grandpa was born."; "when did Grandpa move to Tucson?" answers from moments ("In the 1980s, from what you said on Nov 9."). A date with no year never gets an age.
- **No pronouns.** Copy names the person and never guesses a pronoun ("Grandpa · 3 years since passing", never "since he passed").
- **February 29.** A Feb 29 date that comes back shows on Feb 28 in years without one.
- **At 2,000 people and decades.** The design holds for about 2,000 who and 20,000 dates: a who page shows a few yearly dates and 3 moments, never the whole run; the moments page folds by decade; Coming up covers only dates that come back, a year ahead, grouped by month with its own cap per month; Today shows 3. No screen lists every date at once, and nothing slows as moments pile up.
- **Later.** Moments are what the life timeline and milestones for the person and their people will read (Next Steps); nothing in the MVP draws a timeline.

### Preferences, Favorites and Who You Are, In Detail

The owner's words: "store user preferences for different topics (stores, brands, gas station) and favorites (favorite movies). Maybe this is fully accounted for under Facts?" It is: they are facts about the person themself, with a kind and a topic.

- **Three kinds of facts about you.** **Who you are** ("vegan", "lives in Seattle", "works in product design", "allergic to penicillin"); **preferences** by topic ("Groceries: Costco for bulk, Trader Joe's for the rest", "Gas: the Shell on Main", "Coffee: oat milk"); **favorites** ("Movies: Arrival, Spirited Away"). Each has its source and is said or inferred, like any fact.
- **How they get in.** Said outright ("I'm vegan", "my favorite movie is Arrival") they are confirmed. Inferred from what the person does or says ("you've mentioned Costco four times for bulk") they are shown as inferred until confirmed. A favorite can come from a connected app's category the person switched on (A-List's movies you rated).
- **Never inferred: the sensitive things.** Health, religion, politics, sexuality and similar are kept only when the person says them about themself, never inferred from anything.
- **Where they're used.** In answers and suggestions ("a vegan bakery near the venue" draws on "vegan"), cited like any fact. A preference never changes what Helm does on its own; it only informs. A pattern about when things work (Tuesday evenings don't) is not a preference: it is kept under What I've noticed and may shape what Helm itself plans (What Helm Learns, In Detail).
- **Change and remove.** On "How [name] sees you", every one can be corrected, confirmed or removed, with Undo; a removed one is never brought back by an old turn.

### Tasks, In Detail



A task is a one-off thing to do. Steps run again and again are a procedure (Procedures, In Detail); a job that falls due on a cadence is a repeating task (Next Steps, Issue 70); something you want to become automatic is a habit (Words We Use).

- **What a task carries.** Title, project, importance and urgency (kept apart: something can matter without being due), deadline (a day, or a day and time), how long it takes, energy (low, medium, high), context (at home, out, at a computer, on the phone), a place, a who, what it waits on (another task or a person), notes, kept media, and steps. Only the title is needed.
- **Steps.** A task can have a few steps of its own ("Order flowers": pick the arrangement, confirm the count, pay the deposit), ticked as they go, shown as "2 of 3". Steps belong to that task only; when the same steps keep coming back, the assistant offers to make them a procedure.
- **Not yet.** A task can wait for a condition: "not until Apr 1", "after the venue is booked". Until then it stays out of Today and out of counts of what is open; a project shows such tasks folded under "Not yet (3)", and each appears on its own once its condition is met.
- **Waiting on someone.** "Waiting on Dana" shows on the task with "Dana got back to me", which clears it by hand. If a task it waits on is done or deleted, the wait ends by itself.
- **Repeating things.** Repeating tasks are not in the MVP. "Water the plants every Monday" makes one task for the next Monday and says "Repeating tasks aren't in Helm yet. Made one for Mon, Nov 9." In Next Steps, a repeating task is for something that falls due and can be late (water the plants every Monday, renew the license every two years); something kept up rather than finished (pills, water, practice) is a habit and never overdue (Habits, In Detail).
- **Done and gone.** Ticking a task done (or saying "done with the dentist") shows Undo on the line. A done task leaves the lists and stays findable in its project and in Search. "Clear done tasks" on a project's list removes done tasks from view in one action, with the count it cleared.
- **Overdue.** A task past its deadline says "Was due Thu, Nov 5" in the warning colour and leads its project's list; it is never moved or re-dated silently. Today offers it first.
- **Delete.** A task deletes from its page with a confirm and Undo; its plan slots go with it, and a past day still shows it as it was.

### Projects and Health, In Detail

- **Starting one.** By saying so ("start a project for the move") or from Projects' "New project": a name, an optional emoji, a one-sentence objective, an optional target date. Projects shows nothing until the first exists.
- **What a project holds.** An objective in a sentence, milestones with dates, its people in their roles (Key first, with photos), its tasks (overdue first, then by deadline, then undated, then "Not yet"), its facts, its procedures and their runs, and its kept media. Built so far: the objective, the target date, its milestones, its people and its tasks; facts, procedures and media on a project come later.
- **People in a project.** "Dana is key for the wedding" or "add Maya to the move" links them with a role (People, Pets and Organizations, In Detail); a who linked to a task in the project is added as Involved, with a line and Undo.
- **Health is evidence, not a score.** A project says what it can show, at most two parts in this order: what is overdue, the next dated open task ("Pick the ceremony readings in 20 days"), how long nothing has been done ("Nothing done in 12 days · 3 open"), and what a key person still has open. With no tasks it reads "No tasks yet"; with open tasks and no dates, "3 open, no dates yet"; with dated open tasks and nothing overdue it says "On track: nothing overdue" when no part applies. With every task done: "Everything is done. Finish this project?" It never shows a percentage, a progress bar or a colour grade. Only "overdue" takes the warning colour.
- **Finishing.** "Mark finished" moves it behind "Finished (3)" and asks nothing; the toast says how many tasks are still open and carries Undo. Open tasks stay open, in Today and the lists, and Reopen (on the finished bar and in its ⋯) puts the project back.
- **Deleting.** The confirm names what happens: "Delete Wedding? Its 14 tasks and 2 procedures stay, with no project; its people keep their pages." Undo follows.
- **What matters most (Next Steps).** A project or goal the person has said matters carries one quiet line under its name with its place in their order ("Comes first · you said Nov 8", "After the wedding", "Can wait"); nothing is shown for one they haven't placed (What Matters Most, In Detail).
- **A goal is a project about becoming (Next Steps).** Same record and view, under its own "Goals" divider, with a why, milestones and a small regular step; it reads as showing up and milestones reached rather than as health, and is set aside (paused, with its habits) rather than finished when the person lets it rest (Goals, In Detail). A project or goal can also be set aside until a date from a Pause or Hold Off line, and comes back on its own on that date (Pause or Hold Off, In Detail).

### Memory and Sources, In Detail

- **What Helm remembers.** People, pets and organizations (People, Pets and Organizations, In Detail), places with their photos, facts ("Dana is the florist", "the venue holds 120", "Mom takes metoprolol 25 mg"), facts about the person themself (Preferences, Favorites and Who You Are, In Detail) and, in Knowledge, notes. Each has its own view one tap from home (People, Places, Knowledge), shown once its first item exists; "How [name] sees you" holds the facts about the person.
- **A fact or a note.** A fact is about the person's own life: someone, somewhere or something they are working on, or themself. A note is general knowledge they chose to keep, true for anyone. The assistant files "the venue's wifi password is…" as a fact about the venue and "remember that buttercream softens above 75°F" in a note; the result line says which, with Undo.
- **Every fact says where it came from.** The turn or file, the date, and whether it was said or inferred: "From what you told [name] on Nov 3 · You said this" vs "[name] inferred this from what you told it on Nov 3", or "From the letter you sent on Nov 9". An inferred fact looks different from a confirmed one everywhere it appears, including in answers and spoken ones ("I think, from what you said on Nov 3, …"), and becomes confirmed only on the person's tap or words.
- **Matching your own people and places.** A name links to an existing record only when there is exactly one sure match; otherwise the Inbox asks. A new person, place or project is written the way the person's others are.
- **A contradiction is a question.** When a new turn says something different from a fact (the follow-up moved from Mar 5 to Mar 12), both are kept with their sources and the Inbox asks which is true now. The old one stays as "Was Mar 5 until Nov 8", never deleted.
- **How the assistant finds and changes things.** Each kind of thing (a task, a person, a habit, a note, a place) has one home in Helm, and the assistant knows where each one lives. It reads a kind of thing through one door and changes it through one action, the same ones the screens use, never by reaching around them. That is why every change it makes comes with an Undo and a line in Activity, why nothing is kept twice where two copies could disagree, and why whatever is private stays private whichever way it is reached. It is also what lets new kinds of things be added safely: a new kind gets its home, its door and its action before the assistant can touch it.
- **Correct, confirm, forget.** Correcting a fact makes it confirmed, with the correction as its source. Forgetting removes it from what the assistant uses, with Undo; an old turn never brings it back, though a new one that says it again makes it a new fact.

### Knowledge, In Detail

The owner's words: "a personal knowledge base of sorts"; then "I've been iterating on how I wash my car… I should be able to view it, ask questions regarding it, iterate, tell it feedback"; and in round 6c: "when I go to read about the topic, it should be this general document I can read that's formatted properly… see where the different parts came from… the history for the doc." Knowledge is that: what the person learns, kept as topics that are living documents, nested their way, and searched by the assistant as part of their records.

- **Two things in Knowledge.** A **topic** is a living document: one readable page the assistant writes and keeps up from everything filed under it. A **note** is one piece of knowledge (something said, a saved answer, a file, a line written by hand) that feeds one or more topics, or none. The person reads topics; notes are what they are made of. The view, the button and the empty states say Knowledge.
- **The car-care journey.** Over a few weeks the owner tells the assistant "I use two buckets now, one for rinsing", saves a general answer about ceramic coatings, sends a Markdown file of a detailer's checklist and says "the cedar air freshener lasts about a month". The first time, the assistant asks which topic ("Washing", new). Later lines go there on their own, each with Undo. When "Fragrance" appears beside "Washing", it proposes "Car care" above both. Opening Car care shows a short overview and links to Washing and Fragrance; Washing reads as a proper page with "Interior", "Exterior" and "Ceramic coat" sections. Tapping the ceramic-coat paragraph shows it came from the answer saved on Nov 5 and the file sent on Nov 7. "History" shows each change, and the latest one can be undone.
- **A topic reads as a document.** A title, an optional emoji, and a body with headings, lists, bold and links, written plainly in every tone. The assistant arranges what is filed into sections that make sense and keeps it current as more arrives. It quotes the person's own words as they said or wrote them.
- **Your words stay yours.** The assistant adds to a topic on its own when something plainly belongs there, as a new version with Undo. It never removes or rewrites the person's words (what they said, wrote or edited) unless asked, and never deletes a topic or a note on its own. It may rearrange its own wording around them (a heading, an order, a joining sentence), and says so in History.
- **Every part says where it came from, and when.** A faint mark beside each paragraph or list item; tapping it opens "Where this came from". "Show sources" at the top puts the date beside every part at once.
- **Topics nest, up to 3 levels.** Car care › Washing › Ceramic coat. A parent's document opens with a short overview the assistant writes of what its subtopics cover, then a link to each with its last change ("Washing · changed Nov 7"), then anything filed on the parent itself. A topic's page shows its path at the top ("Car care › Washing"). A fourth level is refused in words: "Topics go three levels deep. Put Coating brands under Ceramic coat?"
- **Grouping.** When two or more topics plainly belong together and no parent exists, the assistant proposes one; it never creates a parent without asking. Moving a topic under an existing parent that plainly fits happens on its own, with Undo. The person can move, rename or un-nest any topic by hand or by asking.
- **Loose notes.** Something that fits no topic and that the person chose "Leave it loose" for stays a loose note, listed under "Loose notes" in Knowledge, searchable and cited like the rest. Moving it into a topic later is one tap or one sentence.
- **One note, several topics.** A note can feed more than one topic (the round 3 answer stands); each topic's document cites it, and editing the note updates every topic that uses it, each as a new version.
- **Ways in:**
  - *Save a general answer.* "Save to my knowledge" under a "Not from your records" block.
  - *Tell the assistant.* "Remember that…" or "note: …", when it is general rather than about the person's own things.
  - *Send a Markdown file.* Filed into a topic, or as a loose note, when it is something to keep.
  - *Write by hand.* Knowledge → "New note", or "Write" inside a topic's document.
  - *Paste something long.* A paste too long for a turn offers "Save to my knowledge"; no AI reads it at save time.
  - *Make a topic from what you've said.* "Make a topic of everything I've told you about the car" drafts one from the person's turns, facts and kept files, listing what it used, with Undo.
- **Where it goes: asked once, then filed.** When nothing fits, which is always the case the first time, the assistant asks which topic, one question in the conversation (Questions, In Detail). Once a topic plainly fits, it files there itself and says so, with Undo and "Change topic". When it could fit two, it asks. This covers only knowledge the person chose to keep, in the ways above; facts the assistant notices about the person's own life follow Facts, not topics.
- **Word for word.**
  - First-time topic question: "Where should this go?" with up to 3 topics that might fit, then "New topic: Washing" (its suggested name, editable), then "Leave it loose". Spoken: "Where should this go? Car care, or a new topic called Washing?"
  - Auto-file line: "Added to Car care › Washing: two buckets, one for rinsing · Undo · Change topic". Spoken: "Added to Washing."
  - A general answer saved: "Saved to Car care › Washing · Undo · Change topic".
  - Grouping proposal: "Group Washing and Fragrance under Car care? · Group them · Not now". After it: "Grouped Washing and Fragrance under Car care · Undo". "Not now" stops it proposing that group again.
  - Moved under an existing parent: "Moved Fragrance under Car care · Undo".
  - "Where this came from" sheet: title "Where this came from"; one row per source, newest first, each one of:
    - "You said this · Nov 3, 8:14 PM", with the words quoted and "Open the conversation"
    - "You wrote this · Nov 7"
    - "Saved from a general answer · Nov 5 · Not checked", with "I checked this"
    - "From a file you sent · Nov 7 · detailer-checklist.md", with Open
    - "General knowledge, added when you asked · Nov 7", marked "Not from your records"
    - then a muted line, "Added Nov 5 · last changed Nov 7", and the sheet's two actions, "Correct this" and "Remove this part" (with Undo).
  - Bringing new notes in, at the top of a topic that has new notes: "Bringing in 2 new notes…" while the document underneath stays readable; past the day's limit "2 new notes will be added tomorrow · Read them now" (opens them as a list); on a failure "2 new notes aren't in the document yet · Try again".
  - History row: "Nov 7, 8:20 PM · Added 'Two buckets, one for rinsing' from what you said". The newest row carries "Undo"; older rows open that version with "Restore this version". Other rows read "You edited Exterior", "Revised when you asked: shortened Exterior", "Moved Fragrance under Car care", "Grouped under Car care", "Rearranged the sections".
- **Ask while you read.** With a topic open, the question is answered from that topic first (quoting the part), then the rest of the records, then general knowledge under "Not from your records", which can be added with "Add to this topic".
- **Tell it what to change.** "That step's wrong, I dry with a blower now", "make the exterior part shorter", "turn this into steps": the assistant revises the topic as asked, shows what changed (lines added and taken out), and keeps the earlier version. This is how the person's own words get changed: only when asked.
- **History.** Every change to a topic is a version: who or what made it, when, and what changed. An older version can be read and restored as a new version, never edited. The newest change can be undone from the History row or from its line in the conversation.
- **How sure.** A part that came from a general answer says "Not checked" until the person taps "I checked this" or edits it.
- **How the assistant uses Knowledge.** When answering, it looks through topics and notes with the rest of the person's records, before any general knowledge, and cites them with the book mark: "Your topic · Car care › Washing · Nov 7 version".
- **The same thing twice.** Something already in a topic word for word says "Already in Washing" with Open, and adds nothing. Two parts that disagree are both kept, each with its date, and the topic says so ("Two of your notes differ: 3 days or 5").
- **Edit and delete.** "Edit" on a topic edits its document by hand (what the person types is theirs). Deleting a topic has a confirm naming what happens: "Delete Washing? Its 6 notes stay, as loose notes or in their other topics. Its subtopic Ceramic coat moves up to Car care." with Undo. Deleting a note removes it from every topic that cites it, each as a new version.
- **Many.** Search from 8 topics or notes; copy a topic as Markdown on a tap. With hundreds of notes, the assistant reads the few that best match the question and says which it used.
- **Empty.** "What you learn and want to keep shows here, as topics you can read. Tell [name] to remember something, or write your own." with New note.

### Today, In Detail

Today is one view among the others, one tap from home, for when the person wants to see their day laid out. "What's on today?" asked anywhere answers in a sentence or two from the same plan.

- **Opens on today.** A summary line ("2 events · 1 planned · 2 suggested") and, only when there are any, "2 need you", a door to the Inbox. A day strip reaches yesterday and the next 6 days.
- **Calendar is the truth for fixed things.** Events from the calendars the person chose appear as they are and can't be moved from Helm. A gap of 45 minutes or more shows as "Free · 1h 30m".
- **Connected apps add their fixed things.** A planned showing from A-List, a flight or a timed activity from Waypoint, a vet visit from Nine Lives or a pickup from Going Once shows among the events with its app's logo and counts as busy. The same thing on Google Calendar and in an app (titles that match, starting within 15 minutes of each other) shows once, with both sources. Due dates from an app list under "Due soon" with their app, never as Helm tasks and never planned into a gap. Pinch's planned meals show as one quiet line per meal slot, not as busy time.
- **Helm's plan is a suggestion.** The first time Today opens (or is asked about) on a day with no plan, Helm's own code (not the AI) suggests a few tasks for gaps that fit their length, energy and context: overdue first, then due soon, then what matters most. It leaves 15 minutes around events, fills at most about half the free time, and never plans before the day's start or after its end (8 AM to 9 PM unless the person changes "Your day"). A task with no length is planned at 30 minutes and says it's a guess. Once the person has said what they want done today, those aims come first and the plan also leaves breaks (Your Day, In Detail).
- **Keep, move or skip.** A suggested task is dashed until kept. Keep makes it planned; "Another time" offers slots today and tomorrow; "Not today" puts it back in "Any time" with Undo. Planning again never touches what was kept or skipped. "Keep the first one" or "move the dentist to tomorrow" works by voice on this view. Other days show their events, deadlines and anything kept, with "Plan this day".
- **Key dates this week.** Under the summary, at most 3 quiet lines for birthdays, anniversaries and other dates that come back in the next 7 days, close people first, then regular, soonest first; then "+2 more this week", opening Coming up (Key Dates, In Detail). Moments never show here. Never spoken unless asked.
- **Procedures for the day.** An event or trip today with a procedure attached shows its run ("Before Mom's appointment · 2 of 6").
- **Habits for the day (Next Steps).** Each habit due today is a bare line in the timeline at its window's start, through-the-day ones at the top ("💧 Water · 2 of 6"), the window open now first, its stage word in the line's quiet meta while it is open ("Holding · until 10:00 AM"); ticked on the line with Undo, and still tickable, muted, after its window ends. They never join the summary line; the planner keeps a habit's length free at its time and never plans a habit into a gap. A pushed reminder opens Today at its line (Habits, In Detail).
- **Plans that learn (Next Steps).** What Helm suggests for gaps avoids the times its noticed patterns say don't work, and says so where it shows, with Undo; a suggestion to change something the person set shows on that thing's line on the day it applies. Among tasks that are otherwise level (no deadline or importance between them), those in a project or goal higher in What matters most are suggested first. Today shows one offer at a time, whether a pattern, the offer to take a formed habit off Today or a Pause or Hold Off line; the rest wait on their own pages (What Helm Learns, In Detail). Rest days and shift days stay as they are.
- **Places without tracking.** Tasks for a place group under it ("When you get home"); Helm never reads the phone's location unless the person chooses City only or Precise (Next Steps, Where You and Your People Are, In Detail). "I'm home", said or tapped, brings those tasks up for 3 hours.
- **Rest days stay rest days.** No suggestions on a day when an event that started the day before ended after 2:00 AM: "Nothing planned after a late night. Tell [name] if you want something today." (the line names the day it is on: "…something tomorrow", "…something Thursday"). An event that runs past noon on the day (a conference, a week away) is not a night out. A calendar marked as shifts is more exact: nothing timed is suggested on a shift day, and nothing at all on the day a night shift ends ("Nothing planned after a night shift."). "Plan my day anyway" is always one tap away.
- **A full day.** "Your calendar is full today. Nothing planned." Due tasks still list below.
- **Late in the day.** Opened after the day's end, Today suggests nothing more and says "Nothing more planned today." Tomorrow is one tap away. In the last 2 hours of the day, home leads into the wind-down first (Your Day, In Detail).
- **When the calendar changes.** A kept task that a moved or new event now overlaps says "Now overlaps Brunch with Maya's parents" with Another time; it is never moved silently.
- **Without a calendar.** Today lists "Due soon" and "Worth doing today", with no times, and one quiet row to connect a calendar; "Not now" folds it into Settings for good.
- **When the calendar can't be read.** "Couldn't load your calendar." with Try again; Helm suggests nothing timed until it loads and shows the untimed lists instead. Offline: kept tasks show and "Your calendar shows once you're back online." If Google stopped sharing: "Google stopped sharing your calendar with Helm." with Reconnect.
- **Travel.** When the device's time zone differs from Helm's: "Your phone is on Pacific time; Helm plans in Eastern." with Switch. An event set in another zone shows in the person's time, with its zone flagged.
- **Past days are for looking up.** Yesterday keeps its plan and its events as they were (titles and times only), with what got done ticked. Nothing on it is editable, and unfinished tasks are offered with "Bring to today", never moved silently. A past day keeps the aims it planned and the wind-down's result, as built.

### Your Day, In Detail

Issue 128. Today shows the day; this is the day's own ritual: say what you want done, get it planned, wind down. It is two cards on home and one sealed record per day, and it works with no calendar.

- **One day card at the top of home, on the first open each day.** After the person's day start, the first open shows "What do you want to get done today?" The person answers by voice or typing ("finish the deck, call Mom, gym"). Each aim becomes a task, or links to one that already exists (the normal turn filing, with its one Undo); an aim is never a copy of a task. Once answered or dismissed the card folds to one line ("Today: 3 aims · 2 planned"), and "what's my plan today?" brings it back at any time.
- **Yesterday's unfinished things are in the same card.** Above the question, "From yesterday" lists the aims and tasks left undone, each with "Bring to today" and "Not today". Nothing moves on its own: a "Not today" leaves the task where it was, and a bring-over makes it one of today's aims. An unfinished aim the person already sent to Tomorrow in the wind-down arrives already brought; one sent to Later or dropped does not show again.
- **Home's top holds at most 3 cards.** They are Needs you, the day card (the morning card or the wind-down card, never both) and the grouping card (Related Things, In Detail). When more would show, the rest wait where they live (Needs you has its own page).
- **Each aim gets a length.** Said ("an hour on the deck") or guessed from the person's finished tasks that had a length (the middle length of up to 5 in the same project, else the same context, else 30 minutes) and shown as a guess: "about 1h?", tap to fix. The fix is the task's own length, so it stays for next time.
- **The plan puts aims first, and leaves room.** Today's own code (not the AI) plans the aims first, then overdue, then due soon, then what matters most. It keeps 15 minutes around events and a break after each planned task sized to the task: about 5 minutes after something light (take out the trash, do the dishes), 15 after something that takes effort, 30 after something heavy (clean the car, clean the apartment). The assistant judges how heavy a task is once, when it is made, and the plan only reads that. It never fills more than about half the free time; the aims count against that half too. If the aims don't fit it says so up front, before anything is kept: "These fit. The gym doesn't today unless you move the call." Keep, Another time and Not today work as they do on Today.
- **The wind-down card.** On the first open in the last 2 hours of the person's day, the day card becomes "How did today go?" with the aims in three groups: Done, Still possible tonight, Won't happen today. Each unfinished aim has Tomorrow, Later and Drop it, and an optional reason, said or tapped from the same fixed signals What Helm Learns already uses (tired, reluctant, too busy, unwell or in pain, bored, stressed). Skipping the reason is fine. Tomorrow puts the aim on tomorrow's card already brought; Later leaves the task where it is with no day; Drop it removes the aim and leaves the task. Folded: "Wound down: 2 done, 1 to tomorrow, 1 dropped."
- **The reason is the start of a pattern, worked out overnight.** What was said at the wind-down is read by the nightly pass What Helm Learns already runs, never while the person waits, and the "why" across days ("deep work slips after afternoon meetings") comes from there under the same rules (2 days of evidence, the back-off, never for health or care). It feeds the next morning's plan and is shown the way every noticed pattern is.
- **Done on the server, not when the app happens to be open.** The day turning over (unfinished aims offered again, the wind-down marked ready), a calendar change that now overlaps a kept aim, and re-planning what no longer fits are done by Helm's server on a timer. The phone only draws and writes what a tap does. Nothing is pushed: both cards wait for the next open. Re-planning never moves a kept aim: it says "Now overlaps Brunch with Maya's parents" and offers another time, as Today does.
- **Shown on the calendar.** Today and the day strip show what was planned and what got done; a past day keeps its plan with done ticked. Once the person has chosen the one Google Calendar kept in step (Issue 127), kept aims are events in their slots there, marked done when ticked, and moved or removed when the person picks Tomorrow or Drop it. Nothing is written to Google before that calendar is chosen, and a private task is written as "Busy".
- **Quiet when it should be.** A dismissed card stays folded all day ("Skipped today's aims."). On a rest day the question is still asked but the plan says "Nothing planned after a late night." With no calendar the aims list untimed under "Worth doing today". After the day's end the morning card doesn't appear; the wind-down shows instead.
- **Offline and failures.** Aims said offline are kept on the phone and filed when it reconnects ("Saved here. It will be filed when you're back online."); the card shows them as said. A plan that couldn't be made says "Couldn't plan today. Your aims are saved." with Try again. A wind-down answer given offline is kept and applied on reconnect; the server's rollover then finds it already answered.
- **Privacy.** The aims, their order, lengths and reasons are sealed with the rest of the person's words. The server opens only the aims' task references and results to roll them over, the way the nightly pass and scheduled actions run while the person is away, and "What the AI sees" says so. The server never opens a task's words.

### Related Things, In Detail

Issue 130. Things about one subject often sit in different places: a Going Once sale "Fall move out", a Runway Horizon event for moving, and Helm's own tasks and topics about the move. Helm's nightly pass (the one What Helm Learns already runs, never while the person waits) looks across the person's records and the connected apps for things that belong together and keeps the grouping as ready context, so saying "moving" later finds a project that is already there.

- **A grouping is a project or topic with its linked sources, each one cited.** The sale, the event, the tasks and the topics show under it with their app's logo, and each says what it was linked from. The assistant uses it as context in any later turn; it is never a copy of what it links.
- **Certain is quiet, unsure is asked.** A thing that clearly belongs in a project or topic that already exists is linked without a question: one Activity line with Undo and one quiet line on the next open. Making a new project or group, or any link the code is less than sure about, is proposed on the third home card: "These look like one thing: Moving", the sources listed by app logo, with "Group them", "Not related" and "Not now". A new parent topic is always proposed, never made unasked.
- **One at a time, under the back-off.** At most one grouping is offered per open and the rest wait. The same back-off as every learned offer applies: "Not now" waits and the wait grows, "Not related" stays no until new evidence arrives.
- **Asked at once.** "Anything related to my move?" is answered immediately from what is already known; it doesn't wait for the night.
- **Private stays private.** A private thing is never named on the card or in a line unless the private-things rules allow it; it can still be grouped, and shows on the card as "a private item".
- **Only what is already readable.** It adds no new access to any connected app: it groups what a connected app already shares with Helm and nothing more. What it can see of Going Once and Runway is whatever those apps' sharing already offers.

### Google Calendar, In Detail

Google Calendar is one connection among the others (Connected Apps, In Detail), and the one Today offers on its own.

- **Optional, on a tap.** "Connect Google Calendar to plan around your events" is offered on Today, with the reason beside it, never on load. Helm works fully without it.
- **Read only.** Helm asks Google to read events only, and reads each event's title, time and calendar, and (Next Steps) its place and whether it meets on video: never its description, guests or meeting link. It never creates, changes or deletes an event in the MVP; keeping one chosen calendar in step both ways is Issue 127.
- **While Helm is in testing.** Until Google reviews Helm, Google shows a warning that the app isn't verified, and may ask to reconnect about once a week. The Connect screen says so in one line before the person leaves for Google, so the warning isn't a surprise.
- **Coming back from Google.** Declined: back on Today, nothing changed. Signed in but the calendar permission unticked: "Helm needs permission to see your events to plan around them. Nothing was connected." with Try again.
- **The real calendar is many calendars.** After connecting, the person picks which ones count as busy; holidays and subscriptions start off. One tap marks a calendar as shifts. An event on two calendars counts once; declined events are ignored; tentative ones count as busy and say so; all-day events show at the top and block nothing unless the person says they do (a day off, a trip). A calendar added in Google later appears in Settings, off; one deleted in Google drops out quietly.
- **Disconnect.** From its row in Connected apps; Today drops back to the no-calendar version and nothing else changes (Disconnect and Delete, In Detail).

### Connected Apps, In Detail

The owner's words: a pipeline for apps to read data from one another, with Helm as the first reader. In Helm it is one idea: the assistant can read the places your life already runs in, says where everything it read came from, and changes nothing there.

- **What the person can do.** Connect Google Calendar, Gmail and Drive (on more than one Google account) and any of the site's apps, and pick people from Google Contacts (Importing From Google Contacts, In Detail); choose, for each, which of the few things it offers Helm may read; see when each last synced and anything wrong; disconnect, with or without deleting what Helm copied.
- **Two ways of reading.** A site app and Google Calendar are *kept in step*: what they share is copied into Helm, sealed like the rest of the person's things, so Today can plan around it, Search can find it and the assistant can link it to a project. Gmail, Drive and Runway's Transactions are *read when asked*: nothing is copied; they are searched only to answer a question, and only the lines an answer quotes are kept, with the answer.
- **The Connected apps workspace.** Settings → Connected apps. Connected first, then what can be connected: the three Google services, then the site's apps the person uses; apps they have never opened fold under "More apps". Each connection is one row (logo, name, account where there is one, status), and opens its own page with:
  - **Account**: "personal@gmail.com", or "Your moondreams account" for a site app.
  - **Status**, one of: "Connected · synced 7:40 AM" (or "Read when you ask" for Gmail and Drive); "Couldn't sync since Nov 6" with the cause in a sentence and Try again; "Google stopped sharing with Helm" with Reconnect; "Not available yet" (Gmail and Drive for anyone but the owner, Privacy, In Detail).
  - **What Helm may read**: one switch per category the source offers, with a line saying what it holds ("Planned showings: the movie, time and theater"), and below them, in muted text, "Never shared: your membership number, share links" so the person knows the edges.
  - **Last successful sync** and, when there are any, the errors since, newest first, each in a sentence.
  - **Disconnect**.
- **Read only, in words.** Every connection's page says "Helm can read this. It can't change anything in Pinch." A capture that asks for a change elsewhere ("add milk to my Pinch list") is never reported as done there: "Helm can't change Pinch yet. Made a task: Add milk to the Pinch shopping list." with "Open Pinch". No result line, answer or button ever says "Added to", "Updated in" or "Sent" about another app or service.
- **Group apps share only what is yours to see.** Waypoint, Going Once and Nine Lives hold other people's things too. A source shares only what the person can already see in it: their own plans, to-dos and sales, and messages sent to them (a buyer's question), never another member's private items or anything the app keeps from them.
- **The site lists every grant too.** A grant is the person's, between two apps, so the site's account page has its own "Connected apps" index: every reader, what it reads from each source, and a door to change it. Helm's own page shows and edits only Helm's grants. A "Stop sharing" list inside each source app is Next Steps.
- **Helm shares nothing.** In the MVP Helm is only a reader; no other app can read Helm.
- **Offered, never pushed.** Today's "Connect Google Calendar" row and People's "Add from Google Contacts" are the only places a connection is offered outside Settings. Nothing asks to connect apps at first run, and the carer never sees a list of apps.

### Where a Synced Thing Shows Its Source, In Detail

- **Every copied thing names where it came from.** One line, everywhere it shows (Today, Search, an answer's source, a project): the source's logo and name, the account when the provider has several ("Google Calendar · work"), and when Helm last saw it ("synced 7:40 AM"). Its sheet adds "Open in Waypoint" (or the Google service) and "Read only: change it in Waypoint."
- **Freshness is stated, never implied.** Something older than its source's usual sync says so in the warning colour: "As of Nov 6 · couldn't reach Pinch since". Today never plans around an event it hasn't seen in the last day without saying "may be out of date".
- **A copy is never edited in Helm.** It has no Edit and no Undo; the assistant can link it to a project or make a Helm task about it ("Bring the vaccination card to Miso's visit"), and that task is Helm's own, with the copy as its source.
- **Gone at the source, gone in Helm.** Something deleted or past in its app drops out of Helm at the next sync, quietly, like a calendar event; a Helm task or procedure run linked to it stays and says "From Miso's visit, Nov 12, no longer in Nine Lives".

### When Sources Disagree, In Detail

- **One source is the authority for each thing.** The calendar is the authority for an event's time; the app that holds something (Waypoint for a trip's times, Nine Lives for a due date) is the authority for it; what the person told Helm is the authority for Helm's own facts. Mail and documents are never the authority over a calendar or an app.
- **Two outside sources: shown, not asked.** When an email says the tasting is at 2:30 and the calendar says 3:00, the event shows the calendar's time and one line under it: "An email from Sweet Layer Bakery on Nov 2 says 2:30 PM." with Open. Helm plans with the authority's time and changes neither; there is no Inbox question, since nothing in Helm can settle it.
- **Found while answering: noted once.** When a mail or Drive search for a question turns up the disagreement, the answer writes it on the event as one noted fact, with Undo: "Noted the 2:30 PM on Cake tasting." It is the only thing an answer ever writes, and it never changes the event's time.
- **An outside source and a Helm fact: asked.** When a connected app contradicts a fact in Memory (a fact says the follow-up is Mar 5, Nine Lives says Mar 12), it is the usual "Which is true now?" question in Needs you, both with their sources. Picking the app's value changes Helm's fact only, never the app.
- **The same thing twice.** One event on Google Calendar and in A-List or Waypoint (titles that match, starting within 15 minutes of each other) shows once with both sources; if their times differ, the calendar's shows and the line says what the app says.

### Several Accounts, In Detail

- **A provider can be connected more than once.** "Add another Google account" on the workspace connects a second account (work beside personal). Each account is its own connection with its own email, status and switches, and each Google service on it is connected on its own tap: connecting Gmail on the work account asks Google only for mail, not for the calendar.
- **Accounts are named by their email,** and the person can give one a short label ("work"), which is what source lines show.
- **Site apps have one account:** the person's moondreams account, the same sign-in as Helm's, so a site app connects with a switch and no trip to another site.
- **Calendars from two accounts are one day.** Today merges them; an event on both counts once; a shifts calendar can be on either.

### Disconnect and Delete, In Detail

- **Disconnect stops reading at once.** The confirm names the connection and what Helm holds from it: "Disconnect Pinch? Helm stops reading it now. Your meals and list stay in Pinch." with one choice, ticked: "Also delete what Helm copied from Pinch (12 planned meals, 9 shopping items)". The grant ends with it: Helm asks the provider to forget it and keeps nothing that could read again.
- **Ticked (the default):** every copy is deleted at once, and answers that quoted the source keep their own words but have the quoted lines replaced by "Quoted from Pinch, deleted Nov 9". For Gmail and Drive, which copy nothing, this is the only thing deleting does.
- **Unticked:** the copies stay, marked "From Pinch · no longer updated", are searchable and can be cited (with that line), but Today no longer plans around them. They are deleted 90 days after the disconnect, and the connection's row says so ("Deletes Feb 7 · Delete now").
- **What Helm made stays.** Tasks, procedure runs, facts and past days' plans the person or the assistant made are Helm's own: they stay, with their source written as text ("From Miso's visit in Nine Lives, Nov 12").
- **Reconnecting starts fresh,** with each category back at its default, and reads again from the source.
- **Stopping from the site's account page** (its Connected apps index) is the same as disconnecting with the box ticked; a Stop sharing list inside each source app comes later and will work the same way.

### What Is On at First, In Detail

- **Nothing is connected until the person connects it.** Sharing between apps starts off, as anything that shares does.
- **What starts on: names and dates to plan with.** Plannable things (what, when, where) are on; money detail, health notes and other people's names start off on every source.
- **Connecting turns on the source's "On" categories** (the table above) and nothing else. Each is one switch off; the page says so before the first sync: "Helm will read your planned meals and shopping list. Switch off either one."
- **Google Calendar** keeps its own first step: which calendars count, with holidays and subscriptions off.
- **Gmail and Drive** are read only when a question needs them, from the moment they are connected; each answer says when they were searched. Gmail's sent-mail style switch starts off (How You Write, In Detail).
- **Google Contacts** reads nothing until the person ticks people in its picker, and only those.
- **Worth the Wait** connects with nothing on; its one category, Box counts, says what it holds before it is switched on: "Helm will see your boxes and how many things are in each, and how many are revealed. It never sees what anyone wrote."
- **Runway's Transactions** starts off; its switch says what turning it on means: "Lets Helm answer questions like 'what did I spend at Costco?'. Helm reads each purchase's date, place, amount and category only when you ask, and keeps just the few an answer quotes (up to 5, blanked when the connection ends)."

The owner's blueprint stopped at "distinguish live data"; this is how long Helm keeps each kind of thing it reads.

### One Pipeline for the Whole Site

Connected apps are a site standard, not a Helm feature. Each of the site's apps publishes what it shares through one contract (its kinds of thing, its categories, its "never"), and a reader goes through that contract, never into another app's data. Helm is the first reader; any app can become one later through the same grants, and an app that one day moves to its own domain keeps sharing through its contract unchanged. A new app joins by declaring what it shares, with its "On" set as small as the table above.

### Procedures, In Detail

The owner's words: lists "are more like procedures: reusable routines (what I pack before a trip; at work, the checklist for starting a new initiative)". A procedure replaces round 5's checklist: a routine kept once and run each time. A one-off list is a task's steps.

- **What a procedure is.** A title, steps, optional groups the person names ("Toiletries", "Electronics"), an optional intro line, its versions, and what it is usually run for (trips, a kind of event, a project). Procedures has its own view, one tap from home.
- **Where they come from.** Asked for ("make a packing procedure for trips"), drafted by the assistant from what the person has said and their notes (listing what it used), made from a task's steps that keep coming back ("You've had these steps on 3 tasks. Make them a procedure?"), written by hand, or sent as a Markdown file.
- **A run.** "Start a run" (or "let's pack for Austin") makes a fresh copy of the steps, attached to what it is for: a trip (from Waypoint or a Helm project), a calendar event, a project, or nothing. A run is ticked step by step ("4 of 12") on its own; the procedure is untouched. A run can be started by voice and ticked by voice ("done with chargers"), each tick spoken back. An event or trip with a run shows it, and so does Today on that day.
- **Offered, never pushed.** For an event or trip that matches what a procedure is usually run for ("Flight to Austin" and "Packing for a trip"), the event shows "Run Packing for a trip?" dashed until started, when the person opens it; "Not now" stops it for that event.
- **A run can differ.** Adding or skipping a step in a run changes that run only; adding one asks once, on the run: "Add 'Charger for the camera' to Packing for a trip for next time?" Yes makes a new version of the procedure.
- **Revised like a note.** By hand, or by asking ("add a step about the passport", "split this into before and on the day"), with what changed shown and Undo. Editing steps or groups makes a new version; a tick never does. Older versions can be read and restored as a new version. A run keeps the steps it started with.
- **Runs are kept.** A procedure lists its past runs (date, what it was for, "11 of 12"); a past run is for looking up and can't be ticked again. "Run it again" starts a new one.
- **When what it's attached to goes.** A run on an event no longer on the calendar stays, saying "From Cake tasting, Nov 8, no longer on your calendar".
- **Copy as Markdown.** A procedure, or a run with ticks as `[x]`; the toast says "Copied 12 steps, with ticks". Google Docs export is Next Steps.
- **Round 5's checklists.** Nothing was built, so there is nothing to move; "checklist" is not used in copy. Other documents (briefings, research, drafts) are Next Steps.

### Scheduled Actions, In Detail



**Next Steps; Issue 115 (its outbound kinds on Issues 67 and 93).** Something the assistant does for the person at a set time, without being asked each time (Words We Use): "every Sunday at 6, put next week's groceries on the calendar". A repeating task or a procedure is the person's own job coming round; a scheduled action is the assistant doing the work. The owner's words: "similar to how Google Calendar does it, which has a lot more customizability… but ease of use should be as easy as it is for Google Calendar." So it repeats the way a calendar event does: a one-tap choice for the usual cases, a Custom screen for the rest, and the same rule said in words wherever it shows.

- **Made by saying it.** "Every Sunday at 6, put next week's groceries from Pinch on my calendar" makes one, shown as a line that says what, when and what it may change, with Undo: "Every Sunday at 6:00 PM: next week's groceries from Pinch onto your calendar · First on Sun, Nov 15". Anything a calendar can repeat can be said: "every other Tuesday at 8", "the last Friday of every month", "weekdays at 7 until the end of March", "every 3 months on the 1st, 6 times". The reply says the rule back in the same words the sheet uses, with **Change**: "Every 2 weeks on Tuesday at 8:00 AM · First on Tue, Nov 17 · Change". Helm's code checks the rule before it is kept; the assistant never decides alone what "the last Friday" is. What it couldn't settle (which day? which list?) is one skippable question. Its time is Your day's clock.
- **How often, in one tap.** Inside Change, "How often" lists the usual choices, worded from the action's first date (here Mon, Nov 23): "Does not repeat" · "Every day" · "Every weekday (Mon–Fri)" · "Every week on Monday" · "Every month on the fourth Monday" (or "on the 23rd": whichever the person said, or the date when they said neither, with the other one row below it) · "Every year on Nov 23" · "Custom…". The choice in use is ticked; picking another updates the words line under it, and nothing changes until Save.
- **Custom.** A small screen, top to bottom:
  - "Repeat every [1] [day · week · month · year]".
  - For weeks, the days as pills, several allowed: S M T W T F S, the first date's day picked to start.
  - For months, two choices: "On day 23" or "On the fourth Monday" ("On the last Monday" when it is the month's last; "On the last day" when the first date is the month's last day).
  - "Ends": "Never" · "On [date]" · "After [10] times".
  - Under it, the rule in words and the next three dates: "Every 2 weeks on Tuesday and Thursday at 8:00 AM, until Mar 1 · Next: Tue, Nov 17 · Thu, Nov 19 · Tue, Dec 1". A day that some months lack says so: "Every month on the 31st (months without one are skipped)".
  - Done takes the rule back to Change, where Save keeps it; Back leaves it unchanged (Keep editing / Discard once something was changed).
- **One time a run, and never more than once a day.** Each action has one time. "Every 2 hours" or "at 8 and at 6" is answered plainly: "I can run it once a day at most. Make two, one at 8:00 AM and one at 6:00 PM?", and two actions are made only on a yes. "Ends after" goes up to 999 times. A run happens within a few minutes of its time (Helm checks every few minutes), and its line keeps the time it was set for.
- **Where they are.** Browse › Scheduled, a tile once one exists ("Scheduled · 2"), opening a list with search from 8: each row what it does in a few words and its next run ("Next: Sun, Nov 15 · 6:00 PM"), or "Paused until Dec 10". Up to 50. An action whose end has come leaves the list on its own after its last run, with one line in Activity ("Finished after its 10th run: next week's groceries onto your calendar"), and no longer counts toward the 50.
- **Its sheet.** What it does in a sentence, how often in words, the next three runs, the last few runs (each its outcome line), and what it may change ("Your calendar · Pinch's list, read only"). Three actions: **Pause** (until a date, or until asked), **Change** and **End** (a confirm; its past runs stay in Activity).
- **One run, or this and the ones after.** Like a calendar event, each of the next runs opens a small sheet of its own: **Skip this one** (the date stays in the list, crossed through, "Skipped · Undo") and **Move this one** (another day or time, for that run only, up to the day before the run after it; "Moved to Tue, Nov 24 · 9:00 AM · Undo"). **Change** from a run asks "Just this one · This and following", with This and following chosen; Just this one changes only that run's day and time, never what the action does (that is always this and following); Change from the action's own sheet is always this and following. Past runs are never rewritten, since they already happened: a change that moves the time or the rule starts from the next run and says so ("From Sun, Nov 22: every Saturday at 9:00 AM"). Each of these can be said: "skip it this week", "do the groceries on Monday this time", "move it to Saturdays from now on", "pause the grocery one", "stop doing the groceries".
- **Each run is told once, where the person will look.** One outcome line on Today (the next time Helm is opened) and in Activity, with Undo: "Done for you · Sun 6:00 PM: 9 groceries on your calendar for next week · Undo". A run never pushes and is never spoken first; nothing about it reaches the lock screen.
- **What it may do.** Until Helm can change things outside itself (Issue 67), only things inside Helm: make or update tasks, plan a day, refresh a research topic, start a procedure run. Once it can, a run's outside part waits in Needs you to be approved: "Ready: next week's groceries on your calendar · Add it". When acting without asking is turned on for that kind (Issue 93), the run acts and tells, with Undo.
- **When a run can't happen.** Each says what happened and what still works, on Today and in Activity:
  - A connection it needs is gone: "Sunday's grocery list didn't run: Pinch isn't connected. · Reconnect · Skip this one".
  - The person's limit for the day is reached: the run waits and happens the next time Helm is opened, its line dated then ("Ran Mon 8:10 AM, when you opened [name]").
  - It couldn't run on time (Helm itself was down): within 6 hours it runs late and says so; later than that it is skipped: "Sunday's grocery list was skipped: it couldn't run on time."
  - Paused, or the person is away and chose "Pause what runs at home" (Where You and Your People Are, In Detail): nothing runs, and its row says until when. That pause covers the span, not a list of actions: an action made during the time away, or one whose runs fall inside it, is paused for those days too.
- **The same repeat everywhere in Helm.** Repeating tasks and procedures on a schedule (Next Steps) use the same choices, Custom screen and words, so "every other Tuesday" means one thing wherever it is said.
- **Private like anything else.** An action can be private (Private Things, In Detail); its outcome lines follow what it came from.
- **What is kept.** The action with its instruction sealed and, readable to Helm's code so it runs while Helm isn't opened, its repeat rule (written in the same standard calendars use), its time and time zone, its skipped and moved runs, and its next run; each run's changes in Activity with Undo. "What the AI sees" names each action that needs the AI to run (Privacy, In Detail). Delete everything removes them all.

### Where Does It Live?, In Detail



The owner's words: "Knowing what things we should track in Helm and which things should be their own app… I have a tendency to conflate things." Every new need is put through four questions before it is planned, and the verdict is written here.

1. **Does it need a surface you open on purpose?** A screen with its own tools (a metronome, a score, a map), not a line and a tap.
2. **Does it get better from knowing your life?** Your calendar and shifts, your people, your projects, what you said last week.
3. **Who else touches the data?** Helm is one person's, sealed and never shared; anything another person reads or writes is its own app.
4. **How long do you spend in it?** Seconds (a tick, a sentence) belongs in Helm; a session (half an hour at the piano) belongs in an app.

### Hands-Free First, In Detail



**Next Steps, for the whole of habits, goals, learning, Pause or Hold Off and thinking ahead.** The owner's words: assume the person's hands are busy. Everything in this area works by voice from start to end, on top of Speaking and Listening, In Detail; taps stay, and are never required.

- **Every question can be answered aloud.** "Which one?" ("the evening ones", "Mom's", "the first one"), "How was it?" (a few words), the tidy why ("use the tidy one", "keep mine"), an offer about a habit ("make it ten minutes", "not now", "stop suggesting that"), fewer reminders ("only if I'm late"), Pause or Hold Off ("hold it till December", "start it now"), the road trip's to-settle questions ("keep it on video") and learn later ("save it"). An answer that fits none of the choices is asked once more, then waits on screen and in Needs you, as every question does.
- **It says the question, then listens.** In a spoken conversation, a reply that asks something back says the question and its choices in a sentence ("Which pills: yours or Mom's?") and listens again at once, without a tap, for 8 seconds ("Answer without tapping" in Settings → Voice, on by default).
- **Talk over it.** Speaking while it reads the choices stops its voice and takes what was said as the answer.
- **Listening ended too soon.** The question stays open on screen; for 2 minutes "Hey [name], Mom's" (with the wake phrase on) answers it, and a tap on Talk and "Mom's" answers it at any time, since a reply to an open question is read as its answer first. With the screen off or Helm closed, the question waits in Needs you and is answered the same way next time. When the person ended it themselves ("never mind", "forget it"), there is nothing left to answer late: the question went with it (Speaking and Listening, In Detail, "Ending it by saying so").
- **Cards seen, answered aloud.** An offer on Today or a page (the too-tired card, a formed habit's offer, a Pause or Hold Off line) can be answered by "Ask about this" or the wake phrase with the card in view: "make it ten minutes" picks that choice.
- **Nothing only a tap can do.** Each choice in this area has words that pick it, kept with the choice and checked when it is built; a choice that can't be said (a date picked on a calendar) also takes a spoken date ("hold it until December 2nd").
- **Said back, short.** Every spoken answer is confirmed in a few words ("Okay, ten minutes from now on. Undo?"), and "undo that" takes it back.

### Habits, In Detail



**Next Steps.** The owner's words: "I don't need an app to just track my habits, to see if I'm drinking enough water or if I'm taking my pills like I said I would during the right times of the day", and, on reminders: "The ideal habit system is one which helps a user form the habit… a user can opt into notifications while the habit is building. It's an adaptive system. This means we should have a process for sunsetting habits once they've formed." A habit is something you keep up, not something you finish: it has no deadline, is never overdue, and a day without it is just a day. Helm helps it take hold, steps back as it does, and offers to take it off Today once it is part of the day.

**What a habit is**

- **A habit or a repeating task.** The test is the one in Words We Use: does it need to become automatic (a habit: practise piano, water, floss, pills), or just get done (a repeating task: clean the car every weekend, the cat's flea dose each month; Next Steps, Issue 70)? A repeating task falls due, can be overdue and leaves when done; a habit never is. The assistant chooses by that test and says which ("Made a habit: Pills, morning and evening"), with Undo. Once repeating tasks exist, a sentence that could be either is a pick-one: "A habit · On Today each Monday, counted like 3 of 4. Never overdue." or "A task that comes back · Due each Monday. If it's missed, it waits until done."
- **What a habit carries.** A name and emoji; how often (every day, certain weekdays, or a number of times a week); when (one or more windows in the day: a named part such as morning or evening, or a set time, which becomes an hour around it; or "through the day", with no window); an optional count for a day ("6 glasses"); an optional length (practice, 20 min); who it is for (you, or someone in People: Mom); an optional goal; whether it is about health; its stage (below); and whether it reminds you. Only the name is needed; "through the day, every day" is the default.
- **Your day, not the clock.** "Morning" and "evening" are the first and last two hours of the person's day (8:00 to 10:00 AM and 7:00 to 9:00 PM on the default 8 AM to 9 PM day), said on the card that makes the habit so it can be changed; fixed hours only when Your day is unset. With a calendar marked as shifts, "on waking" and "before bed" follow the shift: on the day a night shift ends, "before bed" is the morning it ends. A set time stays that clock time. A habit's day turns over at 3 AM, so evening pills taken at 1:30 AM after a late shift count for that evening.

**Made, ticked and seen**

- **Made by talking.** "I want to drink more water", "pills morning and evening", "Mom takes metoprolol at 9 AM and 9 PM" each make one habit, shown as a line with Undo; what the assistant couldn't settle (how many glasses?) is one skippable question. There is no New habit button: a habit is said, or added from a goal's "Add a habit" (a short sheet: name, how often, when, count).
- **On Today, a bare line at its time.** Each habit due today is a borderless line in Today's timeline at its window's start ("💊 Pills · evening" at 7:00 PM), a through-the-day one at the top with the all-day rows ("💧 Water · 2 of 6"), the window open now first. A once-a-window habit has the round tick; a counted one has a "+" disc. A habit not due today is not drawn; at most 8 lines, then "3 more habits today". Habits never join the summary line, and Today's planner keeps a habit's length free at its time (guitar's 20 minutes) but never plans a habit into a gap.
- **Ticked in a word or a tap, without naming the window.** "Took my pills", "another glass of water", "Mom took her pills", or a tap on the line. Said, it ticks the window that is open now; with none open, the latest one today that has ended unticked, or else the next one to come (pills said at 7:30 AM count for the 8:00 morning window). The reply is the habit's line itself, now ticked, always naming what was ticked, the medicine too when the habit holds one ("💊 Mom's pills: metoprolol 25 mg · morning ✓ 8:05 AM" when "Mom took her pills" was said), so a misheard "took my pills" that ticked the wrong one is seen at once, with Undo and, when it had a choice, the other window one tap away: "💊 Pills · evening ✓ 9:45 PM · Undo · It was the morning ones". The spoken reply is a few words that name it too ("Done: Mom's morning metoprolol."; "Noted" is kept for something kept for later and not acted on), never praise; on a locked phone after the wake phrase it stays unnamed until the person taps the phone (Private Things, In Detail). A tap says so on the line ("Done 9:45 AM · Undo"). When every window today is already ticked, nothing is added and it says so: "Both of today's pills are already ticked." (for a health habit, never a second dose counted on a guess). Two habits that match ("pills" when there are two pill habits today) are one tap or one word: "Which one?" with each, said aloud with its choices when the tick was spoken (Hands-Free First, In Detail). "I took them yesterday too" ticks up to 7 days back; older days are read only. A question ("did I take my pills?") answers and changes nothing.
- **Ticked with no connection, or past the day's limit.** "Took my pills", "gave Mom her pills" or "done with water", said or typed, ticks the habit on the phone itself, with no AI and no connection, so a reminder never goes out for a dose already taken: the phone matches the words against today's habit names and ticks as above ("💊 Pills · evening ✓ 9:45 PM · Undo"), "Which one?" included. Always for health and care habits; other habits tick this way too when the words name one plainly. Anything else in the sentence is kept as said and read once it can be (Capture, In Detail).
- **A few words with the tick.** "Took my pills, felt dizzy" or "practised, the barre chords are getting easier" ticks the habit and keeps the rest as a note on that day's tick, word for word, shown under the day on the habit's page and in the reply ("Noted 'felt dizzy' on this evening's pills"). A note is what the person said, never a reading of it: Helm doesn't call it a symptom or a side effect, and asked about it, answers from general knowledge with the strong health caution, marked.
- **Now and then, one short question.** After a tick on a habit with a length or a goal (practice, a run), Helm may ask one skippable question under the reply, "How was it?", answered in a few words or skipped; at most once a day per habit, never re-asked that day once skipped, spoken only when the tick was. On a health or care habit it asks only when the person added a note first, and only to keep the note where it helps: "Add 'felt dizzy' to your notes for Dr. Patel?" when a doctor or care project is known, otherwise nothing. The answers are notes like the rest, and they go with the learning switch (What Helm Learns, In Detail).
- **A window that passes is quiet.** An unticked line stays, muted, with "Window ended 10:00 AM", and can still be ticked until the day turns over. Nothing turns amber, nothing says "missed", nothing carries to tomorrow.
- **Progress is this week, in the habit's own unit.** The week runs Monday to today. Times for a habit with windows ("11 of 13 this week"), days that reached the number for a counted one ("4 of 6 days"), times for a number a week ("3 of 4"). Today counts once it is done; before that, the days already over. No streak, chain, best week, percentage, ring or bar. "How am I doing with water?" answers from the same figures.
- **A habit's page.** From its line's name, the Habits list, a goal or Search: identity (emoji, name, how often, "Since Oct 2 · from what you said"), its **stage** ("Building · since Oct 2", with a "?" that explains stages; none for a habit kept for someone else), a health habit's line "Health: [name] never moves this, only asks"; **This week**, the count and a Monday-first row of dots (filled when ticked, a hollow ring when not, a dash still to come, blank on a day off); **Earlier weeks**, one line each ("Oct 26 – Nov 1 · 13 of 14"), four, then "Show earlier"; then rows: each window, **Reminders** ("Off", "At the start of each window", "Only when it's getting late · 9:30 PM", "Stopped Dec 14 · formed", "Stay on · health", "Stay on · for Mom", with "Name on lock screen" when it shows), "Part of · 🎸 Get back into guitar", the source, and any pattern about it. ⋯ holds "Change name, emoji or times", "Pause for a while", "Take it off Today" (once Formed), "Stop this habit" ("End this habit" for one kept for someone else), and below a hairline "Delete habit and its weeks" (confirm, Undo).
- **The Habits list.** A small tile in Browse once a habit exists ("Habits · 3"), opening a list A to Z with search from 8: each row the emoji, name, this week's count and its stage when it isn't Building ("Holding"). Paused rows say until when. Folded below: "Formed · 2" (off Today, with the day each left), "Stopped · 1" and "Ended · 1". A habit kept for someone also shows on that person's page.

**A habit's life**

A habit moves through four stages: **Building**, **Holding**, **Formed**, and **Building again** when one that held slips. They are judged once a day by Helm's own code from the ticks alone, never by the AI (what the person says about a habit shapes what Helm notices, never its stage), and they keep moving on a day Helm isn't opened (Helm's server can read when each habit was due and ticked, never its name). A week counts toward a stage only when it is over (Monday to Sunday), and only on the days the habit was due, not paused, and Helm was in use (a tick of any habit, a turn or a task done that day); a week away from the phone is neither kept nor broken, and a week with fewer than 3 such due times is skipped.

A **kept week** is one where every due time was done, or all but one; for a counted habit, the number was reached on every due day but one; for a number a week, the number was reached.

| Stage | How it gets there | What the person sees | What changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Building** | Every new habit starts here | "Building · since Oct 2" on its page; "Building" in the Today line's meta while it is open | Reminders, if on, at the start of each window |
| **Holding** | The last 3 weeks were each kept (from Building or Building again) | "Holding · since Nov 23" | Reminders, if on, come only when it's getting late |
| **Formed** | At least 8 of the last 10 weeks kept, including the last 4, and in those 4 no reminder went out before the tick (or reminders were off); never before 10 weeks | "Formed · since Dec 14" | Reminders stop; a week later, once, the offer to take it off Today |
| **Building again** | 2 weeks in a row not kept, from Holding or Formed | "Building again · since Jan 11 · it held from Nov 23" | Reminders, if they were on, are back at the start of each window |

- **Off Today is not a stage.** A Formed habit the person takes off Today stays Formed ("Formed · off Today since Dec 18") and is no longer judged.
- **Each step is said once, plainly, where the habit is.** A stage change writes one line on the habit's page, under its line on Today that day, and in Activity, with the evidence and no praise: "Holding: kept each of the last 3 weeks. Reminders now come only when it's getting late. · Keep them as they were". A change on a day Helm wasn't opened is told the next time it is, dated the day it happened ("Since Tue: Holding, kept each of the last 3 weeks"). Nothing about a stage is pushed or spoken first.
- **Slipping back is not failing.** "Building again: 2 weeks not kept. Reminders are back at the start of each window." (the second sentence only when reminders are on). One bad week changes nothing. The page keeps when it held ("Held Nov 23 – Jan 10") as plain history, never as a lost record.
- **No countdown.** Nothing says how many weeks are left to the next stage, counts weeks in a row or fills between stages: a week that doesn't count would reset a countdown, which is a streak by another name.
- **What "formed" means, honestly.** The stage's "?" says: "There's no set number of days that makes a habit stick, and Helm doesn't count toward one. It calls a habit formed when you've kept it most weeks for over two months, the last month without needing a reminder. It's Helm's rule of thumb from your own ticks, not a measure of you." Helm never quotes a number of days from research, never says the habit is "automatic" or "locked in", and never calls a habit that slipped "broken".
- **Health and care habits stop short.** A health habit (a medicine, a dose, a treatment) goes from Building to Holding and no further: Helm never calls a medicine formed, so it is never offered off Today. A habit kept for someone else (Mom's doses) shows no stage at all: the record of what was given is the point.

**Reminders while it builds**

Helm never speaks first, with one exception the person turns on: a reminder on a habit, sent as a push by the site's own reminders (the same push other apps send, on each phone or browser where the person turned push on). Nothing else in Helm sends a push.

- **Offered once, where the need starts.** The card that makes a habit with a window carries one quiet capsule: "Remind me while it builds · Turn on". Its small sheet says what to expect ("A reminder at the start of each window, unless you've already ticked it. As it holds they come only when it's getting late, and once it's formed they stop. Change it any time on the habit."; for a health habit, "Reminders for medicine don't fade unless you say so." in place of the middle sentence) and asks the phone for permission on that tap. Not offered again on that habit; the Reminders row on its page is always there. "Remind me to take my pills" said turns them on in the same turn (with "Allow reminders on this phone · Turn on" when this phone hasn't allowed push yet, since a phone only asks on a tap).
- **They keep coming when Helm isn't opened.** Helm's server sends each reminder from what it can read: when each habit is due and its windows, Your day's time zone and quiet hours, its stage and reminder setting, and whether that window has been ticked, checked again the moment before it goes. Nothing depends on the phone opening Helm first, so a nurse who goes a week of nights without opening Helm still gets "before bed" each morning.
- **What a reminder says, by default.** The title is the assistant's name; the words name no habit, because a push travels through the phone maker's service and shows on a locked screen, and Helm's names are sealed:

| When | One habit | Several at once |
|---|---|---|
| At the start, in a named part of the day | "Your evening habit is up" | "Your evening habits are up" |
| At the start, at a set time or through the day | "A habit is up" | "2 habits are up" |
| Getting late, in a named part of the day | "Your evening habit is open until 10:00 PM" | "Your evening habits are open until 10:00 PM" |
| Getting late, through the day | "A habit for today is still open" | "2 habits for today are still open" |

  Never a name, an emoji, a dose, a person, a count of days, "missed", "streak" or praise. Reminders that fall in different parts of the day and go as one use the set-time words. Tapping one opens Today on that day at the line, tick ready; there is no "Done" button on the notification yet.
- **Showing a habit's name, if the person chooses.** The habit's Reminders sheet has a switch, off by default: "Show its name on the lock screen", with "A name shown here isn't sealed like the rest of your things. Our server keeps a readable copy to send it, it passes through Google's push service (Apple's on an iPhone), and anyone who picks up your phone can read it." (for a care habit it adds "For medicine, that's the name of what you take.") With it on, that habit's reminders read "💊 Pills · until 10:00 PM", "💊 Pills is still open · until 10:00 PM" or "💧 Water · 2 of 6 so far"; sent together with habits that don't show theirs, "💊 Pills and 1 more evening habit". Said in a turn ("show water's name on my lock screen"), Helm says the same warning (aloud when the turn was spoken) and asks "Show it?"; only a spoken or tapped yes turns it on, and anything else leaves it off. Turning it off, stopping the habit, deleting it or Delete everything removes the plain copy at once; renaming the habit updates it. On a health or care habit the switch is offered too, off by default, and its warning says what the name gives away: "Pills will show on your lock screen, so anyone who sees your phone can tell you take them." ("…can tell Mom takes them" for a habit kept for someone), then the same sentence about the plain copy. It is never turned on for the person, never offered on the capsule, and never on for a habit by default because another one has it. The same switch, off by default, comes to every other thing that can remind as it is built (Private Things, In Detail).
- **It adapts to the stage.**
  - **Building and Building again:** at the start of each due window (or at the set time). A through-the-day habit with a count gets one in the middle of the person's day, only while it is under half (up to 3 times of the person's choosing on the sheet, each only while short).
  - **Holding:** only when it is getting late: 30 minutes before the window ends (halfway through a window shorter than an hour); a through-the-day habit, 2 hours before the day ends, only while under half.
  - **Formed and off Today:** none. "Keep reminding me" on the line that says it formed, or said, keeps them at Holding's pace from then on; that is the only way a formed habit reminds. If a formed habit kept on Today slips to Building again and had reminders on before it formed, they come back at the start of each window, told with "Keep them off".
  - **Never for what's done.** A reminder checks just before it goes that the window isn't already ticked, and never goes on a paused day, a day the habit isn't due, after its window has ended, or for a habit stopped, ended or off Today. Reminders due within 15 minutes of each other go as one, and a day has at most 6 habit reminders, any more joining the nearest earlier one.
  - **Inside the person's day.** Reminders keep to Your day: a window that falls outside it (a bedtime stretch after the day ends) reminds only when the person turns on "Remind me outside my day" for that habit, which the sheet offers when it applies. These are reminders the person set, so the site's limits for pushes nobody asked for don't apply to them.
- **Each step back is told, and can be undone.** Fading is Helm's own adjustment to what the person asked for, so it acts and tells under the habit's line on Today and on its page: "Water is holding, so reminders now come only at 7:00 PM if you're short. · Keep them as they were". A step back on a day Helm wasn't opened is told the next time it is.
- **Health habits ask first.** A health habit's reminders never step back on their own; once it is Holding, Helm asks, as a dashed card on its page and its Today line: "Pills is holding. Want fewer reminders?" with "Kept each of the last 3 weeks." · "Keep them all" · "Only if I'm late"; "Keep them all" is a no under the back-off (What Helm Learns, In Detail). Until it is answered, nothing changes, however long Helm goes unopened.
- **Care habits ask first, and never stop on their own.** A habit kept for someone else (Mom's doses) has no stage; after 3 weeks ticked in time Helm asks once: "Mom's evening pills have been ticked in time each of the last 3 weeks. Remind you only if they aren't ticked by 9:40 PM?" · "Keep them all" · "Only if late". Its reminders never stop because of anything Helm judged; only the person turns them off.
- **Off.** On the habit's Reminders row, "stop reminding me about water", pausing, stopping or ending the habit, or Settings → Helm on its own → Habit reminders, which lists every habit that reminds and has "Pause all habit reminders" for a holiday. Turning push off on a phone stops it there only.

**Taking a formed habit off Today**

- **Offered once, a week after it forms, under the back-off.** On the habit's page and (on a day it is due) under its line on Today, one dashed offer, counted as Today's one suggestion: "Floss looks like part of your day now. Take it off Today?" with "Kept 9 of the last 10 weeks, the last 4 without a reminder · 58 times since Sep 7." · "Keep it on Today" · "Take it off Today". "Keep it on Today" is a no: "Okay. I won't ask again before Jan 15." (4 weeks, then 8; the third: "Okay. I won't ask again. Say 'take Floss off Today' whenever you like."), and it comes back only if the weeks since the no still read as formed. "Stop asking about this" on the offer's sheet ends it at once. "Take Floss off Today" said is a yes at any time once it is Formed, even during a wait.
- **What it does.** "Floss is off Today. It's under Formed on your Habits list, with its 58 times since Sep 7. · Undo". The habit leaves Today, its reminders end, and Helm stops judging it. Its page stays, read only: "Formed · off Today since Dec 18", its weeks, and its count since it began.
- **What it leaves.** One moment among your own moments, drawn from the habit itself rather than copied ("Floss became part of your day · Dec 18"), so it joins your life timeline when that comes (Issue 89) and goes if the habit is deleted. Answers can still use it ("When did I start flossing every night?").
- **Ticked again after.** "Flossed" said for a habit off Today counts nothing and says so: "Floss is off Today since Dec 18, so I didn't count it. Bring it back?" with "Bring it back" as the turn's one follow-up. Bringing it back, there or from its page, puts it on Today as Formed with reminders off ("Floss is back on Today. Reminders stay off.").
- **Before it forms.** A habit not yet Formed has no "Take it off Today"; it can be paused or stopped.

**Pause, stop, end**

- **Pause and stop.** "Pause water while I'm away" pauses it until a date or until asked; paused days count for nothing either way and its stage waits. "Stop the stretching habit" ends it without it being formed, under "Stopped", weeks kept. Changing when or how often applies from today; past days keep what they had, and a habit's stage is judged from the change on as from its start, except that a Holding or Formed habit keeps its stage through a change of time.
- **Ended, for care.** A habit kept for someone else is never taken off Today or stopped by anything Helm offers; the person ends it ("Mom's off metoprolol now"), and it folds under "Ended" with its weeks and every tick kept.
- **Health and care habits.** A habit that names a medicine, a dose, a treatment or someone's care, or is kept for someone else, is a health habit, set by Helm's code and never unmarked without the person. Helm can miss one (a brand it doesn't know: "start my Lexapro"), so the person can mark it too: "This is medicine or care" on the habit's sheet, or said ("this is a medicine"), with Undo; it then gets everything below and is private. A habit the person marked private that counts something like a dose ("2 tablets") is asked once, under the reply: "Is this a medicine? Medicine reminders don't fade, and Helm never moves it." · "Yes, it's medicine" · "No". Unmarking one is the person's, with one confirm, as for private (Private Things, In Detail). Being a health habit is about how Helm treats it (below); being private is a separate thing that every health and care thing is too (Private Things, In Detail). Its dose and times are kept exactly as said, a different dose said later is a contradiction question, and learning never moves it. "What if she missed a dose?" is answered from general knowledge, marked, with the strong health caution, never from the habit.

**When reminders meet real life**

Each in the words the person sees:

- **This phone can't take reminders.** A blocked browser or an iPhone where Helm isn't on the Home Screen says why in the reminder sheet with the site's own words; the habit is kept and its Reminders row reads "On · no phone can get them yet · Turn on here".
- **A phone stops taking them** (signed out, or its notifications turned off): the row reads "On · not on this phone · Turn on here"; any other phone keeps getting them.
- **Two phones.** Both get each reminder; a tick on either stops the rest.
- **Ticked with no signal.** The tick is kept and stops the reminder once the phone reconnects; if the reminder had already gone, tapping it opens Today with the line already "Done 7:52 PM".
- **Tapped late.** A reminder tapped after the day has turned over opens that day, where the line can still be ticked for 7 days ("Ticked for Tue, Nov 10").
- **Tapped for a habit that changed.** Paused, stopped, deleted or moved to another day since: Today opens with "That habit isn't on Today any more."
- **Away in another time zone.** Reminders follow Your day's time zone, not the phone's, so they come at home times until the person changes Your day; the Reminders sheet says "Times are in Pacific time (Your day)". Saying they'll be away in another time zone brings one offer to use that time for those days (Where You and Your People Are, In Detail).
- **The clocks change.** Reminders stay at the same time on the clock.
- **Helm's server can't send.** Nothing is sent late: a reminder more than 30 minutes late is dropped rather than arriving after its window.
- **Delete everything.** Every pending reminder and every plain copy of a name goes with it.
- **Learning switched off.** Stages and fading reminders keep working; they are the habit's own rules the person turned on.
- **Nothing ticked for weeks.** Reminders keep going while Helm isn't opened, but once nothing at all has been ticked in Helm for 3 weeks, one last reminder says "Habit reminders are paused until you're back. Tick anything to start them again." and then they stop, care and health habits included, so nothing stops without being said. Each Reminders row reads "Paused Dec 3 · nothing ticked for 3 weeks". Opening Helm and ticking anything starts them again, told on Today ("Reminders are back on"); a window that passed while they were stopped is never reminded late.

**What is kept**

- **The habit:** its name, emoji and window labels sealed; its schedule, windows' times, stage and the day it reached it, reminder setting and who it is for readable to Helm's code, on the phone and on Helm's server, so reminders and stages work without the AI and without Helm being opened. A plain copy of the name only for a habit the person chose to show on the lock screen. **Each day:** one small record of that day's ticks and their times, readable as ticks (never what they were for), and any note or answer to "How was it?" with them, sealed. **A stage change:** a line in Activity. Nothing about a habit is shared with any other app.

### Goals, In Detail



**Next Steps.** The owner's words: "Things a user is trying to do. For example, I want to get back into piano or guitar", and their earlier Odysseus Planner, "a tool to allow you to plan for all your goals… break it down by your milestone, your goals, your day-to-day tasks… it just builds you up." The wanting lives in Helm; the doing may be its own app (Where Does It Live?, In Detail).

- **A goal is a project about becoming.** Same record, same Projects view, under its own "Goals" divider above Projects (with "New goal" beside it): a name and emoji, why it matters in the person's words, milestones, the small regular step, its people (a teacher), its tasks, a linked topic in Knowledge (Next Steps) and its procedures. A goal may have a target date or be ongoing; getting better at guitar never has to end.
- **Started by saying it.** "I want to get back into guitar" starts a goal (not a task), shown with Undo, then asks one skippable question: "Why does it matter to you?"; the answer is kept word for word as the why ("Kept that as the why."), however it came out. Then, once, a dashed offer of a regular step ("Want a small regular step? Practise 15 minutes on two evenings a week") with "Add it" and Not now. A wish said in passing ("I'd love to learn Italian someday") is asked first: "Make this a goal?". Starting one can carry a Pause or Hold Off line (Pause or Hold Off, In Detail). Saying how much it matters ("guitar matters more than Italian") places it in What matters most, never a scale picked from a list (What Matters Most, In Detail). "Make the move a goal" or "this is a project, not a goal" switches it either way, with Undo.
- **The why, in three forms.** The owner's words: kept verbatim, Helm offers to clean it up, and it writes a short summary for itself.
  - **Your words, always kept.** The answer as said or typed, "um"s and all, is the original, and is never changed by Helm.
  - **A tidy version, only on a yes.** When tidying would change more than punctuation (a spoken, rambling answer), the reply offers it once, under the original: "Want it tidied? 'I want to play again because it was the thing that made me happy at school, and I'd like to play with my sister at Christmas.'" · "Use this" · "Keep mine". "Use this" makes the tidy version what the goal shows; "Keep mine" or no answer leaves the original showing, and the offer isn't made again for that why. A one-line answer is never offered a tidy-up.
  - **A short summary, for finding and filing.** Helm writes a few words from the why ("Joy from school days; playing with my sister"), used to tag the goal, to match it when the person mentions it, and in answers ("Why did I want guitar again?"); it is never shown as the why. It sits on the why's sheet with the hollow ring of something inferred ("How [name] files it"), can be corrected there, and is written again from the original whenever the why changes.
  - **Seeing what you said.** The goal shows the tidy version when the person chose it, marked "Tidied · See what you said", which opens the original with its day; "Show my words instead" switches back with Undo. Saying a new why ("actually, it's more about the band") replaces it: the new words become the original, the tidy offer and summary start again, and the earlier why stays in the goal's Activity with Undo.
- **A goal's page.** A project page: identity (emoji, name, the why quoted with "Why · You said · Sep 27", or "Why · Tidied · See what you said" when the person chose the tidy version, "A goal · since Sep 27"), the evidence line, then tabs Tasks · Milestones · People. Tasks opens with a "Habits" group (each habit with this week's count and its stage, opening its page; ticks live on Today) and "Add a habit", then the goal's tasks as a project's.
- **Broken down when asked.** "Help me plan guitar" drafts milestones and a regular step from what the person has said and their notes, listing what it used, with anything from general knowledge marked "Not from your records". Each is dashed until kept; nothing is added on its own, since the goal is the person's.
- **Milestones are steps toward it.** "Chords clean: G, C, D and Em", "Play Blackbird all the way through", each with a date or none. Saying "I played Blackbird all the way through" marks it reached, with the day; a reached milestone stays on the goal and shows on the life timeline (Issue 89) drawn from the goal, never copied.
- **The regular step.** One or more habits (Practice guitar · 20 min · Mon, Tue, Thu and Sat evenings), or planned tasks. The habit is the person's, kept at its own windows; Helm suggests moving it when a time keeps not working (What Helm Learns, In Detail). A habit that becomes Formed and is taken off Today leaves the goal's Habits group for "Formed" under it, and the goal says so ("Practice became part of your week · Feb 2").
- **Progress as showing up and milestones reached.** At most two parts: this week's showing up ("Practised 3 of 4 this week"), and milestones ("Chords clean in 22 days", or "2 of 5 milestones reached", a count, never a fraction drawn as a bar). With no regular step: the next milestone and "No habit yet". Quiet for a while: "No practice in 12 days", in the normal ink, with no nudge. Never a percentage, a bar, a colour grade, "behind" or "on track"; an overdue task inside it still says so, as a task.
- **Deleted, or turned into a project.** The confirm names the goal's habits and asks what becomes of them: "Delete Get back into guitar? Its habit: Practice guitar." · "Keep it" (ticked) · "Delete it" (with several habits: "Its 2 habits: …" · Keep them · Delete them); turning it into a project asks the same. Kept, each habit stays as it was, on Today with its weeks and stage, on its own and out of the goal's place in What matters most; deleted, each goes with its weeks, with Undo.
- **Set aside, finish, or reopen.** "Set guitar aside for now" pauses the goal and its habits in one step and folds it behind "Set aside · 1"; nothing calls it failed, and paused days count for nothing. "I've done it" marks it reached, shown on the timeline like a milestone; either comes back with Reopen and Undo.
- **What is kept.** A goal is a project marked as a goal, with its why sealed like any of the person's words: the original, the tidy version and which one shows, and the summary, which points back to the original it was written from; its showing up is counted from its habits' ticks, never stored as a score.
- **A practice app later.** An app for practising (sheet music, a metronome) would be its own app; through Connected apps it could report sessions into the goal's showing up. Not planned here.

### What Matters Most, In Detail



**Next Steps.** The owner's words: "The user should be able to indicate it's importance using natural language, and it's stored and used by Helm. We shouldn't necessarily restrict to 2, nor should we allow just everything to be the same high-importance." Helm keeps one order of what matters to the person, said in their own words, so that its plans, its answers and its Pause or Hold Off lines lean the way the person would.

- **What can be placed.** Projects, goals and habits, except health and care (a health habit, a carer's dose, a care project): Helm never pauses those whatever the order, so a place would mean nothing, and the order shows them together under "Always kept: health and care". A habit with a goal sits where its goal sits unless it is placed itself. A task keeps its own Importance (Must, Should, Could) as in the MVP, and a person keeps their level: "Maya matters most" changes Maya's level (People, Pets and Organizations, In Detail), never this order.
- **Said in plain words, said back with Undo.** The assistant reads any way of putting it into a place in the order; the reply says the order that results:
  - "The wedding matters most" puts it first, alone, and what was first moves to second: "The wedding comes first now, then work. · Undo"
  - "Guitar matters more than Italian" puts guitar just above Italian (nothing moves if it already was).
  - "Work and the wedding matter most" puts both first, side by side; "Guitar matters as much as work" puts it beside work.
  - "Italian can wait" or "the garden isn't important right now" puts it under **Can wait**, below everything not placed.
  - "The wedding matters most until June" holds it first until Jun 30; then it goes back to unplaced, with a line on its page ("No longer first since Jun 30, as you said").
  - "Guitar really matters to me", with no comparison, places it first when nothing else is placed; otherwise one skippable question: "Where does guitar go: before the wedding, beside it, or after it?"
  - About something Helm doesn't have ("the move matters most" with no such project): "There's no project called the move. Start one?" (Start it · No). Unclear what it means ("that matters most" with nothing in view): "Which one?"
- **An order, not a flag.** From the top: first (one thing, or a few side by side), then each next place, then everything the person hasn't placed, then Can wait. There is no scale to pick from and no cap on how many things are placed. Each placed thing keeps the words that placed it and the day ("You said · Nov 8: 'the wedding matters most, then work'").
- **"Which comes first?" when too much is tied at the top.** When what was said would put 3 or more things first side by side (or "it all matters"), Helm makes the change as said, then asks one pick-one under it: "The wedding, work and guitar are all first right now. Which comes first?" with each of them and Skip. The pick goes first; the others share second. Skip leaves them side by side and the question waits in Needs you; until it is answered, the nearest date breaks the tie. It is asked once per tie, and again only when something new joins the top. Two side by side at the top asks nothing.
- **When nothing was said.** Things not placed are ordered by their nearest target date or dated milestone, soonest first, and those with no date come after them with no order between them. Helm never places anything on its own, and never reads importance from how often something is mentioned or done.
- **Where it shows.** "What matters most" in How [name] sees you lists the order, ties side by side, then Can wait and Always kept at the foot, with "Change the order"; a placed project, goal or habit carries one quiet line under its name ("Comes first · you said Nov 8", "After the wedding", "Beside work", "Can wait"). Asked "what matters most to me right now?", the assistant says it in a sentence ("The wedding, then work. Italian can wait."). An unplaced thing shows nothing: no "Not set".
- **Changed by saying it again, or by hand.** Any new sentence moves things the same way; "Change the order" moves a thing up or down, beside another, to Can wait, or out of the order, each with Undo. "Take guitar out of what matters" makes it unplaced.
- **When the thing itself changes.** Finished or reached: it leaves the order and the rest close up, said in the finish line ("Finished the wedding. Work comes first now. · Undo"). Set aside or paused: it keeps its place, marked "Set aside". Deleted: it leaves. Reopened, or the finish undone: it goes back where it was.
- **Where Helm uses it.** Pause or Hold Off never names the first thing or anything above the thing it names, and its "something that comes first is going without" reason reads this order (Pause or Hold Off, In Detail). Today's suggestions lean toward higher places among tasks otherwise level. Answers like "what should I work on?" lean on it and say so ("Starting with the wedding, since you said it comes first"). It is never used to nag, never spoken first and never pushed.
- **Health and care.** Never placed (What can be placed). Said in an order ("my pills matter most", "Mom's care comes first"), the rest of the sentence is applied and the reply says "Mom's care is always kept, so it doesn't need a place."; Pause or Hold Off never names a health or care item.
- **What is kept.** One order per person: each place with the thing it holds, the words that placed it and the day, sealed like everything the person says; how the places stack is counted by Helm's own code, never guessed by the AI. Changes are in Activity with Undo.

### What Helm Learns, In Detail



**Next Steps; replaces "suggestions tuned by what you kept, declined or moved".** The owner's words: "Does the system learn… present to the user: I see that the evenings around these days don't tend to work for you… keep this fluid, which is safe. If the user later changed their mind, how do we prevent the agent from suggesting this… again right away?" Helm learns when things work, what gets done and what the person tells it about how things went, in four layers, and every layer is visible. The owner, later: reading what the person says is essential, since it is how Helm learns their habits and tendencies from how they speak, not only from taps. It is on from the start, with a switch (below).

1. **Notice.** From what Helm already keeps: habit ticks and their times, what Helm suggested on Today and what became of it (kept, done, "Not today", moved), when tasks are ticked done, rest days and shift calendars; and from what the person tells it about a habit, a goal or a plan: a note with a tick ("took my pills, felt dizzy"), a reason or a feeling said in a turn ("don't feel like piano tonight", "I'm tired, skipping the stretch"), and answers to "How was it?" (Habits, In Detail). A pattern needs real evidence over like occasions: at least 4 of the last 5 (4 of the last 5 Tuesday evenings with guitar not practised), across at least 3 weeks; a feeling said about one habit needs 3 times in 2 weeks (below). One bad week is never a pattern, a day Helm wasn't opened at all is not evidence either way, and changing a habit's time starts its evidence over.
   - **How it looks.** Ticks and plans are looked over once a day, on whichever phone opens Helm first that day. What was said is picked out as each turn is read, in the same reading, and kept sealed with the turn: Activity shows it on the turn's row ("Noted for guitar: 'too tired'"), so nothing is noted out of sight. Once a night, for a person with the switch on and something new said that day, Helm's server looks over the day's notes and what was said beside the ticks, and anything it finds is shown the next time Helm is opened. Nothing found is ever pushed or spoken first.
   - **Private things are looked at too.** The owner decided that whether a thing is private doesn't decide what the nightly look reads: Helm learns from everything the person said, private and health things included, so "felt dizzy" three evenings running can be noticed. Private governs only what is named outside Helm (a lock screen, a reminder, a copy, an export, anything sent); what the nightly look sends to the AI service is covered in Privacy, In Detail.
   - **Kept with your things, not on one phone.** Every pattern, everything picked out of a turn, every answer to an offer and every setting here is a sealed record that syncs, whichever device worked it out: a new phone, a second browser or a reinstall shows the same What I've noticed, the same waits and the same "How [name] sees you".
   - **Always answered, then acted on or noted.** Whatever the person says about a habit, a goal or a plan gets a line that answers it; nothing is ever met with silence. Helm acts at once when (a) the person asked for something ("skip guitar tonight"), (b) it changes today's plan or something due within 48 hours ("too tired for guitar tonight" takes tonight's practice off the plan: "Okay, no guitar tonight. Noted you're tired. · Undo"), or (c) it is about health or care ("felt dizzy after my pills" is kept on that day's tick at once). Otherwise it says it's noted: "I never feel like piano on Sundays" gets "Noted. I'll keep an eye on Sundays." With "Notice what works for me" off, the answer and anything acted on stay, but nothing says it's noted or that Helm will keep an eye: "Okay, no guitar tonight. · Undo"; "I never feel like piano on Sundays" gets "Okay, Sundays don't suit piano." and promises nothing.
2. **Show.** Each pattern is a row in **What I've noticed**, the first section of "How [name] sees you" once there is one: the hollow ring of something inferred, the pattern in a sentence ("Tuesday evenings don't tend to work for guitar practice") and one line of evidence and state ("4 of the last 5 Tuesdays · Asking you"). Three rows, then "Show all 7"; asking-you first, then changes Helm made, then the rest; stopped and no-longer-true ones fold behind "Not used · 1". A row opens a sheet: what it is based on (each occasion, newest first, at most 8, with the person's own words when they said something: "Tue, Nov 3 · Not practised · you said 'too tired after work'"), a "Maybe because" line only when the person's own records or words hold a reason ("Sprint review on your Work calendar ran to 7:00 PM on 4 of these days"), its state in words with its date, and its actions. Never a guess about mood, health or energy beyond quoting what was said.
3. **Act carefully, by who set the thing.**
   - **What Helm planned itself** (its own suggestions on Today, the pace of a habit's reminders): Helm acts and tells. The next plan leaves Tuesday evenings empty and says so where it shows: "No suggestions this evening: Tuesday evenings went unused 4 of the last 5 weeks. · Undo"; Activity keeps the line.
   - **What the person set** (a habit's days or times, a task they kept for a slot): a dashed suggestion, once, on the thing's own page and on Today on the day it applies: "Practise on Wednesday evenings instead of Tuesday?" with the evidence, "Move to Wednesday" and Not now. Nothing changes until they say yes.
   - **Health and care** (a health habit, a task about someone's care, a medicine): never moved and never given a new time. At most one question whether what is kept is still right: "Mom's evening dose has been ticked after 10 PM on 5 of the last 7 days. Is 9:00 PM still when she takes it?" with "It's right" and "Change the time" (the person types it).
4. **Remember the answer.** Each pattern is one record per pattern and the thing it is about, with its state, dates and evidence, sealed.
   - **States, in the person's words.** Asking you; Changed by [name] (with the date); Kept; Not now · not before Dec 6; Stopped ("won't suggest this again"); No longer true.
   - **A no waits, then needs new evidence.** Not now, Not true or an Undo is a no. After the first no it waits 4 weeks, after the second 8; the third no is the last, and it is never offered again unless the person asks. Once a wait is over it comes back only on evidence gathered after the no and as strong as before (4 of the next 5 Tuesdays), never on the evidence already turned down, and the offer says so: "You said not now on Nov 8. Since then, 4 of 5 Tuesdays went without practice." The answer is said back as a date: "Okay. Not before Dec 6, and only if Tuesdays keep slipping." The third: "Okay. I won't bring this up again unless you ask."
   - **"Stop suggesting that" ends it now**, at any point; it moves under "Not used" with "You can suggest this again".
   - **"Tuesday evenings work again" retires it.** Helm goes back to planning as before and the evidence starts from nothing. A habit already moved stays moved; moving it back is the person's edit.
   - **"That's right" makes it said.** It turns filled; Helm acts on it for what it plans, and for what the person set still asks before each change.
- **What counts as a signal, both ways.** So it can be built and checked, Helm listens for a fixed set of signals about a habit, a goal or a planned thing, said in any words:
  - **Struggling:** tired, reluctant ("don't feel like it"), too busy, unwell or in pain, bored, stressed.
  - **Working:** enjoyed it, easy, proud, energised, made progress.
  - Anything else said is kept as the person's words on the tick or turn, and used only as a quoted reason, never as a signal.
- **When it's a struggle.** When the person signals struggling about the same habit 3 or more times in 2 weeks, Helm raises it once, as a dashed card on the habit's page and in Today's one suggestion slot, quoting them and offering ways on that fit the signal: "You've said you were too tired for guitar 3 times since Nov 2, each on a weekday evening. Try something different?" with "Try weekend mornings" (a time the ticks show works, when there is one), "Make it 10 minutes" (half its length, or one less a week for a number a week), "Try something else" (asks what instead, in a few words, and offers it as the goal's new step with the old one stopped) and Not now. Too busy leans to a different time, bored to something else, tired, reluctant and stressed to a smaller version; unwell or in pain offers only "Pause it for a while?", never a smaller or harder version, and never a word about health. Nothing changes without a yes (a tap or said); a smaller version keeps its stage. It is ask-first because the habit is the person's, it follows the back-off below, and it is never raised on a health or care habit: there a repeated signal is kept in the notes, and at most one question offers to put it where it helps ("You've noted feeling dizzy after your evening pills 3 times since Nov 2. Add it to your notes for Dr. Patel?").
- **When it's working.** Working signals hold things where they are: a time that keeps getting "enjoyed it" or "easy" is kept clear of other suggestions and is the time Helm offers when something else moves. When the person signals working about the same habit 3 or more times in 2 weeks and it is Holding or Formed, Helm may offer once to grow it: "Practice has felt easy 3 times this week. Make it 25 minutes?" · "Make it 25 minutes" · "Keep it as it is" (a no under the back-off). Never praise, never a score, never on a health or care habit, and growing is never offered while the habit is Building.
- **The same back-off for every offer in Helm.** A suggested plan slot, procedure run, link or role declined with Not now follows the same waits (round 1's "never offered again" is replaced; an MVP Not now already given counts as one no from when it was given). This is Helm's only: other apps keep "never again".
- **Paced.** At most one new pattern shown a day, three waiting at once, and one suggestion on Today at a time, shared by every kind of offer and picked in this order: a question about a health or care item, then a Pause or Hold Off line, then an offer about a habit's life (fewer reminders, taking it off Today), then the rest of what Helm noticed; a Pause or Hold Off line shown at the moment something starts takes that day's one. Patterns are never counted in "need you", never spoken, never on home at rest.
- **Learns when and what, never who you are.** A pattern is about times, days, which things get done and what the person said about them. Helm may quote what they said on the thing it was about ("you said you were too tired 3 times"), and a settled tendency is shown in their words under "What works for you" (How [name] Sees You, In Detail); it never turns a signal into a fact about them ("you have low energy in the evenings"), never guesses a cause they didn't give, never stores unwell or in pain as a health fact, and never adds any of it to "Who you are".
- **The switch.** "Notice what works for me" is on from the start, at the top of What I've noticed (shown before the first pattern as its own row: "[name] looks for what keeps not working, from what you tick and what you tell it about how things went. Once a night it looks over that day's notes, through the same AI service as your conversation.") and in Settings under Helm on its own. Off: Helm stops noticing, stops picking out what was said and its nightly look, stops asking "How was it?", stops acting on what it noticed (its plans go back to plain) and stops Pause or Hold Off lines; what it noticed stays, greyed, until turned on again or forgotten. Notes the person added to a tick stay on the tick, since they are the person's words. A habit's stages and its fading reminders are the habit's own and keep working.
- **Asked about.** "Why didn't you suggest anything for Tuesday?" answers from the pattern; "what have you noticed?" reads the list; "suggest that again" lifts a wait or a stop.
- **Everything it noticed points to where it came from.** A pattern, a quoted feeling and a goal's summary are never kept apart from their sources: each lists the ticks, notes and turns it came from, is worked out again rather than edited, and goes or is worked out again when one of those is deleted or undone. Nothing Helm noticed is stored anywhere else, so what it says and what it shows are always the same.
- **Forget it all.** At the foot of What I've noticed, "Forget what you've noticed" clears every pattern, its answers and what was picked out of turns for it, with Undo; plans and habits already changed stay as they are, and the person's own notes on ticks stay. Anything still being worked out when the person forgets what was noticed, or turns the switch off, is thrown away when it comes back and never shown.

### Pause or Hold Off, In Detail



**Next Steps.** The owner's words: "knowing when a user should pause or stop something… letting the user know that things have reached a point where it would be advisable to pause on something or perhaps unwise to start. Of course, this would need to be based on some sense of priority, timeline, etc." Helm says, rarely and with its reasons, "this might be worth pausing" or "maybe hold off starting this". It advises and never blocks: what the person said is always made first, nothing waits on the answer, and nothing is paused without their yes.

- **Four kinds of reason.** Only what Helm can count from the person's own records, never a guess at how they feel. The figures are first settings, tuned when it is built:
  - **Too many goals at once:** starting a goal while 3 or more are already going; or, looking at what's running, 4 or more going and one of them with nothing done for 21 days (no habit ticked, task done or milestone reached).
  - **A deadline that won't fit:** for a project or goal with a target date or a dated milestone in the next 60 days, the open work due before it needs at least an hour more than the free time Today's planner could give it by then (Your day, less events and the planner's gaps, at how full the person plans a day), with 3 or more open tasks in it. Counted only when at least half of those tasks have a length the person gave; otherwise Helm says nothing rather than guess.
  - **What comes first is going without:** something higher in What matters most (or, where nothing was said, with the nearer date) has had nothing done for 14 days, while something lower had 3 or more things done in the same 14 (What Matters Most, In Detail).
  - **A busy stretch ahead:** in the next 14 days, 3 full days in a row or 5 full days in all, a full day being one the calendar keeps at least three-quarters busy (rest days and shifts count as full). Used when a goal, project or habit starts, and once per stretch for a goal whose regular step falls on those days.
- **How strong before Helm says anything.** Two reasons of different kinds, or one deadline that won't fit; anything less says nothing. A single task gets a line only when its own deadline won't fit and it has a length. Nothing is said in the first 7 days after Helm's first habit or goal, nothing while "Notice what works for me" is off, and nothing about a health or care item: a care project, a health habit or a task about someone's care is never told to pause or hold off, and starting one never carries a line (its time still counts in the figures).
- **Two moments, nowhere else.**
  - **When you start something** (a goal, a project, a habit, or a task with a deadline): the thing is made as said with Undo, then, before any follow-up question, one dashed card under it, not spoken: "Maybe hold Italian until Dec 1?" with a line per reason ("3 goals already going: the wedding, guitar and running." · "6 full days on your calendar through Nov 30.") · "Start it now" · "Hold it until Dec 1". The spoken reply only says "Added Italian as a goal. One thought on timing is on screen."
  - **When you look at it** (its page, and on Today at most one at a time in the one suggestion slot): a dashed card on the thing that could pause: "Pause Italian for a while?" with "The wedding: 9 tasks due by Dec 1 need about 11 hours; your calendar has about 6 free." · "Italian: nothing done in 12 days." · "Keep going" · "Pause until Dec 2".
  - **A task whose own deadline won't fit:** "Friday looks full for this" with "Due Fri, Nov 13: about 3 hours of work, 1h 20m free before then." · "Keep Friday" · "Find a later day".
  - **Asked for:** "am I taking on too much?", "should I pause something?" or "is it a good time to start Spanish?" answers from the same figures, naming at most one thing, even during a wait (asked, not suggested). "Could I go away this weekend?" counts the days away as full and answers with any deadline that would no longer fit, beside what has to be done first (Use Cases This Area Must Carry). Never pushed, never spoken first, never on home at rest, never counted in "need you".
- **Which thing it names.** Never a health or care item, never the first thing in What matters most or anything above what it protects, and never one with its own deadline in the next 2 weeks (a pause doesn't move a deadline). Of the rest: the lowest in What matters most (Can wait first), then the one with the least done lately. "Why, and other choices" lists the others it could be.
- **What each answer does, in its words.**
  - **Hold it until a date:** "Holding Italian until Tue, Dec 1. It starts that morning. · Undo" (a habit stays off Today, a goal or project sits under "Not yet"); on the day, Today says "Italian starts today. · Hold it again".
  - **Pause until a date:** "Paused Italian until Wed, Dec 2. [name] won't plan it until then. · Undo" (a goal pauses with its habits; a project's tasks stay, off Today's suggestions; a habit pauses); on the day: "Italian is back on your plans. · Pause again".
  - **Set another aside instead** (on the sheet, when the reason is too many goals): "Set guitar aside until Dec 2 instead".
  - **Change the date** (on the sheet, for a deadline that won't fit): opens the project's or milestone's date; the card goes once the work fits.
  - **Pause until a day you pick**, and **Find a later day** for a task, open a date.
  - **Start it now, Keep going or Keep Friday** is a no: "Okay. I won't bring this up before Dec 6."; the third: "Okay. I won't raise this again. You can still ask."
  - **Stop suggesting this:** "[name] won't suggest pausing Italian again. You can still ask." "Don't tell me to pause things" asks "Just Italian" · "Any pausing advice"; the second turns all of it off, with "Pausing advice is off · Turn on" in What I've noticed.
- **Under the back-off.** Each line is a record like a pattern, one per reason and the thing it is about (for a start, the kind of thing: "a fourth goal"). A no waits 4 weeks, then 8, then the line is never shown again unless asked, and it comes back only on new figures at least as strong as before and with something in them changed (a task added, a date moved). ### Trusted Browsers, In Detail

Helm follows the site's Trust this browser standard (Runway's is the reference) as it is, with the same words. It says browser, never device: the key is kept in this browser's storage, and two browsers on one phone are two trusted browsers.

- **The default: not trusted.** In every browser, Helm's key arrives when Helm opens with a connection and is held only while it is open. Opened with no connection, Helm can't show the person's things ("Your things unlock with a connection, so they can't open while you're offline. Nothing has been changed or lost.") but still takes a capture.
- **Trust this browser.** One switch in Settings, off by default, and not shown in a browser that can't keep the key safely. Its line says what it does and who else could open Helm: "Open Helm in this browser without a connection. Anyone using it could open your data, so leave it off on a shared device." Turned on: "Helm opens in this browser without a connection. Signing out turns this off." There is no sign-in step before it and no limit on how many browsers.
- **What it does.** Helm still asks its server for the key first and uses the kept copy only when the server can't be reached, so it opens offline what this browser already holds, marked with when it was last brought up to date ("As of 7:40 AM"). Captures still wait for a connection to be sorted, and questions to be answered.
- **Where it is offered.** Never on load and never at first run: the switch in Settings, and the offline screen's last line, "Once you're back online, turn on Trust this browser in Settings so it opens offline next time."
- **Other trusted browsers.** Under the switch, each other trusted browser is one row, named by its browser and system ("Chrome on Android", "Chrome on Linux ×2" when two are alike), with "Trusted Nov 8, last opened today", and its trash as its one door; "Remove all" removes every one. The list's line: "These can open Helm without a connection. One you remove stops opening it the next time it's online." Removing one says "Helm stops opening in Chrome on Android the next time it's online, or on its own after 30 days offline."
- **Offline for 30 days.** A kept key that the server hasn't confirmed for 30 days is dropped, so a lost phone that never goes online again stops opening Helm on its own. Connecting once starts the 30 days again; nothing is lost.
- **The key is removed whenever Helm could be at risk:** the switch going off, signing out or a sign-in ending on its own, Delete all my Helm data, and the server refusing the key.
- **A lost or stolen phone.** Remove its browser from any other trusted browser, or use the site's Sign out everywhere, which signs the person out of every app on the site on every device, this one included, and says so before it does. Neither has an Undo. What the phone already holds can still be read by someone who can unlock it until it goes online or for up to 30 days; Helm does not change the person's key in the MVP.
- **Captures in a browser that isn't trusted.** A capture made before the key arrives is kept encrypted in this browser, sealed so that only Helm's server can open it, until it can be sent, and is cleared on sign-out. In a trusted browser it is encrypted with the person's own key from the start.

| Whether a thing is private (Next Steps) | Change: mark or unmark; a health or care thing is unmarked only by the person | From now on; a copy already made or a reminder already shown stays as it was; unmarking never turns a name on by itself |
| A habit's reminders (Next Steps) | Change: on, off, keep them as they were, show its name on the lock screen | From the next reminder; past reminders are not kept; turning the name off removes its plain copy at once |
| What matters most (Next Steps) | Change: say it again, or Change the order | Today's next plan and the next Pause or Hold Off check use the new order; lines already shown and plans already kept stay as they are |
| A Pause or Hold Off line (Next Steps) | Change: pause or hold until a date, keep going, stop suggesting | A pause or a hold ends on its date or with Undo; nothing already done moves |
| A connection | Change: what it may read, disconnect, delete what it copied | Switching a category off removes its copies at once; past days' plans keep their events as they were |

Helm has no totals the person checks, so no old edit can quietly move a figure; a project's health simply reflects its tasks as they are now, and a habit's "11 of 13 this week" its ticks as they are now, with older days fixed.

### Private Things, In Detail



**Next Steps.** The owner asked whether the person marks something as a health habit or simply says it is private. Both, as one idea the person can understand: anything in Helm can be private.

- **What private means.** A private thing is never named outside Helm: not on a lock screen or in a reminder, not in a copy, an export or anything shared or sent, unless the person turns that on for that one thing. Inside Helm nothing changes: it shows on its page, in Today and in answers, and is spoken in a spoken conversation, as before. One exception: a conversation the wake phrase starts while the phone is locked or the page hasn't been touched answers without naming private things ("Your morning medicine is ticked", "You have one private thing at 3") until the person taps the phone; from then on, as normal. Private is about what leaves, not about hiding things from you.
- **Marked on its own: health and care.** Helm marks private, as it files them, anything about a medicine, a dose, a treatment or someone's care, whatever kind of thing it is: a task ("refill Mom's prescription"), a habit, a repeating task, a note, a fact ("Mom takes metoprolol 25 mg"), a person whose page is about care (Mom's cardiologist), a procedure or an event. The line that files it says so: "Made a task: refill Mom's prescription · Private". Only the person can unmark one, and Helm never unmarks it because a later sentence sounds less medical. When Helm misses one, the person marks it ("this is a medicine", or a habit's "This is medicine or care" switch; Habits, In Detail).
- **Marked by you: anything.** "Keep this private", "keep Dana's surprise party private", or the "Private" switch on any thing's sheet ("Left out of copied lists, and only read as a label when you aren't looking at the screen", growing as reminders and exports are built). Unmarked the same ways ("that doesn't need to be private"), with Undo. A thing filed under a private project or about a private person is private while it belongs there; moved out, it goes back to what it was. If what made it private is deleted or forgotten instead, it stays private as its own mark and says so ("Private · kept private when what it belonged to was deleted"); the person can unmark it.
- **How it looks.** A private thing's page says so under its name, with why: "Private · health and care things are private on their own" or "Private · you said so on Nov 14". Lists and Today show it as normal.
- **What Helm works out from a private thing is private too.** A pattern, a line in How [name] sees you, a note of what was noticed, a line in Activity, a moment, a goal's summary or a line on Today is private whenever anything it came from is private, worked out by Helm's own code every time, never by the AI. A copy or an export leaves those out like any private thing and counts them in the same "N private things left out".
- **Reminders use fixed words, for everything.** Every reminder-like thing, private or not, names nothing by default and uses fixed words ("Your evening habit is up"): habit reminders now, and task reminders and repeating tasks as they are built (a scheduled action never pushes: Scheduled Actions, In Detail). Each has "Show its name on the lock screen", off by default, turned on one thing at a time and never for the person; said, it is asked as yes or no after the warning, as for a habit. On a private thing its warning says what the name gives away ("…so anyone who sees your phone can tell you take them").
- **A copy or an export leaves private things out.** A copy of a list or a day, or an export, leaves them out and says how many: "2 private things left out · Include them" (for that copy only). Copying a private thing from its own page is the person choosing to name it, and copies as usual. In Next Steps, anything that would send or change something outside Helm (a calendar entry, a message draft) names a private thing only in the proposal the person approves, and says it is private there.
- **Health behaviours are their own.** A health or care thing is also kept exactly as said (a dose and its times are never changed, a different one said later is a question), a health habit is never called formed and is never moved by learning, and Pause or Hold Off never touches it (Habits, In Detail; What Helm Learns, In Detail). These follow from it being health or care, not from it being private: a surprise party marked private is planned and moved like any project.
- **The nightly look reads private things too.** Private decides only what is named outside Helm, never what Helm learns from: the nightly look reads everything the person said, private and health things included (What Helm Learns, In Detail; what it sends is in Privacy, In Detail).

### Privacy, In Detail

- **One person, one Helm.** Nothing is shared between accounts, and there is no way to invite anyone.
- **Encrypted from day one.** What the person tells Helm, and everything made from it (the words of every turn, task titles and notes, project names, people, pets and organizations with their details, places, facts, preferences and identity facts, style notes, notes, topic names, procedures and runs, answers, Activity lines, a past day's event titles, every kept file and photo, everything copied from a connected app and every line an answer quoted), is stored encrypted with a key Helm's server holds. Dates, statuses, kinds and levels stay readable so Helm can sort and filter; on their own they identify nothing.
- **What that means on the device.** Unless the person trusts the browser, Helm needs a connection once per visit to open their things; with no connection it says "Your things unlock with a connection, so they can't open while you're offline." and still takes a new turn, typed or spoken, kept encrypted in this browser (Trusted Browsers, In Detail).
- **What the voice service hears.** Only what is said after a tap or the wake phrase, while a conversation is open, goes to Helm's voice service to be turned into words, and the assistant's replies come back from it as speech. Listening for "Hey [name]" uses the offline voice model on the phone once downloaded (nothing leaves the phone), or the browser's own recognition until then (in Chrome, Google's speech service), and sends nothing to Helm's voice service. Offline voice listens entirely on the phone. The sound of the person's voice is never stored (a voice note they choose to keep is the exception). The voice provider sees the words being spoken, and a short list of names the person is likely to say (people, pets, places, projects; never a private one), never a record, a fact, a grant or a key. "What the voice service hears" in Settings names the provider and what its terms promise.
- **What the AI sees.** The assistant runs on Helm's server. For each turn the server opens only what is needed (the records on screen, the people, projects and open tasks that might match, today's and tomorrow's events, the facts about the person that bear on it, the few matching notes and copied things, and any file sent), sends them with the words to Claude through Anthropic's API (a lighter model for quick turns, a Sonnet-class one for the rest), checks what comes back and saves it encrypted. The model reaches the person's things only through Helm's own tools; it never sees a grant or a password. Planning a day is Helm's own code. The first turn says it in one line ("[name] uses AI services to hear and read what you say.") and "What the AI sees" in Settings says it in full, naming each provider. In Next Steps, with "Notice what works for me" on, the same reading also picks out what a turn says about a habit or goal, and once a night the server sends that day's notes and what was picked out to the same provider in one overnight batch to look for patterns; what comes back is sealed, and "What the AI sees" says so. Off, neither happens. "What the AI sees" says how long the provider keeps an overnight request (below), beside the switch that turns the nightly look off. A scheduled action that needs the AI to run is listed there by name, under "On a schedule".
- **What is done overnight, and what never is.** Some work isn't needed straight away, so Helm sends it to the AI service in one overnight bundle, which costs half as much. Only work nobody is waiting for goes that way, such as looking over the day's notes for patterns or something you asked to learn about later; "What the AI sees" lists each kind by name. Nothing you are waiting on is ever done overnight: not a reply, not anything spoken, not a reading of your mail, calendar or other apps, and never anything that sends or changes something outside Helm. An overnight request is deleted at the AI provider as soon as its answer is read, and kept there at most 29 days if that deletion fails. Helm has no zero-retention or health-data agreement with its AI provider for any request, daytime or overnight, so an overnight request is kept under the same terms as a daytime one, only for a shorter time; "What the AI sees" says so in those words. Whether a thing is private doesn't change what goes overnight: private decides only what is named outside Helm. **Wanting it sooner always wins.** Asking for something that was promised for later ("what about the apartment research? I want it now") starts it at once: Helm says how long ("Looking now, about 10 minutes") and the result arrives as usual. Asking "what have you noticed lately?" is answered at once from what Helm already knows. Nobody is ever told to wait for the night.
- **Faces are never recognized.** No photo is used to work out who someone is.
- **Each provider's terms are checked before it ships.** Before Helm uses an AI or voice provider or reads a service, its current terms are read for whether it trains on or keeps what Helm sends, and for how long. Settings states only what those terms promise, in their own limits, and is checked again whenever a provider changes.
- **Connections are locked down.** What each provider grants is kept sealed on Helm's server only, never sent to the device or the model, asks for the smallest permission that works (read only, one service at a time; Google Contacts only when "Add from Google Contacts" is tapped), and ends when the person disconnects or deletes their data.
- **Gmail and Drive while Helm is in testing.** Google lets an unreviewed app read mail and Drive only for people its owner lists as testers, up to 100, and asks them to reconnect about once a week. Until Google's security review of Helm (repeated every year) passes, only the owner can connect them; anyone else sees "Not available yet: Google hasn't reviewed Helm for mail and Drive." The sent-mail style switch lives on the Gmail connection, so the same limit applies.
- **Nothing leaves Helm** except what the person copies on their own tap, and, in Next Steps, the words of a question they asked to learn about later, sent to a video search (YouTube) with nothing about who asked, and research they asked for, sent to the AI provider as above.
- **The phone's location (Next Steps).** Off by default; the person chooses City only or Precise when it is first offered, and only that is read (Where You and Your People Are, In Detail).
- **Live web search for research (Next Steps).** Research uses the AI provider's own web search, which costs per search, so each person has a monthly allowance; it is sent the research's words only, and Settings names it.
- **Private things are never named outside Helm** (Next Steps). Health and care things are private on their own, and the person can mark or unmark anything; a private thing is never in a reminder, a copy or an export unless they turn that on for that one thing (Private Things, In Detail).
- **Reminders say nothing about the thing unless the person chooses** (Next Steps; for every reminder-like thing as it is built, private or not). A reminder is the one thing Helm sends out, through the phone maker's push service, so by default it carries no name, emoji, dose or person: "[name] · Your evening habit is up". To send reminders while Helm isn't opened, Helm's server can read when each habit is due, its time zone, its stage and reminder setting, and which windows were ticked and when; never what the habit is called or who it is for by name. A person may switch on a habit's name on the lock screen, one habit at a time; that habit's name is then kept in a plain copy for sending, said so where it is switched on, and removed when switched off. Delete everything removes every reminder and every plain copy too.
- **Delete everything.** One action in Settings deletes all of a person's Helm data, every kept file and photo, everything copied from connected apps and every connection, each grant withdrawn at its provider. The confirm names what goes ("214 tasks, 31 people and pets, 41 facts, 18 photos and files, 3 procedures, and 5 connections with what they shared") and that it can't be undone.

### When Things Go Wrong, In Detail

The edge states not already covered above, each worded (and, in a spoken conversation, said) so the person knows what happened and what still works:

- **The key can't be fetched.** "Helm can't open your things right now." with Try again; talking and typing still work and keep what is said. Nothing is ever replaced or reset to get past it.
- **One record won't open.** It stays in its list as "Couldn't open this", never dropped and never shown as empty.
- **The cloud voice drops mid-reply.** The rest is said in the phone's own voice and shown in full; nothing is repeated from the start.
- **Recognition hears nothing useful.** "Sorry, I didn't catch that." once, then listening ends; nothing is filed.
- **Noise wakes it.** A wake phrase heard by mistake opens a conversation that ends on its own after 8 seconds of nothing said; nothing is filed from silence.
- **Two places at once.** The conversation is open on one screen at a time; tapping to talk in a second tab ends listening in the first.
- **Answered somewhere else.** A question already answered on another device closes with "Already answered on another device."
- **Answering an Inbox question needs a connection.** Offline, a Needs you question says "Answering needs a connection." and keeps waiting; nothing is answered on a guess.
- **A different account on this device.** Turns waiting on the device belong to the account that made them and are sent only when that account is signed in again.
- **Two tabs or two devices at once.** A turn is read once; the second tab shows the same result. An edit on one device appears on the other; an Undo there respects it.
- **A rename made offline.** The new name shows on this device at once and everywhere else once it syncs; nothing already said is rewritten.
- **Delete all my Helm data, with a trusted browser elsewhere.** Every trust ends; another trusted browser loses its key the next time it is online.
- **The AI answers with something Helm can't use.** A reply that doesn't fit what Helm allows is dropped as a whole and the turn is read again, then reported as in Capture, In Detail; nothing half-applied is ever shown or said.
- **Habit reminders, in Next Steps.** A phone that can't take them, a tick with no signal, a reminder tapped late or for a habit that changed, another time zone, and weeks with nothing ticked: each is worded in Habits, In Detail ("When reminders meet real life").
- **What matters most, in Next Steps.** Said about something Helm doesn't have: "There's no project called the move. Start one?". Said with nothing in view to point at: "Which one?". Two sentences that disagree ("work matters most" a week after "the wedding matters most"): the newer one wins and the reply says the order that results, with Undo. Three or more first at once: "Which comes first?" (What Matters Most, In Detail).
- **Pause or Hold Off with too little to go on, in Next Steps.** No calendar connected: the hours count the whole of Your day, and the sheet says so. Fewer than half of a project's tasks with a length: no deadline line at all, rather than a guess. Learning switched off: no lines, and none waiting.

## What Makes It Helm

These change what gets built, compared with a to-do app, a voice assistant or a chat assistant:

- **A whole life, not a schedule.** People with how much they matter, pets, places, preferences, routines and knowledge sit beside tasks; the calendar is one view, and orders and other sources join later as connected categories.
- **You talk, it keeps.** A voice conversation is the way in, and what is said becomes records with sources, shown with Undo, instead of a chat that scrolls away.
- **It knows what you're looking at.** The assistant is on every screen and acts on the thing in view.
- **Sources over answers.** Every fact, note and answer points to where it came from, inferred is never dressed as confirmed, and general knowledge is never dressed as the person's own records.
- **You can see how it sees you.** What it believes about the person, their tastes and their writing is on one page, and every line can be fixed or removed.
- **Knowledge you can read, and the person's words stay theirs.** What the person learns becomes topics that read as documents, nested their way, each part traceable to where it came from; the assistant adds when something plainly belongs, as a new version with Undo, and changes or removes the person's words only when asked. Nothing is lost to an old chat.
- **Reads everywhere, changes only itself.** The assistant sees the calendar, mail, documents and the site's other apps, says which one each thing came from, and changes nothing outside Helm.
- **Acts inside, asks outside.** Autonomy is set by where an action lands, not by how confident the AI sounds.
- **No made-up numbers.** No completion percentages, productivity scores or streaks; progress is a count of real days and real milestones ("11 of 13 this week", "2 milestones reached"), a day without a tick is never a failure, and "formed" is called Helm's rule of thumb from your ticks, never science.
- **It learns in the open.** From what you do and what you tell it about how things went; what it noticed is a line you can read, check and stop, quoting your words, never a verdict about you; it changes only what it planned itself, asks about what you set, never moves anything about health, and waits before asking again. A switch turns it off.
- **It helps a habit take hold, then steps back.** Reminders you turn on keep coming whether or not you open Helm, fade as the habit holds and stop once it is formed, and a formed habit is offered its way off Today.
- **It advises, never blocks.** Pause or Hold Off names its reasons from your records and leaves the choice with you.
- **What matters is yours to say.** Importance is said in your own words and kept as an order, with no scale and no cap; Helm asks "which comes first?" rather than let everything be first, and never decides it for you.
- **One home for each thing.** Every fact lives in one place and is read from there; what Helm works out from it says where it came from and changes when that does, so nothing is kept twice, overwritten or lost between two copies.
- **Hands-free first.** Everything in habits, goals, learning and thinking ahead can be done by voice, every question answered aloud; taps are there, never needed.
- **Quiet.** No pushes except the habit reminders you turn on, nothing spoken first, short answers.
- **Private where it counts.** Health and care things are private on their own and anything else can be; a private thing is never named outside Helm unless you say so for that one thing.

## How It Feels

- **Composed.** Helm carries itself like an experienced butler for a family with lineage: discreet, unhurried, sure of itself. The look and the voice follow: cool, deep colours, generous space, a warm voice at a natural pace, nothing loud.
- **Easy to talk to.** One tap on Talk, from any screen; interrupt it like a person; it answers in a sentence and keeps listening for the reply.
- **Brief.** It says the least that answers, and shows the rest.
- **Honest.** It says what it did, what it kept, what it guessed, what failed, what it can't know, and when an answer comes from general knowledge rather than from you.
- **Roomy.** A plan with space in it; a rest day stays a rest day.
- **Forgiving.** Every change the assistant makes has an Undo, and nothing said is lost.
- **Yours.** Private to one person and encrypted; nothing leaves Helm. The assistant answers to the name and tone the person picks.
- **Phone first.** Talking is one tap from anywhere, and every screen is built for a thumb.
- **Useful before it is set up.** The first sentence works with nothing set up, nothing connected and nothing named.

## Under the Hood

To be settled by the technical design; the product depends on these:

- **Stack:** React with Redux Toolkit for state, Firestore for data and Storage for kept files and photos, each person's data under their own private path in the app's data.
- **The voice, apart from the thinking:** a voice service with its own layers for listening and speaking, each behind a swappable provider (a cloud one online; offline, an opt-in speech recognizer downloaded to the phone and the site's natural voice, both shared and app-agnostic under `@/lib/speech`, with the recognizer's licence and any copyleft checked and said where it is turned on), one voice identity mapped onto whichever speaks, its own budget and per-person allowance apart from the assistant's, and keys held on the server with short-lived tokens for streaming. The default providers are chosen after a benchmark of 10 to 15 replies across candidates (naturalness, delay, interruption, cost). The shared speech tools are extended, never copied.
- **AI on the server:** The assistant runs in Cloud Functions behind a provider layer: Claude through the Anthropic API, a lighter model for quick turns and a Sonnet-class one for answers and planning, under a $10 a day cap for the whole site; per person, quick turns 60 in 10 minutes and 400 a day, fuller turns 20 in 10 minutes and 150 a day. Voice has its own $5 a day cap and a per-person 60 minutes of listening and 30,000 characters of speech a day. The model reaches the person's things only through Helm's own tools and only proposes structured changes, which Helm's code checks and writes. Shorthand, dates, matching, planning, permissions and state changes are plain code. The assistant's name and tone are passed in with each turn, never stored inside a record's text.
- **Screen context:** each screen publishes which records it shows (ids, capped); the server re-reads those records under the person's own account, and any on-screen summary stays on the device.
- **Records:** one "who" record with a kind (person, pet, organization) and a level, roles on projects, key dates (kind, a date whose year may be unknown, a label, yearly or not) on a who or on the person, facts with a kind (about the world, preference, favorite, identity) and a topic, style notes, notes with versions, procedures with versions and their runs, kept files linked to the records they changed. The shapes leave room for milestones, app cards, other mediums and orders without a rewrite. Everything learned (patterns, what was picked out of turns, answers to offers, the view in How [name] sees you, the personality) is a sealed record that syncs, never kept only on one device; a home and a temporary location on the person and each who; a format and place on events. Next Steps adds habits and their ticks (with who each is for, whether it is about health, its stage, its reminder setting and whether its name shows on the lock screen), a goal as a kind of project, the person's order of what matters most with the words that set each place, and one sealed record per noticed pattern or Pause or Hold Off line with its state, dates, evidence and when it may be offered again; a note on a tick, what a turn says about a habit or goal (picked out in the same reading as the turn), a goal's why in its original, tidy and summary forms, and a nightly look over the day's notes sent as one overnight batch; every derived record names its sources and is worked out again, never edited; patterns, stages and Pause or Hold Off figures are counted by Helm's own code over these records, like the Today plan, never guessed by the AI.
- **Habit reminders** (Next Steps) use the site's push reminders and its sender, planned by a scheduled function on Helm's server from readable fields only (windows, time zone, stage inputs, reminder setting, ticks), so they keep coming and stages keep moving when Helm isn't opened; a reminder carries no sealed text unless the person shows that habit's name, checks that the window isn't ticked before it goes, and follows the habit's stage. A person's order of what matters most is one sealed record, read by Helm's own code.
- **Scheduled actions** (Next Steps) keep their repeat as a calendar rule in the standard Google Calendar uses (RFC 5545), with their floating time, time zone and skipped or moved runs; Helm's code works out each next run from it. There is no timer per action: the scheduled function that already runs every 5 minutes runs each action whose time has passed and works out its next, so a run is at most about 5 minutes late.
- **Encryption:** content fields and kept files are sealed with a per-person key that only the server holds unwrapped, in the pattern Runway uses, under Helm's own keyring; a trusted browser keeps the key through the site's shared trusted-key store.
- **Connections, site-wide:** each source declares one contract (kind, what it can do, how it connects, its status, kept in step or read when asked, its categories); a reader never reads another app's data directly, so an app that moves to its own domain keeps its contract. Google Contacts is read on a pick only.
- **History:** every change the assistant makes is recorded in Activity so it can be undone; facts keep earlier values; notes and procedures keep versions; turns and answers are kept, sealed, to look up.
