# Design & UX

Part of the repo conventions split out of `CLAUDE.md` (see its "Read before you touch" table). Rules here bind exactly as if they were in `CLAUDE.md`; a reference like `CLAUDE.md "X"` in older text means the bullet titled X, now in whichever conventions file holds it.

How Waypoint's phone-first redesign works; new features follow it. Design the phone screen first, then widen.

- **Don't make me think (or read) too much.** A screen is judged by how little a person must read and decide before they can act, the way TurboTax carries a tax return: one question at a time in large type, a running summary of what is answered, and the figure that matters always the biggest thing on the screen. **The amount is the hero** of any money entry: a large centred figure (`@/components/AmountHero`, about 48px, tabular, focused on open with a mouse, never by itself on a touch screen, a range as "It varies" beneath it), never an amount box the size of a name field. **A create form is one open question with the answered ones folded above it** (`@/components/QuestionFlow`: each answer a one-line summary row that reopens it, a nearly-always-right default pre-answered and folded, never asked). **A list row is a glance:** a badge, the name, one muted line with the single most useful fact (never cut: it runs under the figure and clamps at two lines, measured at 320px), the figure and a chevron; the row opens the record, and its actions live there, not as chips on the row. Moving an action off a row counts its taps before and after, and the moment that needs it most keeps it at one tap. **A count that replaces sentences names the soonest thing it counts from `sm` up** ("Rent $30 short on Nov 1 · 4 more"); **on a phone it is the count alone on one line** ("8 warnings ›", in the unit of the list it opens), even for one, with the single-item sentence only from `sm` up (owner, Runway round 4: a two-line sentence with "· 7 more" under it). **The next question's Next is the pinned footer's primary** (Add only at the review), a Next that is off says why in the footer note beside it, the open question is never drawn between other rows: on the first pass it is last, with every answer and pre-answered default folded above it, and once every answer has been seen (and on every edit) the form is only summary rows, each opening its question in its own sheet (a drawer on a phone, a popover or modal from `sm`, ending in Done), so no row moves (owner, Runway round 4: a reopened question opening in place between Name and Ends broke the form's flow), a one-tap answer moves on by itself, a field a chip reveals takes focus, and typed answers ask Keep editing / Discard on every way out (Discard returns to the sheet the form came from). A bulk tick that writes records has one Undo that reverses only what it wrote and never un-does a record made elsewhere. **An amount field takes only what an amount can hold:** digits and one decimal separator (two decimals for money), a minus only where a negative value is allowed, never a space, letter or other symbol, with `inputMode='decimal'`; a paste is cleaned ("$1,234.50", "1 234.50" become 1234.50), never refused (`AmountHero`, `MoneyInput`, `AmountField`). **A shared input that reformats on focus keeps the browser's select-all:** tabbing in and typing replaces the value, and focus given from code to a filled answer (a reopened step, "Change amount") selects it the same way (check both with Playwright: `fill`, and `keyboard.type` straight after the focus). **Nothing is read that wasn't asked for:** a caveat or a "how this was worked out" sits behind an info icon beside its figure; a hint is one short line. Measure at 390×844 and print in the PR: words above a tab's first row (at most 25, the row in the top 40%), lines per row (at most two), and controls on a form's first view (one question, at most four besides the footer). This is how the stages below are shown.
- **A form opens with the one question that fills in the rest, and reveals in stages.** If an answer can pre-fill the form (what an expense pays for), ask it first and show nothing else until it is answered; a picked answer shows as a short summary with "Change" instead of the fields it filled. Then show only the essentials (who it is for, how much) put only truly optional extras behind "Add to this X" chips (day, group, note). A state with its own consequences (paid or unpaid) is never a field of the details form, add or edit: adding offers a second submit above the footer ("Add and mark paid") for the common case, and later changes are their own action with their own small modal. A two-way switch on a field (total or per person) is a quiet link beside its label, not a pair of pills. Two steps and a long form are both worse than this: never show every field at once. A choice that reshapes the form (shared or private) does it in place and carries over what was typed; never tell the person to close the form and start another, and keep a private option inside the same form with one line saying only they see it. A private section sits where it is found without scrolling past a long shared one (above the dues, not below).
- **On a touch screen no field takes focus when a sheet opens; the person's tap raises the keyboard.** Focus on open (and on each next question of a `QuestionFlow`) is for a mouse only (`canFocusFieldOnOpen` in `@/utils/focusUtils`, `(hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)`; on touch the open question's heading takes focus for screen readers), and it waits for the drawer to settle (`runWhenDrawerSettled`, then `focusFieldQuietly`). Nothing in the app moves or resizes a drawer for the keyboard: the browser places it (`interactive-widget=resizes-content` in `index.html`; owner, Runway round 4: a lift-the-drawer hook left a gap between the drawer and the keyboard on a real phone, and focusing on open raised the keyboard during the slide, so both went). Print `document.activeElement` after opening with `hasTouch` and with a mouse. With a mouse it takes focus on open only when it is the screen's question: a screen opened with its subject already chosen (planning a recipe from its drawer) keeps the keyboard down so the real question, the day, stays visible. A sheet whose first button is a consequential row action (Remove) gets a harmless first focus from `sm` up, a Done carrying `data-modal-action`: Dreamer UI's `Modal` focuses the first button, so Enter on open would act.
- **A phone form shows a few inputs at a time.** Many fields on screen at once reads as overwhelming and disorganized on a phone, so everything stays editable but only what is being edited is open. A growing list of rows (ingredients, steps, items) is a column of one-line summaries ("Chicken thigh · 2 lb"); tapping a row opens its inputs one row at a time: in a drawer on a phone for any row with more than one input or free text (an ingredient, a step, nutrition values), in place from `sm` up, and tapping it again or another row closes it. An editor that opens in the list it edits must be told apart from the rows around it at a glance, and a tint is not the answer: a row editor with several fields and a search (an ingredient) is a modal from `sm` instead, the phone's drawer widened (`DetailSheet`). A collapsed row says what is wrong with it ("Add an amount") instead of hiding a bad value, and a freshly added row opens itself. A sheet that fills in many records (twelve missing prices) steps through one at a time with "2 of 12", "Skip for now" and "Save and next", never a scroll of twelve sets of fields. A stepping sheet takes its defaults from context (the order from what matters most, the unit from how the recipe measures it, the store from the row before): the person's own earlier answer is not a guess, an exception to nothing-preselected scoped to a step-through. Optional extras stay behind chips. A form's basics (title, description, times) are summary rows too (`@/components/SummaryEditor`: a caption, the value, a chevron, a drawer on a phone and a popover from `sm`, each ending in Done, Enter closing it without saving the form), except that a blank new record's title editor opens by itself once so typing starts at once. Before calling a form done, count the inputs visible at 390×844 when it opens: a title, a count and the rows as one-liners, not a page of boxes, and a row's drawer shows only that row's fields. References: Pinch's `RecipeForm` and `FillInSheet`.
- **Removing a row from a form shows an undo with a visible countdown, never a silent removal.** The row goes at once and `@/components/UndoSnackbar` (over `useUndoRemoval`) floats "Removed Salt · Undo · 6" above the pinned footer, counting down, with Undo putting the row back in its place; one removal is pending at a time and the removal stands when the count ends. One act wears one glyph: an ingredient's and a step's remove are the same ✕ (a trash is for a form's whole-record delete and a drawer's red Remove row). A whole-record delete keeps its confirm dialog.
- **Field labels and placeholders on a phone are small, and the typed text stays 16px.** A label is `text-xs` muted and a placeholder is short (`Field` with `isCompact`, `COMPACT_INPUT_CLASS` in `@/components/forms/Field`); the input's own text stays 16px because iOS zooms the page into anything smaller. A label as large as the value typed under it makes a short form look long. Check a form of four or more fields at 390px against its labels.
- **The same kind of data uses the same input everywhere.** A city is a searchable `Select` like the time zone (one pattern for picking a place-like value from a long list), not a bespoke search box; check how the app already asks for it before building another control.
- **Help text and explainers lead with what matters and keep the rest one tap away; bold marks one key phrase, never structure.** A choice's help says what the person gets or loses in one or two short sentences ("**Pick the city you'll be in** to see its weather on every day"), not how the system decides. An explainer, consent or help sheet shows the one or two short sentences (about thirty words) the person needs before its primary action, with the honest core in them (who hears, what is kept), and puts everything else behind one quiet "How it works" or "More" line: a collapsed `Disclosure` that opens in place, closed each time the sheet opens, adding no history layer, in short plain lines of one fact each, never a paragraph under a bold sentence. **An explanation has one home:** text beside a help door or info icon (a subtitle, a hint) never repeats what the door opens; the door holds it and the screen keeps only what the person must read to act (owner, Runway round 4: "What do you spend money on?" defined an envelope in its subtitle and again behind "What's an envelope?"). A list of commands or options (a reference sheet like "What to say") leads with the few used most in the moment as phrase · result rows and groups the rest behind "More …", with any setting on the sheet kept visible. When content opened in place can make a modal scroll, its buttons stay pinned in the modal's own overlay and opening focus lands on the action it landed on before, never on the fold. Count the visible words before the primary action and the sheet's share of the screen at 320×568 and 844×390, collapsed and expanded (owner, round 7: the voice explainer and "What you can say").
- **A suggested action is checked for what it costs the person.** Before a message points somewhere else ("use X instead"), ask what that means for them: if it means re-entering what they typed, the form should do the switch itself.
- **A system-added label never doubles one the person typed.** A plan's list option shows the plan's own title (people prefix their own emoji); the app adds no emoji to a picked value's text, and a pick fills what was left empty or auto-filled, never what the person typed.
- **Extra lines on a repeated card are checked for height, width and zero.** A chip or line added to every totals card is looked at for the height jump when the first one appears, at 320px and with five-figure amounts, and renders only where its amount is above zero; prefer one compact line over a chip plus two more. A section header with an action reuses `SectionHeader`'s layout (title row `h-10`, action centered on it) with a 40px hit area, and a "Show all" link under a list is `px-0!` with a 40px hit area so it sits on the rows' left edge.
- **A secondary control for a list (sort, view options) is an icon button beside the search field, not a row of pills.** Filters are the pills; sort is one `@/components/SortControl` icon (a drawer of large options on phones, a dropdown from `sm` up) that looks like its sibling icon buttons (Filter, View options) and tints with `bg-accent!` once a non-default sort is on, never a different border at rest, so it never adds a row that shifts the list and a sort offered from two rows sits alone on a right-aligned row when the list has no search; every view of a tab that lists the person's own records offers the same sorts (Default, Date created, Last edited, Name, each both ways; a non-default sort ignores favorites-first); a control shown only above N items falls back to its default when it disappears, never leaving a hidden active state. Check that a new control doesn't push the list down at 390px before calling it done.
- **The pattern index lives in `CLAUDE.md` ("Pattern index").** Look a pattern up there before designing anything, and record a new one there.
- **A search field looks like one:** filled (`variant='solid'`), roomy (`h-12`), rounded, with a leading magnifier (A-List's `SearchInput`); an outline-only input with just a placeholder is too subtle. A search screen shows results or exactly one empty state, never both: decide from the typed text, not the debounced value (so clearing the field hides stale results at once), and show "Searching…" while the debounce or request lags behind the text.
- **A list screen's search is the first control under its header; the ways into related screens are doors below it, and a door appears only once something is behind it.** Entry tiles above the search pushed it about 330px down on a phone (Kitchen, Recipes), and a row of pills under the search read as filters and wrapped oddly (owner, round 6). **A door looks like a place to go, never like a filter:** `@/components/EntryTile` (a rounded tile with a tinted icon chip, the label, an optional status in words and a chevron, two side by side in a grid on a phone, one alone spanning the row; a tile 10 to 13rem wide, which is a phone's two-up, puts the chip beside the label, the status under it and the chevron on the status line, about 63px against 87px stacked, below 10rem it stacks and from 13rem it is a row) under the search and filters, with no count and no dot (what needs a look is said in words in the tile) and never in a row with a list's scope pills. Put the screen's one add action in the header ("New ingredient"). Measure it at 390×844 and 320×568 with the longest status: the tile's computed `border-radius` is under half its height, it holds a chevron, every label's `scrollWidth <= clientWidth` (a label is cut short, never wrapped), tiles in one row share one height and are at least 40px tall, and the doors add at most one tile row above the first list row (about 110px with a two-line status; print the row's top with and without them). **A door's status counts only what its landing view shows first, or the landing view opens on what the status names** (the Fridge & freezer tile's "1 to eat by tomorrow" lands on the place holding that box), and it leads with the count that needs action (past its date before due soon: "4 past their date, 1 to eat by tomorrow"), so a status cut to one line keeps the urgent part. A first run shows no door, filter, View options or header action that needs data the person doesn't have yet (Fridge & freezer appears with the first box, Stores & prices with the first store or price, the Calendar's Plan and eye with the first recipe or saved meal), and a pill or switch likewise appears with its data: a single remaining option draws no switch, and the act it stood for stays one tap away from where people start. **That waiting is for doors to existing things, never for the way to make the first one:** a pill, tab or header action that is the only way to create a kind of record (Saved meals, Ideas) shows as soon as one record of what feeds it exists, so a threshold never sits above the thing's own minimum (a plate can be one recipe). A door that appears only once something is behind it holds its slot (a blank box of its height) only when something says it will show (this device last saw it), so the rows never jump and a first run never gets a blank band; **a door whose read fails still draws and lands on "couldn't load", never on nothing**, and the read is tried again on a verified reconnect and when the tab returns.
- **Design for 5 and for 50.** Every list a person can grow (members, days, categories, groups, stacks, items) is checked at both sizes before a screen is done. Pick-one and pick-several rows are `PillGroup`/`MultiPillGroup`, which already scale: on a phone they show two rows with "Show all N" (and the count selected), and past 12 options they add a search. Never hand-roll a pill row over data that can grow. A name that can be picked or created ("New group", a stack, a category) is `PickOrCreate` (`@/components/forms/PickOrCreate`): a dashed "New" pill first, existing names after, nothing preselected so the person has to act, never a `Select` with a hidden "Add new…" entry. The same goes for tables, strips and menus: ask what the phone screen looks like with 30 of everything, and cap or collapse it rather than letting the sheet grow. A sentence that lists names (the stock note on a shopping sheet, a list of gaps) is capped at three and "and N more", checked with 50, and a thing made of a few named parts is named by them where they fit ("Adobo chicken & Cilantro lime rice"), never counted ("& 1 more"). A collapsed pill group unfolds only for keyboard focus, never on a tap, so choosing an option never moves the fields below it. A row of pills that is not a question (saved meals to start from) carries a caption that names what it is. **Two groups that can both grow are a switch with counts, never stacked sections or a collapsed disclosure:** the fridge and freezer list's `Fridge · 10 servings` / `Freezer · 6 servings` (what you finished is a quiet link to its own step, not a third group) and the shopping list's To buy / Got it are one thin `PillGroup` that shows one group at a time; the counts follow the search and are in the unit of the rows beneath them (rows, never items minus rows), the shown group is derived each render (a pick with no match falls to the other group, never mirrored with an effect), a group with nothing in it keeps its option so the switch never reshuffles, and a group's bulk action sits with the shown group's header. **When the person needs to see both together, the switch adds "All · n"** (the shopping list: what is left, then what you got under a `SectionDivider` in the same list, the bulk action on the divider): n is the sum of the other counts under the same search, a search never moves the list off All (All is only ever picked), and the three thin pills fit one line at 320px with the largest real counts. For every such switch, ask whether the person ever needs both groups at once (what is left and what is already in the cart); if so it needs an All. Things made together (a batch put away at once) show as one row with a count, never N rows told apart by a tag that is the same on all of them; the records stay one per thing, and one moved alone leaves the row.
- **A collapse earns its place.** "Show all N" / "Show fewer" exists to tame a list that can grow, not to hide the last pill or two: if the collapsed view would still show most of the options and only one to three are tucked away, show them all and render no toggle (`PillGroup` collapses only when more than three pills fall below the two rows; A-List's five "What do you want to share?" pills wrap to three rows on a phone and are all visible). The same goes for any "Show more" link: count what it hides at 390px and 320px, and if it is three items or fewer, drop it. **Choosing never shuffles the options:** an option that is already visible keeps its place when chosen, one that was behind "Show all" takes the last visible slot (never the first), and the fully open list is in its own order.
- **"Other" never gets a sparkle.** In any form or filter, the catch-all option carries a neutral emoji (🧾 expenses, 🛏️ stays, 📌 checklist) or none, never ✨, which reads as a feature rather than a leftover bucket.
- **Two controls with different jobs never share a look.** List every control row on a screen and ask whether two that look alike do different jobs (owner, round 7: a plate's recipes as a second row of pills under Steps / Ingredients). Pills (`Pill`/`PillGroup`) are answers; a short row of options under a question that simply do something (Book / Reserve / Buy tickets, each adding a to-do) is `@/components/SuggestionChips`: a muted caption and dashed chips that hug their text and wrap, so they never read as a second set of answers stacked under the real ones. What a chip makes is a real record of the usual kind (a checklist item), so it is shown as that ("Added to your checklist") with an Undo, and the chips don't come back to add a second one. A switch of which thing (a plate's dishes) is a row of cards that each say their state ("Step 1 of 2", Pinch's `CookDishSwitch`), whose labels wrap to two lines before they truncate, and a switch of which view beside it is one labelled button that swaps the view in place, whose back, Escape and back gesture step out of that view before they leave the screen. Two bordered controls within 24px of each other differ in fill, edge or shape by job, and a disclosure (a note that opens in place) is a plain muted line with a chevron, not a bordered block.
- **Judge a pill's height by its job.** A pill someone taps to answer a question keeps the full 40px target. A filter or view switch (kind filters over a list, Per person / Group / Mine) is a quiet control that shouldn't out-weigh the content, so it is thin: `isThin` on `Pill`/`PillGroup` fits the height to the text and keeps a 40px hit area through a pseudo-element. When adding a row of pills, ask which of the two it is, and check a thin row at 320px. A scope or period choice over a list (Runway's Spend "This month") is the same quiet weight: a thin chip on the list header's title line that opens its options, never a framed summary card with an icon chip, which is a form's question and reads as one (owner, Runway round 7: "too chunky").
- **Calories and macros are emoji plus a number, through `@/components/MacroStats`.** 🔥 calories (whole numbers), 💪 protein, 🌾 carbs, 🥑 fat in grams: inline in a row or drawer line, small tiles in a summary, never a grey sentence of figures. A recipe's facts are one quiet line of words (`@/components/FactsLine`: servings · total time · cost, wrapping between facts with no dangling dot), never a row of pills (pills are for answers), and a number that needs weight or a caveat (a cost, macros) is a `StatTile`, with its warning icon when it leaves something out. Front matter that blends into the text around it is the bug, and so is front matter made of controls that do nothing.
- **A figure shown twice is one function, in one anatomy.** A tile and the sheet it opens say the figure in the same words with the qualifier on the same part (Pinch's `getPeriodTotal` is `getPeriodCostTile`), a sheet's reference lines sit in its fixed head under the figure they qualify, never last in the list that scrolls (measure every child's rect against its scroller at the five sizes, not that the node exists), and a row of stat tiles never sets a figure below its caption's size: a value that doesn't fit takes the tile's whole row (Tailwind's `max-[Npx]` means below N).
- **A count on a record made of parts is in the record's own unit.** A saved meal of two dishes counts whole plates (its own boxes plus the dish with the least in that place), never the parts' servings added, and a breakdown line of bare counts is dropped when each row opens the boxes by name.
- **A tint made with `color-mix` is mixed in `oklab`, never `oklch`,** so its hue survives: oklch drags every colour toward the background's hue and six meal colours turn into three. Check the computed colour of each token. A tile's caption ("Per serving", "For the plate") sits above its tiles, once. A group of figures that can wrap (macros, "per 100 g") wraps between groups, never inside one (`whitespace-nowrap` pieces), checked at 320px. Fields side by side (amount and unit) share one height, and an image placeholder uses `bg-accent` and the list's own shape, never a cool grey.
- **A screen's header names the thing being made or changed, as it is typed.** A recipe or meal form's header is its name ("New recipe" until there is one), so the title field doesn't have to carry it twice.
- **Playful by default.** Rounded shapes, pills over radios and tab strips, an emoji where it carries meaning, warm copy. Pick-one and pick-several rows of a few options are `PillGroup`/`MultiPillGroup` (`@/components/PillGroup`), never a `Select` of three to six items; a `Select` stays for long lists. An app can round everything at once by raising `--radius-md` and up in its theme class (Waypoint) instead of setting `rounded` on each control. An app may theme itself further (A-List's AMC red lives in its own `a-list.css`).
- **Moving between apps is the header's menu, never a trip through Home.** The header's left slot is a hamburger (`@/ui/AppNavPanel`) that slides a `@/components/SideSheet` in from the left at every size: Home, then each app (logo, name, a check on the current one), with the theme switch pinned at the bottom. The list comes from the access-filtered `useAppCatalog().apps`, so an app the person can't open is never offered, and a new app needs only its `/logos/by-app` logo. A row opens the app where the person last left it (`getAppResumePath`); the current app's row says "Back to start" when it is deeper than its front door and goes there, and only closes when already there. The panel is tied to the location it opened on, so Back or any navigation closes it; it locks page scroll while open, restores focus without scrolling, and keeps Escape and the Tab trap working when a click lands on non-focusable panel content. It stays out of the apps' way by living only in the site header (above page content, sliding away with it), covering no bottom bar, and by apps never putting controls in that header strip or along the screen's left edge. Gestures: a swipe right on the header strip opens it and a swipe left on the panel closes it; never an edge swipe (the system's back gesture) or a swipe on page content (calendars and scrollers own those). A touch handler on the header ignores touches that start outside its DOM (`currentTarget.contains(target)`, since React bubbles events from portaled modals and dropdowns through the tree) and any multi-touch. The hamburger is offset (`-ml-2.5`) so its glyph, not its 40px hit area, sits on the page's 16px left edge. When a header control replaces an older affordance (the avatar menu's "Current location" and phone "Home"), remove the old one in the same PR. Not a side rail, bottom dock or floating button: a phone has no spare width or bottom edge (A-List and Waypoint own the bottom bar). **Not while a subview is open:** the header is hidden there and Back is the way out (a long task such as cooking mode asks its own "Stop timers and leave?" first).
- **An app resumes where you left it.** Leaving an app and coming back (from the menu or a hub card) restores its place for the rest of the visit. An app keeps where you are in the URL (`?tab=`, `?trip=`) and lists those params in its registry entry (`resumeParams`, an allowlist: never a code or token); only the app's own page is remembered, never a share link or sub-route (`src/lib/app/appResume.ts`). State held in `useState` is not restored, so a new app with tabs puts them in the URL. A remembered place that no longer resolves (a deleted trip) falls back to the front door quietly; "couldn't find that trip" is only for a link the person opened (resume navigations carry `state.resumed`). The store is cleared on sign-out and account switch.
- **No "Back home" in an app's page.** Home is the first row of the header's menu, or the header's home icon (sm and up), shown whenever the route isn't `/`. Only an app's landing page (`AppEntryFallback`) says "Back home". The site installs as one app (the hub), so its ways home stay in the installed window too. Installed apps (`IS_INSTALLED_APP`, `@utils/pwaUtils`) show no scrollbar anywhere (`html[data-installed-app]` in `src/index.css`, `!important`), so a new scroll container needs no scrollbar styling of its own. Inside a subview there is no header, so Back is the only way home.
- **An image from a URL that can stop working (a cover, a hotlinked photo) is `@/components/FallbackImage`, never a bare `<img>`.** On a load error it renders its `fallback` prop, or nothing, so the page never shows a broken-image icon or alt text; give it a themed `fallback` where a hole would look wrong. Keep the failed-state look in that one component so every app changes together. A picture cropped into a box fills it and shows its middle (`object-cover object-center`): in a box whose height is clamped (`max-h`) the image is absolutely positioned over it, since an `h-full` image in a grid or flex track grows to its natural height, the box clips the bottom and the crop shows the top of the picture.
- **A list that can grow gets a search from 8 rows** (`SearchInput`, `@/components/SearchInput`; Waypoint's `LIST_SEARCH_THRESHOLD`): members, checklist items and the like, filtering by title, name and note, with one muted line when nothing matches. A list of people to pick from (an access list) shows a short preview and a search rather than everyone, and a desktop list is capped at `max-w-3xl` so a row's date stays near its name.
- **Alignment is not optional.** Everything in a column shares one left edge, and a tappable row's text and trailing chevron sit on the same left and right edges as the section header above it (a row `Button` that sets `px-0` needs `px-0!` where a theme pads full-width buttons; measure `row title x == header x` and `chevron right == container right` in the browser): a line that starts with an emoji or icon gets a fixed-width icon column (`w-5 shrink-0 text-center`) so its text lines up with its siblings, never an inline emoji that shifts the first word. Counts and dates in a row are a `whitespace-nowrap` badge or their own cell, never a trailing `· ×3` that wraps onto a line alone, and a date or "N more" in running text never splits across lines (no-break spaces inside the phrase, the break before its label: "Cooked 4 times, / last Oct 13"). A row that opens its record has no second control for it (no pencil beside a chevron, no trash beside an edit row): rename, delete and a door to what the record holds live inside what the row opens, every row of a list opens the same thing, and a row with nothing behind it is not a tap target and has no chevron. Containers pad consistently, and a row with nothing to say is omitted rather than filled with a placeholder like "No format preference". Check every new screen at narrow phone width before calling it done. A label that comes from data (a theater name) truncates or wraps inside its container and never sets the container's width, and a status badge never shares a line with a truncating name on a phone (put the state in the meta text instead, "Quiet since July"), and a row that pairs such a label with a control (a stepper, a switch) puts the control on its own line below `sm` and lets the name wrap, checked at 320px with the longest name.
