# Product

Part of the repo conventions split out of `CLAUDE.md` (see its "Read before you touch" table). Rules here bind exactly as if they were in `CLAUDE.md`; a reference like `CLAUDE.md "X"` in older text means the bullet titled X, now in whichever conventions file holds it.

How a feature should serve the person using it; the review agents and your own iteration add lasting rules here (fold them in, don't append).

- **An app names its people.** Every app's `PRODUCT.md` has a "Who It Is For" section of two or three named people, each with their situation, the journey they repeat, the level they use and what they never want to see. A plan says which person a change serves, and reviewers walk each person's journey and flag what they never want to see. Runway is the reference; `check:app-people` fails an app listed in it that lacks the section.
- **A document fills the form; it doesn't create records.** Reading a confirmation lives inside the form of the thing it describes (a flight, a stay, a rental), only for structured logistics, and never as a general "upload" button. It fills the real fields, says what it couldn't read, and the person reviews and edits them before saving; a checklist of proposed entries is no review. It touches only the fields it read: it never wipes what the person already typed or chose, a field it couldn't read stays empty so the "couldn't read" note is true, a date far outside the trip counts as unread, and it says an AI reads the file and that it isn't saved. Errors from the service never reach the screen verbatim; one warm sentence does, and the detail is logged. A value the source doesn't give ("to taste", "a handful") is kept as its words with no number, never invented: every total leaves it out without calling it a gap, and the form never makes the person type a number to save. A heuristic that rewrites read text (splitting "salt and pepper") fires on a signal in the text (a to-taste tail) or when every resulting part resolves to a known thing, never on shape alone, so "macaroni and cheese" stays whole.
- **A feature some people don't want is a switch, not a second app.** One app stays one app id; a feature can be switched per person from the admin's Features tab (`FEATURE_GATES` in `src/lib/gates/gates.registry.ts`, read with `useIsGateOpen` after the app's shell calls `useLoadFeatureGates`). A switch has a rollout (Off, Testing: admins only, Everyone; a percentage is meant to be the next rollout, added in `isOnByRollout` alone) and two exception lists, always given it and kept out, which win over the rollout, kept out winning over both. An app reads its switches once per page load and waits for that read before drawing, so a kept-out person never sees the feature flash in, and a change reaches someone only when they next open or refresh the site, never mid-session (owner: a feature must not vanish under someone while they use it). The admin's edits to a switch are staged in its sheet and saved together behind a confirm that lists every change, a rollout line saying who it reaches with the exception lists counted (never a bare "nobody" or "everyone") and a person named with their email when two share a name; the person picker lists the app's own people first, most recently active at the top, with everyone else behind "Show everyone". Switched off, a feature hides its controls and reads its own data as absent, never deletes or rewrites it, so letting the person back in restores everything (A-List reads an archived movie as on the list). A switch can be permanent for someone who will never want it; it is removed only once it is on for everyone with nobody kept out. The checks are client-side only (a beta site), and the app tells the person with one quiet line, `GatesClosedNote`, at the bottom of its settings, or of its main page when it has none; nothing names the missing feature.
- **Ask once, then keep it one tap away.** An invitation opens as a modal when the person lands (Join, Not for me, Close), and after Close it waits behind an icon button beside the entry point; it is never a banner that pushes the page down. "Once" is per person, not per tab: remember what was shown in `localStorage` keyed by uid. A standing invitation is hidden once the person is on the trip by any path (approve, join code, join by invitation), and an offered Join is never one the rules would refuse.
- **A reading matches the person's own things only when sure, and a new one is written the way theirs are.** Text, a link or a photo read into a form meets the person's ingredients first (`matchIngredient`, a thin Pinch layer over the shared `createOptionMatcher`): the exact name or alias, then the same name once the line's own descriptive words (fresh, large, sliced) drop, when only one ingredient is that (never a word in an ingredient's own name: "pork" is not "Ground pork", "canned tomatoes" is not "Canned diced tomatoes"), then one misspelled word of six letters or more with the same first letter when one ingredient is clearly the closest. A preparation the source adds ("bone-in, skinless chicken thighs") is split off into the line's note, never kept as another ingredient, and a look-alike is never linked ("goat milk" is not "oat milk", "1% milk" is not "2% milk"): a wrong silent link is worse than a New row, so every matcher change is run over a table of look-alike pairs and over the catalog's own names with mutations, counting wrong merges separately from misses. A name nothing is close to is cased like the catalog (`toIngredientName`: sentence case, a short acronym kept, a word with a capital inside it such as "KitKat" left alone) and staged as a New ingredient; a name with close ingredients waits behind a "Choose" chip. A new ingredient made in the recipe form is written with the recipe, so its name is a field in its own drawer until then (a name the person already has becomes that ingredient, one another new ingredient has merges with it, and both happen when the field is left, never per keystroke); a saved ingredient's name is edited where ingredients are, never from here. The other pickers (the meal builder, Pantry add, Stores & prices, Shopping add) and the share importer still write a typed or shared name at pick time; a new reader or importer that proposes an ingredient goes through the same helpers.
- **One shared page for everyone; only its action slot changes.** A link's page has the same hero, content and layout whether the visitor is its owner, signed in or signed out, and one slot holds the action: the owner sees it is theirs and a way back to manage it; a signed-in visitor gets one button that adds it to their own recipes or meals (a meal brings its recipes, and the slot stays as its own confirmation with an Undo); a signed-out visitor gets a sign-in card of the same size with what an account adds in plain words (keep it, plan it, shop for it, cook it step by step), never a locked or greyed button, and a screen that ends the visit (a link that is stopped, mistyped or can't be read) still carries the page's brand row and one way forward. The add is one batch that maps each line to the visitor's own ingredient or makes a custom one, and a cover that fails to copy never fails it. A thunk that copies someone else's document into the person's account rebuilds it from an allowlist with the form's own caps, never a spread, and the beta-tier rules no longer list keys, so the client's allowlist is the only guard. The page's query waits for auth and keeps a previous answer only for the same link, so signing in flips the card in place and one link never shows another's content (a sign-in clears the query cache, so each observer, a cover's included, keeps its own previous value), and a 401 from the public call, which the server gives for any token it won't take, renews the token once and asks again. The card is one footprint in every state: its text block reserves a lead and two hint lines, its leads are short enough never to wrap, and what only one state adds (a signed-out visitor's lines about an account) sits outside it; measure its height in every state at every phone size and in both themes. A hero band is capped by the screen's height as well as its width, and a short landscape phone gets two columns with the card sticky so the action is on screen. A button that is busy keeps its fill (Dreamer UI fades a disabled primary to pale peach with grey text), a clamp's frameless "Show more" is left out on a page checked for chromeless buttons (the text is drawn in full), a swapped-in view closed by the back gesture restores its scroll a frame later (the browser restores its own after a `popstate`), and a copy button that needs a class or a sized footprint is the shared `CopyTextButton` (Dreamer UI's `CopyButton` gives its `className` to a Fragment).
- **Ask for a permission on a tap, with the reason beside it.** Location, notifications and the like are never requested on load; the button says what it is for ("Use my current location") and the feature works without it (a zip code, skipping the step). A microphone is the same: before the browser's prompt a short modal says what is heard, by whom (the browser's speech service and its vendor) and that the app keeps nothing; the yes is saved with the account (`PinchSettings.voice`, so it follows the person to another device while the browser still asks for the microphone on each), listening never starts on load (the one exception: cooking mode's "Hey Pepper" wait, once chosen and agreed to with the browser already holding the microphone permission, waits silently as cooking opens), and a refusal is said once, never retried in a loop.
- **An offer the app makes (a suggestion, nudge or prompt) is noticed, declinable and sized.** It has the weight of what it is worth at that phase (a time-sensitive one such as weather-driven packing is not a quiet row among bigger asks; if it stays quiet, the reason is stated), a whole-offer "Not for me" that drops it to its quietest state and a per-item "I don't need this", both remembered per person and never re-suggested after being declined or taken off the list, and each acceptance creates records of a size the list it joins can hold (count the rows before and after; one record per thing the person ticks off, or one grouped record, chosen on purpose). **An offer to change something lasting needs lasting evidence:** one bad day earns a fix for that day, and a change from now on (a new funding order, a raised amount, a moved schedule) is suggested only when the pattern repeats or the cause is one the change would fix, so a single short pay never pushes a person to reorder a whole plan (owner, on Runway's Priorities door).
- **Two words for one idea are one word in two states.** When a thing has a before and an after (a change planned, then made), the person sees one noun with a state ("To change", "Changed on Oct 6"), never a second noun (a "change" that becomes a "tweak"): a data kind may keep two names, the copy keeps one. Before shipping a feature with a lifecycle, list each word the screens use for it and ask a first-time reader whether two of them mean the same (owner, round 12: change and tweak).
- **The site's apps are beta apps.** Anything a person reads (a title, a description, a link preview, a README) calls them "beta apps"; the earlier "mini" label is never used, in copy or docs.
- **A record speaks in the past, and a blocked action says so plainly.** A view of something that already happened (a confirmed pay, a past purchase) states facts as past ("Was $30.00 short", "Got less than planned"), never as a present need ("Needs a look"). A surface that can't be changed says what can't be done and why in one clause, then gives the door to what can ("Pays before October can't be changed. To fix a balance, correct a pay from October on." with "Go to recent days"), never an indirect principle ("A finished month stays as it was") that leaves a person who came to act guessing (owner, PR 526).
- **An empty field never reads as a value, and a misleading figure is fixed, not listed.** An optional amount with nothing in it says what empty means ("No limit"), never a muted "$0" that reads as a limit of zero. A review or screenshot pass that finds a figure, placeholder or line that could mislead fixes it in the same PR rather than offering it as a possible follow-up (owner, Runway setup polish).
- **A control exists because a request or a journey needs it now.** Before adding a switch, row, door or surface, name the line of the request (or the journey) that asks for it; a control whose only reason is that it would be the right home for something wider (a Profile switch for the whole device, added for one app's reminders) is not built, and goes to the PR's Decide list at most.
- **A reason is asked at the moment of the change, in the flow, and never blocks it; an editor opened from a doing screen comes back to it.** A record of an edit the person will want to read later (why an ingredient or a step changed) is asked where the edit is saved, in place of the form: what changed, a few starter chips and one box, with a save that works without an answer ("Save without a reason"). Only changes that carry meaning ask (a rename or a time change records itself), and a quiet optional row in the form is not asking. When a doing screen (cooking mode) offers to change what it is showing, the editor swaps into that screen's own content and returns to it with its state as it was (never closing the screen, never a second subview over it), and the screen then follows what was saved.
- **A tap on something the app offered is interest, not intent.** A screen reached from a suggestion, feed item or nudge helps the person decide first (what it is, what it's about, a way to sample it, such as a trailer) and asks the commitment question (how badly, when, which format) only after an explicit "add it" tap; the same sheet reached by searching or by planning keeps its form, since naming a thing is intent (A-List's suggested films, owner round 10). After the "add it" tap the screen keeps what they were deciding on (facts, trailer) and the question appears in place or Back returns to the decision, a decision screen whose data can be empty says what to do instead or is skipped, and Cancel never means "I only looked". Write what the tap told us before writing what the screen asks.
- **A switch starts on when it serves the person at no cost to them** (a reminder about their own things) and off when it spends, shares or interrupts. A stored document that predates a switch reads it as off, so the switch never promises what the sender won't do. Every reader of "unknown" (not loaded yet, a load error, a document made without a time zone) is checked when a default flips to on, and fails closed: a settings load error is off for the code that would send something.
- **A control lives at the scope of what it changes.** The theme, the profile and signing in belong to the site, so they live in the site's menu or profile, never on one app's screen, and whether this browser gets notifications from any of the site's apps would too: a switch for it on one app's screen would turn off every other app's. The site does not draw that switch for now (the request that asked for it was about one app's reminders), and an app does not add one. An app offers granular switches only for what it itself sends or shows (a reminder when ingredients are about to expire), and a switch of its own that needs the site's capability asks for it on that tap. A control narrower than its screen (kept per device, per list) says so in its row. Test: if a second app added the same control, would the two duplicate or contradict each other? The same test runs the other way: a row on a settings screen that names one screen's feature (a Calendar switch in Settings) is a control at too wide a scope, and belongs on that screen.
- **Hands-free control reaches the states a tap reaches, honours a guard's purpose, and never ends the act.** Voice (Pinch's cooking mode: `parseCookCommand`) does only what a tap on the screen in view does: it calls the session's own action, never the guarded tap handler, but keeps what the guard is for (a Time's up that has just come to the front takes "next" or "stop" only after a window sized to the recognizer's delay, and the drive check acts inside it and prints the card's age: a 31-minute clock jump is not "0.3 s after"). A command word printed on a visible button means that button's action in that view ("back" on the ingredient list is its "Back to the steps"), it never lands where the footer has no button ("next" over a Time's up on a later step refuses), and an act it refuses is refused for every phrase that names it ("finish", "done", "I'm done" as well as "next" on the last step), in words that are spoken whatever Read steps aloud is set to, as is every other answer. A reading aloud of something that has a view on screen (a step's tips, notes, planned changes) opens that view while it is read, one view at a time, and takes it down when the reading ends. The grammar lists only what "What to say" shows plus plain synonyms: a one-word command counts only as the recognizer's top guess, a lower-ranked guess only as a longer phrase that isn't a move, and everyday words ("go on", "again", "cook the rice") never act. A "not found" answer names the words searched and looks past quantity words ("how many cups of rice"), never "X isn't in this recipe".
- **A hands-free screen is eyes-off: whatever it says in words, it also says aloud, with no setting for it.** A person with wet hands is not looking at the phone, so an answer ("1/4 cup soy sauce"), a refusal ("No tips for this step"), a confirmation ("Paused the pasta timer") and a prompt that waits for them ("Say your tip") are each shown and spoken, whatever Read steps aloud is set to (that setting is only for reading the step itself), and a prompt that waits for speech is also marked by the chime when listening starts (after its spoken words end, or soon after the prompt if it never starts speaking: the chime is cued by the prompt's own wait, never by watching for a speaking-to-listening change, which a voice that can't speak never makes), with what is being picked up shown as it comes in and what will be written shown before and after (an Undo). Audit a hands-free screen by listing every string it draws that isn't the content being followed and asking where the person who isn't looking hears it; a reading that fails (a dropped utterance after a cancel, a voice that never starts) is retried before it is given up and never turns the voice off for the session on its own.
- **Listening has a sound, and the sound is not a command.** A soft rising two-note chime marks listening starting and nothing marks its end (`useSpeechCommands().cue`, WebAudio with no asset, resumed in the tap that turned the feature on or on the next tap anywhere), never while a timer alarm waits to be dismissed and never after a failure; the recognizer drops what it hears while the chime and 200 ms after it sound (`mute`). A reading voice that is better than the browser's is an opt-in that runs on the device (`useNaturalVoice`, `NaturalVoiceOption`): off to start, the download size and its host stated before anything is fetched, remembered per person on the device (its download is per device, so the switch is the one voice choice not saved with the account), removable, and never in the way of the browser's voice, which reads whenever it is off, still loading or has failed.
- **Listening is visible and quiet, and a continuous mic is told to stop.** A lit control (a tinted edge, a pulse that stops under reduced motion, and the filled icon: a mode switch that is also a status) and one status line that has one length in every state, errors and refusals included (one line, two on a short landscape phone, never taller than the idle line), times out, keeps its door ("What to say") in one place while the message truncates first, repeats nothing the lit control already says (idle text is a hint, not "Listening") and sends the full text behind a tap on the mic. The line shares the buttons' row on a short landscape phone and never takes width from a strip beside it (print the strip's width before and after). A browser API that needs a tap (`AudioContext` resume, the first speech) is unlocked in the tap that turns the feature on (print `navigator.userActivation.isActive` at the call), a feature that needs a browser capability is detected once, in one hook that also checks `isSecureContext` (`useSpeechCommands`), where it is missing nothing is drawn, never a disabled control, and a mic that can stay open is chosen, never the default: the default listens in one short window per tap, every way that keeps it open says so in the choice's own words, shows its state where the person looks (a state chip that opens the sheet), ends on a limit it announces, and a tap on the lit control steps down one level. A wake phrase is matched only at the start of what was heard, its spellings live in one constant, a word the content itself uses never wakes it, and a check reads the app's own text aloud against it. **A trigger phrase is judged by how a first-time person says it aloud:** two easy syllables, a word every recognizer knows, not the app's name ("Hey Pepper", not "Hey Pinch", whose name is a step's ingredient word and an awkward thing to say to a phone); a word the content uses may be one half of a two-word phrase because the match is anchored at the start of what was heard, and the cost (someone saying it to another person opens a quiet window) is stated in the docs. A mic the person chose to keep waiting (and agreed to) starts with its screen when the browser already holds the permission (`readMicrophonePermission`, never asking), silently; anywhere else it starts on a tap, because a page cannot listen with the screen off or the tab hidden and must not pretend to. A listener that can stop by itself tells its caller through a callback (`onHalt`) and the caller never mirrors its status in an effect; a countdown on a control is a wall-clock deadline drawn by a leaf component and CSS, never by re-rendering its screen each second; a sheet opened from a control that can disappear is mounted outside that control's own condition.
- **A hand-off to a third party is a hand-off, not a checkout.** The app shows what it can, opens the other site in a new tab straight from the tap, says before leaving what to look for there, and welcomes the person back with one question that has everything it already knows filled in. It never takes payment. Every state of the step before it has a way forward (a failed or empty lookup still offers the other site, and never says "you can still do X" in a view where X is impossible), and every route into the same record (Mark paid, Edit, the welcome-back question) is seeded from the same known data.
- **The screen that manages something shared stays reachable when the shared thing is gone.** A member who emptied their calendar can still open their links to read a PIN or delete one. A frozen snapshot shows clock times with a line saying whose time zone they are in.
- **A prompt asks for exactly what it says.** If it says it needs two things, it shows two inputs and the rest as one summary line with a "Change" link; a disabled primary action says why. A feature that depends on an earlier choice (theater, then showtimes) explains the dependency where the choice is made and preselects when only one option is valid.
- **A time that belongs to a place is shown in that place's time zone, flagged.** A showing's time at a theater is the theater's clock ("7:00 PM CDT"), says whose clock it is, and says what it is for the viewer when the zones differ; it is stored as an instant. A long list of choices (showtimes) collapses to the first handful with "Show N more" / "Show fewer", like Waypoint's lists, and an action that can no longer happen (buying a ticket that exists or a showing that started) is not offered.
- **Copy about someone else's screen is hedged until checked.** What a third party's checkout shows (a fee line, a $0) is verified against the real thing or worded so it holds either way.
- **A lookup that fills a form never holds it up.** It has a short timeout, a failure is never cached as an empty answer, and the fallback is the person's own wording. An offer after saving (an expense, a task) is limited to things that usually cost something and never opens for free time.
- **A stopgap says so quietly.** When a simpler version ships before the real one (a typed theater name until AMC's search is ready), say it once in the field's hint line ("Soon you'll be able to search…"), never a banner or badge, and store the data in the shape the real version will fill in so nothing is migrated later.
- **A workaround upgrades in place.** When a stopgap record (a typed theater) can later become the real thing, each one is flagged where it is listed and carries its own action to upgrade it, which carries everything that points at it (showings, the favorite) along. Never make the person delete and redo.
- **Optional setup is skippable and quiet afterwards.** A last-step "Skip", then one muted line where it matters, never a repeating prompt or an auto-opened overlay.
- **A notification opens what it is about.** Every call to `scheduleReminder` passes a `link` (a path and query inside the app: the trip, the tab, the page), required by the type so a new call site can't forget it; the toast's "View" uses the same link. The tap destination is checked for each app and for a reminder saved before links existed, which falls back to the app (Waypoint to its trip) in the sending Function. Never show a push by hand on top of one Firebase already shows: it doubles and has no link.
- **A push nobody asked for follows one standard, per app and per person.** A reminder the person set themselves (a queued reminder, a timer's push) always goes out and is not counted; everything else (a new idea, an announcement, a wrap-up, a morning note) is held to: nothing from 22:00 to 08:00 local, at most 3 a day, and anything past either is dropped, never held for later (so a push is worth sending only when it is still worth reading unread in the app). The clock is the zone that applies (the trip's, for a Waypoint push; nobody's own zone is stored on the profile), the count is per app and never shared across the site, and pushes that fall due the same morning fold into one (Waypoint's 8 AM note carries the person's due to-dos, and two days before a trip starts the getting-ready details too). Names in a push are a first name and last initial ("Alex R."), never an email. The 9 PM expense wrap-up sits inside the window and counts toward the 3.
- **A reminder can be answered "already done", and expires with the thing it reminds about.** A badge about linked to-dos goes away once the event has started, and a done to-do is how "already booked" is said. An explicit "none needed" mark (`noExpenseKeys`) never outranks real data: open linked work always shows, and tapping the opposite answer in the same prompt takes the mark back. Copy that promises a case ("a table") matches the event types that actually offer it.
- **A total on a list the person shops from prices whole packages, or says on screen that it prices only the amounts and not the till total** (a help icon beside it, on every state, not only when something is left out). Wherever a caveat names ingredients to fix, the text says where to fix them, because a fill-in sheet never opens over a subview. A list shopped from speaks in things to pick up (a count rounds up, a pinch of a spice shows only its name, one ingredient is one row) and is ordered the way the shop is walked, never alphabetically by a data taxonomy.
- **Parsing pasted text for a link never glues tokens together.** Trim the ends, take the one `http(s)://` token among words, refuse several with a plain message, and only strip inner whitespace when no token has a scheme (a link wrapped over lines); merging two tokens makes a plausible wrong link that dedupes against nothing.
- **Failure copy names its cause and never blames the input for a lost connection.** Classify each failure (the input, the service, the network), drive it with a mocked service error and the network off, and read the sentence against the cause; a sentence that points to an action is a visible row or chip on that screen, or it isn't said ("paste its text instead" with no door). Every route out of typed or built work (header, footer, Escape, the gesture, a "do it by hand" row, a switch to another way) asks the same Keep editing / Discard once. A file is checked once for every way it arrives (picker, drop), by type and then by extension, before anything is sent to the reader.
- **Storage failure copy comes from `getStorageErrorMessage`, never a blanket "check your connection".** A permission or quota error can't be retried, and "try again" must not appear where retrying can't help.
- **Say what the app can't know.** A list the app narrows for the person (a suggestion list that drops what they already have) says so beside its heading in an info icon, leading with what is left out and checked true in every mode that shows it, and keeps that explanation with one line of why when its own filter empties it. When a source only covers part of the story (prices for upcoming days only, a fee it never exposes), the screen says so plainly and falls back to a form, instead of showing a blank or a guess. A number that only counts from launch (adoption, usage) says when it began and who isn't counted yet, a not-yet-known value reads as unknown ("Not seen yet") rather than as a negative fact, and a view fed by several queries shows a loading or failed state instead of rendering a missing result as "none": every child that joins against another query's data (a usage list built from the members query) is handed that query's status too, not only its own. That includes every count on a list or nav row ("Couldn't load" or "–", never 0) and the sheet a row opens. A member's "last seen" is the later of its sources, so a visit record can't contradict a newer app open. A legend describes what the screen renders (filled vs outlined), not a color that isn't there. A total says what it leaves out **in its figure, once per surface**: "$5.20 so far" when ingredients are left out ("No price yet" when none is priced, "Not priced" for a past day, a row price "$0.08+"), with a warning icon beside it as the explanation (`HelpTip variant='warning'`, opening like the help icon with what is left out and why), and a figure derived from a partial base (an average, a ratio) is never the partial figure over a count: it divides over the complete records only, names that base ("Avg per fully priced meal") and says nothing under three of them, so it needs no "so far" or "+" (round 7: one priced ingredient made a meal "priced", and "$0.30 a dinner" came from 2 of 16 ingredients priced). A past day's total reads "$0.15+". A state word two screens use for different scopes ("short" for bills only on one, for any item that gets less on another) says on the narrower or wider one which it counts, so the same pay is never short in one place and absent from the other without a word of why. A caveat shared by most rows is said once, in the total or the list's header, and a row flags only the exception; when a figure and its total both leave the same things out, one icon sits beside the first figure the person sees and the marked rows say the rest; wherever a caveat is stated the fix is one tap away or the text says why it can't change.
- **A new record asks how to start, with every way in side by side.** When something can begin from text, a photo, a link or by hand, the ways are one list (`@/components/MethodPicker`, a drawer on a phone, the page's own list from `sm` up) and the list only picks: each way's input lives on its own view, one tap away, never revealed under the list. The manual way is last and quieter, and every reading fills the real form for review instead of saving. A way's title names what the person brings, not what it might be: "From a file" with "A photo, screenshot or PDF" beneath, never "From a photo (or PDF)". A number we calculated for review (a recipe's nutrition) is shown as calculated, editable in place, and one tap resets it; a value the person leaves equal to the calculated one stays derived.
- **Every AI entry point says what happens before the file picker opens or anything is sent** ("An AI reads the picture and fills in the form. It isn't saved."), a reading that fails names its own input (the link, the text, the photo) and tells an unreachable link from a malformed one, and a reading row that couldn't be matched keeps its amount and its fix instead of going quiet. A structured answer's schema makes every field required and nullable: an optional field gets dropped silently (a reader stopped returning notes), while required-but-nullable makes the model answer each one. **A reader is told every limit its answer is cut to** (the cap constants are interpolated into the prompt and the schema descriptions, never retyped) and asked to shorten and drop asides itself (a heading "Bulgogi sauce (don't skip the onion & apple!):" is "Bulgogi sauce"), and the client guard behind it cuts at a word, never mid-word, so a model slip never shows a clipped heading or note.
