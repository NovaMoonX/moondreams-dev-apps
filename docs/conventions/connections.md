# Connections

Part of the repo conventions (see `CLAUDE.md` "Read before you touch"). These files are the source of truth; nothing else keeps a copy.

How one app reads another app's data, or a connected Google service, so every app follows one process and an app can later move to its own domain or project without its readers changing. Read it before touching anything under `src/lib/connections/`, `functions/src/connections/`, an app's `share/` folder, or code that wants data another app owns.

- **An app reads another app only through that app's published read contract.** Never a Firestore path, a slice or a type from another app's folder (`check:connection-boundaries`). Each source app owns `src/apps/<app>/share/manifest.ts` (its categories: id, the person's words, kind, On or Off, and its Never lines; plus an `about` for the source and a `meaning` for each category, one sentence each for an outside assistant that has never seen the app, in the app's own words (Runway's Horizon goals are costs the person knows are coming), never shown in the app, and checked for length by `check:share-fields`) and `functions/src/apps/<app>/share/adapter.ts` (maps its records to the shared kinds (before a category picks a kind, check which of the reader's context reads that kind feeds: a list of many records never takes a kind meant for a few one-line summaries, such as Helm's standing kinds `project`, `countSummary` and `pet`, which keep only 8 in every prompt, so Runway's envelopes and Waypoint's checklist are `artifact`), returns only what that uid can already see in the app, never another member's private items, contacts or messages), registered in `SOURCE_ADAPTERS`; an app that shares nothing declares `shares: []`. A category added later is Off. A category lists what it reads (`reads`, the person's words, each with the `scopeVersion` it arrived in and the `addedOn` day it ships), and widening it bumps its `scopeVersion` with the new read, read against the manifest's Never lines in the same PR so the two never seem to contradict (a new "whether a reveal is waiting" beside a Never "when anything was revealed"); for 30 days after that day a person who hasn't seen it gets a New badge on the reader's settings row and on the connection's row, and the connection page lists what is new. Opening the connection, turning the category on or tapping Got it records it as seen on the grant (`seenScopes`), so it clears on every device. A reader owns a sink (`READER_SINKS`) that seals copies into its own tree with their source, account, freshness and `transformVersion`. Grants live at `connections/{uid}/grants`, are read by their owner from any app and are written only by the platform's callables. Tokens are sealed under `CONNECTIONS_MASTER_KEYS` in `connectionsServer/`, used only inside `functions/src/connections/`. Everything is read only until an app ships its own write permission. An app that moves to its own domain or project keeps its contract: the platform's `SourceClient` switches from in process to `/connections/v1` over HTTP with a grant-scoped service token, and nothing in the reader changes. `check:adapters` proves each adapter against owner, member and non-member fixtures. **Each source app also keeps `src/apps/<app>/share/fields.ts`: one decision (`shared`, `filter`, `never` or `notUseful`, `src/lib/connections/fieldDecisions.ts`) for every stored field of every interface in its `types.ts`, and a reason for every exported type that is not stored.** Money, card, account and contact fields are `never`, not `notUseful`, so a later reader PR can't flip one lightly. The typecheck fails when a field is added, removed or renamed without a decision, and `check:share-fields` (run on pull requests) fails on a new stored type in `types.ts` left undecided, on a name read in the adapter's `share/` files that no map marks `shared` or `filter`, and on a category whose `scopeVersion` is not its newest read's `sinceVersion`. It cannot see a type declared outside `types.ts`, a derived alias, a field read through a helper outside `share/`, or whether a newly shared field got its own read line: those are the reviewer's, so a PR that changes an app's stored types records the decision in the same PR and, when it is `shared`, also updates the adapter, adds the read with a raised `scopeVersion`, and updates Helm's view of it.
- **The pieces are shared and named, so a reader or a source joins without new platform code.** The contract is `src/lib/connections/contract.ts` (import-free; `npm run connections:vendor` writes its server copy and each app's server manifest, and `check:server-copies` fails when one is stale, so edit the `src/` file and re-run it). A reader lists its grants with `useConnections({ readerAppId })`, draws them with `ConnectedSourcesList` (rows the caller words) and changes them only through `connectionCalls` (`grantConnection`, `updateConnection`, `endConnection`, each a transaction that re-reads the grant and checks both apps with the site's `canOpenApp`). A source app registers its manifest in `src/lib/connections/sources.ts` and its adapter in `SOURCE_ADAPTERS`; a reader adds its grant page to `READER_APPS` on both sides. An adapter reads only under the uid's own paths, sends every record through one function that writes exactly `SHARED_RECORD_KEYS`, sends an amount (`amountCents` or a `*Cents` extra) only with its record's `currency` and leaves it null otherwise, since a reader formats money only with one and a bare 12500 reads as dollars, and reuses the app's own thresholds (a "use soon" window is the app's, never a new number); a `detail` is clipped to no more than the reader cuts at (Helm cuts at 120, with no marker) and ends in "…" when it was cut, and a list inside a detail clips each item with "…" and leaves room for its "and N more" tail, counted from the items actually shown, so the tail survives the reader's cut; a category whose past is the point (a vet visit last spring) sets `historyDays` on its manifest entry, and the runner hands that category a window that far back, which the adapter and the reader's sink both use, so the past is read and kept without a second date rule in either. A read that drops documents in memory (archived, already seen) is sized apart from what it sends, so a heavy user's `isFull` is never stuck false and stale copies never stay (A-List's watchlist reads 2,000 and sends 200). A source the server can't read (a seal that won't open, a malformed payload, nothing written yet) is unknown (`isFull: false`, so older copies stay) and logs its reason without the uid; only a source the app cleared on purpose (an explicit `null`) is a full, empty read that removes the copies, so "keep what we had" never silently becomes "stale forever" (Helm, issue 25). `check:connection-boundaries` greps the shared files for every app's words; a hit that must stay (a `SharedKind` named `trip`, a registry key) goes in its allowlist with why, and a stale entry fails. Changing what a shared field means (an all-day `endAt` moving from the day after to the last day) changes every reader and source in the same PR, and when stored copies exist anywhere outside the emulator it also bumps the transform so every copy is read again; a note on the field in the contract says which meaning holds. A reader that shows one thing held by several sources (a showing on the calendar and in A-List) merges the copies by comparing each to the group's first copy, never by chaining matches, and works out every derived value (busy time, range, place) over all the copies, never the shown copy alone: a calendar copy marked Free once hid an app copy's busy evening from the planner (Helm, issue 33). A site app's category may be `live` too (Runway's transactions): its adapter's `query` runs in the function with no token, the router re-checks the grant, the category and the source's `status()` on every call, and nothing is written or cached (Helm, issue 30). A live category writes no `categoryState` and is never stale on any freshness surface (Today's stale line, the grant page's status line and Update now, the refresh check), and a status report says it is read when asked, never "not updated". A live query that cannot open some records says so (`isPartial` plus a count-only log, never a uid) and echoes the range it read as `from` and `to`, so the reader can name real dates, and what it counts matches the source app's own list for the same thing (Runway's Spend). A category in `live` mode that the person picks from (Google Contacts) copies nothing: its adapter's `syncPage` is empty, `readOptions` lists the choices in memory (`ConnectorOption` carries `detail` and `photoUrl`, both null when the source has none), and the reader's own callable re-reads each picked id from the provider, checks the live grant, ignores any name the provider did not return and fetches any picture without the token from an allowlisted host (Helm, issue 38). A refresh from the provider keeps what the person changed: a field edited or cleared and a keyed record removed (a birthday) are never written again, and a provider's value passes the same code checks as a said one, so a 31 April birthday is dropped (Helm, issue 43).
- **Google's consent screen is one for the whole project.** `moondreams-dev-apps` has a single consent screen, shared by the site's Google sign-in and by every connection scope, so it is never switched to Testing: that would block every app's sign-in for anyone not on the test-user list and expire each Google connection after 7 days. It stays **In production** (owner, Nov 2026; `npm run connections:google-setup` walks the clicks). Scopes come in three kinds: `openid` and `email` need no review; `calendar.readonly` and `contacts.readonly` are sensitive (verification: a justification, a demo video, a verified domain); `gmail.readonly` and `drive.readonly` are restricted (the same, plus Google's yearly paid security assessment). Until verified, Google shows its "hasn't verified this app" screen (Advanced, then Continue) and caps the project at 100 people who grant an unverified scope; signing in alone does not count. **Unconfirmed, and the first thing to find out:** whether Google refuses restricted scopes to everyone but the owner on an unverified screen (Helm's `google-mail-and-drive` switch assumes it may); the first Gmail or Drive connection by someone else settles it, and the answer is written here. **A PR that adds a Google scope says its kind in the PR, and the verification work ([#1027](https://github.com/NovaMoonX/moondreams-dev-apps/issues/1027) tracks it) is done before the site opens a connection to people beyond the owner.**
- **An outside account is held as tightly as its token.** One account per outside identity (`g_` + a hash of the provider's subject), sealed with the uid and account id as AAD, and only an email the provider says is verified is kept. Anything that stores a token undoes it on every way out: a refused exchange revokes the token it got, a picker dismissed before a grant exists forgets the account (`releaseUnusedAccount`), and removing an account checks for live grants and deletes in one transaction, then revokes at the provider after the commit. Every reader caps a category at the contract's `MAX_RECORDS_PER_CATEGORY` with one order (events by start, the rest newest first), a capped read forces a full read next run, and duplicates across calendars or zones are matched on the instant, never the written time. An account the provider stopped sharing offers only Reconnect: every control that needs the provider is hidden, never left to fail with "Try again", and Reconnect asks for the connector that failed (read from the failed thing, never a literal). One account serves several connectors, so anything keyed by account (a live read's cache, a failure line) also carries the connector.
