# Connections

Part of the repo conventions (see `CLAUDE.md` "Read before you touch"). These files are the source of truth; nothing else keeps a copy.

How one app reads another app's data, or a connected Google service, so every app follows one process and an app can later move to its own domain or project without its readers changing. Read it before touching anything under `src/lib/connections/`, `functions/src/connections/`, an app's `share/` folder, or code that wants data another app owns.

- **Every app is planned and changed with Nomos and its connections in mind.** Nomos, Helm's assistant, reads what each app shares and is what ties the system together, with the outside assistants that reach Helm (Claude, Gemini, ChatGPT), the connected services (Google) and the other apps around it, so where the app meets them is a question in every plan, never an afterthought, looking for tighter integrations, a better experience and gaps (owner). A new app's plan carries `src/apps/<id>/NOMOS.md` (plan-app `references/nomos.md`): what the person asks Nomos, what the app shares, what the outside assistants get, what it could read from or give to other apps and services, and the gaps. Any later change that adds or changes what an app stores or what a person does in it asks the same question: does a share category, its `meaning`, `share/fields.ts`, the adapter, an outside assistant's tool or a link to another app change, and does `NOMOS.md`? Do it in the same PR, or say in the PR's How it works why Nomos is unaffected. **The people, pets and businesses the person deals with are always shared, and Helm always offers them for the circle:** any app, and any new category in an app, that holds them (cats, vet clinics and vets, trip members, buyers and co-sellers) shares them as `pet`, `person` or `place` and adds its category to Helm's `BRING_IN_SOURCES` (`engine/bringIn.ts`) in the same PR, so the person is offered to bring them in (checked against who they already have, with each merge said first) with no extra work in Helm; a new app's plan says in its `NOMOS.md` which records are people, pets or businesses (owner, Helm issue 141). A plan for that change carries its one **Nomos** line (`orchestrate` "Writing a plan"). An app with no `NOMOS.md` yet gets one the first time a PR changes what it shares.
- **An app reads another app only through that app's published read contract.** Never a Firestore path, a slice or a type from another app's folder (`check:connection-boundaries`). Each source app owns `src/apps/<app>/share/manifest.ts` (its categories: id, the person's words, kind, On or Off, and its Never lines; plus an `about` for the source and a `meaning` for each category, one sentence each for an outside assistant that has never seen the app, in the app's own words (Runway's Horizon goals are costs the person knows are coming), never shown in the app, and checked for length by `check:share-fields`) and `functions/src/apps/<app>/share/adapter.ts` (maps its records to the shared kinds (before a category picks a kind, check which of the reader's context reads that kind feeds: a list of many records never takes a kind meant for a few one-line summaries, such as Helm's standing kinds `project`, `countSummary` and `pet`, which keep only 8 in every prompt, so Runway's envelopes and Waypoint's checklist are `artifact`), returns only what that uid can already see in the app, never another member's private items, contacts or messages), registered in `SOURCE_ADAPTERS`; an app that shares nothing declares `shares: []`. A category added later is Off. A category lists what it reads (`reads`, the person's words, each with the `scopeVersion` it arrived in and the `addedOn` day it ships), and widening it bumps its `scopeVersion` with the new read, read against the manifest's Never lines in the same PR so the two never seem to contradict (a new "whether a reveal is waiting" beside a Never "when anything was revealed"); for 30 days after that day a person who hasn't seen it gets a New badge on the reader's settings row and on the connection's row, and the connection page lists what is new. Opening the connection, turning the category on or tapping Got it records it as seen on the grant (`seenScopes`), so it clears on every device. A reader owns a sink (`READER_SINKS`) that seals copies into its own tree with their source, account, freshness and `transformVersion`. Grants live at `connections/{uid}/grants`, are read by their owner from any app and are written only by the platform's callables. Tokens are sealed under `CONNECTIONS_MASTER_KEYS` in `connectionsServer/`, used only inside `functions/src/connections/`. Everything is read only until an app ships its own write permission. An app that moves to its own domain or project keeps its contract: the platform's `SourceClient` switches from in process to `/connections/v1` over HTTP with a grant-scoped service token, and nothing in the reader changes. `check:adapters` proves each adapter against owner, member and non-member fixtures. **Each source app also keeps `src/apps/<app>/share/fields.ts`: one decision (`shared`, `filter`, `never` or `notUseful`, `src/lib/connections/fieldDecisions.ts`) for every stored field of every interface in its `types.ts`, and a reason for every exported type that is not stored.** Money, card, account and contact fields are `never`, not `notUseful`, so a later reader PR can't flip one lightly. The typecheck fails when a field is added, removed or renamed without a decision, and `check:share-fields` (run on pull requests) fails on a new stored type in `types.ts` left undecided, on a name read in the adapter's `share/` files that no map marks `shared` or `filter`, and on a category whose `scopeVersion` is not its newest read's `sinceVersion`. It cannot see a type declared outside `types.ts`, a derived alias, a field read through a helper outside `share/`, or whether a newly shared field got its own read line: those are the reviewer's, so a PR that changes an app's stored types records the decision in the same PR and, when it is `shared`, also updates the adapter, adds the read with a raised `scopeVersion`, and updates Helm's view of it.
- **The pieces are shared and named, so a reader or a source joins without new platform code.** The contract is `src/lib/connections/contract.ts` (import-free; `npm run connections:vendor` writes its server copy and each app's server manifest, and `check:server-copies` fails when one is stale, so edit the `src/` file and re-run it). A reader lists its grants with `useConnections({ readerAppId })`, draws them with `ConnectedSourcesList` (rows the caller words) and changes them only through `connectionCalls` (`grantConnection`, `updateConnection`, `endConnection`, each a transaction that re-reads the grant and checks both apps with the site's `canOpenApp`). A source app registers its manifest in `src/lib/connections/sources.ts` and its adapter in `SOURCE_ADAPTERS`; a reader adds its grant page to `READER_APPS` on both sides. An adapter reads only under the uid's own paths, sends every record through one function that writes exactly `SHARED_RECORD_KEYS`, sends an amount (`amountCents` or a `*Cents` extra) only with its record's `currency` and leaves it null otherwise, since a reader formats money only with one and a bare 12500 reads as dollars, and reuses the app's own thresholds (a "use soon" window is the app's, never a new number); a `detail` is clipped to no more than the reader cuts at (Helm cuts at 120, with no marker) and ends in "…" when it was cut, a detail joined from parts is clipped after the join with its most useful facts first (a free-text address last), and a list inside a detail clips each item with "…" and leaves room for its "and N more" tail, counted from the items actually shown, so the tail survives the reader's cut; a category that reads a second collection per parent or a profile lookup sets `isFull` from every read's own limit and its record cap, and a failed lookup returns what it has with `isFull` false; a category whose past is the point (a vet visit last spring) sets `historyDays` on its manifest entry, and the runner hands that category a window that far back, which the adapter and the reader's sink both use, so the past is read and kept without a second date rule in either. A read that drops documents in memory (archived, already seen) is sized apart from what it sends, so a heavy user's `isFull` is never stuck false and stale copies never stay (A-List's watchlist reads 2,000 and sends 200). A source the server can't read (a seal that won't open, a malformed payload, nothing written yet) is unknown (`isFull: false`, so older copies stay) and logs its reason without the uid; only a source the app cleared on purpose (an explicit `null`) is a full, empty read that removes the copies, so "keep what we had" never silently becomes "stale forever" (Helm, issue 25). A reader that keeps what a writer used to drop (past-due items) also decides when the summary it reads is too old to trust: Runway's adapter drops a bill that was already past due when a summary more than 7 days old was written, since it cannot say whether it was paid since (Helm, issue 143). `check:connection-boundaries` greps the shared files for every app's words; a hit that must stay (a `SharedKind` named `trip`, a registry key) goes in its allowlist with why, and a stale entry fails. Changing what a shared field means (an all-day `endAt` moving from the day after to the last day) changes every reader and source in the same PR, and when stored copies exist anywhere outside the emulator it also bumps the transform so every copy is read again; a note on the field in the contract says which meaning holds. A reader that shows one thing held by several sources (a showing on the calendar and in A-List) merges the copies by comparing each to the group's first copy, never by chaining matches, and works out every derived value (busy time, range, place) over all the copies, never the shown copy alone: a calendar copy marked Free once hid an app copy's busy evening from the planner (Helm, issue 33). A site app's category may be `live` too (Runway's transactions): its adapter's `query` runs in the function with no token, the router re-checks the grant, the category and the source's `status()` on every call, and nothing is written or cached (Helm, issue 30). A live query reads the same args from every reader (`LiveQueryArgs`: `text`, `from`, `to`, `timeZone`); an instant is filtered on the person's day through `functions/src/connections/liveDay.ts`, a date-only value on its UTC day, and "this year" or "today" is the person's. The reader's tool passes on everything the manifest promises (the subtitle, a due day, every scalar extra, a signed amount in the record's own currency); a field read and then dropped by the tool is a finding (Helm, issue 124). A live category writes no `categoryState` and is never stale on any freshness surface (Today's stale line, the grant page's status line and Update now, the refresh check), and a status report says it is read when asked, never "not updated". A live query that cannot open some records says so (`isPartial` plus a count-only log, never a uid) and echoes the range it read as `from` and `to`, so the reader can name real dates, and what it counts matches the source app's own list for the same thing (Runway's Spend). A category in `live` mode that the person picks from (Google Contacts) copies nothing: its adapter's `syncPage` is empty, `readOptions` lists the choices in memory (`ConnectorOption` carries `detail` and `photoUrl`, both null when the source has none), and the reader's own callable re-reads each picked id from the provider, checks the live grant, ignores any name the provider did not return and fetches any picture without the token from an allowlisted host (Helm, issue 38). A refresh from the provider keeps what the person changed: a field edited or cleared and a keyed record removed (a birthday) are never written again, and a provider's value passes the same code checks as a said one, so a 31 April birthday is dropped (Helm, issue 43).
- **A source app may publish read actions beside its categories, and every call re-checks the grant.** `ConnectorManifest.actions` (`AppActionManifest`: id, `kind: 'read' | 'change'`, the share categories it draws on (read) or the change permission it needs (change), a label; `[]` for a source with none) names what an assistant may ask for in one call (a day's plan, one recipe), the adapter implements each under `actions`, and `runSiteAction` reads the grant on every call, keeps nothing and answers `switchedOff` naming the exact categories when none it draws on is on; a partly-on action returns what it can with `heldBack` naming each category it left out. Helm offers them as the app's own tools ("An app offers an assistant a handful of tools", next) to its own assistant and to outside assistants (behind "Read your apps", else `assistantCannotRead`), built from the manifest alone, so a new app's actions need no Helm code. An action never returns what no category covers, and a category an action needs for a field is part of its description. **A change is its own permission, never a share category:** `ConnectorManifest.changes` (`id`, `label`, `meaning`) are switches on the person's connection, all off at connect, written by `updateConnection` (`changes: { id: boolean }`, ids the manifest doesn't declare refused) and read live on every call; an outside assistant also needs its own "Change things in your apps" (`canChangeApps` on its client doc) or answers `assistantCannotChange`. A change applies at once in one transaction that also writes the app's own record of it (Pinch's `outsideChanges`, written only by the server; a change that creates a record of another kind keeps its id on that record so Undo can take it back), never deletes, and is taken back through the one generic `undoAppChange` callable (`SourceAdapter.undoChange`, in a transaction that refuses with a sentence once what it touched has moved on), from the app's own screens and from Helm alike, even after the switch is turned off. An Undo's label names everything it takes back ("Undo and remove the recipe and its new ingredient"), so a record the change created never disappears unannounced, and one it may leave behind (a created record something else now uses) is said in the entry's note. Who made a change is worded once per surface kind: a list row says the assistant's own name ("From Gemini"), a drawer or detail says it with the way it came ("Added by Gemini, through Helm"), both from the app's one pair of helpers. **A change's reply to the assistant is built from what the assistant sent and the read categories that are on, never from the stored before and after text:** the person's own record (the app's change record, a history note) keeps the full lines, but what goes back to the outside assistant says "Steps replaced (5 steps)" and "Serves 8" unless the category that covers that content is on (`runSiteAction` hands a change every category that is on, and none when the asking assistant's own "Read your apps" is off, `canRead: false`), so a switched-off category is never read by diffing, by a merged total, by an "already on the list" or by a refusal that echoes a stored title; each field is quoted under the category whose read returns it (a description is Full recipes content, not Recipes), and a change by name matches only what its categories allow, so with no read it is refused and asks for an id. **A change that writes names into one of the app's own lists follows the app's own reader rules:** a name the assistant sends (an ingredient, a store, a breed) is matched the way the app's reader matches what it reads (`createOptionMatcher` with the app's ignored words, a descriptor moved into the line's note: "lean ground beef" is the person's Ground beef with "lean" in the note), a new entry is made only when nothing matches and the assistant says it is a real new thing, its id is kept on the change record for Undo, and the app offers a read that says which of the person's names a word is, so the assistant asks before it writes rather than guessing. **A change that replaces a whole list carries the version it read:** the read returns the record's `updatedAt` as `version`, the change requires it back as `basedOn` and refuses, writing nothing, with a sentence telling the assistant to read again unless it equals the record's version exactly (a future or invented number, or a record with no stored version, never passes). A change that fails to file in Helm says so in its reply (the change is in the app with its Undo there, never promised in Helm) and is logged.
- **An app offers an assistant a handful of tools, grouped by what they do, never one per action.** Every tool an assistant is offered costs it attention on every turn, and a site of many apps would otherwise hand it hundreds (Pinch alone had 12 actions), so each manifest action names the `tool` it is offered under, and the manifest lists those tools (`tools`: `id`, `title`, the person's words, such as "Add or edit a recipe"); actions sharing a tool become its options, picked by a required `action` field, and the tool is named `{app}_{tool}`. **Every app offers the same kinds, under the same names, each at most once and only those it needs** (owner: "a read, which is always safe; add, which is safe as well because a person can just delete it; and save, which could be destructive"): **`read`** holds every read and changes nothing; **`add`** holds the changes that only create (each undoable, and the person can delete what was made); **`save`** holds every change that can alter what the person already has, together with any create that takes the same fields (a recipe's add beside its edit, which would otherwise carry a recipe's whole field list into `add`), and is the only kind marked destructive. So Pinch offers `pinch_read`, `pinch_add` and `pinch_save`, and an app nobody should change offers only `read`. **Deleting is not offered:** a later `delete` kind needs its own switch, a restorable delete rather than an erase, and a line in the decision record, and the check refuses one until then. The rules, all held by `check:helm-mcp` over every adapter, each with a broken manifest that proves it fires: the tool ids are those kinds, each declared once; a read sits only under `read`, a change never does, and a change that alters what exists (`changesExisting`) sits only under `save`, which must hold at least one; a change tool groups only changes whose fields agree (a field two options share accepts exactly the same values, its limits and choices included, since Helm's server checks a call against the first option's copy, its default included, before the option sees it, and a shared field carries no refinement or transform, which that check could not see) and never mixes a read with a change; at most 40 offered to one outside assistant in all (past that, a client that loads every tool starts to choose badly; raise it only with a line in the decision record); names at most 64 characters, every option's description long enough to say what it returns and where its ids come from, and every `{app}_x` a description names is a real tool. A grouped option's fields are all optional in the schema, each described "For get_recipe, …", and the option checks its own shape when called, answering `refused` with "Nothing was run" and the fields it takes when one is missing, so a wrong call teaches the assistant instead of failing silently. The manifest also gives **`askedAbout`** (at least 3 of the person's everyday words for what the app holds: meals, groceries, what to cook), and every one of the app's tools opens with its `about` and those words, since an outside assistant has never heard of the app and picks tools by matching the person's words. **A switch decides what runs, never what is listed.** A switched-off option stays in its tool's list marked "Off right now: the person turns on X in Helm > Connected apps > Pinch", so the assistant can say exactly what to turn on (a tool list is read once at connect, and a stateless server can't tell the client it changed); a change tool stays listed too when the assistant's own "Change things in your apps" is off or every option in it is off, its description opening with what is off, where it is turned on, and that the assistant tells the person that switch rather than saying it can't be done, and the guide says the same once (a hidden edit tool left Claude saying "I don't have a tool here that edits" Pinch recipes, never naming Edit recipes). A change that adds to something the assistant can't read (links on a recipe whose links are private) only appends, checks for a duplicate by what an item points to (a video by its id, a page by its comparable URL, never lowercased), and replies only with what the assistant sent plus a count of what was skipped, never naming a stored item. Every call still re-checks the grant and answers `assistantCannotChange` or `switchedOff`. Each tool carries a `title` (the manifest's, with "in Pinch" added) and honest hints: reads `readOnlyHint`, a change `destructiveHint` only when an option can alter or replace what the person already has (`changesExisting` on the action: an edit is, an add is not), `openWorldHint` false; a result whose status is a refusal (`switchedOff`, `assistantCannotRead`, `assistantCannotChange`, `refused`, `notFound`) is returned with `isError: true`, so the assistant reads it as a failed call and corrects course. The server's guide (`helmGuideFor`) is a short map that names each app and its `{app}_` prefix with its `askedAbout` words, never a list of an app's tools, and stays under 4,000 characters with every switch on. **Who gets which:** an app's read tool goes to Helm's own assistant and to outside assistants alike; its change tools go to outside assistants only, until Nomos has its own way of asking first and showing Undo; the guide is the outside assistants' instructions, and Nomos's own prompt never lists app tool names. Why, what else was weighed and when to revisit: `docs/decisions/2026-11-25-assistant-tools.md`.
- **What a reader puts in front of the person for a day needs them that day.** A connected item that is done, paid, automatic (autopay), cancelled, archived, from an ended trip or outside the day's window never asks for attention; it may still be answerable ("did I pay rent?"). An adapter sends the status facts a reader needs to tell (a task's `isDone` and `isAutomatic` extras: a paid bill is `isDone`, an autopay payment `isAutomatic`) and leaves out what can never need the person (a finished trip's to-dos, a cancelled or withdrawn record); a category that is about acting (bills to pay) includes what is overdue, not only what is ahead. One pure helper decides for every surface (Helm's `needsYouOnDay` in `engine/needsYou.ts`, vendored to the server), so the schedule, the day card, Needs you and the assistant's context never disagree; a date-only due day is compared as a day in the person's zone, never as an instant, and an end date covers its whole day. A new status a source adds is sent as an extra, read by the helper and given a case in `check:helm-needs-you` (Helm, issue 143: ended trips' to-dos and paid or autopay bills were listed on Today).
- **Google's consent screen is one for the whole project.** `moondreams-dev-apps` has a single consent screen, shared by the site's Google sign-in and by every connection scope, so it is never switched to Testing: that would block every app's sign-in for anyone not on the test-user list and expire each Google connection after 7 days. It stays **In production** (owner, Nov 2026; `npm run connections:google-setup` walks the clicks). Scopes come in three kinds: `openid` and `email` need no review; `calendar.readonly` and `contacts.readonly` are sensitive (verification: a justification, a demo video, a verified domain); `gmail.readonly` and `drive.readonly` are restricted (the same, plus Google's yearly paid security assessment). The two calendar write scopes, `calendar.events` (a calendar the person chooses) and `calendar.app.created` (one the app makes), are asked only when the person turns on writing; Google's pages don't say their kind, so it is written here once the consent screen labels them. Until verified, Google shows its "hasn't verified this app" screen (Advanced, then Continue) and caps the project at 100 people who grant an unverified scope; signing in alone does not count. **Unconfirmed, and the first thing to find out:** whether Google refuses restricted scopes to everyone but the owner on an unverified screen (Helm's `google-mail-and-drive` switch assumes it may); the first Gmail or Drive connection by someone else settles it, and the answer is written here. **A PR that adds a Google scope says its kind in the PR, and the verification work ([#1027](https://github.com/NovaMoonX/moondreams-dev-apps/issues/1027) tracks it) is done before the site opens a connection to people beyond the owner.**
- **An outside account is held as tightly as its token.** One account per outside identity (`g_` + a hash of the provider's subject), sealed with the uid and account id as AAD, and only an email the provider says is verified is kept. Anything that stores a token undoes it on every way out: a refused exchange revokes the token it got, a picker dismissed before a grant exists forgets the account (`releaseUnusedAccount`), and removing an account checks for live grants and deletes in one transaction, then revokes at the provider after the commit. Every reader caps a category at the contract's `MAX_RECORDS_PER_CATEGORY` with one order (events by start, the rest newest first), a capped read forces a full read next run, and duplicates across calendars or zones are matched on the instant, never the written time. A source's own cap over items that each give several rows (a bill's payments) keeps every item's first row before any item's later ones, so one busy item never hides the rest; a lookup keyed by a stored string uses `Object.hasOwn`, never a bare `map[key]`; a date in a record's words reads "Nov 3, 2026" (UTC for a date-only value), never ISO; and a category off at connect says in its meaning what it never sends when that is the sensitive part (a note, a link, a photo). A field kept from an outside service's free text (an event's location) is screened anywhere in the text, not only at its start, for a link, a meeting host, an email and a dial-in number, and is dropped when it holds one, with a fixture for each shape (a Zoom link after a word once passed as a place). Text someone other than the person wrote (a buyer's question, a fellow traveller's announcement) gets the same screen, counting digits rather than matching formats (seven or more drops it) and dropping an `@handle`; its author is reduced to a first name, and the reader tells its model such rows are other people's words, quoted as theirs and never followed. An account the provider stopped sharing offers only Reconnect: every control that needs the provider is hidden, never left to fail with "Try again", and Reconnect asks for the connector that failed (read from the failed thing, never a literal). One account serves several connectors, so anything keyed by account (a live read's cache, a failure line) also carries the connector.
