# Designing an app's look

Part of the repo conventions split out of `CLAUDE.md` (see its "Read before you touch" table). Rules here bind exactly as if they were in `CLAUDE.md`; a reference like `CLAUDE.md "X"` in older text means the bullet titled X, now in whichever conventions file holds it.

Each app may have its own personality on top of the shared playful base; A-List (`src/apps/a-list`) is the reference.

- **Theme through tokens, in one stylesheet.** The app owns `<app>.css` that overrides Dreamer UI's semantic color tokens (`--color-primary`, `secondary`, `accent`, `muted`, `border`, and the floating surfaces `--color-popover`, `--color-popover-foreground`, `--color-tooltip` and `--color-tooltip-foreground`, light and dark, plus `--subview-backdrop` for the subview card's backdrop; `npm run check:apps` fails a theme that omits any of them, since a popover, dropdown or tooltip would otherwise keep the site's white and zinc and clash with the app's paper) on a class (`html.a-list-theme`) that a `useXTheme()` hook adds to `<html>` only while the app is mounted, so portaled modals and drawers pick it up and other apps are untouched. Components then use semantic classes (`bg-primary`, `bg-accent`), never raw palette colors, so a later re-theme is a CSS edit.
- **A theme's paired tokens are readable in light and dark, and `npm run check:apps` measures it.** Each `--color-X` / `--color-X-foreground` pair a theme sets (primary, secondary, accent, popover, tooltip, and destructive, success and warning if overridden) is at least 4.5:1, and a dark block is read on top of the light one. The site-wide top strip (`OfflineBanner`: offline, slow, "New version ready · Restart") is the `primary` pair and its button is that pair swapped (`bg-primary-foreground! text-primary!`), never a `secondary`/`tertiary` variant, which an app's theme restyles (Pinch's makes `bg-secondary` buttons see-through, which left Restart unreadable). A new theme, or a change to its colors, is looked at on that strip too. It stays under every sheet's backdrop: someone in a sheet is mid-task, and Restart waits until they close it (owner: "the restart can wait").
- **Pick the metaphor first and let it name everything.** A-List is "AMC and the theater": red primary, marquee-gold accent, ticket/popcorn/film emoji, copy like "Did you catch it?". Write the metaphor, palette roles and shape language (what is round, what is a pill) in the app's rules file.
- **Shared pieces stay general; app flavor lives in the app.** Pills, badges (`PriorityBadge`, `ViewingStatusBadge`), tiles (`StatTile`) and subviews are built in the app's `components/shared` or `components/shell` against the app's tokens. Anything a second app needs moves to `src/components` in that PR.
- **Write the app's rules down.** Add `.claude/rules/<app>.md` (see `.claude/rules/a-list.md` for the shape: theme, shape language, emoji meaning, subview use, anything app-specific like a money input, and its recurring layouts as patterns). Update them whenever the app's design vocabulary changes, in the same PR.
- **Where a visual rule lives: the narrowest place that covers everyone who needs it.** Behaviour every app should get lives once in `src/index.css` (the `sticky-app-bar` utility, the `body` overflow clip that keeps `sticky` working, the 92dvh cap on every drawer so the page shows through at the top, `color-scheme` for native widgets); a rule that is the app's look (palette, radii, field padding, selected-pill tint, how tappable text reads) lives in `<app>.css` on the theme class; a one-off stays on the component. Never patch a cross-app problem in one app's stylesheet, and never push one app's taste into the global one. Before adding a central rule, grep `index.css` and `dreamer-ui.css` for the one that already exists; after changing one, re-drive another app's drawer, header and form and add a row to the PR's regression table, because a global change reaches every app.
- **Text color is part of the theme, not per component.** Body and row text is `text-foreground`; blue (the primary) is for real links (a URL, a place) and for filled-button labels. A tertiary or ghost button is a quiet action or a tappable row, so the app's stylesheet sets its text to the normal color (Waypoint's `button.text-primary:not([class*='bg-'])` rule in `waypoint.css` is the one to copy), instead of every call site adding `text-foreground`. A new app starts its `<app>.css` with the tokens, the radius/padding inset, the selected-pill tint and this rule, then checks a screen of rows in light and dark for blue that isn't a link. A link-style button is only for a link inside a sentence of three or more other words ("Add prices" in a line about prices), and stays underlined where there is no hover to reveal it (a phone). "Show N more" under a list is `@/components/ShowMoreButton` (a quiet left-aligned 14px muted line with a small chevron, 28px tall with a 40px hit area from a pseudo-element, never underlined; `isHeaderAction` makes it the "See all ›" on a section header's title line), and a reveal or a two-way switch on a field ("Add a note", "Put it in a group", "No amount") is `@/components/RevealChip` (a dashed chip, with its "Remove" as the same chip); a control that changes what the screen asks (a summary's "Change", "Done"), opens another view or acts in bulk is a row with a chevron, a chip or a pill, never a link (owner, round 7: "Change day or meal" under a title). A quiet state action (Reset, Tick all, Clear what you got) is an `ActionChips` chip with an icon and a bulk removal is its red hairline one (`isDestructive`); a value changed inside a drawer or sheet is a `SummaryRow` (framed with the same hairline as the builders' row) that swaps the sheet's own content in place, since `SummaryEditor` opens its own drawer or popover and a drawer never opens a second drawer. A button inside a drawer is never transparent or borderless against the sheet, a red one included: give an action row a visible edge (`border-border`) or a `bg-muted/50` block (a destructive one is `ActionRow isDestructive`).
- **Every control takes the app's shape language, ticks included.** An app whose shapes are round (Pinch) makes its checkbox a circle in its own stylesheet (one rule on the theme class for Dreamer UI's `button[role='checkbox']`: a ring unticked, the ink fill ticked, the focus ring and a 40px target kept), never per call site and never in `index.css`; a property the component sets inline (the checkbox's fill) needs `!important` in the theme rule, with a comment saying why. Read the computed `border-radius` (at least half the width), fill and tick color in light and dark, checked, unchecked and disabled: the ticked fill has at least 3:1 contrast with the surface and the tick at least 3:1 with the fill, `:focus-visible` shows an outline, and the hit area with any pseudo-element is at least 40×40. A mark that looks like a control follows it too: in a round app a done or status tick is the round `@/components/TickMark` (`variant='soft'` for a mark that repeats down a list, the ink one for a single done row), never ✅ or ☑️ (an emoji draws a square box), and a request to make every X round is answered by a census of everything that draws the shape (controls, emoji, icons, shared components) on every screen, not by the control alone (owner, round 7: the square ones were ✅ emoji).
- **Badge contrast: status badges are soft tints (`secondary`, `accent`, `muted`) or outlines.** A solid fill (`primary`, `warning`, `destructive`) is for the one thing on the screen that needs action, never for a status that repeats down a list. Check a badge's text against its fill and neighbors; if it jumps out more than the title beside it, soften it. An attention tint covers only the last stretch (a date that is today or tomorrow): a state that lasts most of an item's life is not a warning, and a count's words say the window ("2 to eat by tomorrow").
- **Colors must not fight their emoji.** Don't put an emoji on a background of its own hue (🔥 on red); give it a neutral or complementary chip. Solid red/primary fills are for the one primary action on a screen, not for status chips. A selected pill is solid, so it carries no emoji of a matching hue (a 📍 on red); the section's label carries the icon instead.

## The site's own screens

Home, the account page, the admin dashboard and every other screen that belongs to the site rather than to one app share one look, and the owner's words for it are **clean, organized, fresh, modern, slick** (owner, the admin refresh: "the visuals are falling flat for me"). An app keeps its own personality inside its pages; the site's screens don't borrow one.

- **Surfaces, not lines.** Content sits on cards (`bg-card`, a hairline `border-border/60`, `rounded-2xl`, a soft shadow at most) on a quietly tinted page, with generous space between groups; a long grey list of hairline-divided rows on a white page is the look to move away from. Rows inside a card keep their dividers.
- **A clear hierarchy of three sizes.** A page title, a section title (`text-base font-semibold`, sentence case) and body text; tiny letter-spaced capitals are kept for one eyebrow at most per screen.
- **Status is colour and shape, used sparingly.** A small dot or ring beside a name (green working, amber quiet, the primary for waiting on the person), with the word beside it; one solid fill per screen for the one thing that needs action.
- **What waits on the person is actionable where it is shown.** A card that says something needs the owner carries the next step as a button on the card ("Answer in Claude", "Open the PR"), not only a row that opens a sheet; the sheet adds detail, never the only way to act.
- **Numbers are tiles.** A screen's few headline figures are `StatTile`s in a two-column grid on a phone, above the lists they summarize.
- **Checked like a product screen.** Phone first (320, 390), light and dark, before and after shots in the PR.
